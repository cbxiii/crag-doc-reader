# CORAL REEF TROUBLES IN AMERICAN SAMOA

DMWR biological report series no. 66

Like other South Pacific Islands, American Samoa finds itself caught in the whirl of rapid environmental change.

--- SENTENCE 2 ---
Its human population growth is skyrocketing (see Figure 1), while its natural resources are declining.

--- SENTENCE 3 ---
Coral reefs in American Samoa were severely damaged in recent years by natural disasters and pollution.

--- SENTENCE 4 ---
The reefs were hit by a major infestation of the corallivorous starfish Acanthaster planci in the later 1970s, and by devastating hurricanes in 1990 and 1991.

--- SENTENCE 5 ---
More recently, the reefs experienced a major coral bleaching episode in 1994, which affected up to 80 per cent of the coral at some locations.

--- SENTENCE 6 ---
As a result, live coral coverage around Tutuila Island has dropped from about 60 per cent to 10 per cent within the past two decades.

--- SENTENCE 7 ---
Coupled with this are major changes in the species composition and abundance of reef fishes.

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
In the past 14 years, the species composition of the 20 most abundant species has changed dramatically and fish numbers have dropped 75 per cent (see Figure 2 on page 34).

--- SENTENCE 9 ---
The 1994 subsistence catches of reef fish were the lowest on record.

--- SENTENCE 10 ---
A slow recovery is underway, however.

--- SENTENCE 11 ---
Biologists note that many coral recruits are present on the reefs.

--- SENTENCE 12 ---
What concerns us now is the interference with the natural recovery process by human disturbances.

!

--- SENTENCE 13 ---
#### Sedimentation

After every heavy rainfall, chocolate-coloured plumes of sediment are flushed out of the streams and onto the coral reefs.

--- SENTENCE 14 ---
Poor urban and agricultural land-use practices are presumably the sources of this sediment.

--- SENTENCE 15 ---
There is ample evidence in the literature to indicate that such high levels of sedimentation adversely impact coral growth, survival and recruitment.

## Eutrophication

Only about 20 per cent of homes in American Samoa are hooked to a sewer line.

--- SENTENCE 16 ---
An abundance of filamentous algae in nearshore waters indicates that nutrient enrichment may be another problem for the coral to contend with.

--- SENTENCE 17 ---
### Pollution

Recent surveys discovered that local nearshore fish in some areas are contaminated with toxic substances, particularly heavy metals.

--- SENTENCE 18 ---
The most serious problem occurs in Pago Pago Harbor, where a health advisory notice was issued warning the public not to eat any fish caught there.

--- SENTENCE 19 ---
Studies to locate the sources of this contamination continue.

## Overfishing

Some highly prized resources such as giant clams (*Tridacna* spp.) have been overharvested.

--- SENTENCE 20 ---
One species is locally extinct and the other two are scarce.

--- SENTENCE 21 ---
Several of American Samoa's government agencies have developed regulations, guidelines and educational activities aimed at reducing these impacts.

--- SENTENCE 30 ---
Interagency meetings produced the following list of needs for American Samoa:

- A planning workshop with scientific experts to formulate realistic management strategies, suitable for small island staff, to monitor and protect reef health;
- Quantitative assessments of reef health;
- Determination of causes of reef fishery declines;

- Reduction of non-point sources of sedimentation and eutrophication;
- Increase in public awareness of reef value and vulnerability;
- Better enforcement of existing regulations.

--- SENTENCE 31 ---
Red warning flags of environmental stress are flying high in American Samoa, but the situation is not hopeless.
