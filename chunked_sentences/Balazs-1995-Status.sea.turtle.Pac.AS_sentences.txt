# Status of Sea Turtles in the Central Pacific Ocean

--- SENTENCE 15 ---
Except for the Hawaiian Archipelago, sea turtle populations in the Central Pacific Ocean and other areas of Polynesia have not been systematically surveyed and only limited information exists on their occurrence and present survival status.

--- SENTENCE 16 ---
This report will sammarize and review what is known for a number of locations within the region, specifically the Hawaiian Archipelago, Line Islands, Phoenix Islands, Cook Islands, American Samoa, Western Samoa, Tokelau, Tuvalu, Wake, Johnston, Howland, and Baker (Figure 1).

--- SENTENCE 17 ---
While the information for most of these areas is clearly inadequate, there is nevertheless evidence to indicate that the numbers of turtles have declined within historical times.

--- SENTENCE 18 ---
At those islands with indigenous human populations, the traditional conservation systems that served to protect turtles and other marine resources from overexploitation have deteriorated considerably, and in some cases vanished altogether.

--- SENTENCE 19 ---
Three interrelated factors contributing to this breakdown have been the introduction of money economies, the decline of traditional authority, and the imposition of new laws and practices by colonial powers (Johannes 1978).

--- SENTENCE 20 ---
In Polynesian societies, sea turtles are known to have played an important role in certain religious ceremonies, in mythology and art, in the production of implements and medication, and as high protein food sources generally reserved for chiefs and priests (Buck 1932; Emory 1933, 1947; Emory, Bonk, and Sinoto 1968; Kalakaua 1888; Pukui and Elbert 1971).

--- SENTENCE 21 ---
Of the islands covered in this report, only the ones under United States jurisdiction currently have governmental regulations for sea turtles.

--- SENTENCE 22 ---
Under the U.S. Endangered Species Act, all sea turtles at these U.S. areas are fully protected.

--- SENTENCE 25 ---
The olive ridley, Lepidochelys olivacea, and the loggerhead, Caretta caretta, have been recorded, but only as rare visitors.

--- SENTENCE 26 ---
The Hawaiian hawksbill population is small and only known to occur in coastal waters of the 8 main and inhabited islands at the southeastern end of the 2,450-km-long archipelago.

--- SENTENCE 27 ---
Several nestings have been documented on the island of Hawaii where black volcanic sand beaches are utilized.

--- SENTENCE 28 ---
A single nesting has also been recorded on the island of Molokai (Ernst and Barbour 1972).

--- SENTENCE 29 ---
Leatherbacks are regularly sighted in offshore waters at the southeastern end of the archipelago, but nesting does not take place.

--- SENTENCE 30 ---
During August 1979, at least 10 leatherbacks ranging from 60 to 120 cm in carapace length were sighted in pelagic waters to the northwest of the Hawaiian Archipelago between 40° to 43°N and 175° to 179°W (G. Naftel, in litt.).

--- SENTENCE 31 ---
Green turtles are by far the most abundant of Hawaiian sea turtles, with mixed aggregations of adults

and immature individuals larger than 35 cm residing in coastal waters throughout the archipelago where they feed on several kinds of benthic algae.

--- SENTENCE 32 ---
In excess of 90 percent of all nesting occurs on 6 small sand islands at French Frigate Shoals (23°45'N, 166°10'W), a 35 km long atoll situated in the middle of the archipelago.

--- SENTENCE 33 ---
Tagging has demonstrated that long-distance migrations to this site are periodically undertaken by adults from numerous resident foraging areas, all of which are within the Hawaiian chain (Figure 2; Balazs 1976a, 1979).

--- SENTENCE 34 ---
Hawaiian green turtles therefore appear to be genetically isolated from other populations in the Pacific.

--- SENTENCE 35 ---
Systematic monitoring of the breeding colony at French Frigate Shoals was initiated in 1973 and has continued during each subsequent year.

--- SENTENCE 36 ---
The number of females nesting annually has been found to fluctuate considerably, with the range extending from 94 in 1976 to 248 in 1978 (Figure 3).

--- SENTENCE 37 ---
No population trends are apparent for the 7-year study period.

--- SENTENCE 38 ---
The production of hatchlings since 1973 has ranged from approximately 12,500 in 1976 to 32,900 in 1978.

--- SENTENCE 41 ---
However, predation by tiger sharks (*Galeocerdo cuvier*) on both adults and immature turtles throughout the chain may be substantial (Balazs 1979).

--- SENTENCE 42 ---
Prior to 1973, the annual breeding colony at French Frigate Shoals was incorrectly estimated by previous workers to contain as many as 2,600 to 5,200 turtles (Hendrickson 1969; Amerson 1971).

--- SENTENCE 43 ---
Hawaiian green turtles exhibit the rare behavioral trait among sea turtles of coming ashore to bask or rest, but only at certain undisturbed sand beaches or rock ledges in the uninhabited Northwestern Hawaiian Islands (Wetmore 1925; Balazs 1976b; Eliot 1978; Lipman 1978).

--- SENTENCE 44 ---
This behavior provides access for the tagging of males as well as females, both at the breeding grounds and at a number of resident foraging areas.

--- SENTENCE 45 ---
However, caution is being exercised in these research activities so that normal behavioral patterns will not be adversely affected.

--- SENTENCE 46 ---
Large numbers of green turtles were commercially exploited in the Hawaiian Archipelago until 1974 when the State of Hawaii adopted a protective regulation banning this activity.

--- SENTENCE 47 ---
In 1909 all of the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands except Midway were designated as a Bird Reservation, which in 1940 became known as the Hawaiian Islands National Wildlife Refuge.

--- SENTENCE 48 ---
However, the exploitation of turtles in these areas, particularly at French Frigate Shoals, periodically continued until at least 1969 (Amerson 1971; Balazs 1975a).

--- SENTENCE 49 ---
Of concern at the present time is the well-documented, drastic decline of the foraging and basking aggregations in the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands at Laysan Island, Lis-

ianski Island and, to a lesser extent, at Pearl and Hermes Reef.

--- SENTENCE 50 ---
Furthermore, the forthcoming development of various commercial fisheries in this segment of the chain represents a potential threat to the remaining aggregations.

--- SENTENCE 51 ---
Terrestrial areas in the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands are under review by the Fish and Wildlife Service for designation as Critical Habitat under the U.S. Endangered Species Act (Dodd 1978; see also Balazs 1978).

# Howland and Baker (United States)

Howland (0°48'N, 176°38'W) and Baker (0°13'N, 176°28'W), 2 low coral islands, were designated National Wildlife Refuges in 1974 and are now uninhabited.

--- SENTENCE 66 ---
Turtles were reported to be "abundant" in the waters around Howland by residents present in May and June of 1935 (Bryan 1974).

--- SENTENCE 67 ---
No information on turtles exists for Baker.

--- SENTENCE 68 ---
Feral cats, which are known in some areas to be predators of hatchlings and eggs, are present on both of these islands.

#### Line Islands

All of the Line Islands are low coral islands and atolls.

--- SENTENCE 69 ---
The Northern Line Islands consist of Kingman Reef, Palmyra and Jarvis, under the jurisdiction of the United States; and Washington, Fanning and Christmas under the jurisdiction of the newly independent nation of Kiribati (formerly the Gilbert Islands).

--- SENTENCE 70 ---
The Southern Line Islands are also under the jurisdiction of Kiribati and consist of Malden, Starbuck, Vostok, Caroline, and Flint, all of which are now uninhabited.

--- SENTENCE 71 ---
Information on sea turtles exists only for the following locations.

--- SENTENCE 72 ---
• Palmyra (5°53'N, 162°05'W).

--- SENTENCE 73 ---
From 1958 to 1965, green turtles were periodically seen in shallow waters at the eastern side of the atoll.

--- SENTENCE 74 ---
On one of these occasions a group of 11 adults was observed foraging together (P. Helfrich and J. Naughton, personal com-

munication).

--- SENTENCE 75 ---
Similar observations have also been made during recent years (M. Vitousek, personal communication).

--- SENTENCE 76 ---
There are no reports of nesting.

--- SENTENCE 77 ---
Algal collections at Palmyra have included *Pterocladia*, a known food source of green turtles (Dawson 1959; Balazs 1979).

--- SENTENCE 78 ---
The atoll is now used as a copra plantation and has a small resident human population.

--- SENTENCE 79 ---
Along with Midway and Wake, the U.S. government is considering Palmyra as an international storage site for nuclear wastes.

- Jarvis (0°23'S, 160°01'W).

--- SENTENCE 80 ---
A low level of nesting, apparently involving green turtles, was recorded along the western coast of Jarvis by residents present in August of 1935 (Bryan 1974).

--- SENTENCE 81 ---
The island was designated a National Wildlife Refuge in 1974 and is now uninhabited.

--- SENTENCE 82 ---
Feral cats are present on the island.

--- SENTENCE 83 ---
- Fanning (3°52'N, 159°20'W).

--- SENTENCE 84 ---
Turtles were reported to "abound" at Fanning in the 1850s (Burnett 1910, quoted by Wiens 1962).

--- SENTENCE 85 ---
The atoll has been continuously inhabited since 1852 and used principally as a copra plantation.

--- SENTENCE 86 ---
A small number of turtles are regularly sighted in the lagoon, and a low level of nesting still takes place.

--- SENTENCE 87 ---
The residents capture turtles whenever possible.
- Christmas (1°59'N, 157°30'W).

--- SENTENCE 88 ---
When Captain James Cook discovered uninhabited Christmas atoll in late December of 1777, between 200 and 300 green turtles were captured during the 8-day visit (Beaglehole 1967).

--- SENTENCE 89 ---
Turtles were taken both in the shallow lagoon and on the beaches, with weights ranging from 20 to 90 kg.

--- SENTENCE 90 ---
Publicity resulting from Captain Cook's visit caused numerous whaling vessels to stop at the atoll for provisions (Bryan 1942).

--- SENTENCE 91 ---
Green turtles were still abundant in 1838 (Tresilian 1838, quoted by Wiens 1962).

--- SENTENCE 92 ---
Christmas has been inhabited and used as a copra plantation since 1902.

--- SENTENCE 93 ---
Nuclear weapons' testing was conducted over the atoll by the British from 1956 to 1958, and by the United States in 1962 (Inder 1978).

--- SENTENCE 94 ---
In 1975 a visitor noted that some nesting was still taking place, but no details were available (D. Crear, personal communication).
- Vostok (10°06'S, 152°23'W).

--- SENTENCE 95 ---
In June of 1965, Clapp and Sibley (1971a) saw several turtles in the waters surrounding Vostok, but no signs of nesting were found.

--- SENTENCE 96 ---
M. Vitousek (personal communication) was informed that numerous turtle tracks were seen on the beaches during a visit made in recent years, but no details are available.

--- SENTENCE 97 ---
Vostok is only 1.2 km².
- Malden (4°1'S, 154°58'W).

--- SENTENCE 98 ---
No evidence of turtles has been found during several recent visits to Malden (E. Vitousek, personal communication).

--- SENTENCE 99 ---
- Caroline (9°58'S, 150°14'W).

--- SENTENCE 100 ---
Dixon (1884) reported that turtles were seen at Caroline, but not in great numbers.

--- SENTENCE 101 ---
No turtles were seen by Clapp and Sibley (1971b) during a 2-day visit in June 1965.

## Phoenix Islands

The Phoenix group is under the jurisdiction of Kiribati and consists of 8 low coral islands and atolls.

--- SENTENCE 102 ---
Only Canton is now inhabited.

--- SENTENCE 103 ---
- Canton (2°50'S, 171°43'W).

--- SENTENCE 104 ---
Green turtles nest along the northern, eastern and western shores of Canton throughout the year, but greater numbers are present during October and November (Balazs 1975b).

--- SENTENCE 105 ---
The total annual number of nesting females using the atoll may involve as many as 200 turtles.

--- SENTENCE 106 ---
Large populations of ghost crabs (Ocypode spp.) and hermit crabs (Coenobita perlitus) are present and probably prey heavily on hatchlings.

--- SENTENCE 107 ---
It is not uncommon for nesting females and hatchlings to become disoriented and travel inland where they die of hyperthermia.

--- SENTENCE 108 ---
Adult males and females in groups of up to 40 individuals have been observed foraging close to shore in water less than 50-cm deep (J. Hass, in litt.).

--- SENTENCE 109 ---
Algal collections from Canton have included Caulerpa racemosa, Codium arabicum, Gelidium pusillum, and Pterocladia (Dawson 1959).
- Enderbury (3°07'S, 171°03'W).

--- SENTENCE 110 ---
Green turtles nest along Enderbury's western and eastern shores (Balazs, 1975b; J. Keys, in litt.).

--- SENTENCE 111 ---
King (1973) listed this island as one of the most important nesting sites for green turtles in the Central Pacific.

--- SENTENCE 112 ---
Two Korean fishing vessels were wrecked on the island during recent years.

--- SENTENCE 113 ---
- Phoenix (3°43'S, 170°43'W).

--- SENTENCE 114 ---
Turtle bones were found on Phoenix during a visit in 1924 and nesting was presumed to take place (Bryan, 1942).

--- SENTENCE 115 ---
Feral rabbits are present on the island.

--- SENTENCE 116 ---
- Birnie (3°35'S, 171°31'W).

--- SENTENCE 117 ---
During a low-altitude overflight of the Phoenix group in January 1978, the author found the beaches of Birnie to be covered with turtle tracks.

--- SENTENCE 118 ---
Birnie is the smallest island in the Phoenix group (0.5 by 1.25 km) and the only one that has never been inhabited or mined for guano.

--- SENTENCE 119 ---
- Hull (4°30'S, 172°10'W).

--- SENTENCE 120 ---
Green turtles nest along the northeastern and southeastern shores of Hull (Balazs 1975b; J. Keys, in litt.).

--- SENTENCE 121 ---
When the U.S. Exploring Expedition visited this atoll in August 1840, a Frenchman and 11 Tahitians were found to have been stationed there to catch turtles (Wilkes 1845).

--- SENTENCE 122 ---
In May 1974 large numbers of dead fish and an adult male green turtle were found washed ashore from inside the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 123 ---
The cause of this mortality could not be determined.
- Sydney (4°27'S, 171°16'W).

--- SENTENCE 124 ---
Turtle tracks have been observed on Sydney's northwestern shore (Balazs, 1975b).

--- SENTENCE 125 ---
Evidence of trespassing by crewmembers of foreign fishing vessels has been found on this atoll during recent years.

--- SENTENCE 126 ---
- Gardner (4°40'S, 174°32'W).

--- SENTENCE 127 ---
Turtle tracks have been observed on Gardner's southwestern shore (Balazs 1975b).

--- SENTENCE 128 ---
Evidence of trespassing has also been found on this atoll.

--- SENTENCE 129 ---
• McKean (3°36'S, 174°08'W).

--- SENTENCE 130 ---
No information on turtles exists for McKean.

--- SENTENCE 131 ---
Several foreign fishing vessels have been wrecked on the island.

# American Samoa (United States)

American Samoa consists of the mountainous volcanic islands of Tutuila and the Manua group (Ofu, Olosega, Tau), and Swains and Rose Atoll which are of coral origin.

--- SENTENCE 133 ---
Approximately 94 percent of the Polynesian inhabitants reside on Tutuila (port city—Pago Pago).

--- SENTENCE 134 ---
Rose is the only uninhabited island in the group.

--- SENTENCE 135 ---
Certain terrestrial areas, including Swains and Rose, are under review by the Fish and Wildlife Service for designation as Critical Habitat under the U.S. Endangered Species Act (Dodd 1978).

- Tutuila (14°16'S, 170°40'W) and Manua group (14°10'S, 169°35'W).

--- SENTENCE 136 ---
Green turtles and hawksbills occur in the waters surrounding these islands, but apparently only in small numbers.

--- SENTENCE 137 ---
There is some indication that the hawksbill is the more abundant species.

--- SENTENCE 138 ---
Occasional nesting on isolated beaches is thought to take place (Coffman 1977; S. Swerdloff, W. Pedro and R. Wass, personal communication).
- Swains (11°03'S, 171°05'W).

--- SENTENCE 139 ---
Green turtles and hawksbills are known to nest at Swains (Swerdloff, personal communication).

--- SENTENCE 140 ---
Turtle eggs were observed being gathered by the native inhabitants during July and August 1963 (Pedro, personal communication).

--- SENTENCE 141 ---
The atoll is only 2 km in diameter.
- Rose Atoll (14°33'S, 168°09'W).

--- SENTENCE 142 ---
Green turtles, and probably some hawksbills, nest on the islets (Rose and Sand) at Rose Atoll.

--- SENTENCE 143 ---
An account in the 1800s stated that large numbers of turtles nest during August and September, and that numerous sharks prey on the hatchlings (Graeffe 1873, quoted by Hirth 1971a).

--- SENTENCE 144 ---
On 7 October 1970, Hirth (1971a) counted 35 and 301 nesting pits of varying age on Sand and Rose Islets, respectively.

--- SENTENCE 145 ---
Fishermen in Pago Pago confirmed that the peak nesting season is August and September.

--- SENTENCE 146 ---
On a low-altitude overflight in October 1974, 75 adult turtles were counted within the lagoon (P. Sekora, personal communication).

--- SENTENCE 147 ---
During a 5-day visit in May 1976, only 3 adults and 1 immature green turtle were observed, and no nesting took place (Coffman 1977).

--- SENTENCE 148 ---
During a daytime visit on 29 March 1978, Coleman (1978) recorded 1 recently excavated pit on Rose and 4 that he estimated to be 1-month old.

--- SENTENCE 149 ---
Other older pits were noted, as well as a single adult green turtle in the lagoon and the rib bones of a turtle on Sand Islet.

--- SENTENCE 150 ---
Numerous black-tipped sharks (Carcharbinus melanopterus)

--- SENTENCE 151 ---
20- to 40-cm long were present.

--- SENTENCE 152 ---
Direct observations of predation on hatchlings by rats have been made during recent years (Swerdloff,

personal communication), but the extent and significance are unknown.

--- SENTENCE 153 ---
Mayor (1921) was the first author to record rats at Rose.

--- SENTENCE 154 ---
Hirth (1971a) stated that Rose Islet "swarms with rats (possibly Rattus exulans)."

--- SENTENCE 155 ---
Coleman (1978) found that rats were "extremely abundant" and thought that black rats (Rattus rattus) might be present.

--- SENTENCE 156 ---
Following the recommendations of Hirth (1971a), Rose Atoll was designated a National Wildlife Refuge in 1974 (see also Rockefeller and Rockefeller 1974).

#### Western Samoa

Western Samoa is an independent nation consisting of 2 large islands of volcanic origin (Savaii and Upolu) and several offshore islets.

--- SENTENCE 157 ---
The islands are located between 13° to 15°S and 168° to 173°W. Approximately 72 percent of the 152,000 Polynesian inhabitants reside on Upolu Island.

--- SENTENCE 158 ---
Green turtles and hawksbills occur in the surrounding waters of both Savaii and Upolu.

--- SENTENCE 159 ---
The green turtle has been reported by fishermen to be the more abundant (Hirth 1971a).

--- SENTENCE 160 ---
It is uncertain whether this species nests in the area.

--- SENTENCE 161 ---
Hawksbills are known to nest, but now only on the offshore islets of Namua, Nuutele, and Nuulua located at the western end of Upolu.

--- SENTENCE 162 ---
The nesting season extends from October to June, with most activity occurring in January and February.

--- SENTENCE 163 ---
Nesting tracks counted by Witzell (1972a) suggest that not more than 45 females use these beaches each season.

--- SENTENCE 164 ---
The number of hawksbills is believed to have declined considerably, due mostly to human exploitation of eggs and nesting females (Witzell 1972a, 1972b, 1974).

--- SENTENCE 165 ---
The coasts of both Upolu and Savaii were reported to have abounded with turtles in the early 1800s (Williams 1837).

--- SENTENCE 166 ---
In 1971 a hatchery project was initiated by the Fisheries Division and 2 U.S. Peace Corps volunteers in an attempt to replenish the hawksbill population.

--- SENTENCE 167 ---
This effort has continued until the present time.

--- SENTENCE 168 ---
During each nesting season as many freshly laid eggs as possible are transferred from the 3 islets and reburied at a protected facility on the adjacent mainland shore of the Aleipata district.

--- SENTENCE 169 ---
Hatchlings are held for up to 3 months in concrete tanks before being released into offshore waters.

--- SENTENCE 170 ---
Marginal scutes have been notched for identification purposes.

--- SENTENCE 175 ---
Educational programs have also been periodically conducted to inform the populace of the need to conserve sea turtles (Witzell 1972b, 1974).

--- SENTENCE 176 ---
Based on the advice of an FAO sea turtle consultant, plans were prepared for a ranching industry in which hawksbill hatchlings would be grown to a size suitable for stuffing and export to Japan (Banner 1971).

--- SENTENCE 177 ---

# Tokelau

Tokelau is a New Zealand dependency consisting of three atolls (Atafu, Fakaofo, Nukunonu) located between 8° to 10°S and 171° to 173°W.

--- SENTENCE 180 ---
The total Polynesian population is 1,600.

--- SENTENCE 181 ---
Hirth (1971a) reported that green turtles and, to a much lesser extent, hawksbills nest in Tokelau during September and October, but that their numbers were rapidly declining.

--- SENTENCE 182 ---
In 1977, 1 of the remaining nesting sites was along the southern portion of Taulagapapa Islet at Nukunonu Atoll (N. Walton, personal communication).

#### Tuvalu

Tuvalu, formerly known as the Ellice Islands, is a newly independent nation comprised of 9 coral islands and atolls located between 5° to 10°S and 176° to 180°E.

--- SENTENCE 183 ---
From north to south, the group consists of Nanumea, Niutao, Nanumanga, Nui, Vaitupu, Nukufetau, Funafuti, Nukulaelae, and Niulakita.

--- SENTENCE 184 ---
The total Polynesian population is estimated to be 9,000.

--- SENTENCE 185 ---
The capital of the group, Funafuti Atoll (8°30'S, 179°10'E), is 18 by 25 km and contains 30 islets (Inder 1978).

Hedley (1896), quoted by Wiens (1962), stated that "the green turtle was the only one found at Funafuti," but no additional information was provided.

--- SENTENCE 186 ---
Carr (1965) listed the atoll as a minor green turtle nesting area, while Hirth (1971b) included it among important nesting sites in the western hemisphere.

--- SENTENCE 187 ---
No other information on turtles is known to exist for Funafuti or other members of the group.

--- SENTENCE 188 ---
However, in 1972 an intense tropical cyclone struck Funafuti and deposited an 18-km-long rampart of coral rubble along the atoll's southeastern outer reef (Maragos, Baines, and Beveridge 1973).

--- SENTENCE 189 ---
The impact of this new formation on

available nesting habitat could be substantial.

--- SENTENCE 190 ---
The coinage of Tuvalu includes a \$1-piece displaying the green turtle.

--- SENTENCE 191 ---
Furthermore, the commemorative stamps issue in 1976 features a leatherback and the uninhabited atoll of Niulakita.

--- SENTENCE 192 ---
This would suggest that leatherbacks either nest or are sighted in the area.

#### Cook Islands

The Cook group, a self-governing state associated with New Zealand, consists of 15 volcanic islands and atolls located between 9° to 23°S and 156° to 167°W.

--- SENTENCE 194 ---
The islands of volcanic origin include Rarotonga, Aitutaki, Atiu, Mitiaro, Mauke, and Mangaia, while the coral atolls, most of which are in the northern portion of the group, include Palmerston, Suwarrow, Pukapuka (not to be confused with Pukapuka in the Tuamotu Archipelago), Nassau, Manihiki, Rakahanga, Penrhyn (Tongareva), Manuae, and Takutea.

--- SENTENCE 195 ---
There are approximately 18,000 Polynesian inhabitants, 54 percent of which live on Rarotonga.

--- SENTENCE 196 ---
Information on the occurrence of turtles exists for the following locations.

--- SENTENCE 197 ---
• Palmerston (18°04'S, 163°10'W).

--- SENTENCE 198 ---
Powell (1957), quoted by Weins (1962), indicated that green turtle eggs were "fairly plentiful" at Palmerston and that both turtles and eggs were frequently used for food by the 85 inhabitants.

--- SENTENCE 199 ---
Carr (1965) considered Palmerston to be a major Pacific nesting site for green turtles.

--- SENTENCE 200 ---
Although Hirth (1971b) included the atoll in a list of important nesting sites in the western hemisphere, it was stressed that the number of turtles involved was unknown and that the situation warranted immediate research attention.

--- SENTENCE 201 ---
In 1977 each family on the atoll had a tradition of raising 15 hatchlings in floating cages for 1 to 3 months before releasing them as a restocking effort (S. Kavakana and D. Brandon, personal communication).

--- SENTENCE 202 ---
This practice apparently started in the 1950s following recommendations offered by Powell (1957).

--- SENTENCE 203 ---
However, other reports in 1977 indicated that, instead of being released, many of the turtles were gutted, injected with formalin, and sent as curios to relatives in New Zealand.

--- SENTENCE 204 ---
From 1972 to 1977 a decline in the number of nesting turtles was observed by the inhabitants, thereby prompting the local Island Council to prohibit the use of spearguns (T. Wichman, personal communication).

--- SENTENCE 205 ---
Approximately 4 to 5 turtles are sent from Palmerston each year to the market in Rarotonga where the meat is not readily accepted by the residents and sells for only US\$0.45 to 0.90 per kg.

--- SENTENCE 206 ---
Large shells, however, bring US\$50.00 or more in the growing tourist trade.

--- SENTENCE 207 ---
Hatchlings were reported to be present at Palmerston in January (Brandon 1977), but the range of months in which nesting takes place is unknown.

--- SENTENCE 208 ---
Prior to 1862 Palmerston was uninhabited.

--- SENTENCE 209 ---
In that

- year an Englishman (William Marsters) and 3 women from Penrhyn settled on the atoll and founded the colony that now exists (Bryan 1942).

--- SENTENCE 210 ---
- Pukapuka (10°53'S, 165°49'W).

--- SENTENCE 211 ---
Green turtles and some hawksbills nest on one of the uninhabited islets at Pukapuka.

--- SENTENCE 212 ---
Turtles and eggs that are taken from this location must be shared among the native inhabitants of the atoll (D. Clark, personal communication).

--- SENTENCE 213 ---
- Manihiki (10°25'S, 161°01'W).

--- SENTENCE 214 ---
Green turtles, and possibly some hawksbills, nest at Manihiki.

--- SENTENCE 215 ---
The natives take both the turtles and eggs for food.

--- SENTENCE 216 ---
Hatchlings are also raised for a few months and preserved with formalin for shipment to relatives in New Zealand (T. Wichman, personal communication).

--- SENTENCE 217 ---
- Rakahanga (10°02'S, 161°05'W).

--- SENTENCE 218 ---
Both Carr (1965) and Hirth (1971b) list Rakahanga as a nesting site for green turtles.

--- SENTENCE 219 ---
Gill (1876), quoted by Wiens (1962), stated that "Several species of turtle—loggerhead, hawksbill, green turtle, etc.,—are very plentiful on Rakahanga in the breeding season."

--- SENTENCE 220 ---
Although loggerheads (as well as leatherbacks) have occasionally been sighted in the Cook Islands (Brandon, 1977), this is the only known report of nesting.

--- SENTENCE 221 ---
The northwestern point of Rakahanga is named Te Mata

--- SENTENCE 222 ---
i Pahonu and relates to sea turtles.

--- SENTENCE 223 ---
- Penrhyn (9°0'S, 157°59'W).

--- SENTENCE 224 ---
Green turtles and some hawksbills are known to nest at Penrhyn and forage in the adjacent waters.

--- SENTENCE 225 ---
During 1976 between 40 and 50 turtles of unknown sizes were taken principally for their shell.

--- SENTENCE 226 ---
A few were sent to Rarotonga, but most were used for trading with Japanese, Korean, and Taiwanese fishing vessels that illegally visit the atoll (Brandon 1977 and personal communication).
- Suwarrow (13°15'S, 163°06'W).

--- SENTENCE 227 ---
Brandon (1977) lists Suwarrow as a nesting site for turtles, but no details are provided.

--- SENTENCE 228 ---
One of the islets is named Turtle Island.

--- SENTENCE 229 ---
Only one person lives on the atoll.

--- SENTENCE 230 ---
- Manuae (19°16'S, 158°58'W).

--- SENTENCE 231 ---
In May of 1975 the Cook Islands' Government donated Manuae for use as the first World Marine Park (Allen 1975; see also Shadbolt 1967).

--- SENTENCE 232 ---
However, questions of ownership of the atoll have prevented this action from being carried out.

--- SENTENCE 233 ---
Manuae is listed by Brandon (1977) as a nesting site for turtles, but details are not provided.
- Takutea (19°49'S, 158°18'W).

--- SENTENCE 234 ---
Takutea is also listed as a nesting site for turtles by Brandon (1977), but again no details are provided.

--- SENTENCE 235 ---
From 1974 to 1977, studies on the rearing of green turtles as a village industry were conducted at Rarotonga with financial support from the South Pacific Commission (Baird 1975; see also Powell 1957; Anonymous 1972).

--- SENTENCE 236 ---
As part of this project, laboratory experiments were also carried out at the University of the South Pacific in Fiji (Raj 1975).

--- SENTENCE 237 ---
The findings of this work were similar to those made in Western Samoa, in that problems of disease and a suitable, inex-

pensive food supply could not be resolved (Brandon 1977, Balazs 1977, Anonymous 1978).

#### Recommendations

The survival status of sea turtle populations occurring in the areas covered by this report can be enhanced through implementation of the following recommendations.

- 1.

--- SENTENCE 238 ---
Where appropriate, island governments should attempt to reinforce the traditional conservation systems that formerly served as a buffer for sea turtles.

--- SENTENCE 239 ---
The absence of governmental regulations for most of the areas covered makes it imperative that some protective action be undertaken, but in close consultation with native inhabitants.
- 2.

--- SENTENCE 240 ---
Known rookeries should be intensively monitored during the peak period of at least 1, but preferably several, nesting seasons to determine the number of females present.

--- SENTENCE 241 ---
As a minimum, this should include Rose Atoll, Enderbury, Birnie, Palmerston, Penrhyn, and the offshore islets of Namua, Nuutele, and Nuulua in Western Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 242 ---
Tagging with durable tags (see Balazs, this volume) should be carried out as an integral part of this work to gain some insight into the occurrence of international migrations, hence shared usage of resources.
- 3.

--- SENTENCE 243 ---
Based on the results of Recommendation 2, and in harmony with Recommendation 1, governments should be encouraged to designate certain islands and their surrounding waters as sea turtle sanctuaries.

--- SENTENCE 244 ---
This would be a relatively uncomplicated undertaking in the Phoenix group and Southern Line Islands where most of the islands are currently uninhabited and unused by man.

--- SENTENCE 245 ---
The illegal landings on uninhabited islands by foreign fishing vessels should be investigated by an international task force.

--- SENTENCE 246 ---
Such violations constitute a worldwide problem and usually involve the theft and destruction of natural resources that include sea turtles.

--- SENTENCE 247 ---
A comprehensive appraisal should be undertaken of the 9-year-old hawksbill hatchery in Western Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 248 ---
The results of this little-known conservation experiment may be of considerable value to worldwide efforts aimed at saving endangered hawksbill populations.

--- SENTENCE 249 ---
The predation on hatchlings by rats at Rose Atoll should be quantified and control methods implemented if the conditions warrant.
- 7.

--- SENTENCE 250 ---
Military agencies of the U.S. government administering islands covered in this report should undertake

a thorough investigation of the aggregations of sea turtles occurring at such sites.

--- SENTENCE 251 ---
This would include Johnston Atoll, Kingman Reef, Wake, and Midway in the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands.

--- SENTENCE 252 ---
Any plans for the storage of nuclear wastes and other highly toxic substances on Pacific islands should include a careful evaluation of the potential impact on both nesting and foraging aggregations of turtles.

8.

--- SENTENCE 253 ---
The Hawaiian Islands National Wildlife Refuge should encompass a substantial amount of surrounding marine habitat to serve as a buffer against the forthcoming development of commercial fisheries.
