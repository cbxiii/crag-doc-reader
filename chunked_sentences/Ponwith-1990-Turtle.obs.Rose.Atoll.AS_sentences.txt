### SUMMARY

Nightly surveys of Rose Island were conducted to monitor turtle activity from 18 October to 28 November, 1990.

--- SENTENCE 3 ---
Two turtles were tagged and one tag recovery was noted.

--- SENTENCE 4 ---
A total of 19 turtle or turtle track observations were made, 10 of which involved the tagged turtles.

--- SENTENCE 5 ---
We observed 128 pits on Rose and 26 pits on Sand Island.

#### INTRODUCTION

A six week project, jointly funded by the Department of Marine and Wildlife Resources (DMWR), the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USF&W), and Office of Development Planning was conducted to eradicate the rat population on Rose Atoll.

--- SENTENCE 6 ---
Based on a review of past trip reports, this is the longest visit to Rose on record for DMWR or USF&W staff.

--- SENTENCE 7 ---
Thus, it provided an excellent opportunity for DMWR and USF&W to gather data of broader scope than that past trips, which avereaged three to five days duration.

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
This is a summary of turtle activity data collected during the study.

#### **METHODS**

Nightly surveys were conducted on Rose Island from 18 October to 28 November, 1990.

--- SENTENCE 9 ---
Surveys consisted of one to two observers walking the perimeter of the island at two hour intervals, documenting all observations of turtles and fresh turtle tracks, false pits and nests.

--- SENTENCE 10 ---
All observations of fresh tracks and of turtles were recorded on a field form.

--- SENTENCE 11 ---
For each observation, the number of false pits (pits in which no eggs were deposited) that were dug was recorded in addition to whether or not a nest had been dug.

--- SENTENCE 12 ---
Nests were defined as those freshly dug areas that were completely filled with sand (flush with surrounding sand level), whereas false pits were defined as those freshly dug areas that were unfilled or partially dug.

--- SENTENCE 13 ---
Five false pits on Rose Island were marked with stakes to track how long they remain visible.

--- SENTENCE 14 ---
An attempt was made to tag each turtle observed although there were cases where this was not possible.

--- SENTENCE 15 ---
The tags and tagging apparatus used were provided by National Marine Fisheries Service (NMFS), Honolulu Lab.

Sand Island was visited intermittently and surveyed for turtle tracks and pits.

--- SENTENCE 16 ---
New pits were marked with spray paint and the location recorded on a map.

#### RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

All turtles seen during the study, both hatchling and adult, were green turtles (Chelonia mydas).

--- SENTENCE 17 ---
Specifics pertaining to each sighting are summarized in Table 1.

--- SENTENCE 18 ---
The north by northwest end of the island supported the highest concentration of turtle activity based on turtle observations and previously dug pits (Figs. 1 and 2).

--- SENTENCE 19 ---
This area is characterized by a broad reach of coral rubble above the high tide line and some areas of

sand within the adjacent margin of Tournefortia.

--- SENTENCE 20 ---
Two turtles were tagged and one tag recovery was made during the study period.

--- SENTENCE 21 ---
Each of these three turtles made multiple visits to the beach at Rose, accounting for 10 of the 12 turtle observations (excluding the seven sightings of tracks only).

--- SENTENCE 22 ---
NMFS records show that one turtle we observed, tag recovery HIMB 5804, was tagged on Rose Island on 11 November, 1981.

--- SENTENCE 23 ---
Curved carapace length at tagging time was 106 cm compared to 109 cm on the recovery date.

--- SENTENCE 24 ---
The tag, found on the right flipper, had been applied to one of the hard plates on the flipper rather than in the fleshy tissue that separates the plates.

--- SENTENCE 25 ---
The tag appeared to be secure and in good condition.

--- SENTENCE 26 ---
The turtle's entire left front flipper had been amputated, leaving a stump approximately 5 cm in length.

--- SENTENCE 27 ---
Although the wound had healed, the scar tissue appeared to be irritated.

--- SENTENCE 28 ---
Individual turtle hatchlings were seen on seven occasions during the study, and a pair was seen once.

--- SENTENCE 29 ---
A hatchling was observed being attacked by a crab during one of the night surveys.

--- SENTENCE 30 ---
Another hatchling crawled into the main camp tent one evening, presumably attracted by the light.

--- SENTENCE 31 ---
We observed 128 turtle pits on Rose Island (Fig. 2).

--- SENTENCE 32 ---
All pits except those suspected to be a year old or older (filled with debris or with spray paint from the October 1989 trip still visible) were counted.

--- SENTENCE 33 ---
It is probable that most of the older pits counted were false pits rather than nests.

--- SENTENCE 34 ---
Eighteen turtle pits were observed on Sand Island upon arrival in October (Fig. 3).

--- SENTENCE 35 ---
Two pits appeared to be fresh, probably dug within the first two weeks of October.

--- SENTENCE 36 ---
The remaining sixteen pits had been dug sometime since our last visit to the island in August, 1990.

--- SENTENCE 37 ---
As was the case on Rose Island, many of the pits had not been filled, indicating they did not contain eggs.

--- SENTENCE 38 ---
During the duration of our stay, approximately eight pits were dug on Sand Island.

--- SENTENCE 39 ---
The area at the base of the lone tree on the island had the highest concentration of pits and in many cases, turtles excavated areas where pits had been previously dug, making it difficult to keep an accurate count.

--- SENTENCE 40 ---
## RECOMMENDATIONS

Rose and Sand Island turtles' tenancy to dig several pits before laying their eggs confounds efforts to quantify nesting activity there.

--- SENTENCE 41 ---
If the ratio of blank pits to actual nests remains constant over the years, pit counts can be used as a relative index of abundance, but not as indicator of successful nesting.

--- SENTENCE 42 ---
However, if data were maintained on the number of false pits dug per nest for each witnessed nesting event, that ratio could be applied to future pit counts to get a rough indicator of egg-bearing nests.

--- SENTENCE 43 ---
Pit counts would also be more valuable if it were possible to

differentiate between pits dug by year or by nesting season.

--- SENTENCE 44 ---
One approach would be to stake every pit on the island.

--- SENTENCE 45 ---
Another would be to stake test pits from the various substrate types and with different levels of exposure (i.e. surrounded by vegetation, on vegetation margin, leeward, windward) and track how long they are visible.
