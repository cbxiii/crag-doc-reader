## *1) Data Analyses for Fagatele Marine Sanctuary and National Parks Service*

## *Coral*

--- SENTENCE 19 ---
The data are of sufficient quality to detect clear trends in time space and depth

!

--- SENTENCE 20 ---
The dominant signal is that there was a huge shift in amount of coral between 1998 and 2001 suggesting that the reefs are recovering from the disasters of the previous 20 years.

--- SENTENCE 21 ---
The number of species declined but the area covered increased.

--- SENTENCE 22 ---
Spatially, patterns were detected within Fagatele bay with the dominant trend in both the species composition and the pattern of species coverage showing clear contrast between the inner (2, 3, 4 5) and outer sites (1 & 6).

!

The Tutuila data also showed spatial pattern with three north-eastern sites being distinguished from the rest.

--- SENTENCE 24 ---
Within Fagatele the depth pattern was clear, wide difference between the species composition and patterns of coverage between the 1m and 3 depths followed by a steady gradient from 3 to 12m.

--- SENTENCE 46 ---

### **Design**

The design has changed over time, in particular the number of depths covered for example in 1985 depth 9 metre was not taken, in 1998 and 2001 depth 18 metres was added.

--- SENTENCE 47 ---
In addition, as can be seen from table 1, the design has many holes in - it is extremely unbalanced.

--- SENTENCE 48 ---
Some depths are not practically available for sampling at some sites.

--- SENTENCE 49 ---
Analysing such a data set is difficult, and the results become more tentative.

--- SENTENCE 50 ---
To identify trends in space time or depth, the data have to be corrected for the missing values, since simple statistics will be biased due to the missing values.

--- SENTENCE 51 ---
Either data are thrown away until the remainder has no holes - is balanced - or the data must be manipulated statistically to correct for the missing values.

--- SENTENCE 52 ---
In the first case this would lead to the loss of 70% of the data if we wished to keep 1985 in the analysis, 38% at best.

--- SENTENCE 53 ---
I regard this as an unacceptable loss of useful information and would suggest statistical correction, as can be seen in the results section of the report this has worked well.

--- SENTENCE 54 ---
I will consider possible changes to the design later in the report.

## **Size**

There seems to be no problems with the mean size data (the diameter of individual coral colonies).

--- SENTENCE 106 ---
However, unlike density this is a conditional variable: if the coral is not recorded at a site then the density is logically and usefully recorded as zero, size is however unrecorded; a value of zero is misleading, if there are no coral colonies then there is no size.

--- SENTENCE 107 ---
The existence of a value of size is conditional on the density being greater than zero.

--- SENTENCE 108 ---
This restricts the usefulness of this variable for analysis, its main purpose is to allow the calculation of coverage.

--- SENTENCE 109 ---
## **Coverage – surface area per sq. metre**

There is a fundamental problem with the percent cover as presented in past reports and the current form of data.

--- SENTENCE 110 ---
By examining the Excel spreadsheet calculations I discovered that the percent cover for the individual species has been wrongly calculated.

--- SENTENCE 111 ---
It had been defined in earlier reports, correctly, as the area of a circle with the average radius (mean size) of a single colony for that species recorded for that sample multiplied by the estimated density of colonies per sq metre.

--- SENTENCE 112 ---
It is therefore the estimate of total horizontal area of this species in a square metre (not quite proportional or percentage coverage since it can exceed 1 if there is 3–D complexity, e.g. layering.

--- SENTENCE 113 ---
Previously however it had been calculated in the Excel spread sheets as the mean area for the individual species times the total density of all species in the sample.

--- SENTENCE 114 ---
Once we have settled the problems with the density estimates that were mentioned above, a new data set with the correct figures can be prepared.

--- SENTENCE 115 ---
For the purpose of these analyses the densities were corrected as above, and the area per metre<sup>2</sup> was calculated.

--- SENTENCE 918 ---
**State of the coral and fish communities 2001/2002**

The health of reef communities is an extremely slippery concept that however quite justifiably dominates the thinking of reef community managers.

--- SENTENCE 919 ---
Given that there seems to be no sensible objective way of defining it, still less of measuring it directly, In order to identify whether patterns in the data that have emerged in these data analyses I have used a subjective approach – sometimes called the "Delphic approach" – to define which are healthy sites and which are not.

--- SENTENCE 920 ---
This approach, popular in marketing circles (hence the fancy name for a common sense idea) simply relies on the consensus of a group of people that know the state of the communities at the sites.

--- SENTENCE 921 ---
I took the sites that I had analysed and had 3 people: Nancy Daschbach, Peter Craig, and Eva rank them (independently) from "sick" to "healthy".

--- SENTENCE 922 ---
My ad hoc measure of "health" was simply the average of these assessments.

--- SENTENCE 923 ---
For example for one of the analyses (Alison Green's fish survey) the rankings of the Tutuila sites were:
