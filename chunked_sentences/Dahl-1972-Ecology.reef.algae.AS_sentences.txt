### Ecology and Community Structure of Some Tropical Reef Algae in Samoa

ARTHUR LYON DAHL

Department of Botany, Smithsonian Institution, Washington, D.C., U.S.A.

While benthic marine algae other than encrusting forms are not often conspicuous in coral reef habitats, they frequently form complex associations with high species diversity but low biomass.

--- SENTENCE 2 ---
Data from the initial phase of a detailed ecological survey of the algae on the fringing reefs around Tutuila, American Samoa, demonstrate the widespread occurrence of algal turfs, and illustrate the interactions between species that help to maintain the algal communities.

--- SENTENCE 3 ---
Associations between algal species are often mutually beneficial, and create significant microhabitats.

--- SENTENCE 4 ---
Algal communities also help to control substrate availability, and are themselves altered by the types of substrate available.

--- SENTENCE 5 ---
In spite of their low biomass, other algae must be considered along with encrusting forms as significant components in the coral reef ecosystem.

#### INTRODUCTION

--- SENTENCE 6 ---
Coral reefs are one of the world's most complex and productive ecosystems and are the only biological community known to produce massive geological formations.

--- SENTENCE 7 ---
Although these reefs are widely distributed along coastal margins in tropical waters, the diversity of coral reef forms and their complexity have made it difficult to characterize ecological features that may be common to many reef areas and that may help to determine the nature and form of reef development.

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
American Samoa, a small group of largely volcanic islands in the South Pacific (lat.

--- SENTENCE 9 ---
170°W), is typical of many island areas in which coral reefs are a prominent local resource.

--- SENTENCE 10 ---
As part of a long-term survey and monitoring program, 10 line transects were established across the intertidal and high subtidal portions of the fringing reefs around Tutuila Island and on nearby Aunuu Island, representing a variety of exposures and habitats.

--- SENTENCE 11 ---
Additional sites were also examined in detail.

--- SENTENCE 12 ---
The initial surveys were established in January 1970, and most stations were repeated in July 1970.

--- SENTENCE 13 ---
Collections and rough quantitative estimates of percent coverage were made for each 10 m transect segment across the reef flat.

--- SENTENCE 14 ---
A general description of the area and several environmental parameters were also noted for each transect.

--- SENTENCE 15 ---
This approach served to characterize the major reef flat features and populations on a repeatable basis within the limited time available.

--- SENTENCE 16 ---
This paper will focus on one aspect of the data from this program concerning the occurrence of algal turfs and their role in the reef community structure.

--- SENTENCE 17 ---
Algal turfs can be defined as relatively dense associations of one or more species of filamentous or foliose algae of small stature, attaining a height or thickness of 1 to 30 mm.

--- SENTENCE 18 ---
Turfs are a frequently occurring algal feature, particularly in tropical marine benthic habitats, and have been periodically noted and described (eg., 1, 3, 4, 6, 7).

--- SENTENCE 19 ---
Generally, however, attention has been concentrated on the individual species within the turf, rather than on the nature of the associations between species or the role of the turf community in the ecosystem.

--- SENTENCE 20 ---
Turfs may be of particular significance in tropical reef areas where other benthic algae are scarce.

--- SENTENCE 21 ---
They may make a significant contribution to productivity, aid in the consolidation of reef materials, and control substrate availability.

--- SENTENCE 22 ---
They are frequently highly complex and diverse assemblages.

--- SENTENCE 23 ---
Species composition, community structure, and habitat vary greatly, even within the same area.

--- SENTENCE 24 ---
A much greater mass of data

(36)

will be required before any generalizations about the turf community can be substantiated.

--- SENTENCE 25 ---
It is nevertheless useful to consider this association as a unit with respect to its role in coral reef ecosystems.

--- SENTENCE 26 ---
Little information has been published on the marine algae of American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 27 ---
The paper by Setchell is perhaps the most substantial, yet it was apparently based on fragmentary collections and is incomplete (5).

#### RESULTS

The fringing reefs around Tutuila Island.

--- SENTENCE 28 ---
American Samoa, are relatively small, extending no more than a few hundred meters from the shore.

--- SENTENCE 29 ---
On the south shore, the reefs extend for most of the length of the island and generally consist of a shallow moat near the shore (200 to 400 mm depth at low tide), a relatively solid reef flat extending out perhaps 100 m., which is barely exposed by the lower tides, and the reef front itself (Fig. 1).

--- SENTENCE 30 ---
Living corals occur primarily on the outer portion of the reef flat and beyond.

--- SENTENCE 31 ---
Breaks in the reef are generally associated with areas of freshwater runoff.

--- SENTENCE 32 ---
Reef development on the north shore is primarily restricted to the bays.

--- SENTENCE 33 ---
All the reefs tend to be highly variable in structure and dominant organisms, depending on the local conditions.

--- SENTENCE 34 ---
The flora of the basaltic outcrops was distinctly different from that of adjacent carbonate rock substrate.

--- SENTENCE 35 ---
For further information on the structure of the reefs at Tutuila, see Mayor (2).

--- SENTENCE 36 ---
A profile of the reef site near Nuuuli (Fig. 1) illustrates several of the features of the Samoan reef flat habitats.

--- SENTENCE 37 ---
The inshore area consists largely of sand and a loose coral rubble of fragments less than 100 mm long.

--- SENTENCE 38 ---
Scattered small coral heads towards the seaward edge of the moat merge into the solid reef flat extending to the outer edge of the Semiconsolidated calcareous rubble predominates on the inner portion of the reef flat with an increasing percentage of living coral towards the reef front.

--- SENTENCE 39 ---
Algal turfs cover most of the available solid surface in the reef moat with fleshy crusts also common on coral fragments.

--- SENTENCE 40 ---
Turfs decrease in coverage toward the outer margin of the reef flat to be replaced by crustose forms and then living coral.

--- SENTENCE 41 ---
Halimeda opuntia is common in the interstices of the rubble on the reef flat.

--- SENTENCE 42 ---
In terms of percent coverage, turfs are frequently the most significant algal component in shallow reef areas.

--- SENTENCE 43 ---
On some other transects, the relative role of encrusting coralline algae increased, particularly on loose coral rubble and on the outer margin of the reef, but turf remained dominant in the more stable areas.

--- SENTENCE 44 ---
Extensive turfs were also noted along subtidal channels and on the dead portions of coral heads.

The species composition of Samoan algal turfs is both complex and highly variable.

--- SENTENCE 47 ---
One sample of about 400 mm² from a basaltic substrate at Lepisi Point contained over 30 species.

--- SENTENCE 48 ---
Some species occur only in turfs; others are found elsewhere, often growing in turf in a reduced form.

--- SENTENCE 49 ---
Some of the dominant turf genera include Jania, Polysiphonia, Ceramium, Hypnea, Cladophora, Gelidium, and Laurencia.

--- SENTENCE 50 ---
A detailed listing of the algae present is beyond the scope of this paper.

#### Discussion

How do such complex associations as the algal turf community arise, and what significance do they have in the reef ecosystem?

--- SENTENCE 52 ---
From the present monitoring surveys, it is only possible to conclude that such turfs are widespread and are frequently composed of numerous species in close association.

--- SENTENCE 53 ---
It may be useful to suggest possible contributing factors, but detailed experimental confirmation will be required.

--- SENTENCE 54 ---
First, a number of features can be noted about the turf habitat.

--- SENTENCE 55 ---
It would appear to have high potential for productivity, with ample light and a supply of nutrients washed onshore by reef currents and carried down from the adjacent land.

--- SENTENCE 56 ---
There would also be high grazing pressure, at least intermittently when high tides permit grazing fish and other animals to emerge or to come inshore from the outer reef.

--- SENTENCE 57 ---
At least some turf areas are subject to exposure at low tide, as well as to environmental extremes such as high light intensity, and salinity variations with heavy rains and freshwater runoff.

--- SENTENCE 58 ---
There may well be species interactions within the turf community that help to counter these ecological pressures.

--- SENTENCE 59 ---
For example, the entrance to Pala Lagoon near Nuuuli consists largely of broad shallow sand flats carpeted with a simple association of Halimeda and Dictyota.

--- SENTENCE 60 ---
A dense mat of Dictyota and scattered Padina largely covers the area, anchored in place by hummocks of Halimeda.

--- SENTENCE 61 ---
The Halimeda provides the only firm attachment in the sand for the Dictyota, the Dictyota alters conditions under the mat in a way that may or may not be beneficial to the Halimeda.

--- SENTENCE 62 ---
In the turf community, Jania, Laurencia, or Hypnea may serve a similar anchoring function for other algae, or even for the substrate.

--- SENTENCE 63 ---
In one area of loose rubble on the reef flat near Anapeapea, the coral fragments have been bound together by turf to form a dense resilent mat.

--- SENTENCE 64 ---
Such binding might foster the consolidation of the rubble into reef rock by coralline algae or chemical processes.

--- SENTENCE 65 ---
One might also expect certain algae with greater resistance to grazing or abrasion to serve as a shelter for more delicate forms.

--- SENTENCE 66 ---
The dense turf form could similarly provide protection from extreme light intensities or exposure, producing an internal microhabitat more favorable to survival and growth.

--- SENTENCE 67 ---
There may also be nutritional, antibiotic, or other biochemical benefits within the close confines of the community.

--- SENTENCE 68 ---
A dense turf association may be better able to regenerate rapidly after injury, or to change its species composition with changing conditions.

--- SENTENCE 69 ---
It is, in all likelihood, a dynamic rather than static association.

--- SENTENCE 70 ---
The significance of algal turfs for the whole reef ecosystem is similarly open to conjecture.

--- SENTENCE 71 ---
This may well be an area of high productivity, in which rapid growth is balanced by heavy grazing, resulting in low biomass but high turnover.

--- SENTENCE 72 ---
Turfs may also control substrate availability, preventing the settlement of coral larvae and other organisms.

--- SENTENCE 73 ---
The widespread occurrence of algal turfs in tropical reef habitats suggests that they may play a significant part in reef structure and function.

--- SENTENCE 74 ---
Not until the roles of this and other reef communities are more fully explored will we begin to understand the complex ecosystem that is a coral reef.
