### I. CORAL REEF DESTRUCTION

During July 1973, John Flanigan, a high-school teacher with the Department of Education of American Samoa, discovered that most of his spectacular demonstration coral reef had inexplicably died.

--- SENTENCE 4 ---
Both he and, independently, James Betcher had last visited this area in late November 1972 and had noted luxuriant coral growth.

--- SENTENCE 5 ---
During July and August 1973 we reconnoitered the area and found extensive coral death of recent occurrence in the reefs bounded by Coconut Point, the Pago Pago Airport, and out to beyond the reef edge.

--- SENTENCE 6 ---
Mr. Betcher had noticed the change about 2 months earlier.

--- SENTENCE 7 ---
Tutuila, American Samoa, located at lat.

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
170° 40'W., is a small volcanic island surrounded by fringing reefs, the broadest extending about 1,000 meters from the shore in the south midportion of the island.

--- SENTENCE 9 ---
Protected by this reef lies a shallow estuarian lagoon separated from it in part by a narrow sandy peninsula referred to as Coconut Point.

--- SENTENCE 10 ---
In recent years the building of a modern airport across the lagoon to the reef edge was accompanied by much dredging of the lagoon, which now opens to the sea through Avatele Passage, a narrow gap between the runway embankment and the reef flats adjoining Coconut Point.

--- SENTENCE 11 ---
The distance between these two landmarks is about 400 meters and encompasses partially dredged sand flats and coral reefs.

--- SENTENCE 12 ---
Before 1973 great thickets of staghorn acroporid corals occupied the deeper areas.

--- SENTENCE 13 ---
Some of these stands were over 30 meters across and 2 meters high.

--- SENTENCE 14 ---
Our survey showed that all the corals of the dominant suborder.

--- SENTENCE 15 ---
Astrocoeniina, had died within an area of at least 8 hectares (20 acres).

--- SENTENCE 16 ---
This included all the Acropora, Montipora, and Pocillopora corals, while members of two other suborders, Fungiina and Faviina, appeared healthy.

--- SENTENCE 17 ---
It looked to us as if all the coral had succumbed at the same time, perhaps 3 or 4 months before, as all the skeletons were covered with the same-length strands of brown algae.

--- SENTENCE 18 ---
Where this occurred in shallow water the demarcation point between living and dead coral was sharply delimited, and even over the reef edge scuba divers reported that most of the corals to a depth of 6 meters were dead.

--- SENTENCE 19 ---
There was no

evidence of siltation, and a review of dredging records indicated almost no activity within a mile during the months when this happened.

--- SENTENCE 20 ---
We found that mollusks, calcifying algae, and echinoderms were present in expected numbers.

--- SENTENCE 21 ---
A rare angelfish, *Pomacanthus imperator* (Bloch), occupied the same niche under a rock that it had for two years with no apparent distress.

--- SENTENCE 22 ---
There was no evidence of infestation by crown-of-thorns starfish (*Acanthaster*) on this or any reef on Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 23 ---
Pala Lagoon has over 0.5 meter of tidal flow, the water all rushing in and out the narrow Avatele passage.

--- SENTENCE 24 ---
During high tides water passes also over the reef crests and reef flats paralleling the Coconut Point peninsula to create an inshore stream running next to the beach.

--- SENTENCE 25 ---
This is about 40 meters wide, and at high tide its greatest depth was about 1 meter.

--- SENTENCE 26 ---
This streams toward the tip of the peninsula where it joins the outflow from Pala Lagoon to form a fanshaped discharge sweep.

--- SENTENCE 27 ---
Figure 1 indicates how all the dead coral lay within this precise area.

--- SENTENCE 28 ---
On the west edge of the coral kill some banks of Acropora were only partially dead, with a western rim of living coral.

--- SENTENCE 29 ---
The same was noted over the reef flat, so that coral death could be marked out almost to the meter.

--- SENTENCE 30 ---
Finally, it was noted that in the stream channel the precise dividing line between normal coral growth and dead coral seemed to lie at a fish trap erected across the stream.

--- SENTENCE 31 ---
For many years Tongan fishermen have erected fish traps on various areas of this reef.

--- SENTENCE 32 ---
These are weirs made of commercial chicken wire strung on sticks across the current so that fish are directed into a cul-de-sac where they are speared.

--- SENTENCE 33 ---
It was noted that healthy Pocillopora grew within 30 meters upstream of this net, but all the dominant corals below the trap were dead.

--- SENTENCE 34 ---
Circumstantial evidence suggested that something had occurred here to kill certain corals that had come in contact with water passing this point.

--- SENTENCE 35 ---
It was as if some noxious agent, that had a selective action on the Astrocoeniina corals and nothing else, had been added to the water.

--- SENTENCE 36 ---
We postulated that something may have been added to the water to aid in catching fish.

--- SENTENCE 37 ---
Fish poisons have been used in the tropical Pacific for generations for this purpose, and pesticides would have been available for such a stunt, but nobody in the vicinity seemed to know anything about the dead coral, and certainly nobody volunteered information suggesting that an attempt had been made to poison fish since that was punishable by a \$600 fine.

--- SENTENCE 38 ---
The present study was then drawn up to investigate the effects of manufactured biocidal agents on living corals.

--- SENTENCE 39 ---
A literature search uncovered no references on the effects of pesticides on reef corals.

--- SENTENCE 40 ---
Coconut Point coral kill of 1973, Tutuila, American Samoa (A and B are transect lines).

### Laboratory Studies

All laboratory studies were performed during September and October 1974 at the Hawaii Institute of Marine Biology at Kaneohe, Oahu, Hawaiian

!

--- SENTENCE 41 ---
The purpose was to assess the effects of certain toxic agents on living corals.

--- SENTENCE 42 ---
Following are the methods and the results:

Coral heads of Pocillopora damicornis (Linnaeus), the same species involved in the Samoan kill, were broken up and 10 similar growing tips were suspended in identical glass jars each containing 2 liters of freshly filtered sea water.

--- SENTENCE 43 ---
To each of eight jars a measured amount of a toxic agent was added to give a decremental series ranging from 2 parts per million (ppm) concentration in the first jar to 10 parts per billion (ppb) in the eighth.

--- SENTENCE 44 ---
The agents tested are listed in Table 1.

--- SENTENCE 45 ---
Until recently they have all been available commercially.

--- SENTENCE 46 ---
They were added directly to the sea water, or alcohol in small quantity was used as a vehicle.

--- SENTENCE 47 ---
To nine of the jars I added the dye sodium alizarinate (Alizarin Red S) to give an ambient concentration of 10 ppm.

--- SENTENCE 48 ---
This hydroquinone dve is an indicator of biological calcification, and when present in sea water during growth of corals it is incorporated into the skeleton in proportion to the amount of calcium carbonate deposited (Lamberts, 1973, 1975).

--- SENTENCE 49 ---
The depth and distribution of the magenta color that remains after the living tissue has been removed can then be observed directly and can be compared with other specimens in the same or similar series to assess the ability of the organism to form skeletal tissues.

--- SENTENCE 50 ---
In these experiments, the ninth bottle with alizarin and the tenth with clear sea water served as controls.

--- SENTENCE 51 ---
Laboratory lighting, water temperature, and aeration were standardized and controlled.

Also, we made other in vitro studies using a precision Cole-Parmer pump, which delivered a known constant flow of sea water and additives to test tanks.

--- SENTENCE 80 ---
Three 10-liter glass aquaria were set up so that a constant flow of water would cascade from one to another.

--- SENTENCE 81 ---
Corals of the genera Pocillopora, Fungia, and Cyphastrea, representing the three suborders involved, were placed in each tank.

--- SENTENCE 82 ---
When possible, a single coral head was divided and part placed in each tank.

--- SENTENCE 83 ---
The first and highest aquarium was supplied with filtered sea water and

served to hold the controls.

--- SENTENCE 84 ---
The second received the overflow from the first plus sufficient Alizarin Red S to give a constant concentration of 10 ppm.

--- SENTENCE 85 ---
The third tank received the overflow from the second plus a solution of the biocide to be tested.

--- SENTENCE 86 ---
DDT was added to give an ambient concentration of 0.5 ppm, and in the second series endrin was added to give a constant 1.0 ppm concentration.

--- SENTENCE 87 ---
Each of these substances was added for 48 hours, after which the sea water was allowed to run clear for a day before coral tissue was removed with a jet of water so that alizarin deposition could be compared.

--- SENTENCE 88 ---
My observations on living corals subjected to pesticide and herbicides showed that with the greatest concentrations used in my laboratory, some of the terminal polyps died when exposed to DDT or endrin.

--- SENTENCE 89 ---
With some others there was evidence of stress effect manifested by decreased tissue irritability and lack of vigor during the exposure to these chemical agents.

--- SENTENCE 90 ---
Mercury is a protoplasmic poison and is notoriously toxic to animal life, including corals.

--- SENTENCE 91 ---
Stress reactions were noted in coral polyps exposed to a concentration of 10 ppb, and 0.5 ppm or greater was lethal.

--- SENTENCE 92 ---
Copper, as CuSO<sub>4</sub>, often used as an algicide, did not kill coral in these experiments at a concentration of 2 ppm.

--- SENTENCE 93 ---
Commerical bleach (Clorox) killed coral tissue when the concentration was 2 parts per thousand if there was prolonged contact.

--- SENTENCE 94 ---
Practically speaking, bleach in order to kill coral must be in a concentration of about 2 liters per cubic meter of sea water (1 gallon per 2 cubic yards).

--- SENTENCE 95 ---
Similar results were found using my tank studies.

--- SENTENCE 96 ---
Corals were not killed and did not appear damaged by either DDT or endrin in these 48-hour trials.

--- SENTENCE 97 ---
The presence of abundant alizarin in the cleaned corals indicated that skeletal calcium carbonate had been deposited in the amount and manner expected in normal growth.

--- SENTENCE 98 ---
When all specimens from each series were compared, it appeared that high concentrations of pesticides might depress the formation of skeletal calcium slightly but did not eliminate it.

--- SENTENCE 99 ---
Deposition of skeletal calcium, however, as deduced from observing the alizarin deposition, seemed enhanced over the normal controls when the chemicals were in the sea water in the 0.5 ppm range.

--- SENTENCE 100 ---
This suggests that small amounts of such agents, toxic in large quantities, might stimulate the metabolism of the coral polyp during the time it is present.

--- SENTENCE 101 ---
At this point nothing is known about long-term effects, if any.

--- SENTENCE 102 ---
Coral polyps are apparently very hardy animals.

--- SENTENCE 103 ---
A level of certain pesticides such as endrin or parathion that would have been lethal to much of the marine life on the reef had little or no demonstrable effect on the living coral, which continued to deposit skeletal material in abundance.

--- SENTENCE 104 ---
Each coral species and each individual colony of coral polyps has its own growth pattern and pace of growth; so there is always a variation in the amount and pattern of skeletali-

zation and hence of the pattern of the alizarin dye visible.

--- SENTENCE 105 ---
It was only after the above experiments had been concluded that I abandoned plans for in situ studies using the same pesticides and herbicides on living coral on the Samoan reefs themselves.

#### Observations in American Samoa

Tutuila, American Samoa, was revisited between October 15 and November 12, 1974, to resurvey the area of the coral kill.

--- SENTENCE 106 ---
Climatic conditions allowed satisfactory observations of the reef but never optimum ones.

--- SENTENCE 107 ---
The fish trap that had separated the living from the dead *Pocillopora* had been destroyed, but pieces of it remained submerged at the site and were found to consist of medium-mesh galvanized chicken wire.

--- SENTENCE 108 ---
Samoans living in the vicinity claimed to have torn down the net.

--- SENTENCE 109 ---
Samoans have been at enmity with the Tongans for centuries.

--- SENTENCE 110 ---
Still, two new fish traps of similar design had been erected, one near the edge of the reef at the northern limit of the coral kill and the other within the western limits.

--- SENTENCE 111 ---
The Tongan fishermen who managed them denied ever using any poisoning agents to catch fish.

--- SENTENCE 112 ---
The entire area was rechecked repeatedly by wading or swimming with face mask and recording data on an underwater slate.

--- SENTENCE 113 ---
Healthy heads of *Pocillopora* were found in profusion 40 meters upstream from the fish-trap wreckage, some being over 15 centimeters in diameter, indicating an age of several years.

--- SENTENCE 114 ---
Here was found also some *Montipora* of the suborder Astrocoeniina and moderate amounts of *Porites* and *Pavona*.

--- SENTENCE 115 ---
Extensive banks of the latter continued on and served as a marker of the inner margin of the reef flat proper.

--- SENTENCE 116 ---
Downstream below the trap, both in the inshore lagoon stream and in the deeper areas that had contained the extensive coral banks, most of the coral skeletons had now been broken up and washed away or reduced to rubble.

--- SENTENCE 117 ---
In this entire area of destruction only one sparse 2-by-2-square meter patch of *Acropora formosa* (Dana) was found.

--- SENTENCE 118 ---
It was this species that made up most of the big banks previously seen.

--- SENTENCE 119 ---
There were a few small heads of *Pocillopora damicornis* usually growing on dead *Acropora* branches.

--- SENTENCE 120 ---
It is probable that these early colonizers had become established after the massive destruction had occurred.

--- SENTENCE 121 ---
The boundaries of the coral kill were again verified and measured.

--- SENTENCE 122 ---
Total area involved was about 8 hectares (20 acres); however, some of this was sandbar and the area of actual coral death was about 6 hectares (15 acres).

--- SENTENCE 123 ---
Coral of the suborder Fungiina was alive and consisted of *Pavona* and *Porites*, both of finger and solid species.

--- SENTENCE 124 ---
Suborder Faviina was represented by numerous healthy colonies of *Lepastrea purpurea* (Dana) and *Galaxea fascicularis* (Linnaeus).

--- SENTENCE 125 ---
One of the chief contributors to the inshore reef edge was *Psammocora con-

--- SENTENCE 126 ---
*

tigua (Esper), which was growing vigorously.

--- SENTENCE 127 ---
Based on skeletal morphology *Psammocora* has been classified by various taxonomists as either in the suborder Astrocoeniina or in Fungiina.

--- SENTENCE 128 ---
In this instance its response was like that of the members of the latter suborder.

--- SENTENCE 129 ---
During the latter visit to Samoa I had access to a study of the corals in this area done by Dr. James Maragos in 1972 (Helfrich, 1975).

--- SENTENCE 130 ---
At that time a group of University of Hawaii scientists made an environmental impact study of the Pala Lagoon during which transects were laid out, two of them (labeled A and B) being in the area I was studying.

--- SENTENCE 131 ---
He made coral counts and estimates of coral cover, and I repeated the same observations so that our results could be compared.

--- SENTENCE 132 ---
Water currents were also observed.

--- SENTENCE 133 ---
At high tide an object floating with the surface currents along the inshore stream toward the tip of Coconut Point traveled at the rate of 10 meters per minute.

--- SENTENCE 134 ---
The water depth was measured along the contour of the sandy bottom at the site of the wrecked fish trap.

--- SENTENCE 135 ---
It was estimated that at high tide about 250 cubic meters of sea water passed this site per minute.

--- SENTENCE 136 ---
It was obvious that if a toxic agent alone was responsible for the destruction noted, huge amounts must have been used or else it must have been far more toxic to live corals and more specific for certain kinds than anything I tested.

--- SENTENCE 137 ---
Living coral on the reef flat is usually interspersed with sand or rubble areas and patches of rock often overgrown with calcifying algae so that many species may be present and still the total area covered by live coral is usually less than 50 percent.

--- SENTENCE 138 ---
In observations along transects A and B both in 1972 and in 1974 the diversity and amount of coral cover were virtually the same with the exception that all the Astrocoeniina corals reported as abundant in some points during the first study were entirely absent in the second.

--- SENTENCE 139 ---
These transect lines missed all the large thickets of Acropora in the areas dredged for airport fill; still, Acropora corals alone constituted a fourth of all corals reported on these two transects.

--- SENTENCE 140 ---
My survey showed that large areas previously covered with these corals now showed only rock or rubble and, in the deeper areas, sand.

--- SENTENCE 141 ---
Voucher specimens of living and dead corals were taken for possible analysis as well as many photographs for future reference.

--- SENTENCE 142 ---
Two other areas of similar coral kill were observed on Tutuila reefs during the later visit.

--- SENTENCE 143 ---
One, far out in the north shore bay of Masefau where it was not readily accessible to humans, covered about 2/3 hectare and had consisted mostly of Acropora formosa and Pocillopora danicornis.

--- SENTENCE 144 ---
It was surrounded on all sides by living corals of the same genera.

--- SENTENCE 145 ---
The dead branches were covered by strands of brown algae and showed no gross disintegration.

--- SENTENCE 146 ---
The second, half the size but otherwise similar, was at the edge of moderately deep water in

Faga'itua Bay.

--- SENTENCE 147 ---
These were observed with Dr. Ronald Needham, a resident marine zoologist who asserted that these areas had appeared healthy 4 months previously.

#### Conclusions

My studies indicate that reef corals are surprisingly tolerant to the pesticides and herbicides that I tested but will succumb to general protoplasmic poisons.

--- SENTENCE 148 ---
There are thousands of such compounds known and many combinations were used in the Samoan agriculture industry.

--- SENTENCE 149 ---
Only a preliminary survey using a few of the better-known and representative agents could be made and of these DDT and endrin have already been banned for commercial use in the United States.

--- SENTENCE 150 ---
In many instances these substances are lethal to fish and marine invertebrates in much smaller concentration than that which caused only a mild decrease in the activity of the coral polyps (Portmann, 1970).

--- SENTENCE 151 ---
Undoubtedly, higher concentrations of this select group of biocidal agents would have shown greater toxicity; however, I surmised that if reef corals continued to function well after continuous exposure to these agents for 24 hours there was little likelihood that there would be a deleterious effect from such agents if added to the sea water that swept over a coral reef to be immediately diluted in the open ocean.

--- SENTENCE 152 ---
It appeared as if all the corals had been exposed to some noxious agent at the same time as it was being carried along by natural water currents.

--- SENTENCE 153 ---
The contours of the area of dead coral suggested that this may have happened near high tide or as the waters were being swept out of Pala Lagoon on a falling tide.

--- SENTENCE 154 ---
The sea water passing over the reef and then across the fish trap in question was copious in any tidal cycle but far less than the volume exchange in Pala Lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 155 ---
The University of Hawaii survey estimated that this could approach a million cubic meters per tidal cycle, or 10 times the amount that I estimated to be passing by the fish trap.

--- SENTENCE 156 ---
It would have required that 500 grams of any biocidal agent be added to the inshore stream per minute to maintain a concentration of 2 ppm in the flowing water.

--- SENTENCE 157 ---
Larger applications would certainly give heavy concentrations locally but the swift water overturn would make any effect transitory.

--- SENTENCE 158 ---
These reef corals must have been extremely sensitive to some toxic agent that was not a general protoplasmic poison but specific for only a select suborder of otherwise hardy animals.

--- SENTENCE 159 ---
No biocidal agent that I encountered gave any effects like this.

--- SENTENCE 160 ---
I could conclude only that the pesticides and herbicides I evaluated could not under ordinary circumstances have caused this kind of havoc.

--- SENTENCE 161 ---
Various studies of coral-reef destruction implicate the consequences of industrialization such as sedimentation, oil spills, blasting, and eutrophication.

--- SENTENCE 162 ---
There are also many natural phenomena causing reef damage such as storms and earthquakes, thermal extremes, sea-water dilution, or reef drying, as well as predation by natural enemies that live on the reefs.

--- SENTENCE 163 ---
Undoubtedly there are many other causes that singly or in combination serve to destroy coral reefs and reef corals.

--- SENTENCE 164 ---
Massive, unexplained coral die-offs may not be uncommon on Samoan reefs and probably happen elsewhere, but they have received little attention.

--- SENTENCE 165 ---
It is probable that coral polyps like other living organisms are subject to diseases either intrinsic or caused by bacterial or viral invaders.

--- SENTENCE 166 ---
It is possible that an epizootic malady could affect only one suborder among corals as it does in other zoological orders.

--- SENTENCE 167 ---
Attributing this particular coral kill to an extraneous pollutant is entirely on circumstantial evidence which initially to me was convincing.

--- SENTENCE 168 ---
If no toxic agent in itself would cause such a curious pattern of coral death, we can speculate that the suborder Astrocoeniina may have been weakened by some epizootic disease which may also have been present in other areas of Samoa as noted.

--- SENTENCE 169 ---
The unusual configuration that began at a fish trap may have been coincidental; however, it is not beyond reason to believe that actual death may have resulted from a noxious agent acting on weakened corals.

--- SENTENCE 170 ---
It is doubtful if a single process could have produced such an unusual result.

--- SENTENCE 171 ---
As with other natural disasters to coral reefs, the corals of Coconut Point will probably reestablish themselves if allowed to do so.

--- SENTENCE 172 ---
It is hoped that the present survey may serve as a base line to chronicle the regrowth of another coral garden.

--- SENTENCE 173 ---
# Summary

The dominant suborder of reef corals died suddenly in an 8-hectare area on an American Samoan reef while two nondominant suborders of coral and other life were spared.

--- SENTENCE 174 ---
Circumstantial evidence linked this to a fish trap.

--- SENTENCE 175 ---
Pesticides were suspected, but laboratory studies of representative biocides indicate that short-term exposure of reef corals to some commonly used pesticides caused no discernible harm.

### II.

--- SENTENCE 176 ---
RECOLONIZATION OF A DENUDED CORAL REEF

--- SENTENCE 177 ---
The coral reefs of Tutuila, American Samoa, were well known to John Flanigan, a teacher in Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 178 ---
As an avid naturalist he was dismayed when in July 1973 he found one of his favorite reef areas denuded of most of the previ-

ously living coral.

--- SENTENCE 179 ---
At that time we mapped the area of Coconut Point, Figure 1, and found that scleractinian corals of the dominant suborder Astrocoeniina had recently died leaving their algae-covered unbroken skeletons in place.

--- SENTENCE 180 ---
Two nondominant suborders of reef coral seemed unharmed.

--- SENTENCE 181 ---
An 8-hectare area was involved, mostly reef flat swept by sea water which had passed through a fish-trap weir.

--- SENTENCE 182 ---
We concluded that it was not coincidental that only certain corals, in the lee of this net made of chicken wire, were dead.

--- SENTENCE 183 ---
A year later I again surveyed the area, made coral counts, procured voucher specimens, and took photographs.

--- SENTENCE 184 ---
I concluded then that this phenomenon was probably due to an epizootic infection of unknown type affecting only the astrocoeniinid corals, but that sudden death had been triggered by a chemical substance entering into the water, presumably to kill fish.

--- SENTENCE 185 ---
Coral reefs are complex structures of living animals and plants.

--- SENTENCE 186 ---
Although to the casual observer they may appear immutable, they are a balanced ecosystem in which every element is subject to change; a constant reshaping takes place with a reciprocity between build-up and destruction—first one component being in ascendancy and then the other.

--- SENTENCE 187 ---
Every organism is involved in both actions, including the hard corals which together with certain calcareous algae are the foundation builders of all coral reefs.

--- SENTENCE 188 ---
Corals reproduce sexually, disseminating minute larvae which settle on some stable surface and increase by asexual division as they form a reef mass with their calcium carbonate skeletons.

--- SENTENCE 189 ---
In time these die and others take their place.

--- SENTENCE 190 ---
This study concerns such a succession and coral recolonization.

--- SENTENCE 191 ---
Studies of the recolonization of destroyed coral reefs have for the most part been directed toward the damage inflicted by the spiny starfish Acanthaster plancii.

--- SENTENCE 192 ---
When such a massive infestation has passed over a reef there remain remnants of viable coral tissue, and remarkable recovery can occur in as little as five years (Randall, 1973; Pearson, 1977); however, the coral species profile may then be altered.

--- SENTENCE 193 ---
Shinn (1976) noted that reef recovery following a hurricane is often rapid and is accomplished mostly by asexual regeneration of scattered coral debris.

--- SENTENCE 194 ---
Recovery of a reef destroyed by cold is much slower and is accomplished by settling of viable planula larvae.

--- SENTENCE 195 ---
This is similar to the phenomenon which follows the destruction of a coral reef by a lava flow.

--- SENTENCE 196 ---
Grigg and Maragos (1974) estimate that it may take 50 years for complete recovery of a sheltered reef, while in exposed areas with rapid seawater turnover, complete recolonization and substantial recovery may occur in 20 years.

--- SENTENCE 197 ---
I have uncovered no reports on the recolonization of a reef which was selectively denuded of its dominant corals while leaving substantial numbers of nondominant species, as well as the algae, echinoderms, mollusks and fish, relatively undisturbed except through loss of habitat.

--- SENTENCE 198 ---
The following is an assessment of the recolonization of such a reef suddenly bereft of the coral genera Acropora, Montipora, and Pocillopora five years before.

### Methods

During an environmental impact assessment for a Pala Lagoon dredging project, Maragos (Helfrich, 1975) recorded the bottom cover and coral counts of two transects that lay within my study area.

--- SENTENCE 199 ---
The exact location of these transects could only be estimated from the published maps, so specific transects were established during my 1974 study (see Part 1, fig. 1).

--- SENTENCE 200 ---
Transect A extends 400 meters at a compass heading of 95° mag.

--- SENTENCE 201 ---
from a concrete bunker erected in 1942 on the southeast tip of Coconut Point almost to the reef edge.

--- SENTENCE 202 ---
Transect B runs from the same bunker at 135° mag.

--- SENTENCE 203 ---
across Avatele Passage 400 meters to where Avatele Point meets the Pago Pago International Airport runway embankment.

--- SENTENCE 204 ---
Where practical, iron stakes were driven every 50 meters to mark transects.

--- SENTENCE 205 ---
Following previous methods, bottom cover estimates were made at 10-meter intervals along these transects.

--- SENTENCE 206 ---
The percentage of bottom coral species was determined along transect A at 25-meter intervals to give 16 determinations.

--- SENTENCE 207 ---
Live corals were identified, voucher specimens procured, and at a favorable low tide selected photographs of the area were taken.

--- SENTENCE 208 ---
The entire 8-hectare area was again examined as well as the surrounding reef areas during November 1978 to estimate coral growth and recolonization.

--- SENTENCE 209 ---
Work was done on foot at low tide in the shallows or with mask and snorkel in Avatele Passage and in the deep borrow pits excavated when the airport runways were constructed.

--- SENTENCE 210 ---
I was assisted by Mr. Flanigan on three of these excursions.

# Findings and Discussion

Erosional changes have occurred along the Coconut Point shoreline (Catell, 1977).

--- SENTENCE 212 ---
The bunker from which transect A begins, although constructed on land, was on the beach in 1969 and by 1978 was separated from land by 35 meters, with much water from the inshore lagoon flowing west of it except at low low tides.

--- SENTENCE 213 ---
The sand which has been eroded has been deposited on an extensive sand flat immediately south of Coconut Point and also in the borrow pits.

--- SENTENCE 214 ---
Huge thickets of *Acropora formosa* occupied these same deeper areas prior to 1973 at which time they all died.

--- SENTENCE 215 ---
Water levels have changed over portions of the reef flat so the water depth along transect A is now considerably less than when I first waded in it in 1969.

#### Conclusions

A portion of Coconut Point reef was denuded of many of its scleractinian corals 5 years before the present study.

--- SENTENCE 303 ---
Recolonization by natural processes has occurred since then and is proceeding rapidly.

--- SENTENCE 304 ---
Most of the species that had died out have reestablished themselves and do not appear abnormal.

--- SENTENCE 305 ---
Although reef contours have been changing since they were formed and will continue to do so, these natural processes have been modified by human interference, in this instance by the dredging of landfill borrow pits.

--- SENTENCE 306 ---
This may in part account for increased beach erosion along Coconut Point.

--- SENTENCE 307 ---
Many of the huge coral thickets had occupied these transient deep areas which are gradually being

filled in by sediment.

--- SENTENCE 308 ---
Initially these corals were established on a firm substrate immediately following the initial dredging.

--- SENTENCE 309 ---
It has yet to be shown what growth patterns will follow a destruction as recorded on this reef.

--- SENTENCE 310 ---
The Acropora thickets previously found in these particular areas will probably never be reestablished as they were in 1969 because of changes in substrate; however, it is reasonable to predict that an extensive well-developed reef can be established in as little as ten years provided the physical conditions remain adequate and there are nearby reefs to furnish viable larva for settlement.

--- SENTENCE 311 ---
Only five years have passed since an extensive coral kill was documented and the Nu'uuli reef is well on its way to renewing itself, although it will take many more years before this reef can be considered to be mature.
