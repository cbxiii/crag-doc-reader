## **Abstract**

One new site was added in the northwest, at Masacre Bay.

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
The mean benthic cover for all 12 sites was unchanged from 2005 to 2007.

--- SENTENCE 9 ---
All differences between years of all benthic categories were less than 3%, and due to random chance.

--- SENTENCE 10 ---
Thus, there was no loss of corals, but also no evidence of increase in corals.

--- SENTENCE 11 ---
This does not provide support for the view that the reefs are actively recovering from previous disturbances, rather, they appear to be in a stable equilibrium.

--- SENTENCE 12 ---
As in previous years, crustose calcareous algae (CCA) had much higher average cover on the south side than the north side, and higher turf cover on the north than the south.

--- SENTENCE 13 ---
However, the new site at Masacre Bay was much more like that on the south, with high CCA cover and lower turf cover, illustrating that the rule is not absolute.

--- SENTENCE 14 ---
The live coral index declined slightly from 2005 to 2007, however it remains much higher than the average for the South Pacific, and the decline may have been from more careful recording or from random effects.

--- SENTENCE 15 ---
All dead corals are old and covered with algae, usually coralline algae.

--- SENTENCE 16 ---
Coral cover on Tutuila was lower than reported at four remote unpopulated atolls in the Line and Phoenix Islands, but more than at two populated atolls in the Line Islands.

--- SENTENCE 17 ---
The amount of coral plus CCA cover was much higher than on the two populated atolls in the Line Islands, and nearly that of the unpopulated atoll in the Line Is.

--- SENTENCE 18 ---
When coral cover + CCA is plotted as one variable, and turf + fleshy macroalgae plotted as a second variable, Fagasa and Vatia stand out as being quite different from the other sites in having low values of the first, and high values of the second.

--- SENTENCE 19 ---
This suggests that these two sites may be more heavily impacted than other sites.

--- SENTENCE 20 ---
Both appear to have significant sediment impact.

--- SENTENCE 21 ---
Trends for the three years were examined for each site.

--- SENTENCE 22 ---
Fagamalo had a modest increase in coral cover and a decrease in CCA.

--- SENTENCE 23 ---
Fagasa had a modest increase in coral cover, a decrease in turf, and an increase in fleshy macroalgae.

--- SENTENCE 24 ---
Tafeu had an increase in the corallimorph *Rhodactis* sp.

--- SENTENCE 25 ---
(a large polyp related to corals that does not have a skeleton) and a decrease in turf.

--- SENTENCE 26 ---
Vatia had an increase in fleshy macroalgae and a decrease in exposed sand.

--- SENTENCE 27 ---
In 2007 the macroalga *Dictyota* appeared suddenly in large quantities, growing over *Halimeda,* sand and live coral.

--- SENTENCE 28 ---
This species had not been recorded previously at any transect at any site in any year.

--- SENTENCE 29 ---
It is one of the types of algae that have grown over reefs in the Caribbean.

--- SENTENCE 30 ---
Changes at Aoa, Aunuu, and Amaua were small and not systematic.

--- SENTENCE 31 ---
Fagaalu had a decrease in turf from 2005 to 2006, but no change since then.

--- SENTENCE 32 ---
Nuuuli had an increase in branching coralline algae (BCA) and a decrease in CCA recorded.

--- SENTENCE 33 ---
Likely the decrease in CCA was due to it being covered by BCA, and it is still there under the BCA.

--- SENTENCE 34 ---
Fagatele had a decrease in coral cover from the first to the second year that may have been due to a change in the placement of the transect tapes.

--- SENTENCE 35 ---
In 2007 a small amount of the encrusting ascidian *Diplosoma simile* appeared in the transects.

--- SENTENCE 36 ---
This species is capable of growing over corals and killing them.

--- SENTENCE 37 ---
There was a report that it became quite common in one area of the bay, but then

receded in the following year.

--- SENTENCE 38 ---
There was little change at Leone.

--- SENTENCE 39 ---
Changes in the mean cover of coral lifeforms in the transects at the 11 sites were small, with a slight reduction in the amount of encrusting corals and increase in the branching (including columnar) corals.

--- SENTENCE 40 ---
There was a slight decrease in *Montipora* (which is usually encrusting) and slight increase in *Porites* over the three years.

--- SENTENCE 41 ---
There was little change in coral species other than a small decrease in encrusting *Montipora*.

--- SENTENCE 42 ---
The number of coral species in transects did not change over the three years, nor did the diversity measure, H'.

--- SENTENCE 43 ---
Monitoring of reef flats was begun for the first time this year.

--- SENTENCE 44 ---
Outer reef flats were dominated by turf, and turf was the most common cover item on the inner reef flat.

--- SENTENCE 45 ---
Coral cover was higher on the outer reef flat than the inner, but turf was higher on the inner flat than the outer.

--- SENTENCE 46 ---
There was slightly more CCA on the outer flat than inner.

--- SENTENCE 47 ---
When comparing the reef flat with the slope, the slope had higher coral cover and much higher CCA cover than the flat, but much less turf.

--- SENTENCE 48 ---
A cohort of juvenile table corals (*Acropora hyacinthus*) were observed.

--- SENTENCE 49 ---
They were most common near the reef crest on both the reef flat and reef slope.

--- SENTENCE 50 ---
They were most common at Fagasa, where transects revealed 0.8 colonies per m2 .

--- SENTENCE 51 ---
They were abundant enough that if they all survive to maturity they will produce near 100% coral cover in some areas.

--- SENTENCE 52 ---
In most places they are much less common than Fagasa.

--- SENTENCE 53 ---
Sizes were measured and the distributions are essentially unimodal, suggesting a single recruitment event.

--- SENTENCE 54 ---
They appear to be about 3 years old.

--- SENTENCE 55 ---
Transects were also taken at several sites in the harbor on the outer reef flat or on rock walls.

--- SENTENCE 56 ---
Coral cover was highest near the mouth of the harbor where it reached high maximum levels (over 60%), but decreased to zero near the head of the harbor.

--- SENTENCE 57 ---
A small oyster is abundant near the waterline on rock walls in the harbor, so estimates of the oyster cover were made for several sites in the harbor.

--- SENTENCE 58 ---
Oyster cover was high near the head of the harbor and low at the mouth of the harbor.

--- SENTENCE 59 ---
Oysters are filter feeders feeding on plankton and may be a good bioindicator of plankton concentrations.

--- SENTENCE 60 ---
Visibility was then measured in the harbor with a Secci disc.

--- SENTENCE 61 ---
Clarity was low near the head of the harbor, and high near the mouth.

--- SENTENCE 62 ---
Plankton concentrations are high at the head of the harbor, probably because of nutrient inputs and very limited flushing, and low near the mouth of the harbor where flushing is much better.

--- SENTENCE 63 ---
Conditions near the head of the harbor apparently are still not good enough to support coral.

--- SENTENCE 64 ---
Coral bleaching monitoring continued, and mass coral bleaching of staghorns in backreef pools was recorded for the fourth year in a row.

--- SENTENCE 65 ---
At Alofau, these staghorns spend almost the entire year bleached, with only a short period of recovery.

--- SENTENCE 66 ---
So far, most corals recover each year.

--- SENTENCE 67 ---
The bleaching record at the airport pool follows the sea surface temperature (SST) very closely.

--- SENTENCE 68 ---
There continue to be relatively few visible invertebrates.

--- SENTENCE 69 ---
In general, spatial patterns of benthic cover, both between sites (along the shore) and in distance from shore (inner reef flat, outer flat, slope) showed strong variation and clear patterns, while temporal patterns (changes over time) at the same location had little or no variation at some sites, and strong changes at only a few sites, with no overall average changes.

--- SENTENCE 70 ---
Thus there are strong spatial variations, but little or no temporal variation so far.

The benthic methods were the same as in 2006, with a few minor changes.

--- SENTENCE 75 ---
In the core monitoring, two 50-m tapes were laid on a depth contour between 8 and 10 m deep.

--- SENTENCE 76 ---
A space between them of about 15 m was kept.

--- SENTENCE 77 ---
Benthic categories were recorded under each 0.5 m point on the tape.

--- SENTENCE 78 ---
Benthic categories included live coral, dead coral, dead coral with algae, crustose calcareous algae, branching coralline algae, fleshy macroalgae, turf algae, rock, sand, rubble, soft coral, and sponge.

--- SENTENCE 79 ---
"Branching coralline algae" included a soft feathery species that was the most common in that category.

--- SENTENCE 80 ---
That species is *Cheilosporum spectabile*.

--- SENTENCE 81 ---
Corals were identified to lifeform, genus, and species when possible, and if the macroalgae was *Halimeda* or *Dictyot*a, that was recorded.

--- SENTENCE 82 ---
Soft corals were recorded to genus when possible.

--- SENTENCE 83 ---
Lifeforms included encrusting, massive, foliose, branching, columnar, submassive, mushroom, *Millepora*, *Acropora* branching, *Acropora* table, *Acropora* digitate, and *Acropora* encrusting.

--- SENTENCE 84 ---
Horizontal visibility was recorded using the tape.

--- SENTENCE 85 ---
Two transect tapes were done on the first dive, and an additional two tapes were done on the second dive.

--- SENTENCE 86 ---
Invertebrates were recorded on a return pass.

--- SENTENCE 87 ---
Sites were re-located using the GPS and markers as indicated in the 2005 report.

--- SENTENCE 88 ---
One day was required for each site.

--- SENTENCE 89 ---
The same 11 core sites on Tutuila as recorded in 2005 were repeated, which are shown in the map above.

--- SENTENCE 90 ---

--- SENTENCE 91 ---

--- SENTENCE 92 ---

--- SENTENCE 93 ---
Until changes in coral cover or other rugosity changes are apparent, repeating e measurement of rugosity is not worth the increased risk of running out of air.

--- SENTENCE 94 ---

--- SENTENCE 95 ---

One view of coral reefs is that some disturbance events, such as hurricanes, are natural and that coral reefs have adapted to them over millions of years.

--- SENTENCE 145 ---
If a hurricane hits Tutuila an average of about once in 5 years, 300,000 hurricanes would have hit it in the 1.5 million years the island has been in existence.

--- SENTENCE 146 ---
This view requires that coral reefs recover from disturbances.

--- SENTENCE 147 ---
The natural state of affairs might be that after each disturbance kills coral, the corals recover over the following years or decades, until a new disturbance comes along.

--- SENTENCE 148 ---
Thus reefs would repeatedly cycle between disturbance and recovery.

--- SENTENCE 149 ---
The reefs of Tutuila have been exposed to a series of disturbances in recent decades, with a crown-of-thorns starfish outbreak in 1978 that killed most of the coral, hurricanes in 1981, 1987, 1990, 1991, 2004 and 2005, and mass coral bleaching in 1994, 2002 and 2003.

--- SENTENCE 150 ---
Craig et al. (2005) interpreted the ups and downs of coral cover recorded in the long-term monitoring programs by Birkeland and Green as being due to periodic disturbance and recovery.

--- SENTENCE 151 ---

--- SENTENCE 152 ---
This view suggests that reefs on Tutuila are likely to be in a recovery stage.

--- SENTENCE 189 ---
One of the major problems in interpreting such data or comparing different studies is the criteria for "dead coral."

--- SENTENCE 190 ---
When coral dies, a process starts in which it is partly eroded, partly reduced to rubble or sand, and partly consolidated into the reef.

--- SENTENCE 191 ---
Dead corals become covered with filamentous algae within days, may later become covered with coralline algae, and are bioeroded and consolidated into reef rock over time.

--- SENTENCE 192 ---
Thus, initially they are very easy to recognize as dead corals, distinct from reef rock, but over time they slowly transition from dead coral into reef rock.

--- SENTENCE 193 ---
The amount of dead coral that is recorded heavily depends on what the criterion is for distinguishing dead coral from reef rock is, and it is not an easy distinction since it is a continuum with no sharp demarcation.

--- SENTENCE 194 ---
In this monitoring program, when the outline of a coral could be distinguished it was generally recorded as dead coral, even though it was virtually always le at n covered with some combination of crustose calcareous algae and turf.

--- SENTENCE 195 ---
It is not possib this point to estimate the amount of time the coral has been dead, though coral that is still near-white has been dead for a very short period of time, weeks or less.

--- SENTENCE 196 ---
It appears that in areas where crustose calcareous algae dominate, they will dominate dead surfaces withi about 6 months of death.

--- SENTENCE 197 ---
In spite of all this, in American Samoa on reef slopes currently there are extremely few recently dead (near-white) coral colonies, so few that in most years not even a single colony was counted.

--- SENTENCE 198 ---
There are some dead corals where the outline of the colony is clear but it is covered with crustose calcareous algae and/or turf algae, and there are relatively few such corals that are badly enough eroded to be intermediate between recognizable coral and reef rock (that is virtually always covered with crustose calcareous algae and/or turf).

--- SENTENCE 199 ---
Thus, the figures for live coral index for the second and subsequent years of the program are likely to be pretty accurate.

--- SENTENCE 200 ---
It is much less certain whether figures from other locations are comparable, since the criteria for recognizing dead coral are not reported, and it is not reported whether there are many colonies that are partly eroded and intermediate between well-preserved dead coral and reef rock.

--- SENTENCE 201 ---
The situation in backreef pools and reef flats is likely to be different; approximately half of all the staghorn coral in some of the backreef pools is dead standing coral covered with turf and/or crustose calcareous algae, and there are patches of collapsed rubble (which is older, likely over 5 years old) from staghorn as well.

--- SENTENCE 202 ---
There is little dead coral of other species in the pools.

--- SENTENCE 203 ---
Another limitation to the live coral index is that when a disturbance event occurs, the live coral index will show it clearly due to the dead coral, but with time the dead coral will become much less obvious and at some point will no longer be recorded as dead coral.

--- SENTENCE 204 ---
