## Coral reefs are important

Healthy coral reefs are among the most biologically diverse ecosystems on Earth, with high cultural and economic significance.

--- SENTENCE 2 ---
They provide billions of dollars in **food, jobs, recreational opportunities, coastal protection**, and other important goods and services to people around the world.

--- SENTENCE 3 ---
The global decline in coral reefs has had significant ecological, social, cultural, and economic impacts on people and communities.

--- SENTENCE 4 ---
The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA)

--- SENTENCE 5 ---
Coral Reef Conservation Program (CRCP) leads efforts to monitor and conserve coral reef resources and the ecosystem services they provide for current and future generations.

--- SENTENCE 6 ---
In 2011, CRCP established the National Coral Reef Monitoring Program (NCRMP)—an integrated and focused monitoring effort with partners across the U.S. and its territories.

--- SENTENCE 7 ---
Scientists collect biological, physical, chemical, and socioeconomic data to provide a robust picture of the condition of U.S. coral reefs and inform management decisions.

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
For more information, visit coralreef.noaa.gov.

## reefs are under threat

Coral reefs are sensitive ecosystems that thrive when conditions are right—warm, clear, shallow waters that contain low nutrient levels.

--- SENTENCE 13 ---
However, factors at both local and global scales can disrupt these conditions.

--- SENTENCE 14 ---
Threats such as **fishing impacts** and **land-based sources of pollution**  can be managed at a local level, while coral reefs will also benefit from the reduction of greenhouse gases that fuel **climate change and ocean acidification**.

--- SENTENCE 15 ---
Over-fishing leads to a reduction in the amount of reef fish species in many locations, which can disrupt the delicate ecological balance on the reef.

--- SENTENCE 16 ---
Physical impacts from fishing activities, like anchoring and marine debris, can also damage coral reefs.

--- SENTENCE 17 ---
Derelict fishing gear such as gill nets, trap line, and monofilament fishing line is prevalent in American Samoa and can entangle and kill reef organisms.

--- SENTENCE 18 ---
Land-based sources of pollution result from agriculture, deforestation, land clearing, storm water, impervious surfaces, coastal development, road construction, and oil and chemical spills.

--- SENTENCE 19 ---
This pollution impacts coral reefs in many ways, including 1) sedimentation, which smothers corals with dirt, 2) nutrient enrichment, which can lead to overgrowth of corals by seaweeds, and 3) the introduction of toxins and diseases into the system.

--- SENTENCE 20 ---
These pollutants can directly harm or kill corals, and also indirectly affect reefs by disrupting critical ecological functions, food webs, and fish populations.

--- SENTENCE 21 ---
Climate change and ocean acidification impacts threaten coral reefs at local to global scales through sea-level rise, mass coral bleaching, and disease outbreaks.

--- SENTENCE 22 ---
Increasing water temperatures and ocean acidification reduce coral growth.

--- SENTENCE 23 ---
In the long term, failure to reduce carbon dioxide and other heat-trapping gases that cause ocean acidification and rising temperatures will continue to impede conservation and management efforts for coral reefs.

# CORALS & ALGAE

Corals & algae make up the base of the coral reef ecosystem, providing food and shelter for fish, shellfish, and marine mammals.

--- SENTENCE 28 ---
They are also important economic and tourism resources.

--- SENTENCE 29 ---
The five indicators for corals & algae are:

- Coral reef cover, which includes corals, algae, and crustose coralline algae.

--- SENTENCE 30 ---
- **Coral populations**, a measure of the population's ability to reproduce and sustain itself.

--- SENTENCE 31 ---
- Herbivory, a measure of the level of grazing pressure by fish on corals and algae.
- **Mortality**, which measures the amount of recently dead coral.
- Diversity, a measure of the number of different species of coral present.

--- SENTENCE 32 ---
The coral-eating crown-of-thorns sea star (COTS) has a serious impact on Indo-Pacific reefs.

--- SENTENCE 33 ---
Several recent COTS infestations on American Samoa reefs may be linked to nutrient inputs.

--- SENTENCE 34 ---
Corals at Swains and Tutuila Islands experience most of the impacts.

--- SENTENCE 39 ---
Coral reefs serve as habitat and food for fish species.

--- SENTENCE 40 ---
These fish are important to the ecology of the reef, national and global economies, and the livelihoods of local villagers.

--- SENTENCE 41 ---
The four indicators chosen for fish are:

- Reef fish, a measure of the amount of fish present.
- **Sustainability**, which is indicative of whether fishery stocks still have abundant large breeding-sized fishes.

--- SENTENCE 42 ---
- Sharks and other predators, a measure of the amount of fish that eat other fish.

--- SENTENCE 43 ---
- **Diversity**, a measure of the number of different species of fish present.

--- SENTENCE 44 ---
Sharks are important to the ecological function of coral reefs.

--- SENTENCE 45 ---
Their removal negatively affects the entire coral reef food web.

--- SENTENCE 46 ---
Shark populations in American Samoa today are only 4–8% of historical populations.

--- SENTENCE 47 ---
Large reef fish such as Bumphead parrotfish are considered locally extinct while others like Humphead Wrasse are heavily pressured.

--- SENTENCE 48 ---
Local management actions such as banning SCUBA spearfishing and the take of large reef fish species, if properly supported and enforced, will help these fish populations recover.

Climate affects all components of a reef system, from the building blocks of coral to the reproductive success of fish.

--- SENTENCE 100 ---
Climate change and ocean acidification influence reefs across the globe, but conditions vary at the regional and local level.

--- SENTENCE 101 ---
The three climate indicators are:

- Temperature stress, which evaluates the frequency and severity of high temperature events.

--- SENTENCE 102 ---
- Ocean acidification, indicating if the water chemistry is suitable for coral growth and other calcifiers.
- Reef material growth, which directly measures coral growth in relation to water chemistry.

--- SENTENCE 103 ---
High temperature stress, bleaching, and mortality hit American Samoa reefs hard in 2015.

--- SENTENCE 104 ---
Severe bleaching and mortality occurred on shallow inshore and lagoonal reefs along southern Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 105 ---
These shallow habitats have limited water circulation, which worsens the effects of high temperature stress.

--- SENTENCE 106 ---
Higher survival occurred on forereef habitats in deeper waters throughout American Samoa.

## human connections

Coral reef management agencies protect coral reef resources through management plans, public education, and facilitating village and community involvement in natural resource management.

--- SENTENCE 109 ---
The three indicators for human connections are:

- Awareness, an indicator of residents' familiarity with threats to and the importance of reefs.

--- SENTENCE 110 ---
- Support for management actions, an indicator of support for reef management activities.
- Pro-environmental behavior, an indicator of residents' participation in activities to protect the environment.

--- SENTENCE 111 ---
The majority of residents participate in fishing, swimming, and beach recreation.

--- SENTENCE 112 ---
Fishing is largely for subsistence rather than commercial or recreational purposes.

American Samoans believe that coral reef resources are important and this is reflected in their support for management efforts.

#### STATUS REPORT FRAMEWORK

The American Samoa status report presents the results of an analysis of indicators that were chosen to reflect key ecosystem processes and values.

--- SENTENCE 122 ---
The report provides a geographically specific assessment of American Samoa shallow (0–100 feet) coral reef condition for the period 2012–2016.

--- SENTENCE 123 ---
Data were collected by NOAA NCRMP using a survey design that is representative at the island level; results should not be interpreted at the site level.

--- SENTENCE 124 ---
Uncertainty in the data was considered differently for each indicator and the precision of scores was not determined.

--- SENTENCE 125 ---
Data for each indicator were measured against a reference value and converted to a numeric score.

--- SENTENCE 126 ---
For the American Samoa status report, three fish indicators and five benthic indicators were assessed to evaluate biological condition, three indicators were assessed to evaluate climate impacts, and three indicators were assessed to evaluate human connections.

--- SENTENCE 127 ---
These indicators were then averaged into an overall score for each theme and sub-region, and then to an overall Coral Reef Condition score for American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 128 ---
For more detailed information on methodologies, indicators, thresholds, and grading, visit <a href="http://www.coris.noaa.gov">http://www.coris.noaa.gov</a> (keyword: status reports).
