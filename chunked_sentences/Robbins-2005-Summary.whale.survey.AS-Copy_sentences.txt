## 8 ABSTRACT

American Samoa is a poorly understood wintering site for humpback whales in Oceania.

--- SENTENCE 3 ---
Here we describe the results of photo-identification and biopsy sampling performed on 27 days between September 18 and October 7, 2003-2005.

--- SENTENCE 4 ---
Work was performed from a 7-meter vessel working in the coastal waters of Tutuila, the primary island of American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 5 ---
The detection rate was consistently low, averaging 5.5 humpback whales per day in all three years.

--- SENTENCE 6 ---
The most common sightings were of singletons (31%) and paired whales (23%).

--- SENTENCE 7 ---
Four singletons were confirmed to be singing, but not all were checked for that behavior.

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
Mother-calf pairs were alone (15%) or escorted by a single large whale (12%).

--- SENTENCE 9 ---
One mother was observed on two occasions prior to her first sighting with a calf, making it likely that the birth took place in American Samoa waters.

--- SENTENCE 10 ---
Competitive activity was observed in 9% of groups sighted, despite the low densities observed.

--- SENTENCE 11 ---
Competitive groups were small, ranging from only three to six whales (average=3.8).

--- SENTENCE 12 ---
Overall, the behaviors observed were consistent with those at other low-density breeding grounds, although apparent feeding was also noted on one occasion.

--- SENTENCE 13 ---
Of 50 individuals with sufficient photo-documentation, none were re-sighted there between years.

--- SENTENCE 14 ---
However, 11% (n-4) of those photo-identified prior to 2005 were successfully matched to other breeding sites in Oceania, including the Cook Islands, Tonga and Moorea (French Polynesia).

--- SENTENCE 15 ---
Together, these results indicate that American Samoa is part of a widely dispersed breeding population.

--- SENTENCE 16 ---
In the future, molecular genetic analysis of skin samples from this area (n=45) may belp to clarify breeding stock structure and migratory destinations.

#### DISCUSSION 89

- The results of this study indicate that American Samoa is a low-density wintering area where mating and calving 90
- 91 likely take place.

We observed the full range of behaviors typically found on humpback whale breeding
- 92 grounds, including singing and competitive group activity.

- 93 before and after calving strongly suggests that at least some births occur in American Samoa waters.
