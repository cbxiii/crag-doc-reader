### **Abstract**

 Because the tsunami in Sept 29, 2009 not only destroyed the boat ramps at Pago Pago Harbor and Fagasa, and damaged almost all DMWR vehicles beyond repair by immersion in saltwater, plus all three DMWR boats were inoperable almost the entire year, only a small amount of data was collected.

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
Only 2 sites on the reef slopes were surveyed in 2010.

--- SENTENCE 9 ---
Coral cover for the two sites has not shown any trend over the last 6 years, and in 2010 was 32%, very similar to that of the average of all 12 sites in previous years.

--- SENTENCE 10 ---
The live coral index remains very high compared to other reference averages such as those for the Pacific, South Pacific, Indo-Pacific, and world.

--- SENTENCE 11 ---
The most common coral lifeform remains encrusting, and *Acropora*, *Montipora*, and

--- SENTENCE 12 ---
*Pavona* are the most common genera.

--- SENTENCE 13 ---
The number of genera per site in these two sites has remained stable over the last 6 years.

--- SENTENCE 14 ---
Encrusting *Montipora* is the most common species at Aunu'u and *Pavona varians* the most common at Faga'alu.

--- SENTENCE 15 ---
Aunu'u had more coral species than Faga'alu.

--- SENTENCE 16 ---
The number of species was steady at Faga'alu but increased in the last two years at Aunu'u.

--- SENTENCE 17 ---
Algae and other categories did not show trends over years except that coralline algae increased in 2010 in Faga'alu.

--- SENTENCE 18 ---
There were very few invertebrates, with the orange sponge *Stylissa* being the most common, sets of small worm holes being second most common, and

--- SENTENCE 19 ---
grooves made by

alpheid (snapping) shrimp being third most common.

--- SENTENCE 20 ---
There was no clear trend in the number of kinds of invertebrates.

--- SENTENCE 21 ---
Cover was also measured on outer reef flats at four sites.

--- SENTENCE 22 ---
Coral covered an average of about 35%.

--- SENTENCE 23 ---
There was no clear trend in reef flat coral cover.

--- SENTENCE 24 ---
The live coral index was high and showed no trends.

--- SENTENCE 25 ---
Staghorn corals in the backreef pools bleached in the austral summer season (December to May) as they had in the previous 6 years in the airport, but may have been less at Alofau (or the higher levels may have occurred during a gap in monitoring).

--- SENTENCE 26 ---
Quantitative data are now available after the tsunami for a few sites.

--- SENTENCE 27 ---
Coral cover did not decrease at Faga'alu or Aunu'u at 9 m depth or at Faga'alu at 4 m depth, but decreased at 18 m depth at Faga'alu from 60% cover to 28% cover.

--- SENTENCE 28 ---
At 18 m depth at Faga'alu the transect was on a delicate plate coral community.

--- SENTENCE 29 ---
There was a sharp line below which all plates were removed by the tsunami, and above which the plates were all intact.

--- SENTENCE 30 ---
The dividing line between these two zones was shallower than the transect in some areas and deeper in others.

--- SENTENCE 31 ---
A transect placed deeper would likely produce very low coral cover, and a transect placed shallower but still in the plate zone might produce near 60% coral cover.

--- SENTENCE 32 ---
Coral cover did not decrease at Nu'uuli at 4 m but did decrease from 30% to 18% cover at 18 m deep.

--- SENTENCE 33 ---
There was no visible sign of damage at this location, so that change is most likely due to a small change in transect location.

--- SENTENCE 34 ---
In Appendix 1, a short essay on the state of coral reef habitat written for the Fisheries Council concluded that while the reefs are not perfect, most variables indicate they are relatively healthy.

--- SENTENCE 35 ---
Coral cover and the live coral index are higher than the average for the Pacific, South Pacific, and particularly the Caribbean (coral cover).

--- SENTENCE 36 ---
Water clarity is good, coralline algae cover high, and macroalgal cover is low.

--- SENTENCE 37 ---
There are few filter feeders, bioeroders, or introduced species and no invasive species known.

--- SENTENCE 38 ---
Sedimentation has damaged only small areas, and nutrients have only produced small areas of macroalgae blooms.

--- SENTENCE 39 ---
The harbor is heavily impacted.

--- SENTENCE 40 ---
Habitat quality outside the harbor provides little support for suggesting that the lower fish biomass or low large fish abundances we have are due to poor habitat quality.

## **Methods**

The original 11 core sites are shown in the map below.

--- SENTENCE 43 ---
All are on Tutuila and nearby Aunu'u.

!

--- SENTENCE 44 ---
The benthic methods were the same as in 2009.

--- SENTENCE 45 ---
In the core monitoring, four 50-m tapes were laid on a depth contour between 8 and 10 m deep.

--- SENTENCE 46 ---
A space between them of about 10 m was kept.

--- SENTENCE 47 ---
Benthic categories were recorded under each 0.5 m point on the tape.

--- SENTENCE 48 ---
Benthic categories included live coral, dead coral, dead coral with algae, crustose calcareous algae, branching coralline algae, fleshy macroalgae, turf algae, rock, sand, rubble, soft coral, and sponge.

--- SENTENCE 49 ---
"Branching coralline algae" included a soft feathery species that was the most common in that category.

--- SENTENCE 50 ---
That species is *Cheilosporum spectabile*.

--- SENTENCE 51 ---
Any rock that is not colored white has turf on it, and was recorded as turf.

--- SENTENCE 52 ---
Corals were identified to lifeform, genus, and species when possible, and if the macroalgae was *Halimeda* or *Dictyota*, or something else that was identifiable, that was recorded.

--- SENTENCE 53 ---
Soft corals were recorded to genus when possible.

--- SENTENCE 54 ---
Hard coral lifeforms included encrusting, massive, foliose, branching, columnar, submassive, mushroom, *Millepora*, *Acropora* branching, *Acropora* table, *Acropora* digitate, and *Acropora* encrusting.

--- SENTENCE 55 ---
Horizontal visibility was recorded using the tape.

--- SENTENCE 56 ---
Two transect tapes were done on the first dive, and an additional two tapes were done on the second dive.

--- SENTENCE 57 ---
Invertebrates were recorded on a return pass.

--- SENTENCE 58 ---
Sites were re-located using the GPS and markers as indicated in the 2005 report.

--- SENTENCE 59 ---
One day was required for each site.

--- SENTENCE 60 ---
In 2008, a total of 12 sites were recorded, including the original 11 plus Masacre Bay.

--- SENTENCE 61 ---
For 2010, however, the lack of operating boats and boat ramps meant that only two sites were monitored in the calendar year.

--- SENTENCE 62 ---
The tsunami made the boat ramps inoperable at Pago Pago and Fagasa for the entire year.

--- SENTENCE 63 ---
As in 2007-2009, the rugosity measurements were omitted, because a third team member was not available and when included it lengthened dive times to the point where running out of air was a distinct possibility, thus reducing the margin of safety.

--- SENTENCE 64 ---
Further, it appears that the measurement depends primarily on exactly where the chain falls, and that changes in rugosity caused by coral growth will take quite a few years before they would be detectable.

--- SENTENCE 65 ---
A hurricane could make changes in rugosity quickly by removing corals, and if significant hurricane damage occurs, the rugosity measurements can be repeated.

--- SENTENCE 66 ---
Until changes in coral cover or other rugosity changes are apparent, repeating the measurement of rugosity is not worth the increased risk of running out of air.

--- SENTENCE 67 ---
In future years it is hoped that an additional team member can record the rugosity measure, or additional boat dives are available to take the rugosity measure.

--- SENTENCE 68 ---
In the meantime, it will be considered a lower priority item, and will be done on an opportunistic basis.

--- SENTENCE 69 ---
When laying the tape, the primary consideration is to keep the tape between 8 m and 9 m deep.

--- SENTENCE 70 ---
The tape is passed along the sides of projections, including live corals such as *Pocillopora* and table corals, which usually have an overhanging side.

--- SENTENCE 71 ---
If it is passed around first one side of one projection and then the other side of another, it is anchored securely from wave action moving it either way at that point.

--- SENTENCE 72 ---
An attempt is made to anchor the tape in this fashion as often as possible, but in some areas there is little to anchor the tape on.

--- SENTENCE 73 ---
A continuing problem is what to do about clefts in the reef.

--- SENTENCE 74 ---
A cleft that is narrow and deep is crossed straight to an anchoring point on the other side.

--- SENTENCE 75 ---
If it is large, then the tape may be laid along one side of it, going up toward shallower water but staying at 8-9 m depth, and then when the bottom rises to that depth, crossing to the other side and continuing on that side out of the canyon.

--- SENTENCE 76 ---
The principle problem with that is finding an anchoring point near the head of the canyon that can hold the tape at the head.

--- SENTENCE 77 ---
The tape is read at each point by reading the substrate under the point at the time at which the diver is directly above the point.

--- SENTENCE 78 ---
A string and weight are not used, as surge and the movement of the tape in the surge makes that a much more difficult and slow procedure.

--- SENTENCE 79 ---
If the tape is stretched between two points far apart and the surge is heavy, the tape can move a meter or more in either direction with each wave.

--- SENTENCE 80 ---
This opens up an opportunity for bias, as the point on the tape sweeps across a variety of benthic patches.

--- SENTENCE 81 ---
If the point on the bottom is recorded that is first seen from a vertical viewpoint, then bias is minimized.

--- SENTENCE 82 ---
An attempt is made to minimize bias in laying the tape by choosing a route based on depth and anchoring points for the tape, not the substrate.

--- SENTENCE 83 ---
The direct observation underwater of what is under points makes it easier to identify species, and so allows greater taxonomic resolution than video techniques.

#### **Reef Slopes**

 It was only possible to collect transect data at two sites, Aunu'u and Faga'alu.

--- SENTENCE 101 ---
In order to look for trends and include the 2010 data, it is necessary to compare the 2010 data with just the Aunu'u and Faga'alu data from previous years.

--- SENTENCE 102 ---
The small number of sites increases the amount of variation between years and decreases the ability to detect changes.

--- SENTENCE 103 ---
As seen in Figure 1, live coral cover was very steady over the six years of monitoring, with no trend.

--- SENTENCE 104 ---
Turf decreased in 2010 and was replaced by crustose calcareous algae.

--- SENTENCE 105 ---
Since there was no trend before 2010 and variation in turf has been high, this may not be a real trend.

--- SENTENCE 106 ---
These two sites just happen to average about the same coral cover as the whole island (all 12 sites).

--- SENTENCE 107 ---
Trends in benthic cover for the two sites surveyed in 2010.

!

--- SENTENCE 108 ---
The live coral cover index (live coral/(live coral + dead coral) was high, with no trends apparent.

--- SENTENCE 109 ---
It was calculated for just the two sites for all years, so they are comparable between years.

--- SENTENCE 110 ---
The live coral index remains well above the PROCFish average for the South Pacific (Secretariat of the Pacific Community, 2005).

--- SENTENCE 111 ---
High values are good.

--- SENTENCE 112 ---
The proportion of corals that are alive is an important measure of reef health.

--- SENTENCE 113 ---
There is very little dead coral around Tutuila currently.

--- SENTENCE 114 ---
The PROCFish program based at SPC in New Caledonia surveys coral reefs in many Pacific countries.

--- SENTENCE 115 ---
They have devised a "live coral index" which is the ratio of live coral to all coral (live and dead).

--- SENTENCE 116 ---
A reef where most corals are alive is healthy compared to a reef where most corals are dead.

--- SENTENCE 117 ---
Their index averages about 55% in the South Pacific (Secretariat of the Pacific Community, 2005), but the index averages about 93% here in Tutuila (Figure 13).

--- SENTENCE 118 ---
In the Philippines, 48% of 844 reefs had an index below 50% (Gomez et al. 1994a,b).

--- SENTENCE 119 ---
In Indonesia, unpolluted reefs had an average of index of 75% while polluted reefs averaged 48% (Edinger et al. 1998).

--- SENTENCE 120 ---
The Reef Check program also has reported an index that can be converted to the live coral index, which turned out to be just below 80% for the Indo-Pacific and just over 80% for the world (Hodgson, 1999).

--- SENTENCE 121 ---
Thus, Tutuila has a higher (better) live coral index than the South Pacific average, the Philippines, Indonesia, the whole Indo-Pacific, and world.

#### **Trends at Individual Sites**

 Data in 2009 was gathered from Aunu'u before the Sept 29 tsunami.

--- SENTENCE 147 ---
The six years of records in Figure 15 show no clear trends and no evidence of any effect of the tsunami.

--- SENTENCE 148 ---
The reef at Aunu'u in 2010 looks just like it did in 2005.

--- SENTENCE 149 ---
[](_page_21_Figure_3.jpeg)

 Faga'alu shows no trend in coral cover, with low coral cover continuing (Figure 16).

--- SENTENCE 150 ---
Crustose calcareous algae cover was the highest yet and the turf algal cover the lowest yet.

--- SENTENCE 151 ---
However, these two show strong random variation over the years with no clear sign of an overall trend.

--- SENTENCE 152 ---
It may be that turf growing over crustose coralline algae at the site makes the distinction difficult, or that the exact location of the tapes makes a big difference.

--- SENTENCE 153 ---
The reef slope at Faga'alu at the study site consists of a large field of rubble consisting of cylindrical branches, covered with coralline algae.

--- SENTENCE 154 ---
The coralline algae have cemented the rubble together enough that the tsunami did no damage to the rubble field, even though there was dramatic evidence of damage deeper on the slope.

--- SENTENCE 155 ---
The rubble is clearly from a staghorn species, and since some branches are large diameter it seems most likely that they are from *Acropora nobilis*.

--- SENTENCE 156 ---
The field looked identical to the present when it was first surveyed in 2005.

--- SENTENCE 157 ---
Whatever killed it, did so well before 2005, because it was all collapsed in 2005, none of it was standing.

--- SENTENCE 158 ---
It could have been broken by a hurricane, or eaten by crown-of-thorns starfish, or killed by mass coral bleaching, or killed by disease.

--- SENTENCE 159 ---
*Acropora* is one of the most vulnerable genera to all of these.

--- SENTENCE 160 ---
So the cause of their death

is not known.

--- SENTENCE 161 ---
But the lack of any increase in coral cover is consistent with the observation of no signs of recovery, including no signs of new recruits.

--- SENTENCE 162 ---
There are more corals on the shallower part of the slope, and a dense community of living plate corals deeper, but in a wide depth band around medium depths, the community is dominated heavily by coralline algae-covered rubble with no signs of change.

--- SENTENCE 163 ---
Obviously at one time this area supported a lush coral community, and in spite of probably 10 or more years since their death, there is no sign of recovery underway.

--- SENTENCE 164 ---
Why there is no sign of recovery is not at all clear.

## **Appendix 1.*

--- SENTENCE 281 ---
*

The following document was provided as a summary of the condition of the benthic communities of the coral reefs, as habitat, to the Fisheries Council for incorporation into their Coral Reef Plan Team report.

--- SENTENCE 282 ---
The State of Coral Reef Habitat in American Samoa, 2011 Douglas Fenner, Ph.D.

 Vroom (2010) stated, "…all researchers would likely agree that high biodiversity, high fish biomass, intact apex predator communities, low incidences of disease, and the ability to accrete calcium carbonate faster than erosion occurs are among key factors in defining health

--- SENTENCE 283 ---
Biodiversity should be relative to the local species pool.

--- SENTENCE 284 ---
Also, relatively low levels of anthropogenic sedimentation and nutrification are key factors, along with good herbivore stocks and only moderate levels of brown macroalgae.

--- SENTENCE 285 ---
The submarine slope around Tutuila consists of reef flats, reef slopes, shelf, bank reefs on the shelf, deep escarpment and deep slope.

--- SENTENCE 286 ---
The reef flats, slopes and bank reefs have coral on them.

--- SENTENCE 287 ---
The shelf is the largest component and the reef slopes are probably the narrowest.

--- SENTENCE 288 ---
The reef slope and reef flat are the most studied.

--- SENTENCE 289 ---
Five different monitoring programs have all reported coral cover of about 30% on Tutuila reef slopes using transects.

--- SENTENCE 290 ---
The Territorial Monitoring Program (TMP) of DMWR finds no downward trends in coral cover in at least 4 years, and maybe a slight increase; both the Key Reef Species and CRED programs show increases.

--- SENTENCE 291 ---
Crustose coralline algae cover is high on reef slopes, particularly on the upper slopes and particularly on the south side of the island.

--- SENTENCE 292 ---
Turf is more abundant on reef flats than coralline algae, and coral is less abundant on reef flats than reef slopes, but is still about 8-21% cover.

--- SENTENCE 293 ---
The predominant cover on the reefs is encrusting, both encrusting coralline algae and encrusting corals.

--- SENTENCE 294 ---
This may provide less hiding cover for fish than would branching coral, though the reef matrix provides many hiding holes most places.

--- SENTENCE 295 ---
The coral cover is higher than current averages for the Pacific and South Pacific and particularly the Caribbean, which has 8% cover.

--- SENTENCE 296 ---
However, it is lower than the coral cover reported from the Pacific and Caribbean from the past, and an estimate of coral cover from Tututila in the past.

--- SENTENCE 297 ---
However, those numbers are higher than the 35-40% cover reported from the most pristine coral reefs remaining around the world.

--- SENTENCE 298 ---
Vroom (2010) reports reefs here have very similar levels of coralline algae and macroalgae to the near-pristine US remote islands, to the north, and slightly lower coral cover here.

--- SENTENCE 299 ---
The rate of coral cover change found by TMP is 1% increase per year, compared to 5-9% annual losses for different regions of the world's reefs.

--- SENTENCE 300 ---
The percentage of corals alive (over 90%) is much higher than for the whole Pacific (55%).

--- SENTENCE 301 ---
The tsunami of Sept. 29, 2009, did significant damage to reef areas in Vatia Bay, Fagatele Bay, and Leone Bay, and lesser damage elsewhere.

--- SENTENCE 302 ---
Heavily damaged areas were rare, moderately damaged areas more common, and lightly damaged or undamaged areas the most common.

--- SENTENCE 303 ---
Within about 6 months, all the rubble moved in Fagatele Bay was completely covered with coralline algae, while none is at Vatia.

--- SENTENCE 304 ---
Hurricane Wilma did additional damage in Vatia on Feb. 24, 2011, but little elsewhere.

--- SENTENCE 305 ---
Sedimentation rates near the mouths of streams are much higher than inside bays, which are in turn higher than outside bays.

--- SENTENCE 306 ---
The water on outer reef slopes away from streams is relatively clear, with low nutrient levels.

--- SENTENCE 307 ---
There is damage to small areas near stream mouths, and both Vatia Bay and the reef flat next to Coconut Point have had dense blooms of brown macroalgae.

--- SENTENCE 308 ---
Those are no longer present in Vatia, but persist at Coconut Point.

--- SENTENCE 309 ---
The reef slopes are dominated by calcareous algae, most by coralline algae, but also by the green macroalga *Halimeda*.

--- SENTENCE 310 ---
They contribute to building the reef, and are not known to bloom during phase shifts, unlike brown algae.

--- SENTENCE 311 ---
The reefs have remarkably little brown macroalgae.

--- SENTENCE 312 ---
Reefs in the harbor are in very poor condition.

--- SENTENCE 313 ---
There are only a few introduced marine species, none of which are invasive.

--- SENTENCE 314 ---
There are very few bioeroders or filter feeders, and calcium accumulation on the reef appears to be very good.

--- SENTENCE 315 ---
Disease incidence is low.

--- SENTENCE 316 ---
Macroinvertebrates, including herbivorous urchins, are in general uncommon to rare, for unknown reasons, but very likely this is natural.

--- SENTENCE 317 ---
Some may be hidden from sight.

--- SENTENCE 318 ---
Macroinvertebrates are food for some types of fish.

--- SENTENCE 319 ---
Hawaii and the Marianas also lack abundant large non-cryptic invertebrates.

--- SENTENCE 320 ---
There have been no bleaching events in the last 7 years, but 3 events before that.

--- SENTENCE 321 ---
Peter Houk reports a negative correlation of human population with coral diversity, but TMP has been unable to replicate that using slightly different variables and different sites.

--- SENTENCE 322 ---
The largest single disturbance on the territory's coral reefs was the crown-of-thorns starfish outbreak around 1978.

--- SENTENCE 323 ---
Over 90% of all corals were eaten.

--- SENTENCE 324 ---
Observers report that they remember that table corals and staghorns were common, but areas dominated by other corals were not unusual.

--- SENTENCE 325 ---
Most of our reefs are now dominated by encrusting corals and only a few patches have high densities of tables and staghorns, except perhaps the banks where tables are common.

--- SENTENCE 326 ---
Thus the reefs may still be recovering from that event.

--- SENTENCE 327 ---
One reef patch at the mouth of Vatia Bay has shown remarkably rapid recovery, but other areas have recovered slowly.

--- SENTENCE 328 ---
The cause is not known, but does not seem to correlate with human populations.

--- SENTENCE 329 ---
Benthic reef communities are by no means pristine, but relatively healthy and far healthier than places like the Caribbean.

--- SENTENCE 330 ---
Habitat quality outside the harbor provides little support for suggesting that the lower fish biomass or low large fish abundances we have are due to poor habitat quality.
