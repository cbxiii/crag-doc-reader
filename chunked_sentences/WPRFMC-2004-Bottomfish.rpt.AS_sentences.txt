# Bottomfish and Seamount Groundfish Fisheries of the Western Pacific 2003 Annual Report

#### 1.0 Introduction

The 2003 annual report provides a set of descriptors and indicators of the bottomfish fisheries from American Samoa, Guam, Hawaii and the Northern Mariana Islands.

--- SENTENCE 111 ---
The descriptors are designed to document recent trends in landings, effort, participation, revenue and prices.

--- SENTENCE 112 ---
Should management action be recommended, descriptor information will aid in assessing potential impacts of the action on fishery participants.

--- SENTENCE 113 ---
The indicators are quantifiable and measurable tools used to identify signs of stress in the stocks or the fishery.

--- SENTENCE 114 ---
Based on changes over time in indicator levels, the Bottomfish Plan Team (BPT) may identify "yellow light" situations (i.e., where stress is first detected) and recommend that either management action or additional study be undertaken or "red light" situations where immediate management action is needed.

--- SENTENCE 115 ---
The annual report is organized as follows: The introduction section defines and briefly explains the descriptors and indicators.

--- SENTENCE 116 ---
The next section briefly summarizes time trends in descriptor and indicator levels, through the current year, and recommends any areas of concern for each island area.

--- SENTENCE 117 ---
Reports from each island area are appended.

--- SENTENCE 118 ---
The introduction describes the history and present characteristics of the fishery.

--- SENTENCE 119 ---
Results of the current year's descriptors and indicators are presented in detail, in relation to past temporal trends.

--- SENTENCE 120 ---
Figures are supported with information on source of the data, methods of calculation, and data interpretation.

--- SENTENCE 121 ---
Table 1 summarizes 2003 bottomfish statistics for the region.

--- SENTENCE 122 ---
The appended report from each area includes a summary of the new area specific and region-wide recommendations.

--- SENTENCE 123 ---
Finally, additional appendices contain information on NMFS 2003 administrative and enforcement activities, habitat conditions, protected species interactions, and 2003 BPT membership.

--- SENTENCE 124 ---
Table 2 lists scientific, common English and local/indigenous names for bottomfish management unit species (BMUS) for each area (American Samoa, Guam/Northern Marianas, and Hawaii).

Time trends in economic performance are characterized by plots of total ex-vessel revenue, aggregate average price levels, and for Hawaii, price trends over time for major species.

#### 1.2 Definition of Indicators

Indicators were developed as tools for identifying signs of stress in the stocks or the fishery which deserve further investigation and/or a management response.

--- SENTENCE 171 ---
Analyses consider how the indicators change over time.

--- SENTENCE 172 ---
Indicators for Hawaii include 95% confidence intervals.

--- SENTENCE 173 ---
To the degree possible, similar variance estimates are expected from the other areas in future annual reports.

--- SENTENCE 174 ---
The indicators are defined as follows:

# 1.2.1 Aggregate Catch-Per-Unit-Effort

If the current year's aggregate catch-per-unit-effort (CPUE) is less than 50% of the average aggregate CPUE for the first three years of available data, there may be cause for concern.

--- SENTENCE 175 ---
CPUE information is available for all areas; research CPUE is available for SE Hancock Seamount for all years since 1985, except in 1992 and 1994-2003.

--- SENTENCE 176 ---
#### 1.2.2 Mean Fish Size

--- SENTENCE 177 ---
If there has been a significant reduction in mean fish size for a species over time, the stock may be stressed by the fishery.

--- SENTENCE 178 ---
Mean size information is provided for nine species in Hawaii.

--- SENTENCE 179 ---
No mean size information was available at this time for American Samoa, Guam or the Northern Marianas.

--- SENTENCE 180 ---
If over 50% of the catch for a species is below the size of first maturity, the stock may be stressed by the fishery.

--- SENTENCE 181 ---
Information for this indicator by species is available only from Hawaii.

--- SENTENCE 182 ---
Spawning Potential Ratio

The spawning potential ratio (SPR) is the ratio of the spawning stock biomass per recruit, at the current level of fishing, to the spawning stock biomass per recruit that would occur in the absence of fishing.

--- SENTENCE 183 ---
According to the overfishing definition contained in the Bottomfish FMP (Amendment 3, 1990), if SPR is less than or equal to 0.20, recruitment overfishing has occurred (i.e., spawners have been reduced to 20%, or less, of their unexploited stock level).

--- SENTENCE 184 ---
Data to calculate SPR were not available from Guam or the Northern Marianas.

--- SENTENCE 185 ---
An estimate of the "worst case" SPR was calculated for American Samoa's bottomfish complex using Dory Project data to estimate the virgin population CPUE and information on percent of immature fish from Hawaii.

--- SENTENCE 186 ---
In Hawaii, SPR was calculated for five major species in the Hoomalu and Mau Zones, of the NWHI, and the MHI; some SPR values changed slightly from previous year's reports due to improvement in the calculations.

--- SENTENCE 187 ---
SPR for armorhead was calculated annually since 1985, except for 1992 and 1994-2003.

#### Introduction

Bottomfishing utilizing traditional canoes by the indigenous residents of American Samoa has been a subsistence practice since the Samoans settled into the Tutuila, Man'ua and Aunu'u islands.

--- SENTENCE 475 ---
It was not until the early 1970's that the bottomfish fishery developed into a commercial scheme utilizing motorized boats.

--- SENTENCE 476 ---
A government subsidized program, called the Dory Project, was initiated in 1972 to develop the offshore fisheries into a commercial venture, and resulted in an abrupt increase in the fishing fleet and total landings.

--- SENTENCE 477 ---
In 1982, a fisheries development project aimed at exporting high-priced deepwater snappers to Hawaii caused another notable increase in bottomfish landings and revenues.

--- SENTENCE 478 ---
Between 1982 and 1988, the bottomfish fishery comprised as much as 50% (by weight) of the total commercial landings.

--- SENTENCE 479 ---
Beginning in 1988, the nature of American Samoa's fisheries changed dramatically with a shift in importance from bottomfish fishing towards trolling.

--- SENTENCE 480 ---
In the past eight years, the dominant (by weight of fish landed) fishing method has been longlining.

--- SENTENCE 481 ---
During the early 1980's, fisheries data was collected from the bottomfish fishery by interviewing only commercial vessels.

--- SENTENCE 482 ---
In the current Offshore Creel Survey on Tutuila that started on October 1, 1985, commercial, subsistence and recreational domestic fishing boats landing catch in five designated areas were interviewed and their catch recorded.

--- SENTENCE 483 ---
For two weekdays and one weekend/holiday per week, DMWR technicians normally sampled offshore trips between 0500 and 2100 hours.

--- SENTENCE 484 ---
In the past three years, the sampling period was increased to cover boats that come in earlier or after the normal sampling period.

--- SENTENCE 485 ---
Two DMWR samplers based on Tau and Ofu collect fisheries data from the Manu'a islands fleet.

--- SENTENCE 486 ---
Boat-based fishing in American Samoa used to be mainly trolling and/or bottomfish.

--- SENTENCE 487 ---
In the past six years, record longline landings were recorded with revenues around the one million-dollar mark.

--- SENTENCE 488 ---
Bigger foreign boats are entering the local fisheries but these are rigged for longlining and more of these are expected to enter the territory's longline fishery.

--- SENTENCE 489 ---
Limited entry options have been initiated to check this increase.

--- SENTENCE 490 ---
The bottomfish fishery of American Samoa was typically commercial overnight bottomfish handlining using skipjack as bait, on 28-30 foot aluminum/plywood Alias.

--- SENTENCE 491 ---
Lower quality bottomfish imported from western Samoa helps satisfy the demand for bottomfish but at the same time result in unattractive prices for local bottomfish fishermen.

--- SENTENCE 492 ---
The adverse effects of three hurricanes that struck American Samoa in 1987, 1990 and 1991 can be seen in some of the trends in the fishery as depicted by the data in this report.

--- SENTENCE 493 ---
Recent changes in the fishery and improvements in the Offshore Creel Survey necessitates modifications to algorithms used to process the data for this report.

--- SENTENCE 494 ---
Hence the continuous improvements to DMWR's data processing systems by WPacFIN staff.

# Appendix 5

# STATUS OF BOTTOMFISH STOCKS 2003 APPLICATION OF CONTROL RULES

Reference values for biomass and fishing mortality are needed for application of the control rules.

--- SENTENCE 761 ---
Since estimates of biomass and fishing mortality are not available for any of the areas involved, proxies of CPUE and effort at msy, respectively, are used to establish reference values.

--- SENTENCE 762 ---
The current values for CPUE and effort are compared to the reference values and their ratio determines the current status of the fishery relative to control rule thresholds.

--- SENTENCE 763 ---
The best available reference value estimates are used for each region.

--- SENTENCE 764 ---
It should be noted that refinement of reference value estimates and standardization of catch and effort data for all fleets are an ongoing activities and those applied here are apt to change as better data is available.

--- SENTENCE 765 ---
For the outer island areas of Guam, CNMI, and American Samoa estimates of MSY, shown in Table 1, are very crude and are based solely on the catchability estimate, q, obtained from a research depletion study conducted at Pathfinder Reef in the CNMI and the length of the 100 fathom contour in these island areas.

--- SENTENCE 766 ---
These estimates refer to the deepslope resources only and do not include shallow complex resources, emperors and small snappers.

--- SENTENCE 767 ---
Reference values for biomass and fishing mortality in terms of current regional commercial fishing activity, CPUE and effort, have not been established.

--- SENTENCE 768 ---
In cases like this the control rules allow estimation of reference values based solely on a time series of CPUE and effort values obtained from the commercial fleet.

--- SENTENCE 769 ---
Table 2 presents Guam CPUE and effort data as presented in the 2003 Guam module.

--- SENTENCE 770 ---
CPUE at MSY is calculated as 50% of the maximum 5 year average and effort at MSY is calculated as the average effort over the period prior to CPUE dropping below the CPUEmsy estimate, 1980-1994 in this case.

--- SENTENCE 771 ---
These values are used as the biomass and fishing mortality reference values found in Table 1.

--- SENTENCE 772 ---
Table 3 lists similar CPUE and effort data as presented in the 2003 modules for American Samoa and the CNMI.

--- SENTENCE 773 ---
CPUE at MSY is estimated using a 5-year running average as described above.

--- SENTENCE 774 ---
Effort at MSY, however, cannot be estimated in the same manner because CPUE never drops below CPUEmsy and a simple long-term average would not be an appropriate limit in developing fishery situation as found in these areas.

--- SENTENCE 775 ---
The estimate of effort at MSY presented in Table 1 for each of these areas is calculated from the crude deepslope MSY value listed and the CPUEmsy value obtained above for each area.

--- SENTENCE 776 ---
These should be a conservative estimates since it is based solely on the deeper species complex.

--- SENTENCE 777 ---
For Hawaii a longer time series of more complete data have allowed the application of a simple dynamic surplus production model.

--- SENTENCE 778 ---
A three parameter model was fit to the NWHI daily CPUE and the MHI per trip CPUE time series with parameters of intrinsic rate of increase, r; Mau zone carrying capacity, k; and MHI catchability, q. NWHI zone q values used in the model were based on standardized estimates obtained from a research depletion study carried out in the CNMI.

--- SENTENCE 779 ---
A four-step pattern of MHI q was used to simulate changes in catchability expected from changes in technology and experience of MHI fishermen.

--- SENTENCE 780 ---
Carrying capacity values for the Hoomalu zone and MHI were based on the Mau zone k adjusted by relative length of 100 fathom contour for the zones.

--- SENTENCE 781 ---
The reference values obtained for each zone are presented in Table 1.

--- SENTENCE 782 ---
The control rule uses the reference values to establish thresholds.

--- SENTENCE 783 ---
The current status of the fishery is determined by the ratio of current values of CPUE and effort compared to the reference values.

--- SENTENCE 784 ---
The maximum fishing mortality threshold (mfmt) is set at the effort at MSY, such that overfishing is occurring when the current effort ratio is greater than 1.00.

--- SENTENCE 785 ---
The biomass threshold, MSST, is defined as 1.0 minus natural mortality.

--- SENTENCE 786 ---
Natural mortality for species of the bottomfish complex is poorly estimated.

--- SENTENCE 787 ---
Various sources report natural mortality estimates ranging from 0.30 to 0.90.

--- SENTENCE 788 ---
We have selected the precautionary value of 0.30 for the purpose of establishing the MSST.

--- SENTENCE 789 ---
The resulting MSST is 0.70.

--- SENTENCE 790 ---
The current status of the stocks for the various island areas are presented in Table 4 and Figure 1.

--- SENTENCE 791 ---
In 1998 the State of Hawaii established bottomfish restricted fishing areas encompassing 20% of the 100 fathom contour in the MHI.

--- SENTENCE 792 ---
Commercial CPUE and effort data does not reflect any benefit in terms of increased biomass or decreased fishing mortality obtained from these closures.

--- SENTENCE 793 ---
In Table 4 the MHI and Archipelago-wide biomass and fishing mortality ratios include potential benefits due to closure ranging from 0-20%, giving an upper and lower estimate of current condition.

--- SENTENCE 794 ---
As can be seen in Table 4, American Samoan and CNMI fisheries are undeveloped with healthy stocks and minimal fishing effort (mortality).

--- SENTENCE 795 ---
In Guam the stocks are greater than needed to obtain MSY, but effort is high indicating that overfishing is occurring.

--- SENTENCE 796 ---
In Hawaii stocks are below that necessary for MSY and only slightly above the msst of 70% CPUEmsy and effort continues to be above the mfmt threshold indicating overfishing.

--- SENTENCE 797 ---
The MHI is the zone that contributes most of the problems in terms of both reduced biomass and overfishing.

--- SENTENCE 798 ---
The Mau zone has recently shown a high level of fishing effort, as well.

--- SENTENCE 799 ---
Reference values for the Hancock Seamount armorhead fishery are obtained from a time series of Japanese trawl data covering the entire range of the northern Pacific stock.

--- SENTENCE 800 ---
CPUEmsy is calculated from this time series as 50% of the highest 5 year running average of CPUE (in terms of mt per trawl hr).

--- SENTENCE 801 ---
Effort is calculated as the mean of effort values prior to a drop in CPUE.

--- SENTENCE 802 ---
MSY for the US EEZ is calculated as 9% of the total MSY for the stock, because about 9% of the fishing grounds and historical catch is from the US seamounts.

--- SENTENCE 803 ---
There is a moratorium in effect since 1986 for trawling within the US EEZ.

--- SENTENCE 804 ---
Trawl catches obtained at Colahan Seamount (just outside of the US EEZ) are used as a proxy for conditions at the unfished Hancock Seamounts.

--- SENTENCE 805 ---
The latest estimates of CPUE for armorhead, the 2002 value, remain well below the

7.5 mt/hr level expected at MSST indicating that this stock remains overfished.
