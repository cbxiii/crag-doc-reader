--- SENTENCE 3 ---
## A. SEA TURTLE STATUS

1.

--- SENTENCE 4 ---
Two turtle species are found in American Samoa, the green turtle (<a href="Chelonia mydas">(Chelonia mydas</a>) and the hawksbill (<a href="Eretmochelys imbricata">Eretmochelys imbricata</a>).

--- SENTENCE 5 ---
Their population numbers in American Samoa are not well known, but available evidence suggests that turtle numbers have declined significantly from historic levels.

--- SENTENCE 6 ---
To evaluate the current status of turtles, residents in 58 coastal villages were interviewed, public sightings were monitored, and surveys on remote islands were conducted.

--- SENTENCE 7 ---
We estimate that turtles in the whole Territory now total only about 120 nesting females (species combined) per year (Tuato'o-Bartley et al. 1993).

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
Most hawksbills nest on Tutuila Island, while most green turtles nest on the remote Rose Atoll.

--- SENTENCE 9 ---
Basic biological information (e.g., nesting and feeding areas, nesting seasons, population size) is not well known, but migration patterns are particularly interesting.

--- SENTENCE 10 ---
Two tagged green turtles from American Samoa were recovered in Fiji.

--- SENTENCE 11 ---
These data are in agreement with the more extensive tagging results obtained from French Polynesia, which indicate that turtle populations in American Samoa should be viewed in a broad regional context (Fig. 1).

--- SENTENCE 12 ---
Major threats to the recovery of turtles in American Samoa are human consumption (most turtles and eggs encountered by villagers are still harvested) and incremental habitat loss due to the activities of a rapidly expanding human population (Fig. 2).

--- SENTENCE 13 ---
It is also apparent, given the large-scale migrations indicated in Figure 1, that Samoan turtles are impacted by subsistence harvests and incidental catches in commercial fisheries elsewhere in the South Pacific.

- 2.

--- SENTENCE 17 ---
Both turtle species are listed and protected by the U.S. Government under the Endangered Species Act; thus it is illegal to possess or harvest either species in American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 18 ---
However, most people are unaware of these regulations and enforcement of existing laws is generally lacking.

--- SENTENCE 19 ---
- 3. Education Activities.

--- SENTENCE 20 ---
There is a lack of public awareness programs to help teach the public about turtles and how they might be protected in American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 21 ---
The concept of turtle conservation faces several difficulties in the Territory.

--- SENTENCE 22 ---
Although many people we interviewed acknowledged that there were considerably fewer turtles on their beaches than when they were children, few indicated a concern for the future of turtles.

--- SENTENCE 23 ---
Some expressed the opinion that "turtles were placed in the ocean by God for man to take," and therefore all turtles should be taken.

--- SENTENCE 24 ---
Others expressed the thought that if they did not take the turtle, someone else would.

--- SENTENCE 25 ---
It is clear that a concerted campaign in American Samoa is needed to educate the public about the need to conserve sea turtles and enforce existing regulations protecting turtles and their habitats.
- 4.

--- SENTENCE 26 ---
(a) A "Recovery Plan" for Pacific turtles in US-affiliated islands is being prepared jointly by NMFS and USFWS.

--- SENTENCE 27 ---
(b) A recent project to eradicate rats at Rose Atoll should improve turtle nesting success there, because the rats had been observed eating turtle eggs and hatchlings.

## B. MARINE MAMMAL STATUS

1.

--- SENTENCE 29 ---
Little is known about any of the marine mammals in American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 30 ---
Small numbers of humpback whales (Megaptera novaeangliae) migrate into our waters to breed and calve, primarily from August through October.

--- SENTENCE 31 ---
These whales presumably belong to one part of the Antarctic Group-5 stock which does not appear to be recovering from previous whaling exploitation.

--- SENTENCE 32 ---
Carcasses of about 8 whales recently washed up on our northern shores (around January 1993).

--- SENTENCE 33 ---
They were tentatively identified as juvenile false killer whales and one Cuvier goosebeaked whale.

--- SENTENCE 34 ---
Both the Marine Mammal Protection Act and Endangered Species Act apply to American Samoa; thus it is illegal to take or possess any whale, dolphin or porpoise, or their products.

--- SENTENCE 35 ---
An annual "Whale Watch" program was conducted whereby the public was asked to report any whale sightings to the Dept. of Marine and Wildlife Resources.
- 4.

--- SENTENCE 36 ---
Document sightings, continue "Whale Watch" publicity.
