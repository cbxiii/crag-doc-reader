# **Summary of Project Results and Findings to Date**

The `mild method' of inducing giant clams to spawn in the hatchery has been very successful.

--- SENTENCE 33 ---
The method basically involves placing the breeders dry in the sun for 2 hours from 11 am to 1 pm, and the introduction of gonad solution when breeders open up after placing them in the spawning tank.

--- SENTENCE 34 ---
Gonads of any giant clam species can be used for the gonad solution.

--- SENTENCE 35 ---
The use of chemical stimuli to induce spawning, e.g. intragonadal injection of serotonin, is not necessary.

--- SENTENCE 36 ---
Spawning was found to be possible almost all year round in American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 37 ---
The `intensive' culture of larvae, `indoors', for the first six days is necessary in order to avoid algae blooms.

--- SENTENCE 38 ---
Algae blooms that occur in the stagnant culture water when the `extensive' method of culturing is employed lead to high larval mortalities.

--- SENTENCE 39 ---
Addition of streptomycin to the culture water, at 1 g per 100 litres, prior to the introduction of fertilised eggs to the Larval Tanks on Day 0, improves water quality for the larvae.

--- SENTENCE 40 ---
Complete culture water change every other day while larvae are in the Larval Tanks during the first 6 days, is necessary.

--- SENTENCE 41 ---
It would be necessary to experiment whether addition of streptomycin is required for every change of culture water.

--- SENTENCE 42 ---
Introduction of zooxanthellae, extracted from a freshly sacrificed mature clam of the same species, to the culture water with larvae inside, on Day 5, worked well.

--- SENTENCE 43 ---
In a few cases, it was necessary to add more zooxanthellae and retain larvae in Larval Tanks for an extra day after Day 6 before transferring to the outdoors Settling Tanks.

--- SENTENCE 44 ---
Provision of shade, through the use of a 50% shade-cloth, over the Settling Tanks when larvae are transferred outdoors, helps in controlling algal growth in these tanks, leading to an improved survival rate to the juvenile stage.

--- SENTENCE 45 ---
Addition of fresh 1-micron filtered sea-water, allowing overflowing, in Settling Tanks, was necessary on Day 10.

--- SENTENCE 46 ---
However, the overflow was directed onto a 100-micron NITEX net placed under the over-flow pipe outside, to retain larvae that flow out.

--- SENTENCE 47 ---
This was found to be necessary until after Day 17, for most batches.

--- SENTENCE 48 ---
Removal of algae, by a slow fanning motion of the hands, from the Settling Tanks floor, was necessary starting around Day 55.

--- SENTENCE 49 ---
Juvenile clams become visible at around Day 48 and can be harvested from the Settling Tanks at age 5 months.

--- SENTENCE 50 ---
No significant mortalities were experienced in Raceways, between harvesting from Settling Tanks and transfer to the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 51 ---
Tank space at the hatchery can accommodate the production of 100,000 juvenile clams a year.

--- SENTENCE 52 ---
Site comparison growth trials, using *Tridacna derasa* from the same batch, in three different sites, Nuuuli, Alofau and Ofu indicate excellent and comparable growth rates in all sites for the first 18 months after planting.

--- SENTENCE 53 ---
The clams planted in Nuuuli showed slightly faster growth.

--- SENTENCE 54 ---
However, clam mortality was very high at Ofu (61%), medium for Alofau (35%) and low at Nuuuli (3 & 13%).

--- SENTENCE 55 ---
Most of the mortality recorded from Ofu was due to Hurricane Val and wave action.

--- SENTENCE 56 ---
The highest number of predator snails, *Cymatium muricinum* was recorded from Nuuuli with only three in Ofu for the whole 18-month period.

--- SENTENCE 57 ---
Species comparison experiments using *T. derasa* and *Hippopus hippopus* on different sites indicate faster growth of *T. derasa* than *H. hippopus* in all three sites.

--- SENTENCE 58 ---
Mortalities recorded for the different species within each particular site were not significantly different.

--- SENTENCE 59 ---
However, comparison of sites combined mortalities, showed that Alofau had the highest mortalities (87% & 89%), followed by Ofu with 60% & 50%, and Nuuuli had the least with 10% and 8% for *T. deresa* and *H. hippopus* respectively.

--- SENTENCE 60 ---
The experiment set at Nuuuli showed that a lot more (93%)

--- SENTENCE 61 ---
*Cymatium* snails were found on *T. derasa* than on *H. hippopus* eventhough both species were mixed within all the trays.

--- SENTENCE 62 ---
The species yield comparison study, involving *T. derasa* and *T. gigas* culture in Nuuuli, has shown good growth rates for both species.

--- SENTENCE 63 ---
However, for the first 18 months, *T. derasa* has shown faster growth than *T. gigas*.

--- SENTENCE 64 ---
In addition, *T. derasa* has shown a much more uniform growth whereas *T. gigas* clam length range increases with time.

--- SENTENCE 65 ---
A significant difference in mortality was recorded for both species during the same period.

--- SENTENCE 66 ---
*T. derasa* had a total mortality of 110 (6%) while *T. gigas* had 333 (17%).

--- SENTENCE 67 ---
Of the 1614 *Cymatium* collected from this experiment, 58% was from *T. gigas*.

--- SENTENCE 68 ---
Additionally, a higher mortality, caused by *Cymatium*, on *T. gigas* (45) was recorded as compared to 19 on *T. derasa*.

--- SENTENCE 69 ---
A plot of monthly combined mortality against total number of snails collected from these clams shows a close correlation between the two parameters in that they follow the same pattern However, mortality peaks decrease progressively towards December 1992 although they occur at the same time as those for *Cymatium* infestations.

--- SENTENCE 70 ---
This is an indication of the success of snail patrols in removing snails leading to lower mortalities.

--- SENTENCE 71 ---
The corresponding peaks of both at about the same time could indicate that a major portion of mortality recorded as Unknown and Unrecorded could be due to *Cymatium*.

--- SENTENCE 72 ---
No significant mortalities were recorded for the experimental use of racks for `off-the-bottom' clam culture, to minimize *Cymatium* infestations, as apposed to those directly placed on the bottom.

--- SENTENCE 73 ---
However, total number of *Cymatium* collected from `On-the-Bottom' was higher (50) than from `Off-the-Bottom' (30), and that `On-the-Bottom' clams were affected by snails 3 months earlier.

--- SENTENCE 74 ---
*Cymatium* infestations at Nuuuli has peaks around August 1991, December 1991-January 1992 and July-August 1992.

--- SENTENCE 75 ---
A plot of total rainfall (\*10) for the Territory against total number of *Cymatium* less than 20 mm from Nuuuli, from June 1991 to May 1992, seems to indicate that snail infestation occur 1-2 months after rainfall peaks.

--- SENTENCE 76 ---
Manual removal of the predator snail, *Cymatium*, during snail patrols have been very effective, especially in Nuuuli.

--- SENTENCE 77 ---
This has resulted in minimum mortalities from the snail and thus forms an integral part to successful lagoon farming.

--- SENTENCE 78 ---
The type of area as that used for the Nuuuli nursery seems to be the most suitable kind for giant clam lagoon culture in American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 79 ---
It is mostly enclosed, relatively deep, free from significant fresh-water influxes and quite far from the barrier reef, thus it experiences minimum wave action and swift currents (also refer to recommendations).

# **1.1 Aquaculture Surveys and Studies**

The Territory of American Samoa was included in a survey conducted in 1972 by an FAO consultant looking at the potential for shellfish aquaculture in certain Pacific Islands.

--- SENTENCE 81 ---
A bait-fish project, involving the culture of the Mexican molly, *Poecilia mexicana*, for the pole-and-line tuna fishery, was initiated in early 1970s.

--- SENTENCE 82 ---
At the request of the Office of Marine Resources, American Samoa Government, an evaluation study was conducted in 1977 by the Oceanic Institute and Hawaii of Marine Biology, Hawaii, to assess aquaculture potential of the major inhabited islands in American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 83 ---
In 1985 meetings were held between the American Samoa Department of Marine and Wildlife Resources and the University of Hawaii Sea Grant Extension Service to initiate an aquaculture feasibility survey and extension program in the Territory.

--- SENTENCE 84 ---
This lead to yet another aquaculture site survey in 1986.

--- SENTENCE 85 ---
These surveys identified limited land and water resources for large-scale extensive or semi-extensive fresh-water aquaculture operations and narrow reef flats and pollution for brackish and marine culture.

--- SENTENCE 86 ---
However, smallscale or backyard aquaculture was quoted as having some potential, utilizing `farm parcels located near perennial streams using true eels, tilapia, grass carp, common carp, channel catfish and the Malaysian prawns' (Takata, 1986).

--- SENTENCE 87 ---
For brackish-water and marine culture, species recommended by the reports included mullet, milkfish, mussel, clams, giant clams, marine shrimps and Trochus.

--- SENTENCE 88 ---
Takata (1986) also listed potential sites for aquaculture in the Territory.

--- SENTENCE 89 ---
# **1.2 Species Introductions (other than giant clams)**

Introduction of animals for aquaculture growth trials in American Samoa dates back to the late 50s or early 60s when a species of tilapia was introduced into the Aunuu brackish-water pond from Western Samoa (Van Pel, 1961).

--- SENTENCE 90 ---
No details could be located on this but the species is suspected as *Oreochromis mossambica* (May et al. 1977).

--- SENTENCE 91 ---
Further development of tilapia culture was discontinued.

--- SENTENCE 92 ---
Black tiger prawn, *Penaeus monodon*, post larvae were introduced from Tahiti in the late 70s and were cultured in pens either in the airport pond or Pala Lagoon or both.

--- SENTENCE 93 ---
The preliminary culture results `showed potential promise for future culturing' (Bryan, undated).

--- SENTENCE 94 ---
However, 8,000 post larvae in one of the holding pens disappeared four days after stocking.

--- SENTENCE 95 ---
This was attributed to predation by the crescent perch, *Therapon jarbua*.

--- SENTENCE 96 ---
The bait-fish culture for the skipjack pole-and-line fishery, using *P. mexicana*, was initiated in the early '70s using broodstock imported from Western Samoa (Bryan, undated).

--- SENTENCE 97 ---
This was the only aquaculture project that was pursued on a production level prior to the giant clam project.

--- SENTENCE 98 ---
`A feasibility study conducted in 1983 showed that molly baitfish culture was not economically feasible for American Samoa' (Anonymous, undated) and was closed that year.

--- SENTENCE 99 ---
Grass carp, *Ctenopharyngodon idellus*, was introduced in 1987 and 1988, involving two separate shipments of fingerlings of about 1,000 each, from Hawaii.

--- SENTENCE 100 ---
These were cultured in small tanks but were never released.

--- SENTENCE 101 ---
Some of the fish were donated for the Community College which also raised them in a tank.

--- SENTENCE 102 ---
The fish were not distributed to the private sector as no one was interested (Gaisoa, 1992, personal communication).

--- SENTENCE 103 ---
# **1.3 Culture Trials of Local Species**

Locally caught juveniles of rabbitfish, *Siganus argenteus*, were cultured in pens along with mollies and showed potential promise for culture (Bryan, undated).

--- SENTENCE 104 ---
Additionally, mullet juveniles were also tried using the same method.

--- SENTENCE 105 ---
However, poaching was problematic (Gaisoa, 1993, personal communication).

--- SENTENCE 106 ---
The author was unable to locate results of these small experiments.

# **1.4 Giant Clams**

`In an effort to develop and enhance local reef resources by supplementing the native giant clam populations (family Tridacnidae), yearlings of *T. derasa* were introduced from the Micronesian Mariculture Demonstration Centre (MMDC), Palau, in 1986, to initiate a small scale test project (Itano, et al. 1988).

--- SENTENCE 108 ---
However, surveys conducted by the Department in 1986 indicated that the populations of the native giant clam species were very low and unlikely to have the capacity to re-establish naturally.

--- SENTENCE 109 ---
With the recent and rapid development of giant clam mariculture in the Indo-Pacific Region, the Department of Marine and Wildlife Resources of the Government of American Samoa set the giant clam mariculture project at high priority.

--- SENTENCE 110 ---
Thus, in 1989, the `Commercial Feasibility of Giant Clam Mariculture in American Samoa' project was initiated with funds from the Centre for Tropical and Subtropical Aquaculture (CTSA).

--- SENTENCE 111 ---
Included in the project is the setting up of a hatchery.

--- SENTENCE 112 ---
This project has entered its fourth year (March 1992-February 1993) centring on giant clam species, *T. derasa*.

--- SENTENCE 113 ---
A side project, funded under the Pacific Aquaculture Association (PAA) programs, was started in 1991 looking at *H. hippopus* as an additional/alternative culture species.

--- SENTENCE 114 ---
In the same year, *T. gigas* was introduced into the Territory for comparative experiments with *T. derasa* under the PFDF/MMDC `Regional Yields of Commercial Species Project, Phase I.

With funds from the Pacific Island Network, Sea Grant Extension Service, Centre for Tropical and Subtropical Aquaculture and the American Samoa Government, an Aquaculture Specialist was contracted for two years from July 1990 to July 1992, to manage the project.

# **2.4 Hatchery**

The original design and goal of the hatchery was for the production of limited clam seeds as start-offs for interested farmers and to `serve as a demonstration for those interested in running their own hatchery' (Ponwith 1989).

--- SENTENCE 144 ---
However, extra costs, the unwarranted option of having private and more than one hatchery as well as lack of qualified personnel to run hatcheries, prompted the Department to concentrate on setting up one that would produce enough giant clam yearlings for the Territory.

--- SENTENCE 145 ---
, the hatchery plan called for the setting up of a series of splasher pools, raceways and a larval rearing tank.

--- SENTENCE 146 ---
The use of splasher pools was eliminated as costs and lifespan were not comparable with concrete, in addition to the extra care needed when working with them.

--- SENTENCE 147 ---
# Spawning Tank

One concrete tank was specifically constructed for spawning purposes.

--- SENTENCE 148 ---
This is where the brooders are placed and induced to spawn.

--- SENTENCE 149 ---
The tank measures 20' x 4' x 2' and can accommodate up to 50 *T. derasa* breeders at any one time.

--- SENTENCE 150 ---
# Larval Rearing Tanks

Two 575 gallons round FRP tanks were installed and ready for use at the end of Year 3 of the CTSA funded project.

--- SENTENCE 151 ---
Both tanks are placed indoors and are used for culturing larvae for the first 6-8 days after fertilization.

--- SENTENCE 152 ---
Each tank can accommodate up to 45 million giant clam fertilised eggs at any one time.

--- SENTENCE 153 ---
# Settling Tanks

Two concrete tanks, with a contiguous wall to minimize costs, have been constructed for rearing Day 6-Day 8 larvae to five months old juveniles.

--- SENTENCE 154 ---
Each tank measures 30' x 8' x 2.6' and can accommodate up to 13 million Day 8 larvae.

--- SENTENCE 155 ---
Since each larvae batch takes a 5-month duration in one tank, each can only be used twice a year.

--- SENTENCE 156 ---
Thus only four batches can be attained in one year using these two tanks.

--- SENTENCE 157 ---
However, one of the raceways has been converted into a Settling/Raceway tank, thus increasing the number of possible batch runs to six per year.

--- SENTENCE 158 ---
# Raceways

Six raceways have been constructed with two more to be built this year.

--- SENTENCE 159 ---
All raceways are built in blocks of two, thus two tanks have one contiguous wall in the middle.

--- SENTENCE 160 ---
Each raceway measures 30' x 8' x 2' and can accommodate up to one hundred and thirty two 1020 flats (plastic trays).

--- SENTENCE 161 ---
At a density of 100 clams per flat, each raceway can accommodate 13,200 five-month old clams and 6,600 eight-month old clams at a density of 50 per flat.

--- SENTENCE 162 ---
Thus, the eight raceways can take in 100,000 juvenile clams a year (2 cycles).

--- SENTENCE 163 ---
Aeration

Air for the larval tanks is supplied through 4-inch airstones using two small aquarium pumps, while aeration for concrete tanks and raceways is by a blower.

--- SENTENCE 164 ---
Due to the consistent rough seas and high wave action in the adjacent lagoon area, use of a large electric submersible pump is impossible.

--- SENTENCE 165 ---
The choice was then made for the 3-inch and 4-inch intake Yanmar marine diesel.

--- SENTENCE 166 ---
These have 5 hp generators and maximum pumping capacity of 300 gallons per minute.

--- SENTENCE 167 ---
Spawning Induction Methods

Induction using the mild method has been successfully used at the hatchery.

--- SENTENCE 168 ---
This method involves putting the breeders, dry, in the sun for two hours prior to being moved to the Spawning Tank.

--- SENTENCE 169 ---
When the breeders open up, after placing in the Spawning Tank, about 5 mls of gonad solution is squirted into the incurrent siphon of each clam, using a syringe.

--- SENTENCE 170 ---
Gonad solution using gonads from *T. squamosa* and *T. maxima* has been used to induce *T. derasa* breeders, successfully.

--- SENTENCE 171 ---
# **2.4.3** Larvae and Post-Larvae Rearing Methods

The `extensive' method of rearing larvae to seed clams was used initially.

--- SENTENCE 172 ---
This is the easiest and least expensive method, in which eggs are transferred directly to Settling Tanks after spawning and held there, in stagnant water, for the first 10 days.

--- SENTENCE 173 ---
After Day 10, 1-micron filtered sea-water was added allowing overflowing.

--- SENTENCE 174 ---
However, the over-flow was directed onto a 100-micron Nitex rested in a wash basin.

--- SENTENCE 175 ---
This allows for the retrieval of any larvae that have not settled and come out in the over-flow water.

--- SENTENCE 176 ---
Due to algae blooms that normally start occurring on Day 2 and which intensified in the following days before the initiation of the over-flow at Day 10, the larval culture method used was switched to the `indoors' intensive method.

--- SENTENCE 177 ---
Two FRP 565 gal circular tanks were obtained from USA and are now in use.

--- SENTENCE 178 ---
These tanks are housed within a separate building.

--- SENTENCE 179 ---
When spawning occurs, eggs are counted and transferred into one of these tanks.

--- SENTENCE 180 ---
However, prior to this transfer, the tank is thoroughly cleaned with sponge and liquid ivory soap or clorox and rinsed with fresh-water.

--- SENTENCE 181 ---
One-micron filtered sea-water is added to the tank and filled up to the 1500 l mark.

--- SENTENCE 182 ---
Fifteen grams of streptomycin is then added and aeration provided through two airstones fed by an aquarium pump.

--- SENTENCE 183 ---
Larval tank and culture-water are changed every second day.

--- SENTENCE 184 ---
Changing the culture water is done by draining it onto a Nitex to retain the larvae.

--- SENTENCE 185 ---
Prior to the start of the drain-down process, a piece of PVC pipe (control pipe) is secured perpendicularly onto the end of the drain pipe from the tank bottom using a PVC elbow.

--- SENTENCE 186 ---
This piece should be long enough so that when held upwards and the stand pipe in the culture tank removed, water does not drain out.

--- SENTENCE 187 ---
For sieving the larvae, a plastic mesh laundry basket is lined with the 60 micron mesh Nitex attached to the basket rim by laundry pegs.

--- SENTENCE 188 ---
A 100-micron mesh Nitex is also attached to the basket in the same manner but rests midway between the 60-micron mesh and the rim of a washbasin in which the above nests.

--- SENTENCE 189 ---
The washbasin makes sure there is always water for the larvae on the Nitex.

--- SENTENCE 190 ---
The draining is initiated by removal of the stand pipe in the tank and replacing it with a 6-inch tall one.

--- SENTENCE 191 ---
Then the `control' pipe is lowered towards one side and drained onto the Nitex.

--- SENTENCE 192 ---
The flow rate is controlled by lowering the `control' pipe to the appropriate height.

--- SENTENCE 193 ---
The bottom layer is sieved separately without disturbing the debris layer that sits on the tank floor.

--- SENTENCE 194 ---
If larvae from this layer look healthy when examined under the microscope, they are combined with the rest.

--- SENTENCE 195 ---
After taking samples for estimating the numbers, the larvae are transferred into the other cleaned tank.

--- SENTENCE 196 ---
On Day 5 (after fertilization), zooxanthellae is scraped from the mantle of a freshly sacrificed mature broodstock of the same species as the larvae, using a kitchen knife, and added to the culture water after passing through a 25-micron sieve.

--- SENTENCE 197 ---
Prior to the final drain-down on Day 6, a sample of larvae are checked under the microscope whether they have taken in the zooxanthellae.

--- SENTENCE 198 ---
If they have, the final drain-down is initiated and larvae transferred to the Settling Tank(s).

--- SENTENCE 199 ---
Addition of 1-micron filtered sea-water, allowing over-flow, in the Settling Tanks is started on Day 10.

--- SENTENCE 200 ---
However, the over-flow water is directed onto 100-micron Nitex set under the Settling Tank drain pipe as in normal larvae draining and sieving.

--- SENTENCE 201 ---
A sample of larvae caught on the Nitex is examined under the microscope and if alive and normal, all are returned to the Settling Tank.

--- SENTENCE 202 ---
The use of the Nitex is discontinued when no more larvae come through.

--- SENTENCE 203 ---
Juvenile clams are harvested from Settling Tanks when they are 5 months old.

--- SENTENCE 204 ---
Details of each successful spawning and corresponding batch are recorded on forms appended as Attachment 1(a).

--- SENTENCE 205 ---
Daily observations and records of certain parameters when larvae are cultured in Settling Tanks are entered on forms as that appended as Attachment 1(b).

Juvenile Raceway Culture

Harvesting of 5-month old juvenile clams from Settling Tanks is accomplished by the same method as described by Heslinga et al, 1990.

--- SENTENCE 207 ---
The average size is determined by measuring a sample of 100-200 clams selected at random.

--- SENTENCE 208 ---
The total number of clams is calculated after counting 100 clams into small plastic bags.

--- SENTENCE 209 ---
Clams in one bag would be planted in a flat in the raceways.

--- SENTENCE 210 ---
When clams are 8 months old, they are thinned out at 50 per flat (tray).

--- SENTENCE 211 ---
Sea-water added during the raceway phase is not filtered and that a few surgeon fish are put in to help control algae growth.

# **3.1.2 Larval and Post-larval Rearings**

Application of the `extensive' larvae culture method (direct transfer of eggs from a spawning into Settling Tanks) at the Department hatchery has been proven as unsuitable.

--- SENTENCE 334 ---
This is due to massive and early algal blooms that occur within the first few days after fertilised eggs are introduced into Settling Tanks.

--- SENTENCE 335 ---
Massive algal die-offs form slimy globules throughout the culture water.

--- SENTENCE 336 ---
These entrap and choke swimming larvae and when they settle to the bottom they smother settling larvae.

--- SENTENCE 337 ---
The protocol developed for `indoor intensive' rearing of fertilised eggs to Day 6 larvae, has been successful, although to some degree, not perfected yet.

--- SENTENCE 338 ---
Addition of zooxanthellae on Day 5 postfertilization, while larvae are being cultured in Larval Tanks, has given excellent results.

--- SENTENCE 339 ---
Examination of larvae on Day 6, indicate presence of zooxanthellae in more than 95% of the sample, in most cases.

--- SENTENCE 340 ---
However, when a high percentage do not have zooxanthellae on examination, the larvae were retained in the Larval Tanks for an extra day and more zooxanthellae added.

--- SENTENCE 341 ---
Complete change of the culture water every second day while larvae are in the Larval Tanks has been found to be necessary to avoid bacterial infestation causing high mortality.

--- SENTENCE 342 ---
Most of the losses in larvae batches in Larval Tanks were due to technical errors, which can be attributed as a learning factor.

--- SENTENCE 343 ---
The use of the 50% shade cloth over the Settling Tanks has been effective in not only preventing high temperatures in these tanks but also delaying and minimizing algal blooms.

--- SENTENCE 344 ---
Thus, holding larvae in stagnant water in the Settling Tanks from Day 6 to Day 10 poses no threat from blooms.

--- SENTENCE 345 ---
However, it is necessary to initiate addition of 1-micron filtered sea-water, slowly at first, on Day 10.

--- SENTENCE 346 ---
The use of a 100-micron Nitex to recover larvae that flow out with the over-flow water has been found to be necessary up to Day 17 post-fertilization.

--- SENTENCE 347 ---
With the use of the 50% shade cloth over the Settling Tanks, algal patches `hanging' from Settling Tanks walls start appearing at Day 25 and intensify in days that follow.

--- SENTENCE 348 ---
Removal of these by a fine-mesh scoop net has been effective.

--- SENTENCE 349 ---
It seems that a thin slimy algal layer appears around Day 35 but does not seem to get thicker.

--- SENTENCE 350 ---
Formation of this layer was much sooner without the shade cloth.

--- SENTENCE 351 ---
Baby clams become visible around Day 48 if the tank bottom is examined carefully using a mask and snorkel.

--- SENTENCE 352 ---
Removal of algae from ST floor by medium strong fanning motion of the hands, starting around Day 55, has no ill effect on baby clams.

--- SENTENCE 353 ---
The loose algae is siphoned out at the rear of the tank.

--- SENTENCE 354 ---
Of the total 10 spawnings, 5 were successfully reared to produce 5-months old juvenile clams, with yields from less than a hundred to about 30,000.

--- SENTENCE 355 ---
Two of these batches were greatly affected during Hurricane Val as the seeds were smothered by dirt and leaves blown in the Settling Tanks.

--- SENTENCE 356 ---
The greatest number of juveniles produced was obtained using the latest larval and postlarval rearing protocol described above utilizing the new indoors Larval Tanks, with the 50% shade-cloth over the Settling Tanks.

# **4 RECOMMENDATIONS**

# **4.1 Hatchery**

The use of gonad solution to induce giant clam breeders to spawn is currently in use and is very reliable.

--- SENTENCE 521 ---
Effort should be made to avoid using the Department's limited broodstock for this purpose as gonads of any clam species can be used.

--- SENTENCE 522 ---
The latest protocol of larval and post-larval rearing, developed by previous aquaculture specialist, seems to be working well if followed properly.

--- SENTENCE 523 ---
Provision of shade, using 50% shadecloth, over settling tanks is a necessity, and should be used whenever larvae are transferred outdoors to the settling tanks.

--- SENTENCE 524 ---
Six-month old juvenile clams harvested in January 1993 from the June 1992 spawning, yielded more than 25,000 clams.

--- SENTENCE 525 ---
This is an excellent yield and also the highest from the hatchery so far.

--- SENTENCE 526 ---
However, the protocol needs to be repeated several times in order to assess its reliability.

--- SENTENCE 527 ---
It is also suggested that feeding of larvae while they are in the `indoors' Larval Tanks, be tried.

--- SENTENCE 528 ---
This could mean a training attachment for one week for a project staff member at either ICLARM, Solomon Islands or at Fisheries, Fiji.

--- SENTENCE 529 ---
Only one spawning induction was attempted between July 1992 and December of the same year.

--- SENTENCE 530 ---
In order to achieve maximum juvenile output from the hatchery, and the fact that spawnings have been possible almost all year round, spawning induction should be conducted whenever a Settling Tank is available, if weather permits.

--- SENTENCE 531 ---
On several occasions, broodstock induced during the day spawned after the staff had left at 5 pm.

--- SENTENCE 532 ---
It is suggested that for future spawning induction, observation be continued after 5 pm for possible spawnings, to avoid eggs and developing embryo being left in the spawning tank for the whole night and part of the following morning.

--- SENTENCE 533 ---
The risk of infestation by microorganisms in the spawning tank is very high, leading to the eventual possible total loss later on.

--- SENTENCE 534 ---
It is probably better to handle fertilised eggs than developing embryo.

--- SENTENCE 535 ---
Two water pumps are urgently needed as there is only one currently working.

--- SENTENCE 536 ---
More than 25,000 six-month old juveniles were harvested early January 1993 and if the current working pump breaks down, it could mean a total loss of juveniles.

--- SENTENCE 537 ---
The importance of having a reliable pumping system (including back-up) can never be over-stressed.

--- SENTENCE 538 ---
The remaining juvenile clams (about 1,000 and 21 months old) from the first successful larval rearing presently being kept in raceways at the hatchery should be transplanted to the Nuuuli nursery.

--- SENTENCE 539 ---
It is wasteful and expensive to keep them on land at this age.

--- SENTENCE 540 ---
# **4.2 Lagoon nursery**

Most of the experiments, both at Nuuuli and Ofu, need thinning out as the clams in cages are becoming crowded.

--- SENTENCE 541 ---
Overcrowding would result in stunted growth and misshapen shells.

--- SENTENCE 542 ---
The thinning-out process should be done whenever the need arises.

--- SENTENCE 543 ---
Removal of cage tops has been done and found safe when *T. derasa* are 30 months old.

--- SENTENCE 544 ---
The CTSA funded year 5 project (March 1993-February 1994) proposes the establishment of four new intertidal nurseries.

--- SENTENCE 545 ---
In addition, under the PAA funded year 2 project (July 92-Jul 93), there are also another two intertidal nurseries being proposed.

--- SENTENCE 546 ---
It is suggested that only two sites be developed for these intertidal trials to lessen the workload and make record keeping more manageable.

--- SENTENCE 547 ---
For practical maintenance and proper record keeping and data collection, it is suggested that both sites be established on Tutuila Island in the Nuuuli-Fatumafuti stretch and that none for Ofu.

--- SENTENCE 548 ---
The project staff should proceed now with earmarking these sites.

--- SENTENCE 549 ---
Participation by the `private owners' of the three clam farms established under the project, in maintenance etc, has been very minimal.

--- SENTENCE 550 ---
For example, high clam mortality at Alofau was due to smothering by algae.

--- SENTENCE 551 ---
This indicates the absence of any maintenance on the part of the owner.

--- SENTENCE 552 ---
The other two, at Nuuuli and Ofu, have been fortunate that the Department staff undertakes maintenance there on a regular basis.

--- SENTENCE 553 ---
It is suggested that the private owners be encouraged to increase their participation in the farm management as the Department gradually decrease its role in this area.

--- SENTENCE 554 ---
It is also suggested that no more clams be given to these farms unless a vast improvement is made in maintenance participation.

--- SENTENCE 555 ---
The problem currently faced by the project staff in keeping up with consistent maintenance and data collection schedules for the lagoon nurseries could be an indication of over-load.

--- SENTENCE 556 ---
If this is the case, then it is suggested that no more subtidal nurseries be established, whether it be for private or government.

--- SENTENCE 557 ---
Instead, the Nuuuli nursery should be expanded using the remaining juvenile clams from the first successful batch presently kept in raceways at the hatchery.

--- SENTENCE 558 ---
Mortality and growth data suggest that the best sites for sub-tidal culture for American Samoa are those like the Nuuuli site.

--- SENTENCE 559 ---
It is relatively deep (4-18 ft), situated far from the barrier reefs, and has insignificant fresh-water influx.

--- SENTENCE 560 ---
This would minimize the effects of swift currents and wave action which have caused high mortality in other sites.

--- SENTENCE 561 ---
Manual removal of the predator snail, *Cymatium*, by snail patrols has been very successful, especially in Nuuuli.

--- SENTENCE 562 ---
It has resulted in minimum mortalities from the predator snail and it thus forms an integral part for successful lagoon farming and should be conducted on a regular basis particularly in the infestation periods, July-November and January-March.

--- SENTENCE 563 ---
If the Department desires to establish additional lagoon nurseries, it should look for sites that are similar to Nuuuli.

--- SENTENCE 564 ---
It would probably be worth the effort to make another attempt for the possibility of experimental giant clam culture in the larger Airport Pond.

--- SENTENCE 565 ---
# **4.3 Data Collection and Compilation**

Two current weaknesses in data collection are inconsistencies in following schedules (for census and snail patrols) and recording Mortality Causes.

--- SENTENCE 566 ---
In order for the data to be used comparatively, some consistency in data collection is necessary.

--- SENTENCE 567 ---
Data collectors should make an extra effort to record this while working at a nursery, even if the cause of mortality is unknown.

--- SENTENCE 568 ---
The weekly data-base for each nursery has been modified for easier recording of mortality causes.

--- SENTENCE 569 ---
However, it is suggested that only weekly summaries should be entered into the data-base.

--- SENTENCE 570 ---
A monthly summaries data-base, of almost the same format as the weekly one, has been created for each nursery for easier analysis for reports.

--- SENTENCE 571 ---
Often, the total mortality recorded by snail patrol divers and entered into the weekly data-base does not match with mortality calculated from 3-monthly census.

--- SENTENCE 572 ---
If the count from the census is reliable, then the difference should be noted under a mortality cause column (e.g. Unr for unrecorded) on the census date.

--- SENTENCE 573 ---
This would make part of the analysis easier.

--- SENTENCE 574 ---
Some information has not been filled in the weekly data-base, e.g. Header details.

--- SENTENCE 575 ---
An effort should be made to update and correct all for each nursery.

--- SENTENCE 576 ---

--- SENTENCE 577 ---
The increasing work-load, the biological background requirement involved, as well as the increasing number of different culture trials using different techniques in different environment settings requires the skill of a qualified and experienced specialist.

--- SENTENCE 578 ---
This should be priority for the project.

--- SENTENCE 579 ---
If funding is not available locally for this position, assistance from the Pacific Island Network (PIN), Sea Grant (Honolulu) and Centre for Tropical and Subtropical Aquaculture could be sought.

--- SENTENCE 580 ---
There should be at least three properly qualified and competent SCUBA divers working for the project.

--- SENTENCE 581 ---
Assistance should be available (and sought) from qualified divers working in other sectors of the Department when the need arises.

# **4.8 Native Giant Clam Species**

It would be desirable to conduct spawning and grow-out experiments in an effort to re-establish populations of the native giant clam species, *T. squamosa* and *T. max*ima since surveys conducted by the Department indicated very low populations and the likely inability of the remaining populations to recover naturally.

--- SENTENCE 592 ---
The re-seeding programme could start with stocking marine reserves, e.g. Fagatele Sanctuary.

--- SENTENCE 593 ---
The limited number of suitable subtidal areas in American Samoa for lagoon farming makes it realistic to initiate propagation on the native species for re-seeding purposes.

--- SENTENCE 594 ---
*T. maxima* is abundant on Rose Atoll while *T. squamosa* can be collected from Manu'a or around Tutuila Island.

--- SENTENCE 595 ---
Establishment of broodstock of both species, for spawning purposes, would probably involve a major undertaking by the Department.

--- SENTENCE 596 ---
Since neighbouring countries are interested in re-seeding programmes involving the same species, the American Samoa hatchery can be the source of seeds for countries that do not operate giant clam hatcheries.
