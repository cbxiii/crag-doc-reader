## **Summer Coral Bleaching Event, 2004, on Tutuila, American Samoa**

Report to Dept Marine and Wildlife Resources

By Douglas Fenner July 27, 2004

American Samoa's coral reefs have been impacted by a variety of major events, including periodic severe hurricanes, the most recent of which was Hurricane Heta, which was closest on Jan 6, 2004.

--- SENTENCE 2 ---
Other events have included a Crown-of-Thorns seastar outbreak in 1974 and night scuba spearfishing from 1994-2002.

--- SENTENCE 3 ---
Mass coral bleaching occurred in 1994 (Goreau and Hayes, 1994), and then again in the summers of 2002 (Green, 2002) and 2003 (Craig, personal comm., Mielbrecht, personal comm.), and the bleaching was severe enough to kill some corals.

--- SENTENCE 4 ---
A striking feature of the lagoon pools of Tutuila is the dominance of the coral populations by two groups (finger corals, *Porites cylindrica*, and staghorns (*Acropora*), 3 species).

--- SENTENCE 5 ---
A further striking feature is that a large proportion of the staghorn corals are dead, but virtually nothing else is dead.

--- SENTENCE 6 ---
The dead staghorn coral thickets are mostly still standing, but support a heavy growth of algae, primarily filamentous algae and coralline algae.

--- SENTENCE 7 ---
This indicates that the staghorns did not die within the last few months, but probably not more than a few years ago (as they would have collapsed).

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
Thus it appeared possible that the staghorns might have been killed by mass bleaching in 2002 and/or 2003.

--- SENTENCE 9 ---
The fact that only staghorns were dead suggested that the cause of death was species-specific.

--- SENTENCE 10 ---
Previous reports from elsewhere in the world indicate that corals in the genus *Acropora* are often more sensitive to bleaching than other corals.

--- SENTENCE 11 ---
Craig et al (2001) have reported that lagoon pools in Ofu reach high temperatures for short periods of time on sunny days at low tide when waves cannot make it over the crest to pump water through the lagoon pools.

--- SENTENCE 12 ---
All of these things together suggested that the staghorns in Tutuila lagoon pools may have been killed by previous mass bleaching events, and may be more sensitive to high temperatures (the primary factor causing mass coral bleaching).

--- SENTENCE 13 ---
In early December, 2003, not long after my arrival, I made my first observations of the staghorn corals in the Airport lagoon pool.

--- SENTENCE 14 ---
Essentially no staghorn colonies were bleached.

--- SENTENCE 15 ---
On the 24th of January, I began regular, bi-weekly observations of the staghorns in the Airport lagoon, thinking that the staghorns in this pool might bleach as water heated up in the summer.

--- SENTENCE 16 ---
The percentage of bleached colonies was estimated following a roughly one-hour swim in the lagoon following roughly the same path.

--- SENTENCE 17 ---
On Feb 5th, about 5% of colonies were estimated to be bleached, but on other dates through Feb 28 1% or less of colonies were estimated to be bleached (Figure 1).

--- SENTENCE 18 ---
Then suddenly on March 21, 33% of colonies appeared bleached.

--- SENTENCE 19 ---
All bleached colonies were only partially bleached, with bleaching restricted to upper surfaces, primarily on horizontal branches.

--- SENTENCE 20 ---
There was no difference in the prevalence or intensity of bleaching with depthstaghorns at 20 foot depth were as bleached as at 1 foot depth.

--- SENTENCE 21 ---
However, corals nearer to the crest and reef flat had a higher percentage of bleached colonies than closer to shore (the airport runway).

--- SENTENCE 22 ---
In subsequent observations, the percentage bleached reached a maximum of about 40%, then decreased slowly as the staghorn corals recovered their

zooxanthellae (algae) and color.

--- SENTENCE 23 ---
The course of recovery appears to be roughly linear, instead of being an exponential decay.

--- SENTENCE 24 ---
The time course of recovery took much longer than the time for the bleaching to appear.

--- SENTENCE 25 ---
All bleached corals recovered this year, none died, unlike in the previous two summers.

--- SENTENCE 27 ---
During the bleaching, I also looked at corals in several other lagoons and a few reef slope spots.

--- SENTENCE 28 ---
A total of 5 areas had significant proportions of dead staghorn, Airport, Nuuuli, Alofau, Faga'alu and Utelei.

--- SENTENCE 29 ---
Three of these had significant amounts of bleached staghorns: Airport, Nuuuli, and Alofau.

--- SENTENCE 30 ---
Faga'alu and Utelei had essentially no bleached staghorns.

--- SENTENCE 31 ---
Reef slope sites, such as at Fagatelle Bay, Auto and Alofau, had essentially no bleaching.

--- SENTENCE 32 ---
Among the reef slope sites, staghorns were found only at Fagatelle Bay.

--- SENTENCE 33 ---
There were small numbers of bleached corals scattered among several species at some sites.

--- SENTENCE 34 ---
But it was clear that significant bleaching occurred only among staghorn corals in three lagoons this year.

--- SENTENCE 35 ---
The three lagoons with significant staghorn bleaching are all lagoons that are relatively enclosed, while the two with no bleaching are relatively open.

--- SENTENCE 36 ---
It may be that the water got warmer in the more closed lagoons, causing more bleaching.

--- SENTENCE 37 ---
Even the most closed lagoons in Tutuila are not as closed as those on Ofu.

--- SENTENCE 38 ---
Even during low tide, water from breaking waves comes over the crest and flushes these lagoons.

--- SENTENCE 39 ---
In the lagoon pools of Ofu, there is very little of the 3 staghorn species that bleached on Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 40 ---
Further, I did not see beds of dead staghorn in the Ofu lagoons.

--- SENTENCE 41 ---
There are, however, thickets of the firecoral *Millepora dichotoma* in Ofu lagoon which are almost entirely dead (over 99%).

--- SENTENCE 42 ---
I was able to find only one small branch of this species that was still alive.

--- SENTENCE 43 ---
In the same lagoon, another species of fire coral (probably Millepora

exaesa) is entirely alive.

--- SENTENCE 44 ---
Fire corals have been reported in previous studies to be among the corals most vulnerable to bleaching.

--- SENTENCE 45 ---
The striking contrast between these two species in mortality suggests that they may have large differences in their ability to tolerate bleaching.

--- SENTENCE 47 ---
My original idea was that the staghorn corals in the lagoons could serve as an "Early Warning System" or "canary in the coal mine" for bleaching events.

--- SENTENCE 48 ---
The lagoons are much easier to access on a weekly basis that most reef slopes.

--- SENTENCE 49 ---
Also, the evidence from the dead staghorns suggested that they might be more sensitive to bleaching, and thus bleach first and provide a warning that a mass bleaching event might be starting.

--- SENTENCE 50 ---
This warning could be used as a signal to search other lagoons and the reef slopes to see if a mass bleaching event was starting.

--- SENTENCE 51 ---
The events of this year did demonstrate that the prediction that these lagoon staghorns are more sensitive to bleaching than other corals, and corals at other locations.

--- SENTENCE 52 ---
However, since other corals did not bleach this year, it is not yet clear whether the lagoon staghorns will bleach before other corals, or if they do, by how much earlier (days?

--- SENTENCE 53 ---
The first recorded mass coral bleaching in American Samoa occurred in 1994, and this has been followed with bleaching in the summers of 2002, 2003, and 2004.

--- SENTENCE 54 ---
In 2003, about 20-30% of corals on reef slopes bleached, and some corals died, though we don't have quantitative information on coral deaths.

--- SENTENCE 55 ---
The 2002 bleaching was also reported to cause some coral death.

--- SENTENCE 56 ---
In 2004, however, bleaching was restricted to staghorns in 3 lagoons, and all colonies were only partly bleached and none died.

--- SENTENCE 57 ---
A review of the Sea

Surface Temperatures (SST's) for American Samoa reveal that SST's were rising when Hurricane Heta approached the islands in early January.

--- SENTENCE 58 ---
Heta caused a sudden drop in SST's to winter levels, probably caused by wave mixing of warm surface water with deeper cool water.

--- SENTENCE 59 ---
This was followed by a rising temperature curve very similar to that before the hurricane.

--- SENTENCE 60 ---
It is possible that this resetting of the heating process kept the peak temperatures below what they would have been without Heta.

--- SENTENCE 61 ---
If so, this summer's bleaching may have been lower than it would have been otherwise.

--- SENTENCE 62 ---
Three summers in a row with coral bleaching is a clear change from previous years, with only one bleaching event, eight years earlier.

--- SENTENCE 63 ---
It may be that bleaching is now the normal summer pattern for American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 64 ---
Some studies have projected that bleaching every summer might be common around the world by perhaps 2020.

--- SENTENCE 65 ---
It may be that this pattern has already arrived in American Samoa, and American Samoa is now experiencing what the rest of the world may experience in another 15 years or so.

--- SENTENCE 66 ---
This presents an opportunity for scientists to study the effects of annual summer bleaching long before it arrives elsewhere.

--- SENTENCE 67 ---
It also affords an opportunity for scientists to plan studies for a reliable, predictable annual bleaching event.

--- SENTENCE 68 ---
So far, bleaching outside the laboratory has been predictable only a few weeks in advance (by using satellite recorded SST's to look for hotspot anomalies).

--- SENTENCE 69 ---
However, it may be wise to be cautious.

--- SENTENCE 70 ---
Three summers of bleaching in a row don't prove that every summer in the future will have bleaching.

--- SENTENCE 71 ---
It could be that there has been an unusual period for the last three years, and the pattern will change and bleaching will not be an annual summer event.

--- SENTENCE 72 ---
Weather patterns are notoriously variable.

--- SENTENCE 73 ---
Even if it is an annual summer event, there are very limited infrastructure resources on the island for outside researchers.

--- SENTENCE 74 ---
Currently there is only one small-capacity air compressor, boats are frequently out of operation, and there is no dedicated marine laboratory space.

--- SENTENCE 75 ---
Although there are plans for a marine laboratory, it is likely to be 5 years or more before such a facility may be available, if at all.
