# **Executive Summary**

- Most indices continue to show that the reefs of American Samoa are in relatively good condition.
- Average coral cover on reef slopes is now 31%, higher than the averages for the Pacific, South Pacific, U.S. Pacific, Great Barrier Reef, and Caribbean.

--- SENTENCE 12 ---
The latter two have just 10% and 8% coral cover left.

--- SENTENCE 13 ---
Coral cover is lower than it was here before crown-of-thorns starfish ate almost all the coral in 1978, and lower than it was in the past in the Pacific and Caribbean.
- Average coral cover on both reef slopes and reef flats has increased over the 8 years of this monitoring program, while coral cover has decreased in the Caribbean, Pacific, Indian Ocean, Red Sea, and Great Barrier Reef.
- Coral cover on reef slopes increased on four reefs, was steady on five reefs, and decreased on one reef.

--- SENTENCE 14 ---
- There are very few dead corals, fewer than the averages for the South Pacific, Indonesia, the Philippines, the Indo-Pacific, and the world.

--- SENTENCE 15 ---
- Coralline algae, which is considered good, is plentiful, and macroalgae, which is considered bad, is rare most places.

--- SENTENCE 16 ---
Most of the reef is covered by corals and coralline algae, both of which help build the reef, which is good.

--- SENTENCE 17 ---
- Water on the reef slopes is relatively clear, and has remained so, indicating relatively good water quality.
- Reefs inside the harbor are in poor condition, likely due to sediments, nutrients, and chemical pollution.

--- SENTENCE 18 ---
Coral diversity on slopes inside the harbor is lower than outside, probably due to nutrients and/or pollution.

--- SENTENCE 19 ---
Water quality is particularly low at the head of the harbor, indicated by murky water.

--- SENTENCE 20 ---
- Vatia was badly damaged by the tsunami in the inner bay and by Hurricane Heta in the outer bay.

--- SENTENCE 21 ---
The outer bay is recovering, but the inner bay is not, due to nutrients fueling turf algae.

--- SENTENCE 22 ---
Fagatele Bay was damaged by the tsunami but began recovering immediately.
- A coral disease outbreak followed Hurricane Wilma in Vatia Bay, but ended a few months later.

--- SENTENCE 23 ---
Disease was also found on two groups of *Porites* corals on Ofu.

--- SENTENCE 24 ---
All *Porites rus* corals in front of Vaoto Lodge were infected and partly dead, and a few in Hurricane House pool were infected.

**Abstract**

 In 2011, transect data was collected on the reef slopes and reef sites, and coral biodiversity data was collected on reef slopes and reef flats (for the first time).

--- SENTENCE 27 ---
Both transects and coral biodiversity were also taken from both the reef flat and pools at Ofu as well (for the first time).

--- SENTENCE 28 ---
Bleaching data continued to be taken at both the airport and Alofau pools, year-round.

--- SENTENCE 29 ---
Data was also taken from 81 diseased colonies of *Porites* corals in an Ofu pool.

--- SENTENCE 30 ---
Most indices continue to support the view that the benthic portion of American Samoan coral reefs are in relatively good condition.

--- SENTENCE 31 ---
Mean coral cover was 31% on the reef slope.

--- SENTENCE 32 ---
Coral cover continued to be highly variable from one site to another, but mean coral cover showed much smaller amounts of variation over time.

--- SENTENCE 33 ---
Mean coral cover has increased over the years, and compares favorably to coral cover on the Great Barrier Reef, South Pacific, the whole Pacific, and particularly the Caribbean.

--- SENTENCE 34 ---
However, it is still less than the Caribbean in 1977, Pacific in 1980, American Samoa in 1978, nearpristine reefs around the world, and near-pristine reefs in the U.S. Pacific.

--- SENTENCE 35 ---
The increase in coral cover over time here is in contrast for the means for reefs in the Pacific, Indian Ocean, Red Sea, Caribbean, and global averages, all of which have been decreasing.

--- SENTENCE 36 ---
The "Live Coral Index" which reflects the proportion of corals that are alive, is steady and higher than in the world, the Indo-Pacific, Philippines, Indonesia, and South Pacific.

--- SENTENCE 37 ---
American Samoa has much more crustose calcareous algae plus coral than it has turf plus macroalgae, which is considered good.

--- SENTENCE 38 ---
If the macroalgae is divided into calcifying algae and non-calcifying algae, and all organisms that calcify added up, 70% or more of the substrate is covered by calcifiers, so the reef slopes have good cover of calcifiers.

--- SENTENCE 39 ---
Coral cover increased at four sites, Masacre, Fagasa, Tafeu, and Leone, decreased at Vatia, and was steady at five sites, Aunu'u, Amaua, Faga'alu, Nu'uuli, and Fagatele.

--- SENTENCE 40 ---
The decrease recorded at Vatia was due to the tsunami of Sept. 29, 2009, and/or Hurricane Wilma on Jan. 24, 2011.

--- SENTENCE 41 ---
The tsunami badly damaged the inner half of the bay, and the Hurricane damaged the outer half of the bay.

--- SENTENCE 42 ---
The transects straddle the mid section of the bay.

--- SENTENCE 43 ---
Mean coral cover on slopes inside the harbor was much lower than outside the harbor, which may have been due to pollution in the harbor, since there is a published paper showing lower coral diversity in polluted areas.

--- SENTENCE 44 ---
Coral cover in transects continues to be primarily encrusting, mainly composed of *Montipora grisea*, *Pavona varians, Montipora informis,* and *Pavona chiriquensis*.

--- SENTENCE 45 ---
The second largest type of coral cover is columnar/plate colonies of *Porites rus*., which is actually the single species with the greatest cover.

--- SENTENCE 46 ---
The number of genera showed downward trends but the number of coral species was largely steady.

--- SENTENCE 47 ---
The number of coral species in transects is greater on the South side than the North side, and this has remained so over time.

--- SENTENCE 48 ---
The number of coral species is positively correlated with the amount of coral cover at a site.

--- SENTENCE 49 ---
This is because where there is more coral cover, there are more corals in the transects, and in general as you look at more individual corals you find more and more species because it is a larger sample of the coral population.

--- SENTENCE 50 ---
Coral biodiversity was recorded on roving dives on the slopes, and for the first time several sites in Pago Pago harbor were included.

--- SENTENCE 51 ---
There has been a small decrease in the number of coral species recorded in biodiversity dives since 2005 when monitoring started.

--- SENTENCE 52 ---
Slightly more coral species were recorded on the south side in biodiversity dives than on the north, but in previous years the difference was small or not present.

--- SENTENCE 53 ---
The number and diversity of invertebrates recorded has increased greatly over time, as the author became better and better at spotting small invertebrates of various kinds in the belt transects.

--- SENTENCE 54 ---
It is surely not a real increase in the number of invertebrates.

--- SENTENCE 55 ---
Mean coral cover on outer reef flats was 28.5%, just 2.5% less than on the reef slope.

--- SENTENCE 56 ---
Previous monitoring found it was lower on inner reef flats.

--- SENTENCE 57 ---
Again, variation between sites was large, but variation over time less.

--- SENTENCE 58 ---
Reef flats on the south side had coral cover equal to that on the north, but north reef flats had more turf and south reef flats had more rubble.

--- SENTENCE 59 ---
There was an increasing trend in coral cover on reef flats since 2007.

--- SENTENCE 60 ---
The live coral index was high and stable on reef flats just as it was on the reef slopes.

--- SENTENCE 61 ---
The amount of coral plus crustose coralline algae (CCA; both are considered good) was 50%, and has risen over the years.

--- SENTENCE 62 ---
The amount of turf plus macroalgae (considered bad or mixed/neutral) decreased over the years.

--- SENTENCE 63 ---
The amount of coral plus CCA was higher on the slope than the reef flat, primarily because low tides kill corals on the reef flat, a natural phenomenon.

--- SENTENCE 64 ---
The total cover of calcifying organisms on the reef flat (which includes calcifying macroalgae, *Halimeda*) has increased over the years and the amount of non-calcifying area (everything else) has decreased.

--- SENTENCE 65 ---
There is more calcifying cover on the slope than on the reef flats.

--- SENTENCE 66 ---
Coral cover decreased on three reef flat sites, was steady at four, and increased at three.

--- SENTENCE 67 ---
Vatia reef flat showed a sudden decrease in the time period of the tsunami and Hurricane Wilma.

--- SENTENCE 68 ---
The most common lifeform of corals on the reef flats was encrusting, followed by *Acropora* branching, foliose, branching, and tables.

--- SENTENCE 69 ---
Encrusting corals were the most common lifeform on both the slopes and the reef flats, and *Montipora grisea* was the coral species with the most cover on both slopes and reef flats.

--- SENTENCE 70 ---
The number of genera and species of corals increased on reef flats over time.

--- SENTENCE 71 ---
A total of 19 coral species were recorded only on the reef flat, and 33 species were recorded only on the slope in transects.

--- SENTENCE 72 ---
Another 13 species were more common on reef flats than slopes, and a further 13 were more common on slopes than on reef flats.

--- SENTENCE 73 ---
This supports the view that these two zones are very different places with different coral communities.

--- SENTENCE 74 ---
The number of coral species in biodiversity searches varied greatly from one reef flat site to another.

--- SENTENCE 75 ---
The mean number of corals on reef flats inside the harbor was the slightly more than outside the harbor.

--- SENTENCE 76 ---
There were fewer coral species in biodiversity searches on the reef flats than on the slopes.

--- SENTENCE 77 ---
There were more coral species on reef flats on the north than the south side of the island, while on the slopes there were more on the south than the north.

--- SENTENCE 78 ---
Biodiversity searches on the reef flats found 10 species that were found only on the reef flat and 78 species that were found only on the slope.

--- SENTENCE 79 ---
There were also 10 other species that prefer the reef flat and 27 that prefer the reef slope and eight that prefer pools.

--- SENTENCE 80 ---
Slightly more corals species were found in biodiversity searches on reef flats in the harbor than outside the harbor.

--- SENTENCE 81 ---
More coral species were found outside the harbor on the slope than inside the harbor on the slope which was in turn more than on reef flats inside and outside the harbor.

--- SENTENCE 82 ---
North side reef flats had considerably more coral species than the south side in biodiversity searches.

--- SENTENCE 83 ---
Outer reef flats on Tutuila had more coral cover than on Ofu-Olosega, but inner reef flats on Tutuila had less coral cover than on Ofu-Olosega.

--- SENTENCE 84 ---
In biodiversity searches, there were more coral species in Ofu-Olosega pools than reef flats, more coral species on Ofu-Olosega than on Tutuila, and slightly more coral species on Tutuila reef flats than in pools.

--- SENTENCE 85 ---
Bleaching in the airport pool was less in 2011 than in previous years.

--- SENTENCE 86 ---
At Alofau, bleaching had decreased in 2010, but then increased in 2011 back toward the levels seen

in previous years.

--- SENTENCE 87 ---
A coral disease outbreak in Vatia following Hurricane Wilma killed some corals in the genus *Acropora* and damaged others, but subsided over several months and returned to normal levels.

--- SENTENCE 88 ---
Colonies of *Porites rus* in the pool in front of Vaoto Lodge on Ofu were diseased when observed in 2011, with significant damage from the disease.

--- SENTENCE 90 ---
In sum, most of the indicators of reef health indicate that the reefs of American Samoa are in relatively good condition.

# **Methods**

The 12 reef slope sites are shown in the map below.

--- SENTENCE 92 ---
All are on Tutuila and nearby Aunu'u.

!

--- SENTENCE 93 ---
The benthic methods were the same as in 2010.

--- SENTENCE 94 ---
In the core monitoring, four 50-m tapes were laid on a depth contour between 8 and 10 m deep.

--- SENTENCE 95 ---
A space between them of about 10 m was kept.

--- SENTENCE 96 ---
Benthic categories were recorded under each 0.5 m point on the tape.

--- SENTENCE 97 ---
Benthic categories included live coral, dead coral, dead coral with algae, crustose calcareous algae, branching coralline algae, fleshy macroalgae, turf algae, rock, sand, rubble, soft coral, and sponge.

--- SENTENCE 98 ---
"Branching coralline algae" included a soft feathery species that was the most common in that category.

--- SENTENCE 99 ---
That species is *Cheilosporum spectabile*.

--- SENTENCE 100 ---
Any rock that is not white has turf on it, and was recorded as turf.

--- SENTENCE 101 ---
Corals were identified to lifeform, genus, and species when possible, and if the macroalgae was *Halimeda* or *Dictyota*, or something else that was identifiable, that was recorded in as much detail as possible (usually genus).

--- SENTENCE 102 ---
Soft corals were recorded to genus when possible.

--- SENTENCE 103 ---
Hard coral lifeforms included encrusting, massive, foliose, branching, columnar, submassive, mushroom, *Millepora*, *Acropora* branching, *Acropora* table, *Acropora* digitate, and *Acropora* encrusting.

--- SENTENCE 104 ---
Only the top visible layer was recorded of any multilayer formations such as corals or macroalgae, so all categories of cover add up to 100%.

--- SENTENCE 105 ---
Diurnal, non-cryptic macroinvertebrates were counted in a half-meter wide belt transect beside each 50 m tape.

--- SENTENCE 106 ---
Invertebrates were identified to the most detailed level possible.

--- SENTENCE 107 ---
Spaces between coral branches were not searched.

--- SENTENCE 108 ---
Hard and soft corals were not counted.

--- SENTENCE 109 ---
Horizontal visibility was recorded using the tape.

--- SENTENCE 110 ---
Two transect tapes were recorded on the first dive, and an additional two tapes were done on the second dive.

--- SENTENCE 111 ---
Sites were re-located using the GPS and markers as indicated in the 2005 report.

--- SENTENCE 112 ---
One day was required for each site.

--- SENTENCE 113 ---
In 2008, a total of 12 sites were recorded, including the original 11 plus Masacre Bay.

--- SENTENCE 114 ---
For 2011,10 sites were recorded since the lack of a working boat near the end of the year restricted monitoring fieldwork.

--- SENTENCE 115 ---
Damage to boat ramps were repaired early in the year, facilitating monitoring work.

--- SENTENCE 116 ---
As in 2007-2010, the rugosity measurements were omitted, because a third team member was not available and when included it lengthened dive times to the point where running out of air was a distinct possibility, thus reducing the margin of safety.

--- SENTENCE 117 ---
Further, it appears that the measurement depends primarily on exactly where the chain falls, and that changes in rugosity caused by coral growth will take quite a few years before they would be detectable.

--- SENTENCE 118 ---
A hurricane could make changes in rugosity quickly by removing corals, and if significant hurricane damage occurs, the rugosity measurements can be repeated.

--- SENTENCE 119 ---
Until changes in coral cover or other rugosity changes are apparent, repeating the measurement of rugosity is not worth the increased risk of running out of air.

--- SENTENCE 120 ---
In future years it is hoped that an additional team member can record the rugosity measure, or additional boat dives are available to take the rugosity measure.

--- SENTENCE 121 ---
In the meantime, it will be considered a lower priority item, and will be done on an opportunistic basis.

--- SENTENCE 122 ---
When laying the tape, the primary consideration is to keep the tape between 8 m and 9 m deep.

--- SENTENCE 123 ---
The tape is passed along the sides of projections, including live corals such as *Pocillopora* and table corals, which usually have an overhanging side.

--- SENTENCE 124 ---
If it is passed around first one side of one projection and then the other side of another, it is anchored securely from wave action moving it either way at that point.

--- SENTENCE 125 ---
An attempt is made to anchor the tape in this fashion as often as possible, but in some areas there is little to anchor the tape on.

--- SENTENCE 126 ---
A continuing problem is what to do about clefts in the reef.

--- SENTENCE 127 ---
A cleft that is narrow and deep is crossed straight to an anchoring point on the other side.

--- SENTENCE 128 ---
If it is large, then the tape may be laid along one side of it, going up toward shallower water but staying at 8-9 m depth, and then when the bottom rises to that depth, crossing to the other side and continuing on that side out of the canyon.

--- SENTENCE 129 ---
The principle problem with that is finding an anchoring point near the head of the canyon that can hold the tape at the head.

--- SENTENCE 130 ---
The tape is read at each point by reading the substrate under the point at the time at which the diver is directly above the point.

--- SENTENCE 131 ---
A string and weight are not used, as surge and the movement of the tape in the surge makes that a much more difficult and slow procedure.

--- SENTENCE 132 ---
If the tape is stretched between two points far apart and the surge is heavy, the tape can move a meter or more in either direction with each wave.

--- SENTENCE 133 ---
This opens up an opportunity for bias, as the point on the tape sweeps across a variety of benthic patches.

--- SENTENCE 134 ---
If the point on the bottom is recorded that is first seen from a vertical viewpoint, then bias is minimized.

--- SENTENCE 135 ---
An attempt is made to minimize bias in laying the tape by choosing a route based on depth and anchoring points for the tape, not the substrate.

--- SENTENCE 136 ---
The direct observation underwater of what is under points makes it easier to identify species, and so allows greater taxonomic resolution than video techniques.

--- SENTENCE 137 ---
For coral biodiversity, one hour search dives were conducted at each site.

--- SENTENCE 138 ---
The dive begins at the bottom of the reef (but always well above 30 m deep) and continues as a roving dive as the diver ascends up the slope, searching for as many coral species as can be found.

--- SENTENCE 139 ---
The presence of coral species is recorded underwater, and once out of the water, estimates of abundance of each species are recorded on a 0-5 ("DACOR") scale, with the names "not found," "rare," "uncommon," "common," "abundant," and "dominant."

--- SENTENCE 140 ---
Rare was defined as just 1-2 colonies, and dominant was defined as composing more than half of all corals.

--- SENTENCE 141 ---
The other categories were intermediate values, but not defined as individual corals were not counted, since that would greatly slow the survey and reduce the number of species found.

--- SENTENCE 142 ---
This technique compliments the transect tapes since it covers the entire depth range of the slope, and produces a much larger sample that includes much rarer species than the transect tapes which only produce data on 100 points per tape.

--- SENTENCE 143 ---
So the sample is much larger than the transects, but the quantitative accuracy is much lower.

--- SENTENCE 144 ---
It compliments but does not replace the transects.

--- SENTENCE 145 ---
Sites inside the harbor were added this year in addition to the usual sites outside the harbor.

--- SENTENCE 146 ---
Data collection on reef flats was continued, using transects.

--- SENTENCE 147 ---
In addition, coral diversity data from roving search snorkels on reef flats was carried out for the first time.

--- SENTENCE 148 ---
The methods for both are similar to that on reef slopes.

--- SENTENCE 149 ---
Reef flats are quite different from reef slopes, are a large and important part of the reefs, and are subject to different disturbances than reef slopes, such as low tide events that have no effect on reef slopes.

--- SENTENCE 150 ---
Monitoring reef flats is an important compliment to monitoring reef flats.

## Coral Biodiversity Data

Biodiversity dives are quite different from transect dives.

--- SENTENCE 431 ---
Instead of laying tapes and recording what is under the tape, an hour is spent on a roving dive, moving upward from the bottom of the reef to the top, recording all the coral species that can be found.

--- SENTENCE 432 ---
This technique covers a much larger area than the transects, but because there is no measure of the area covered, and the numbers of colonies of different species are not recorded, quantitative measures of the abundance of the corals cannot be calculated.

--- SENTENCE 433 ---
Estimates of the abundance of each species are recorded after each dive, on a 5 point scale.

--- SENTENCE 434 ---
So this technique is a way to get data on many more, rare, species by covering a larger area, but produces low accuracy data.

# **Reef Flats**

 Reef flats are not often monitored, because most attention is now focused on reef slopes, and reef flats are almost neglected.

--- SENTENCE 478 ---
The first scientists who studied coral reefs had to study reef flats, because that was the only part they could get to, since they didn't have SCUBA gear.

--- SENTENCE 479 ---
But now with the advent of SCUBA gear, attention is usually focused on reef slopes.

--- SENTENCE 480 ---
Reef flats generally have lower coral cover that reef slopes, because exposure to air kills corals on reef flats that grow too high.

--- SENTENCE 481 ---
This is the reason

reef flats are flat, because corals that grow too high are exposed to air too long and die.

--- SENTENCE 482 ---
while corals in deeper spots survive and continue to grow upward, and thus low spots tend to get filled in and high spots cannot grow any more.

--- SENTENCE 483 ---
Low tides do most of the killing of corals, in a process much like moving a lawn, and which is quite natural; it has been going on for millions of years.

--- SENTENCE 484 ---
In periods when there are no unusually low tides, coral cover on reef flats increases (Brown et al. 2011; Scopélitis et al. 2011).

--- SENTENCE 485 ---
Sea level is rising at about 3 mm per year, and over a decade or more can provide a little more depth for corals to grow, and could allow more live coral cover on reef flats, at least until global-warming caused mass coral bleaching kills the corals (Fenner, 2012).

--- SENTENCE 486 ---
Reef flat areas can be measured by remote sensing, but reef slopes cannot because you can't see reefs in deep water from above the water surface.

--- SENTENCE 487 ---
Reef flats are now estimated to have between about four and 12 times as much area as reef slopes, globally (Vecsei, 2004).

--- SENTENCE 488 ---
Thus, reef flats are an important part of coral reefs.

--- SENTENCE 489 ---
In addition, because they are subject to some disturbances like low tides and trampling which don't affect reef slopes, and are closer to sources of human disturbance on land like sediment and nutrients (Field et al. 2011), reef flats need to be monitored as well as reef slopes.

--- SENTENCE 490 ---
There is a significant area of reef flats in American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 658 ---

--- SENTENCE 659 ---
The weaknesses of this procedure should also lead to caution in their interpretation.

# **Disease**

 A disease outbreak was observed in Vatia Bay on 2/19/11 following Hurricane Wilma on 1/24/11.

--- SENTENCE 733 ---
Observations were made along the south side of the bay.

--- SENTENCE 734 ---
The disease observed was areas of white skeleton between live tissue and skeleton which showed progressively darker turf algae with distance from the live tissue.

--- SENTENCE 735 ---
*Acropora abrotanoides, Acropora monticulosa, Pocillopora verrucosa* and *Pocillopora edouxi* were the most commonly affected, and *A. monticulosa* appeared to be the most severely affected.

--- SENTENCE 736 ---
Almost all *A. monticulosa* colonies appeared to be affected and a few were completely dead.

--- SENTENCE 737 ---
Subsequent observations showed that the width of the white area decreased in time, as well as the proportion of colonies that had white areas indicating active disease.

--- SENTENCE 738 ---
The width of the white area is probably an indicator of the speed with which the disease is killing the colony.

--- SENTENCE 739 ---
Observations of other areas following the first observations indicated that the disease was also present on the north side of the bay and that the reduction in disease was a little earlier on the north side than the south side.

--- SENTENCE 740 ---
Observations of other areas indicated that there was a fair bit of white disease on *Acropora nana* near the reef crest at Alofau even though there was no hurricane damage observed at Alofau.

--- SENTENCE 741 ---
Also, heightened levels of white disease were seen on *Pocillopora* at Alofau and some other sites.

--- SENTENCE 742 ---
Fairly low levels of white disease on *Pocillopora* appears to be chronic all over Tutuila, with scattered diseased colonies seen at many sites and over the entire span of the author's observations since 2004.

--- SENTENCE 743 ---
Also, the reefs all have a significant amount of old standing dead *Pocillopora* colonies that are covered with a mixture of turf and coralline algae, indicating significant amounts of mortality over the years.

--- SENTENCE 744 ---
The observed disease on *Pocillopora* after Wilma was greater than this background level, but not all colonies had disease.

--- SENTENCE 745 ---
*Isopora crateriformis* was observed to have a very few diseased colonies on the southwest of Tutuila where they are abundant, the first colonies of that species that the author has ever observed with disease.

--- SENTENCE 746 ---
*Isopora* was previously considered a sub-genus of *Acropora*, but recently has been elevated to genus status.

--- SENTENCE 747 ---
Clearly, *I. crateriformis* is closely related to *Acropora*.

--- SENTENCE 748 ---
Interestingly, no white disease was observed on the staghorn species *Acropora nobilis* in Vatia Bay, nor any of the staghorns (*Acopora muricata*, *Acropora pulchra*, and *A. nobilis*) in any of the backreef pools.

--- SENTENCE 749 ---
Even colonies of *A. nobilis* with damage from the hurricane did not have disease.

--- SENTENCE 750 ---
Thus, although *Acropora* and *Pocillopora* were the only genera affected, not all *Acropora* species were affected.

--- SENTENCE 751 ---
*Acropora hyacinthus* in Vatia had very few affected colonies.

--- SENTENCE 752 ---
Oh the south side, the diseased colonies were all in the mid to outer part of the bay, but then the inner part of the bay had no colonies left due to the tsunami, and some like *A. monticulosa* and *A. abrotanoides* were much more common in the outer bay even before the tsunami.

--- SENTENCE 753 ---
No outbreak of disease was observed following the tsunami.

--- SENTENCE 754 ---
The fact that the disease was well underway when first observed 26 days after the hurricane, plus the fact that the disease was most intense at the only location where the hurricane did significant damage, suggests but certainly does not prove that the hurricane caused the disease outbreak.

--- SENTENCE 755 ---
Disease outbreaks have been reported following bleaching events (Bruno et al 2007; Wilkinson and Souter, 2008), however this report appears to be the first report of a disease outbreak following a hurricane.

--- SENTENCE 756 ---
Higher temperatures are reported to increase disease such as black band (Boyett et al 2007; Bruno et al 2007), which suggests a

mechanism for disease outbreaks to follow bleaching events, since bleaching is caused by high temperatures.

--- SENTENCE 757 ---
However, it is not at all clear how a hurricane might cause a disease outbreak, nor why this relatively mild hurricane would produce disease while disease outbreaks have not been reported following far stronger hurricanes.

--- SENTENCE 758 ---
Speculative possible mechanisms might include stress on the corals that could make them vulnerable to diseases they could resist otherwise, and the possibility that the heavy surge could disperse diseases.

--- SENTENCE 759 ---
But if a hurricane could produce a disease outbreak, why couldn't a tsunami?

--- SENTENCE 760 ---
They both produce violent water motion and damage to corals.

--- SENTENCE 761 ---
The possibility remains that the timing and location of the disease outbreak followed the time and location of the hurricane by chance.

--- SENTENCE 762 ---

# **Appendix 1.

--- SENTENCE 894 ---
Reef Health**

The following document was provided as a summary of the condition of the benthic communities of the coral reefs, as habitat, to the Fisheries Council for incorporation into their Coral Reef Plan Team report.

--- SENTENCE 895 ---
It was based on the report for the previous year.

--- SENTENCE 896 ---
The State of Coral Reef Habitat in American Samoa, 2012.

--- SENTENCE 897 ---
Douglas Fenner, Ph.D.

 Coral reef scientists have not reached an agreement on the definition of coral reef health, but they often mention the provision of ecosystem services, and comparisons with ecosystems that have not been impacted by humans.

--- SENTENCE 898 ---
The benthic substrate and coral communities provide habitat for fish, and are essential fish habitat that is necessary for healthy fish populations and sustainable fish catches.

--- SENTENCE 899 ---
Monitoring habitat is part of Ecosystem-based Management.

--- SENTENCE 900 ---
The coral reef slopes of Tutuila, American Samoa, have about 30% live coral cover in transects, in five different monitoring programs (Fenner et al. 2008).

--- SENTENCE 901 ---
That is slightly higher than for the Pacific as a whole and the South Pacific in particular (Bruno and Selig, 2007) and for 17 countries in the Pacific (SPC, 2005).

--- SENTENCE 902 ---
Coral cover in towboard surveys is nearly as high as the US PRIAS (which are near-pristine) and higher than in the Hawaiian and Marianas archipelagos (Vroom, 2010) (though lower than in transects in American Samoa because transect locations are usually chosen to be better than random sites).

--- SENTENCE 903 ---
Coral cover is not as high as in the Pacific in the past (Bruno and Selig, 2007), nor as high as two estimates of American Samoan coral cover in the past (Wass, 1982; Maragos; although the accuracy of estimates is questionable mainly because sites were not chosen randomly; no quantitative data exists from before the COTS outbreak).

--- SENTENCE 904 ---
Coral cover has increased slightly over the 7 years of the Territorial Monitoring Program, and CRED has recorded increases of average coral cover around Tutuila as well (PIFSC, 2011) as has the Key Reef Species program.

--- SENTENCE 905 ---
Average coral cover in the Pacific has been decreasing (Côté et al. 2006; Bruno and Selig, 2007), so the increase in coral here is better than the Pacific averages for change.

--- SENTENCE 906 ---
There is only a small amount of dead coral, only about 5% cover, much less than in the average for 17 Pacific countries (SPC, 2005).

--- SENTENCE 907 ---
Coral cover on reef flats (about 8-21%) is not as high as on slopes.

--- SENTENCE 908 ---
Coralline algae cover is high (Vroom, 2010) and macroalgae is low and similar to that on near-pristine reefs (Vroom, 2010).

--- SENTENCE 909 ---
Coralline algae is considered good since it requires the same conditions as coral and attracts coral larvae, and macroalgae cover is considered bad because they compete with corals and can take over in phase shifts after a disturbance kills lots of coral.

--- SENTENCE 910 ---
The predominant cover on the reefs is encrusting, both encrusting coralline algae and encrusting corals.

--- SENTENCE 911 ---
This may provide less hiding cover for fish than would branching coral, though the reef matrix provides many hiding holes most places

 The tsunami of Sept. 29, 2009, did significant damage to reef areas in Vatia Bay, Fagatele Bay, and Leone Bay, and lesser damage elsewhere.

--- SENTENCE 912 ---
Heavily damaged areas were rare, moderately damaged areas more common, and lightly damaged or undamaged areas the most common.

--- SENTENCE 913 ---
Within about 6 months, all the rubble moved in Fagatele Bay was completely covered with coralline algae, while none is at Vatia.

--- SENTENCE 914 ---
Hurricane Wilma did additional damage in Vatia on Feb. 24, 2011, but little elsewhere.

--- SENTENCE 915 ---
Sedimentation rates near the mouths of streams are much higher than inside bays, which are in turn higher than outside bays.

--- SENTENCE 916 ---
The water on outer reef slopes away from streams is relatively clear, with low nutrient levels.

--- SENTENCE 917 ---
There is damage to small areas near stream mouths, and both Vatia Bay and the reef flat next to Coconut Point have had dense blooms of brown macroalgae.

--- SENTENCE 918 ---
Those are no longer present in Vatia, but persist at Coconut Point.

--- SENTENCE 919 ---
The reef slopes have more calcareous algae than non-calcareous, the calcareous is mostly coralline algae, but also the green macroalga *Halimeda*.

--- SENTENCE 920 ---
They contribute to building the reef, and are not known to bloom during phase shifts, unlike brown algae.

--- SENTENCE 921 ---
The reefs have remarkably little brown macroalgae.

--- SENTENCE 922 ---
Reefs in the harbor are in very poor condition.

--- SENTENCE 923 ---
There are only a few introduced marine species, none of which are invasive.

--- SENTENCE 924 ---
There are very few bioeroders or filter feeders, and calcium accumulation on the reef appears to be very good.

--- SENTENCE 925 ---
Disease incidence is low.

--- SENTENCE 926 ---
Macroinvertebrates, including herbivorous urchins, are in general uncommon to rare, for unknown reasons, but very likely this is natural.

--- SENTENCE 927 ---
Some may be hidden from sight.

--- SENTENCE 928 ---
Macroinvertebrates are food for some types of fish.

--- SENTENCE 929 ---
Hawaii and the Marianas also lack abundant large non-cryptic invertebrates.

--- SENTENCE 930 ---
There have been no bleaching events in the last 7 years, but 3 events before that.

--- SENTENCE 931 ---
Peter Houk reports a negative correlation of human population with coral diversity, but TMP has been unable to replicate that using slightly different variables and different sites.

--- SENTENCE 932 ---
The largest single disturbance on the territory's coral reefs was the crown-of-thorns starfish outbreak around 1978.

--- SENTENCE 933 ---
About 90% of all corals were eaten.

--- SENTENCE 934 ---
Observers report that they remember that table corals and staghorns were common, but areas dominated by other corals were not unusual.

--- SENTENCE 935 ---
Most of our reefs are now dominated by encrusting corals and only a few patches have high densities of tables and staghorns, except perhaps the banks where tables are common.

--- SENTENCE 936 ---
Thus the reefs may still be recovering from that event.

--- SENTENCE 937 ---
One reef patch at the mouth of Vatia Bay has shown remarkably rapid recovery, but other areas have recovered slowly.

--- SENTENCE 938 ---
The cause is not known, but does not seem to correlate with human populations.

--- SENTENCE 939 ---
Benthic reef communities are by no means pristine, but relatively healthy and far healthier than places like the Caribbean.

--- SENTENCE 940 ---
Habitat quality outside the harbor provides little support for suggesting that the lower fish biomass or low large fish abundances we have are due to poor habitat quality.

# **The Effects of Hurricane Wilma**

Hurricane Wilma was a category 1 hurricane when the center (there was no eye) went directly over Tutuila on Jan 24, 2011.

--- SENTENCE 954 ---
To summarize, the effects of Wilma were greatest at Vatia Bay, and minor to nonexistent elsewhere.

--- SENTENCE 955 ---
The effects at Vatia were greatest near the mouth of the bay and least at the head of the bay.

--- SENTENCE 956 ---
This was exactly the opposite pattern to that of the 2009 tsunami, which was most damaging near the head of the bay and least near the mouth of the bay.

--- SENTENCE 957 ---
Wilma's damage extended beyond the mouth of the bay out along Pola Island.

--- SENTENCE 958 ---
All of Vatia Bay and Pola Island reefs are now heavily damaged.

--- SENTENCE 959 ---
Wilma was followed by white disease on *Acropora* and *Pocillopora*, that on *Acropora* may be the same as that called "white syndrome."

--- SENTENCE 960 ---
There was heightened levels of white disease elsewhere as well, though not as great as in Vatia.

--- SENTENCE 961 ---
Heavily impacted species at Vatia included *A. monticulosa* and *A. abrotanoides*.

--- SENTENCE 962 ---
Nearly all colonies of *A. monticulosa* had it.

--- SENTENCE 963 ---
After a few months the disease slowed and stopped, leaving many colonies partly dead and partly alive.

# **Appendix 3**

# **Descriptions of individual reef sites in Tutuila, American Samoa**

Douglas Fenner, 2013

--- SENTENCE 1084 ---
These are descriptions of some sites, mainly monitoring sites for the Territorial Monitoring Program, which are recalled from memory in the text below.

--- SENTENCE 1085 ---
The reefs of Tutuila consist of fringing reefs that have a reef flat.

--- SENTENCE 1086 ---
There are also a few places that have back reef pools in the reef flat that in most cases were dug out for material to build village land or the foundation of the airport runway, and a slope into deeper water.

--- SENTENCE 1087 ---
The slopes end in a nearly flat looking shelf, which extends about a mile from shore most places (except the Tafuna plains where the land is on top of the shelf, and the west end of the island where it extends over 3 miles).

--- SENTENCE 1088 ---
The shelf undulates and is between 30 and 100 m deep.

--- SENTENCE 1089 ---
The shelf is probably covered mostly by sand, but it also has some "banks" that grow upward from it, mostly in a ring shape that may reflect a drowned barrier reef from previous geological periods.

--- SENTENCE 1090 ---
The banks have corals on them in some areas.

--- SENTENCE 1091 ---
The shelf ends seaward of the banks, where it ends as a vertical escarpment that goes down from about 100 meters to 350 meters depth.

--- SENTENCE 1092 ---
It is bare of corals and most anything else.

--- SENTENCE 1093 ---
## **Reef Slope sites:**

#### Fagasa

 Reefs reach the surface only within bays on the north shore, though there is reef buildup below the surface outside the bays along much of the coast.

--- SENTENCE 1094 ---
The slope at Fagasa has a sharp dropoff at the top where it descends from the reef flat at nearly a vertical slope.

--- SENTENCE 1095 ---
However, there are a variety of mounds on the slope, and by mid-slope on the eastern part of the reef there is a maze of mounds and gullies with good coral on it.

--- SENTENCE 1096 ---
There are a couple of fairly large avas that correspond to the two large streams coming into the bay, one at the boat ramp on the west side of the bay and another near the east side of the bay.

--- SENTENCE 1097 ---
The slope ends in sand, with some mounds on the sand.

--- SENTENCE 1098 ---
The sand is relatively shallow, perhaps 20 m deep at the deepest, and closer to 12-15 m deep nearer the center of the bay and the avas.

--- SENTENCE 1099 ---
The slope near the sand, in the areas where the sand is shallow, is near vertical and nearly devoid of corals.

--- SENTENCE 1100 ---
No currents have been noticed.

#### Tafeu

 Tafeu is a small bay on the north side with no people because there is no flat land.

--- SENTENCE 1101 ---
It has the least human impact of any site in the TMP program.

--- SENTENCE 1102 ---
The slope is quite lumpy, with lumps and grooves.

--- SENTENCE 1103 ---
There is good coral cover.

--- SENTENCE 1104 ---
There is one very large massive Porites colony, several meters tall.

--- SENTENCE 1105 ---
There is also a fairly large area covered with corallimorph polyps, Rhodactis sp., called "Matu-malu" in Samoan.

--- SENTENCE 1106 ---
The *Rhodactis* seems to be relatively stable in area.

--- SENTENCE 1107 ---
No currents have been noticed.

#### Vatia

 Vatia is a narrow bay on the north shore.

--- SENTENCE 1108 ---
A TMP monitoring site is on the west side.

--- SENTENCE 1109 ---
Before the tsunami, at mid-depths, the west side of Vatia had lumps of coral such as *Porites rus* or massive *Porites*, separated mostly by sand, some of which was covered by *Halimeda* algae.

--- SENTENCE 1110 ---
The upper part of the slope in the inner harbor mostly had *Porites cylindrica* and *Porites rus*, while the upper slope in the outer bay had more diverse corals.

--- SENTENCE 1111 ---
The slope ends in nearly flat sand at about 25 m depth.

--- SENTENCE 1112 ---
In the years prior to the tsunami, a brown macroalga, *Dictyota*, covered a large part of the reef slope, some even growing on coral.

--- SENTENCE 1113 ---
After the tsunami, almost all the coral on the slope in the inner part of the bay was destroyed and replaced by rubble.

--- SENTENCE 1114 ---
In the middle and outer parts of the bay there were rubble flows on the upper slope, between lumps where corals survived.

--- SENTENCE 1115 ---
Damage was much greater in the inner part of the bay than the outer.

--- SENTENCE 1116 ---
In the inner part of the bay, the rubble after the tsunami quickly became covered with green filamentous algae and has stayed that way at least to 2012, indicating a lack of resilience.

--- SENTENCE 1117 ---
This is likely due to nutrient buildup in the water of the inner bay.

--- SENTENCE 1118 ---
The tsunami removed the *Dictyota* macroalgae.

--- SENTENCE 1119 ---
At least two kinds of red macroalgae grew after the tsunami.

--- SENTENCE 1120 ---
Then Hurricane Wilma struck, and damaged the outer part of the bay much more than the inner part.

--- SENTENCE 1121 ---
On the east side of the bay, there are areas where the coral is all intact, and other areas where the living corals and a layer about 20 cm thick of rubble that it was living on were removed, with no trace of them on the reef slope.

--- SENTENCE 1122 ---
The edge between the intact reef and where corals and the rubble they were attached to were removed, is very sharp.

--- SENTENCE 1123 ---
It appears that the living corals were growing on a bed of cylindrical coral rubble, suggesting a prior coral community of *Acropora* such as *A. nobilis* staghorn.

--- SENTENCE 1124 ---
The rubble was covered with a thin layer of calcareous algae, but where the coral and upper layer was removed, the rubble just has a light layer of filamentous algae.

--- SENTENCE 1125 ---
The upper slope has crustose calcareous algae that extends down only about 3 m down the slope and ends rather abruptly there.

--- SENTENCE 1126 ---
No currents were noticed.

#### Masefau

 The slope on the side of the bay toward Tutuila is quite degraded, lots of dead rubble with macroalgae growing on it, a little live coral on the upper slope.

--- SENTENCE 1127 ---
The center of the bay is sand, and nearer to the head of the bay it looks like there has been a lot of moving around of rubble and sand by tsunami or hurricanes.

--- SENTENCE 1128 ---
## Aunu'u

 The Aunu'u site is west of the small harbor, where the surf line goes out from the land, along the northern part of the surf line as it goes away from land.

--- SENTENCE 1129 ---
The slope is fairly steep, probably a bit more than 45 degrees.

--- SENTENCE 1130 ---
There is high coral cover consisting of diverse corals.

--- SENTENCE 1131 ---
Close to land, the slope reaches flat bottom at only about 10 m deep, but this depth increases westward to at least 30 m deep.

--- SENTENCE 1132 ---
There are a few lumps out from the base of the reef at about 30 m deep.

--- SENTENCE 1133 ---
Near shore there are good size patches of coral on the nearly flat bottom at the base of the slope.

--- SENTENCE 1134 ---
Sometimes there is little or no current, at other times currents parallel to the reef surface can be fairly strong.

### Amaua

 The TMP site starts just east of the ava.

--- SENTENCE 1135 ---
The slope is steep in the medium depths and doesn't have much coral cover.

--- SENTENCE 1136 ---
Below the steep slope, the slope continues at about a 45 degree slope.

--- SENTENCE 1137 ---
That lower slope has high macroalgae cover which is mostly *Halimeda*, but also has some low mounds of *Lobophyllia* coral, and below that some *Mycedium* plates.

--- SENTENCE 1138 ---
To the west of the ava, there are many *Lobophyllia* colonies.

--- SENTENCE 1139 ---
The upper slope becomes lumpy and has more coral cover than the steep mid-section.

--- SENTENCE 1140 ---
The mid-section has some avas with the bottom covered with blocks about one meter diameter.

--- SENTENCE 1141 ---
No currents were noticed.

--- SENTENCE 1142 ---
#### Faga'alu

 The Territorial Monitoring Program site is just south of the prominence where the reef projects out into the ocean, and extends onto it.

--- SENTENCE 1143 ---
The slope is about a 45 degree angle.

--- SENTENCE 1144 ---
The mid-portion of the slope is entirely covered with cylindrical stick rubble, which is covered with and lightly cemented together with crustose calcareous algae.

--- SENTENCE 1145 ---
There are a few corals in that zone, but their cover has not increased measurably in the 9 years of the monitoring program.

--- SENTENCE 1146 ---
Below that zone, plate corals begin.

--- SENTENCE 1147 ---
The plate corals are overlapping much like shingles on a roof.

--- SENTENCE 1148 ---
The most common species is *Mycedium*.

--- SENTENCE 1149 ---
The plate corals commonly form large continuous areas with near complete live coral cover.

--- SENTENCE 1150 ---
Average coral cover including the gaps between plate coral formations is about 65%, one of the highest on slopes anywhere on the island.

--- SENTENCE 1151 ---
The upper boundary between this zone and the rubble zone undulates between about 15 m and 18 m deep.

--- SENTENCE 1152 ---
The plate corals extended down to around 25 meters deep, where the slope begins to decrease and rubble and sand that continue below as a floor, begin.

--- SENTENCE 1153 ---
The floor is probably 30 m or more deep.

--- SENTENCE 1154 ---
The tsunami of 2009 removed most of the plate corals on the lower part of this zone.

--- SENTENCE 1155 ---
The break between undamaged living plates above and the dead area below where the living plate corals were removed, is very sharp, and varies some in depth.

--- SENTENCE 1156 ---
On the upper parts of the slope, around 5 meters depth, there are more corals, primarily various species of *Acropora*.

--- SENTENCE 1157 ---
Coral cover in the shallow zone may (or may not) be increasing.

--- SENTENCE 1158 ---
The cause of the dead coral rubble in mid-depths is unknown.

--- SENTENCE 1159 ---
It is almost certainly to be from a branching *Acropora* such as *Acropora nobilis* or possibly *A. abrotanoides*.

--- SENTENCE 1160 ---
*Acropora* are among the most sensitive corals to most things that kill corals, such as hurricanes, disease, crown-of-thorns starfish, and mass bleaching.

--- SENTENCE 1161 ---
So the cause of their death is not known.

--- SENTENCE 1162 ---
But it is clear that at one time there was a lot of *Acropora* growing on this slope, and they are no longer there.

--- SENTENCE 1163 ---
No currents have been felt.

#### Coconut Point

 The TMP site at Coconut Point begins just south of the ava, and extends south from there.

--- SENTENCE 1164 ---
In shallow water, the slope gradually gets steeper so that at mid-depths it is steep, perhaps a 70 degree slope.

--- SENTENCE 1165 ---
Then near the bottom of the slope, the angle of slope decreases as the floor is approached at around 30 m deep.

--- SENTENCE 1166 ---
The steep slope has high crustose coralline algae cover, and at times has had considerable cover of a soft, branching red alga which is actually a coralline alga.

--- SENTENCE 1167 ---
Coral cover is higher on the upper part of the slope where it is not so steep.

--- SENTENCE 1168 ---
In some areas on the lower slope there are plate corals, *Mycedium*.

--- SENTENCE 1169 ---
No currents have been felt.

--- SENTENCE 1170 ---
#### Fogama'a Bay

 Near the center of the bay there is a vertical rock face at the water line which extends a short way under water.

--- SENTENCE 1171 ---
Below that the slope is not steep, maybe 20 degree slope, and begins with low coral cover.

--- SENTENCE 1172 ---
Coral cover increases with depth.

--- SENTENCE 1173 ---
At about 10 m depth there is a patch of pure *Merulina* plate corals, with the plates at angles.

--- SENTENCE 1174 ---
The patch extends quite a ways horizontally on the slope, but not far vertically.

--- SENTENCE 1175 ---
Below the patch there is moderate coral cover, down to about 30 m depth where the floor is reached.

--- SENTENCE 1176 ---
No currents were noticed.

#### Fagatele Bay

 Fagatele Bay has a large reef with an unusual shape.

--- SENTENCE 1177 ---
In the eastern half of the bay, the reef slope begins with a near vertical drop from the reef flat to about 3 m depth.

--- SENTENCE 1178 ---
From there, the slope is very gradual, so gradual it is hard to see which way it slopes.

--- SENTENCE 1179 ---
Far from shore it reaches a depth a little more than 10 m where it then descends more steeply, at around 45 degrees or more.

--- SENTENCE 1180 ---
In effect, then, there is a shelf between about 3 m and 12 m depth.

--- SENTENCE 1181 ---
The shelf has some fairly mild ridges and gullies running out toward deeper water.

--- SENTENCE 1182 ---
The inner part of the shelf now has little coral largely due to the 2009 tsunami, but the outer shelf has good coral cover.

--- SENTENCE 1183 ---
There were some large mounds of delicate plate corals (*Echinopora lamellosa*) which were completely removed by the tsunami.

--- SENTENCE 1184 ---
The upper part of the shelf has low coral cover, but there are a few large massive *Porites* on the inner part of the shelf.

--- SENTENCE 1185 ---
There is a huge colony of *Pachyseris rugosa* out at the edge of the shelf in one spot, the largest the author has seen anywhere.

--- SENTENCE 1186 ---
It is surrounded by many small daughter colonies.

--- SENTENCE 1187 ---
It has steep slopes below it.

--- SENTENCE 1188 ---
There is an increased number of mushroom corals near the bottom of the steep slope.

--- SENTENCE 1189 ---
At the bottom of the steep slope there is nearly flat sand, but at some locations more coral can be seen in the distance.

--- SENTENCE 1190 ---
In the western half of the bay, there is no reef flat, and the reef descends from near the shore at roughly a 45 degree angle or a bit less, all the way down to 30 meters.

--- SENTENCE 1191 ---
Coralline algae cover is high at all depths on the shelf and down the slope.

--- SENTENCE 1192 ---
Although the by points towards the southwest and most of the year waves come from the east, wave action in the bay is strong.

--- SENTENCE 1193 ---
Because the bay is on the outer edge of the Tafuna plains, there is no shelf beyond the bay to reduce the strength of the waves.

--- SENTENCE 1194 ---
Coralline algae does better in heavy wave motion than in calm.

--- SENTENCE 1195 ---
The 2009 tsunami produced some flows of rubble which was easily distinguishable from the intact reef.

--- SENTENCE 1196 ---
Within 6 months, the rubble which had near zero percent coralline algae cover had 100% coralline algae cover.

--- SENTENCE 1197 ---
No currents were noticed.

--- SENTENCE 1198 ---
#### Leone

 The reef in Leone Bay has a very gentle slope in front of the big Catholic church at around 10 m depth.

--- SENTENCE 1199 ---
Coral cover is high, and there are ridges and gullies that run toward the edge of the reef.

--- SENTENCE 1200 ---
The floor of gullies typically is coral-covered as well as the ridges.

--- SENTENCE 1201 ---
To the west, the slope becomes greater reaching around 45 degree angle.

--- SENTENCE 1202 ---
In the western area, at the bottom of the slope the slope becomes much less and corals are on lumps with flat sand between them.

--- SENTENCE 1203 ---
The flat sand is at about 30 m depth.

--- SENTENCE 1204 ---
The ridges and gullies are larger in shallower water, and at about 8 m depth plate corals (*Isopora crateriformis*) becomes more common and in about the 2-5 m range become the dominant coral with high cover.

--- SENTENCE 1205 ---
From about 4 m down to perhaps 8 m, there is an area of high coral cover of table corals (*Acropora hyacinthus*) and staghorns (*Acropora nobilis*).

--- SENTENCE 1206 ---
No currents were noticed.

--- SENTENCE 1207 ---
To the west of Leone, the high cover of the plate corals in shallow water continues.

--- SENTENCE 1208 ---
Also, moderately strong currents were found.

## **Reef flat sites:**

#### Fagamalo

 In Fagamalo, the reef flat is not right at the low tide line and flat.

--- SENTENCE 1209 ---
Rather, it has a slight slope to it and has some areas higher than others.

--- SENTENCE 1210 ---
Coral cover is generally low, but there are some areas with better coral cover.

--- SENTENCE 1211 ---
#### Fagasa

 The reef flat at Fagasa has little or no coral cover except out near the edge of the dropoff.

--- SENTENCE 1212 ---
In the first few meters back in from the dropoff, there is good coral cover, with encrusting *Montipora* and young table corals, almost all of which are *Acropora hyacinthus*.

--- SENTENCE 1213 ---
There was a mass settlement of *A. hyacinthus* on the outer edge of the reef flat and the very top of the slope, around about 2005.

--- SENTENCE 1214 ---
Most have survived and grown, and in areas they are now growing into each other or over each other.

--- SENTENCE 1215 ---
Additional recruitment appears to continue, but it is much less than the original pulse.

--- SENTENCE 1216 ---
In one area, the tables reached a size of about 30-40 cm diameter and then were all killed.

--- SENTENCE 1217 ---
The area was back from the reef flat edge, and there are live tables near the edge.

--- SENTENCE 1218 ---
It appears that in a period of extra low tides, waves splashed the corals near the edge of the reef flat keeping them alive, while the waves did not splash the tables back from the edge and they dried out too much and died.

--- SENTENCE 1219 ---
This is probably why there is significant live coral only near the edge of the reef flat.

#### Vatia

 The reef flat on the east west side of Vatia bay is pretty bare, with some rubble areas and a few areas with very short "microatolls" of *Porites*.

--- SENTENCE 1220 ---
These "microatolls" are very short rings of live coral that can be up to around 1 m diameter, but only about 2-4 cm tall.

--- SENTENCE 1221 ---
The best coral is near the outer edge of the reef flat, before the reef flat drops away nearly vertically to start the upper slope.

--- SENTENCE 1222 ---
Coral cover on the outer reef flat was reduced after the tsunami and Hurricane Wilma.

--- SENTENCE 1223 ---
#### Aoa

 The reef flat in Aoa bay is very wide.

--- SENTENCE 1224 ---
Most of it is flat sand.

--- SENTENCE 1225 ---
By the time you reach about half way out, scattered corals appear.

--- SENTENCE 1226 ---
The best coral is near the outer edge of the reef flat.

--- SENTENCE 1227 ---
Corals there are somewhat patchy, with more some places than others.

--- SENTENCE 1228 ---
#### Aunu'u

 The reef flat above the slope where the slope site is, receives very heavy wave action and is very dangerous to snorkel.

--- SENTENCE 1229 ---
At low tide it may be walked.

--- SENTENCE 1230 ---
There are scattered clumps of small corals.

#### Alofau

 The inner part of the reef flat at Alofau is mostly loose rubble, with some microatolls made of massive *Porites*.

--- SENTENCE 1231 ---
The mid-section of the reef flat has areas with a fair number of *Acropora aspera* colonies, but a total low coral cover.

--- SENTENCE 1232 ---
The outer reef flat has higher coral cover and high encrusting coralline algae cover.

--- SENTENCE 1233 ---
There is a good diversity of corals, including large patches of *Acropora pagoensis*, the most I know of on a reef flat.

--- SENTENCE 1234 ---
The rubble on the inner reef flat was moved by the tsunami.

#### Amaua

 The reef flat at Amaua has low coral cover and high rubble cover.

--- SENTENCE 1235 ---
Wave action is too rough to approach the outer edge of the reef flat.

--- SENTENCE 1236 ---
#### Lauli'i

## **The State of Coral Reef Fish in American Samoa, 2011.

--- SENTENCE 1314 ---
**

Douglas Fenner

 American Samoa consists of several small, high, volcanic islands, 14 degrees south of the equator, near the middle of the Pacific.

--- SENTENCE 1315 ---
Two small nearly uninhabited or uninhabited more-remote atolls are present as well.

--- SENTENCE 1316 ---
Slopes are steep and densely forested on the high islands, and rainfall is high and erosion active.

--- SENTENCE 1317 ---
The islands have been inhabited for about 3000 years, with western influence growing rapidly in the last half-century or so.

--- SENTENCE 1318 ---
The population has grown exponentially from 2500 in 1900 to about 65,000 today.

--- SENTENCE 1319 ---
Population is concentrated on the largest island, Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 1320 ---
All the islands are surrounded with coral reefs.

--- SENTENCE 1321 ---
About half of the coast has reef flats, with reef slopes beyond the flats.

--- SENTENCE 1322 ---
Tutuila is surrounded by a large shelf about 100-300 feet deep, which has an interrupted ring of bank reefs, an apparent sunken barrier reef, about 1 mile from shore.

--- SENTENCE 1323 ---
The coral reefs have relatively high diversity, commensurate with their location near the middle of the Pacific.

--- SENTENCE 1324 ---
About 945 species of coral reef fish are known.

--- SENTENCE 1325 ---
The benthic coral reef communities are in relatively good condition, with about 30% coral cover, high coralline algae cover, low macroalgal cover, and small amounts of dead coral.

--- SENTENCE 1326 ---
There are periodic major natural disturbances from hurricanes, and more rarely, tsunamis and crown-of-thorns starfish outbreaks.

--- SENTENCE 1327 ---
The basic features of the benthic reef ecosystems are similar in coral cover, high coralline algae cover, and low macroalgae to those of the US Pacific remote island areas (PRIAS), which are nearpristine, and geographically closer than other U.S. archipelagos.

--- SENTENCE 1328 ---
There are a variety of human impacts and threats to the reefs of American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 1329 ---
Nutrient runoff is a problem, coming from piggeries, high phosphate laundry detergent, the soccer field, grey water, leaking sewage lines, septic systems, and other sources.

--- SENTENCE 1330 ---
Nutrients accumulate at the head of long narrow bays with little flushing like the harbor and Vatia Bay, fueling phytoplankton blooms.

--- SENTENCE 1331 ---
Relatively small areas of macroalgal blooms have appeared in recent years around the mouths of a few streams and in narrow bays.

--- SENTENCE 1332 ---
Sediment runoff in streams is obvious during and after the frequent heavy rainstorms, turning streams dark brown.

--- SENTENCE 1333 ---
Silty fresh stream water floats on the surface and is rapidly carried out avas and mixed with and diluted by the vast ocean volume.

--- SENTENCE 1334 ---
However, small areas of damage occur around some stream mouths.

--- SENTENCE 1335 ---
The harbor is quite different from the outer island coasts, with reefs in the harbor having been dredged, filled and severely damaged by nutrients, chemical pollution, and sedimentation.

--- SENTENCE 1336 ---
The harbor is a small part of the territory coast and reefs.

--- SENTENCE 1337 ---
But outside the harbor, coral populations are good, and coralline algae which is even more sensitive to sediment, nutrient-fueled algal overgrowth, and lack of herbivores, is particularly good.

--- SENTENCE 1338 ---
The habitat for reef fish is in relatively good health.

--- SENTENCE 1339 ---
A study of juvenile humphead wrasse found them only in very small areas, which are areas they prefer, but they can survive in other conditions.

--- SENTENCE 1340 ---
Habitat for juvenile reef fish is not unlimited, though the reef flat area is at least as large as that of the reef slopes, if not larger.

--- SENTENCE 1341 ---
The juvenile habitat area is large enough to support large populations of small fish species, and there is no direct evidence that it limits any of the large fish species, each of which is likely to have different juvenile preferences and requirements for habitat.

--- SENTENCE 1342 ---
Mangroves are juvenile reef fish habitat for some species, but there are only small mangrove areas here.

--- SENTENCE 1343 ---
Only a minority of reef fish species in the Indo-Pacific are reported elsewhere to use mangroves as juvenile reef habitat, and even those species can use other areas if mangroves are not available.

--- SENTENCE 1344 ---
For a few species, absence of mangroves are correlated with lower populations on reefs.

--- SENTENCE 1345 ---
Studies of fish near mangroves have failed to show that juvenile reef fish use mangroves here.

--- SENTENCE 1346 ---
Habitat certainly affects all coral reef fish, though for most species the most important feature of the reef is rugosity, i.e., hiding places, and whether the coral is alive has less effect.

--- SENTENCE 1347 ---
Death of corals is reported to decrease populations of some fish species and increase others, eventual collapse of skeletons will decrease populations of almost all fishes.

--- SENTENCE 1348 ---
Habitat, food supply, predation, larval supply, and the like completely determine populations of fish species that are so small that they are not taken by fishing.

--- SENTENCE 1349 ---
For larger fish, all those things apply, and fishing applies as well.

--- SENTENCE 1350 ---
Fishing reduces populations from the natural levels, and unlike the other factors directly kills fish, so documenting the removal of fish by fishing directly documents an effect, while documenting differences in habitats and the other factors also requires demonstrating that those factors have affected the fish populations.

--- SENTENCE 1351 ---
The reef fish communities here differ strikingly from near-pristine reefs in several ways.

--- SENTENCE 1352 ---
They have low fish biomass, about 1/3 that of near-pristine reefs, and they have quite low populations of the largest reef fish species.

--- SENTENCE 1353 ---
On near-pristine reefs, about half of the fish biomass is in the largest reef fish species (often primarily sharks), while on Tutuila reef slopes the biomass in the largest species is negligible.

--- SENTENCE 1354 ---
Large fish are more abundant on the bank reefs which surround Tutuila at a distance of about a mile, and they are more abundant within the archipelago with more distance from the heavily populated Tutuila, but do not reach abundances or biomasses similar to that of near-pristine reefs (which are farther from people).

--- SENTENCE 1355 ---
While the biomass of large fish is greater away from human population than near it, the biomass of small fish is similar or the same near human population compared to farther away, an effect that also occurs in the two other archipelagos that have been studied this way, namely Hawaii and the Marianas.

--- SENTENCE 1356 ---
Further, the effect of reduced large fish species with unaffected small fish extends far from human population centers, 100 miles.

--- SENTENCE 1357 ---
The small fish act as a control for the effects of impacts of variables such as sedimentation, nutrients, chemical pollution, and habitat degradation, since those would affect the small fish as well as the large fish.

--- SENTENCE 1358 ---
The impact of human population on only the large fish is a fingerprint of fishing, since only fishing affects large fish differentially.

--- SENTENCE 1359 ---
The differential effect of fishing on large fish is well known and documented in fisheries around the world.

--- SENTENCE 1360 ---
Sediment and nutrient runoff extend for very short distances from the shores of small islands, and cannot possibly cause decreases of large fish at distances of 50 or 100 miles, but boats can and do easily go those distances carrying fishers.

--- SENTENCE 1361 ---
These patterns can only be explained by fishing, no other viable explanations have been found.

--- SENTENCE 1362 ---
The reef catch not only includes many reef finfish species, but also octopus, giant clams, lobsters, small invertebrates collected by gleaning, and specific traditional fisheries for reef invertebrates such as sea cucumbers and sea urchins, especially in the more traditional Manu'a islands.

--- SENTENCE 1363 ---
There is no aquarium fishery or live food fish trade or commercial sea cucumber fishery, and no export of reef fish.

--- SENTENCE 1364 ---
Both subsistence and commercial reef fishing occurs, using methods such as spear fishing, throw nets, and hook and line.

--- SENTENCE 1365 ---
Traps are not used except for a traditional fishery for newly settled goatfish recruits.

--- SENTENCE 1366 ---
Fishing pressure on Tutuila is currently relatively low, though it still exists and can add up to significant amounts of fish taken per kilometer of reef per year.

--- SENTENCE 1367 ---
One might conclude from the relatively low current fishing pressure that reef fish stocks must be in good condition.

--- SENTENCE 1368 ---
But that does not follow, since it takes time for stocks to recover, and fishing pressure was heavy in the past.

--- SENTENCE 1369 ---
In the 1970's, fishing pressure, as measured by weight of fish caught per unit area of reef, was the heaviest then known in the entire world.

--- SENTENCE 1370 ---
Fishing pressure has declined rapidly since then, even though human populations have grown rapidly.

--- SENTENCE 1371 ---
This is a very unusual situation, and is due to the rapid rise of incomes and a subsequent cultural shift from traditional food sources including fishing, to store-bought food.

--- SENTENCE 1372 ---
Some researchers have made the mistake of concluding that the recent rapid decline of fish catches was due to a rapid reduction of fish stocks happening at the same time, since this is the normal pattern around the world, particularly with rapidly growing populations.

--- SENTENCE 1373 ---
But that assumes that fishing pressure increases with the growing population, which indeed is observed virtually everywhere except here.

--- SENTENCE 1374 ---
But in American Samoa, fishing effort has decreased rapidly with growing income in recent decades, and that has been the main driver of decreasing catches.

--- SENTENCE 1375 ---
Underwater visual surveys have shown steady reef fish populations, possibly with a slight increase, since the 1970's.

--- SENTENCE 1376 ---
That has lead to the conclusion that reef fish stocks are fine, that they are naturally low in biomass.

--- SENTENCE 1377 ---
(However, one study now shows decreasing reef fish stocks since 2002.)

--- SENTENCE 1378 ---
That conclusion neglects the fact that fishing pressure was extremely heavy in the past, and that some fish species only recover slowly from depletion.

--- SENTENCE 1379 ---
The speed of recovery of different species correlates with their size, with small fish species able to recover rapidly or even sustain heavy fishing pressure, while large fish species are unable to withstand heavy fishing pressure and are very slow to recover.

--- SENTENCE 1380 ---
FishBase lists quantitative measures of vulnerability to fishing and resilience (or the speed of recovery) for each species, and shows that large reef species can stand much less fishing pressure than small fish species, and recover much more slowly.

--- SENTENCE 1381 ---
The extreme is found in sharks, since reef sharks typically have only about 1-4 pups yearly or every two years, and so are very slow to increase in population.

--- SENTENCE 1382 ---
The low numbers of large reef fish is a ghost of fishing past, and even small catches will keep their numbers at low levels.

--- SENTENCE 1383 ---
The catch per unit effort (CPUE) can be an index of fish stocks, if it decreases that can indicate decreasing fish stocks.

--- SENTENCE 1384 ---
CPUE for reef fish has not been decreasing.

--- SENTENCE 1385 ---
On the other hand, surveys of older fishermen have found that they report that their reef fish catches are less than they once were.

--- SENTENCE 1386 ---
It appears that increasing population drove increasing fishing pressure up to about the 1970's, and that fishing did lead to the collapse of the large fish populations, but since then the shift to store food has led to decreases in fishing effort.

--- SENTENCE 1387 ---
Small fish were never depleted in the first place (as underwater surveys found) and large fish (which were depleted before underwater surveys began) have not recovered.

--- SENTENCE 1388 ---
The species that is in the very worst shape by far is the bumphead parrotfish, which is close to local extinction throughout the archipelago.

--- SENTENCE 1389 ---
All observers and recorders combined see about one fish per year.

--- SENTENCE 1390 ---
In spite of the massive coverage of the CRED surveys, with tow boarders covering over 100 km of Tutuila coast and all of the coastline of the smaller

islands (several times for the smallest islands), recording all bumphead parrots of all sizes at any distance, plus all of the roughly 7000 dives the CRED team made, not a single individual was recorded by anyone in their last surveys in March, 2010.

--- SENTENCE 1391 ---
On the other hand, four different eyewitnesses, including two expert reef fish biologists, report schools of bumpheads several decades ago, the largest school being estimated at 30-50 fish.

--- SENTENCE 1392 ---
The location where that large school slept was found by the fisherman who reported it, and since they sleep at the same location every night, he was able to spear them one by one until they were gone.

--- SENTENCE 1393 ---
(This likely happened during the scuba spear fishing period.)

--- SENTENCE 1394 ---
The largest school seen in the last decade was just two fish.

--- SENTENCE 1395 ---
So the population has decreased over the decades by at least by a ratio of 30 to 2, and we know the cause, fishing.

--- SENTENCE 1396 ---
This species is critically locally endangered, due to fishing.

--- SENTENCE 1397 ---
Although sharks and humphead wrasse are at low levels compared to near-pristine remote islands, they are not in immediate danger of extinction.

--- SENTENCE 1398 ---
Most of the largest reef fish species are predators, such as sharks, and humphead wrasse which mostly eat invertebrates.

--- SENTENCE 1399 ---
The removal of predators can have major effects on ecosystems in what are called trophic cascades.

--- SENTENCE 1400 ---
Likely such effects are hidden because fishing also takes their prey.

--- SENTENCE 1401 ---
There are many species of very small fish which are not fished at all and thus are not impacted by fishing.

--- SENTENCE 1402 ---
Relatively small fish like surgeonfish and damsels are abundant, and the most abundant species, the surgeonfish *Ctenochaetus striatus*, has large recruitment events most years and huge recruitment some years, indicating that it is more than able to replace itself and is not impacted significantly by fishing.

--- SENTENCE 1403 ---
There are good stocks of herbivorous fish, even when the detritivores (like *C. striatus*) are removed from that category.

--- SENTENCE 1404 ---
The status of intermediate-sized fish is not known in detail, though is likely to be intermediate.

--- SENTENCE 1405 ---
There are some fish which are a disproportionately large portion of the fish catch compared to their proportion of the living fish populations, some of which may be in some trouble.

--- SENTENCE 1406 ---
For example, Alongo (*Acanthurus lineatus*) is less common than Pone (mostly *Ctenochaetus striatus*) yet Alongo forms a larger portion of the catch than Pone.

--- SENTENCE 1407 ---
Both of those species are probably in good condition, yet Alongo clearly has higher fishing pressure than Pone.

--- SENTENCE 1408 ---
The status of spawning aggregations has not been documented.

--- SENTENCE 1409 ---
Fish catch data comes from only a tiny proportion of the actual fish catch, so rare fish are essentially absent from the fish catch data.

--- SENTENCE 1410 ---
Fish catch data is inadequate to assess the status of fish which are in the very worst condition (near extinction due to fishing).

--- SENTENCE 1411 ---
In conclusion, the evidence indicates that the primary cause for the low reef fish biomass and low abundances of the large reef fish is fishing, though there are effects of other factors which are primarily in the harbor where the reef habitat has been largely destroyed, and fish catches are now quite small compared to historical catches, likely due to the degradation and loss of habitat.

--- SENTENCE 1412 ---
It should be noted that the 1/3 reduction in total biomass is the amount that is the normal expected reduction of a virgin fish stock produced by fishing at MSY, it is not overfishing.

--- SENTENCE 1413 ---
However, MSY is defined for individual fish stocks, which are subsets of fish species, not for groups of fish.

--- SENTENCE 1414 ---
A 1/3 reduction of all fished species together is some kind of weighted average, and since there is variation between species some are above and some below.

--- SENTENCE 1415 ---
The overall 1/3 reduction obscures much heavier reductions in some species and lesser reductions in others.

--- SENTENCE 1416 ---
A 1/3 reduction in the total requires reductions greater than 1/3 in as much as half the species, which qualify as overfishing.

--- SENTENCE 1417 ---
Thus, a 1/3 reduction of total biomass implies that some species must be overfished.

--- SENTENCE 1418 ---
The effects of fishing appear to be the largest single human

impact on the coral reef ecosystems of the territory (the COTS outbreak of 1978 which was the largest single impact of any kind was likely mostly natural.

--- SENTENCE 1419 ---
The construction of the airport runway on the reef flat appears to be the largest single construction impact on the reefs.).

--- SENTENCE 1420 ---
It is thought that whole ecosystems are more resilient than ecosystems with large parts missing.

--- SENTENCE 1421 ---
Resilience will be needed to disturbances such as the projected devastating effects of mass coral bleaching coming in a few decades.

--- SENTENCE 1422 ---
The coral reef ecosystem needs to be allowed to return as near as possible to the natural state to regain resilience to the oncoming threats.

--- SENTENCE 1423 ---
In addition, one species, the bumphead parrot, is critically endangered locally.

--- SENTENCE 1424 ---
The director of DMWR publicly announced in July, 2007, at a US Coral Reef Task Force meeting here, that he would protect the large reef fish (based on rarity), but that has not happened.

--- SENTENCE 1425 ---
Banning the take of the largest reef fish species would come at little cost to fishers, since few are being caught due to their very low abundances, and would allow much greater sustainable harvest once they had recovered.

--- SENTENCE 1426 ---
There are several community-based MPAs, and one no-take MPA, but all are small, and large fish, particularly sharks, range widely, much wider than any of the MPAs, so the MPA's are quite ineffective at protecting them, and there is no evidence so far of increased abundances of any fish in MPAs.

--- SENTENCE 1427 ---
The fishing regulations ban scuba spearfishing, destructive fishing methods and removing coral, and specifies minimum sizes for giant clams and lobsters.

--- SENTENCE 1428 ---
However, there are no bag limits, seasonal closures, size limits for fish, or other such limits.

--- SENTENCE 1429 ---
The fisher community appears to be less vocal than in places like Hawaii or Guam.

--- SENTENCE 1430 ---
Traditional controls on fishing, such as the edicts of village chiefs, are much weaker than they once were.

--- SENTENCE 1431 ---
Governor Toniola Tulafono has directed the CRAG agencies to use the best available scientific information and the precautionary principle.
