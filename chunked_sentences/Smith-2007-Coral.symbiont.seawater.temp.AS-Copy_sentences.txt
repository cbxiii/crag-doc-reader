## **Coral symbiont diversity along a seawater temperature gradient**

Tester's Symposium, Honolulu, HI 22-Mar-08

Lance Smith, University of Hawai'i Andrew Baker, University of Miami Tom Oliver, Stanford University

A gradient in seawater temperatures occurs along coral reef habitats on Ofu Island, American Samoa:

--- SENTENCE 2 ---
In a small back reef pool, temperatures reach 34 °C with daily fluctuations of up to 6 °C, while only reaching 31 °C with daily fluctuations of < 1 °C at the same depth on the adjacent forereef.

--- SENTENCE 3 ---
Temperature maxima and daily fluctuations are intermediate in a large back reef pool.

--- SENTENCE 4 ---
More than 50 species of reef-building corals occur in the small pool, including many *Acropora*  and *Pocillopora* species thought to be sensitive to elevated seawater temperatures.

--- SENTENCE 5 ---
Symbiotic zooxanthellae (*Symbiodinium* spp.) are classified into several clades, and corals containing Clade D may be relatively tolerant of elevated seawater temperatures and rapid temperature fluctuations.

--- SENTENCE 6 ---
We monitored seawater temperatures in the small back reef pool, the large back reef pool, and the shallow forereef for a 1-year period.

--- SENTENCE 7 ---
During the summer (March), we sampled zooxanthellae from colonies of *Acropora hyacinthus, Pocillopora eydouxi,* and *Porites lobata* at these three sites.

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
For *A. hyacinthus*, only Clade D was found in the small back reef pool, while a mix of Clades C and D were found at the other two sites.

--- SENTENCE 9 ---
For *P. eydouxi*, a mix of Clades C and D was found in the small back reef pool, while only Clade C was found at the other two sites.

--- SENTENCE 10 ---
For *P. lobata*, only Clade C was found at all three sites.

--- SENTENCE 11 ---
Thus, Clade D may increase the thermal tolerances of *Acropora* and *Pocillopora* species in the Ofu back reef pools.

--- SENTENCE 12 ---
However, the coral communities in the pools are dominated by *Porites* species, including *P. lobata*, yet *Porites*  colonies in the pools and in other warm, shallow coral reef environments contain only Clade C zooxanthellae.

--- SENTENCE 13 ---
These results suggest that host coral physiological processes strongly contribute to the thermal tolerances of *Porites* colonies in the back reef pools.
