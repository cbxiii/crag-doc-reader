--- SENTENCE 4 ---
Abstract: Groundwater is the primary drinking water source on most oceanic islands, including Tutuila, American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 5 ---
Drinking water quality on Tutuila is impacted by anthropogenic pollution sources such as on-site sewage disposal systems, piggeries, and agricultural leachate, particularly across the densely populated Tafuna–Leone Plain.

--- SENTENCE 6 ---
The remineralization of anthropogenically sourced organic matter produces nitrate and dissolved inorganic carbon, which, according to previously published studies, have the potential to mobilize naturally occurring metals.

--- SENTENCE 7 ---
This study provides further evidence that nutrients and dissolved inorganic carbon, along with naturally sourced metal concentrations, become elevated along pollution gradients and show correlation with each other.

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
Across the Tafuna–Leone Plain, nitrate concentrations have a moderately positive correlation with uranium and vanadium.

--- SENTENCE 9 ---
Dissolved inorganic carbon also positively correlate with nitrate, uranium, and vanadium.

--- SENTENCE 10 ---
Similar studies elsewhere suggest that, in addition to nitrate, organic matter remineralization associated with carbonate create conditions to favor natural metal mobilization.

--- SENTENCE 11 ---
Correlation analysis results imply that, while the surveyed trace metals are likely naturally sourced, some become soluble and more mobile in the presence of anthropogenically sourced nitrate and dissolved inorganic carbon, which alters redox conditions in the aquifer.

--- SENTENCE 12 ---
**Keywords:** groundwater pollution; uranium; vanadium; nitrate; dissolved inorganic carbon; American Samoa

## 1.

--- SENTENCE 13 ---
Introduction

Groundwater is the primary source of fresh drinking water for most oceanic islands

--- SENTENCE 14 ---
On Tutuila, the main island in the U.S. territory of American Samoa, groundwater supplies about 90% of the island's fresh drinking water to approximately 55,000 people.

--- SENTENCE 15 ---
Relative to the island's less urbanized areas, the prevalence of residential units on the Tafuna–Leone Plain has the potential to cause a significant and measurable impact to groundwater quality, specifically, releases from on-site sewage disposal systems (OSDS), agricultural plantations, piggeries (small-scale pig farming operations), and a landfill [2].

--- SENTENCE 16 ---
Currently, about 70% of municipal water is pumped from wells on Tutuila's Tafuna–Leone Plain [3].

--- SENTENCE 17 ---
Regular groundwater monitoring on Tutuila is limited to the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) safe drinking water testing standards, and is performed on an annual basis by the island's sole water utility.

--- SENTENCE 18 ---
Water quality parameters in multiple wells across the Tafuna–Leone Plain fail to meet regulatory limits and several districts are under a boil water notice

--- SENTENCE 19 ---
It is therefore important to better characterize the distribution and observable effects of groundwater contamination sources to

Water 2020, 12, 2118 2 of 19

support water resource management.

--- SENTENCE 20 ---
Previous studies conducted in American Samoa have found elevated levels of groundwater nitrogen to be one of Tutuila's primary water quality concerns [2,5].

--- SENTENCE 21 ---
These studies found linkages between watershed population density and dissolved nitrogen in coastal waters, as well as links between groundwater nitrogen in the Tafuna–Leone Plain with wastewater from cesspools, where the prevalent chemical form of nitrogen found in these aquifers is nitrate  $(NO_3^{-})$  [2].

--- SENTENCE 22 ---
Previous studies elsewhere have shown that, in addition to primary contaminants released from anthropogenic sources, secondary contamination also affects water quality, as has been documented on the continental U.S. and in laboratory experiments, where an increase in  $NO_3^-$  resulted in remobilization of redox sensitive metals, such as, uranium (U) and vanadium (V)

--- SENTENCE 23 ---
As polluted groundwater travels through the subsurface,  $NO_3^-$  oxidizes reduced forms of U and V, resulting in U and V mobilization

--- SENTENCE 24 ---
In addition, the stability of dissolved trace metals is greatly enhanced by the availability of dissolved inorganic carbon (DIC) mainly occurring as carbonate ion that forms complexes with U and V (e.g., [8–10].)

--- SENTENCE 25 ---
Anthropogenically introduced organic matter alters groundwater pH and redox conditions during its microbial degradation and the resulting joint release of  $NO_3^-$  and DIC forms soluble carbonate complexes of oxidized U and V species, thus increasing U and V ion concentrations in groundwater (e.g., [8–10]).

--- SENTENCE 26 ---
Building on these observations from previously characterized groundwater  $NO_3^-$  and DIC patterns for Tutuila

--- SENTENCE 27 ---
[2], as well as U and V solubility dependencies on  $NO_3^-$  and DIC documented elsewhere [6], this study tested the hypothesis that U and V will be mobilized in the aquifer by  $NO_3^-$  and DIC.

--- SENTENCE 28 ---
The confirmation of this hypothesis would be manifested by elevated nutrient, DIC, and trace metal concentrations along pollution gradients, with a statistically significant correlation between  $NO_3^-$  and DIC as explanatory variables and U and V as predicted variables of interest.

--- SENTENCE 29 ---
To address this hypothesis, this study explores the relationships and spatial distribution of geochemical parameters of groundwater in Tutuila and looks at possible biogeochemical effects of anthropogenic organic matter and  $NO_3^-$  released into the aquifer.

--- SENTENCE 30 ---
Specifically, the objective of this study is to evaluate the role of anthropogenically sourced dissolved  $NO_3^-$  and DIC in mobilization of trace metals, such as U and V, thereby increasing their transport and bioavailability.

--- SENTENCE 31 ---
In addition, this study also provides an inventory and distribution of multiple chemical species, for example, potentially harmful metals and radon.

--- SENTENCE 32 ---
(<sup>222</sup>Rn) is a noble gas which is produced in geologic formations, including basaltic volcanic rocks [11].

--- SENTENCE 33 ---
It is the daughter product of radium-226 (<sup>226</sup>Ra) and belongs to the uranium-238 (<sup>238</sup>U) radioactive decay series [12].

--- SENTENCE 34 ---
Several parameters control <sup>222</sup>Rn distribution and transport, including mineral surface alterations and pore water content

--- SENTENCE 35 ---
This study looks at <sup>222</sup>Rn levels as a regulated contaminant in drinking water

--- SENTENCE 36 ---
[14] and also as a tracer for aquifer geology characterization.

Since fresh groundwater quantity is limited in Tutuila, it is necessary to understand the factors affecting groundwater quality.

--- SENTENCE 37 ---
These include previously documented primary contaminants, such as nutrients and pathogens, but also secondarily mobilized naturally occurring metals.

--- SENTENCE 38 ---
All of these factors can potentially be anthropogenically influenced, so to reduce the variables that may affect groundwater geochemistry, we focus on the aquifer that contains the most anthropogenic activity.

--- SENTENCE 39 ---
This work provides information water resource managers can use for decision support by developing a better understanding of how human activities not only directly contribute contaminants to water supplies, but also how these constituents can modify the chemical conditions in basaltic island aquifers.

Materials and Methods

## 2.1.

--- SENTENCE 41 ---
Site Location and Sampling

Tutuila is a small (137 km $^2$ ) volcanic island located in the South Pacific (latitude:  $14^{\circ}19'55.24''$  S, longitude:  $170^{\circ}45'23.41''$  W) (Figure 1).

--- SENTENCE 42 ---
The island is constructed of two geologically distinct regions [15].

--- SENTENCE 43 ---
The older region is a Pleistocene age complex of volcanic shields now deeply eroded into a rugged, mountainous edifice, while Holocene age rejuvenated volcanic eruptions later built the relatively flat

Water 2020, 12, 2118 3 of 19

Tafuna–Leone Plain on the shields' southwestern flank, which now supports much of the island's population and infrastructure

--- SENTENCE 44 ---
The Tafuna–Leone Plain (~30 km²) is almost entirely covered with low-density residential development, with small areas of intense development that are located downgradient of production wells.

For this study, groundwater samples were collected from fifty locations, including six coastal springs, five upland springs, and thirty-nine production wells throughout Tutuila from 5–19 August 2013 (Figure 1).

--- SENTENCE 50 ---
Upland springs and coastal spring samples were collected with a one-meter stainless steel push-point sampler (MHE Products) connected to a peristaltic pump that captured groundwater before it mixed with ocean water.

--- SENTENCE 51 ---
Production well samples were collected from wellhead ports, which were located upstream of in-line chlorination.

--- SENTENCE 52 ---
Of the samples assessed in this study, well depths ranged from 30 to 90 m below ground elevation and captured groundwater at the surface of the aquifer that is more susceptible to urban pollution.

--- SENTENCE 53 ---
All of the samples were filtered during sample collection with 0.45  $\mu$ m hydrophilic polyethersulfone capsule filters.

--- SENTENCE 54 ---
Nutrient, metal, ion, and DIC samples were collected in acid-washed, high-density polyethylene (HDPE) bottles and refrigerated immediately.

--- SENTENCE 55 ---
Water for <sup>222</sup>Rn analysis was collected in 250 mL glass bottles without head space.

--- SENTENCE 56 ---
To ensure sample measurements were not impacted by anomalous events caused by weather, natural disasters, etc., four production wells (ILI-84, MMI-89, MOA-93, TAF-33) were each sampled nine times over the span of one year.

--- SENTENCE 57 ---
These sample sites were located in different villages and regions (Figure 2), thus were spatially distributed across the Tafuna–Leone Plain.

--- SENTENCE 63 ---
Chemical Analysis

Groundwater samples were analyzed for dissolved inorganic nutrients (nitrate ( $NO_3^-$ ), nitrite ( $NO_2^-$ ), inorganic phosphorus (P), silicate ( $SiO_4^{\ 4}$ ), ammonium ( $NH_4^+$ ), total N (TN), and total P (TP)) using a Seal Analytical AA3<sup>®</sup> at the University of Hawai'i SOEST Laboratory for Analytical Biogeochemistry (S-LAB).

--- SENTENCE 64 ---
Although this paper discusses the  $NO_3^-$ ,  $NO_2^-$ , and TN analysis, all other nutrient results are included in Table S1 for reference.

--- SENTENCE 65 ---
The diazo reaction method is used to analyze  $NO_3^-$  and  $NO_2^-$  as detailed in Armstrong et al. (1967)

--- SENTENCE 66 ---
[16] and Grasshoff et al. (1983)

--- SENTENCE 67 ---
With a copper–cadmium redactor column, the  $NO_3^-$  is reduced to  $NO_2^-$ , which then reacts with sulfanilamide to form a diazo compound.

--- SENTENCE 68 ---
The compound is coupled with N-1-naphthylethylene diamine dihydrochloride to create a purple azo dye, from which the concentration is colorimetrically determined at 550 nm.

--- SENTENCE 69 ---
TN is determined using an on-line UV digestor according to the procedure established by the University of Hamburg.

--- SENTENCE 70 ---
Persulfate oxidizes organic and inorganic nitrogen compounds to  $NO_3^-$ , which is then reduced to  $NO_2^-$  in a cadmium column.

--- SENTENCE 71 ---
The sulfanilamide/NEDD reaction is used to determine the concentration with colorimetric detection at 520 nm.

--- SENTENCE 72 ---
Concentrations of  $NO_3^-$  and  $NO_2^-$  were not determined for five of the samples (ILI-76, MES-22, PAV-178, TAF-81, VATI-CSP-21).

--- SENTENCE 73 ---
These samples were therefore not considered when reporting geochemical ranges.

--- SENTENCE 74 ---
For a suite of dissolved trace metal analysis, including uranium (U), vanadium (V), chromium (Cr), lead (Pb), nickel (Ni), and copper (Cu), water was filtered in the field and acidified in a clean laboratory to pH  $\sim$ 1.8 using ultrapure 6 M HCl (Seastar Baseline) to a final acid concentration of 0.024 M. Acidified samples were analyzed similarly to the procedure in Shiller (2003)

--- SENTENCE 75 ---
Briefly, prior to analysis, samples were slightly diluted (1.5×) by addition of ultrapure nitric acid (Seastar Baseline) containing known amounts of In, Sc, and Th as internal standards.

--- SENTENCE 76 ---
The diluted samples were approximately 0.16 M in nitric acid and 2 ppm in the internal standards.

--- SENTENCE 77 ---
Trace elements were then determined using a sector-field inductively coupled plasma—mass spectrometer (ICP–MS, Thermo-Fisher Element 2) at the University of Southern Mississippi Center for Trace Analysis (CETA).

--- SENTENCE 78 ---
A low-flow (100  $\mu$ L/min)

*Water* **2020**, *12*, 2118 5 of 19

self-aspirating nebulizer (Elemental Scientific: Omaha, NE, USA) and Teflon spray chamber were utilized.

--- SENTENCE 79 ---
Cs, Re, Pb, and U were determined in low resolution (as masses 133, 187, 208, and 238, respectively) and the other elements were determined in medium resolution (51 V, 52 Cr, 55 Mn, 56 Fe, 60 Ni, 63 Cu, 66 Zn, 85 Mo, 88 Sr, and 137 Ba).

--- SENTENCE 80 ---
Calibration was done with external standards made in 0.16 M ultrapure nitric acid.

--- SENTENCE 81 ---
The standards were independently checked against U.S. Geological Survey standard reference waters and an in-house consistency standard was also measured as a check on long-term stability of the standards.

--- SENTENCE 82 ---
In was used for instrumental drift correction and sensitivity check, with the other internal standards serving as a check on the In.

--- SENTENCE 83 ---
Sample acidification and other preparations for analysis were carried out in a laminar-flow clean bench.

--- SENTENCE 84 ---
Since the Tafuna–Leone Plain is the most heavily urbanized location on Tutuila

--- SENTENCE 85 ---
[\[2\]](#page-15-1), trace metal analysis was refined to this area for deeper investigation.

--- SENTENCE 86 ---
Thus, 22 of the 50 sample sites were analyzed for trace metals (Figure [2\)](#page-3-0).

--- SENTENCE 87 ---
For additional water quality context, each sample was analyzed for concentrations of major ions using a Dionex ICS-1100s (IC) ion chromatography system at the University of Hawai'i Water Resources Research Center Analytical Laboratory.

--- SENTENCE 88 ---
Results from major ion analyses are not discussed in this paper, but are included in Table S1 for reference.

--- SENTENCE 89 ---
All DIC samples were analyzed using a Shimadzu TOC-V analyzer at the University of Hawai'i Water Resources Research Center Analytical Laboratory.

--- SENTENCE 90 ---
All δ <sup>13</sup>C of DIC samples were analyzed using a Delta V Isotope Ratio Mass Spectrometer at the Skidaway Institute Scientific Stable Isotope Laboratory at the University of Georgia.

--- SENTENCE 91 ---
All <sup>222</sup>Rn samples were measured with a RAD-H2O instrument (Durridge, Inc.) in the field on the day of collection and were corrected for radioactive decay to the time of sample collection.

--- SENTENCE 92 ---
At least 10% of all nutrient and major ion samples were run as duplicates and used to assess analytical error.

--- SENTENCE 93 ---
Analytical uncertainties were computed using the standard error of the line of best fit of duplicate samples (Table [1](#page-6-0) and Table S1).

--- SENTENCE 94 ---
Duplicate trace metal samples were not analyzed.

--- SENTENCE 95 ---
Human Impact Levels and Statistical Analysis*

Due to the complexities in land use patterns and groundwater flow, the degree of impact from human land use on any given point is difficult to assess.

--- SENTENCE 96 ---
However, we were able to use previously determined groundwater flow paths [\[2,](#page-15-1)[15\]](#page-16-11) combined with known locations of development

--- SENTENCE 97 ---
[\[2\]](#page-15-1) to bin the wells into categories of being upgradient or downgradient of human development.

--- SENTENCE 98 ---
To accomplish this, we delineated the geologic contact between the young Tafuna–Leone Plain and the old volcanic mountains, and identified the wells that were more than 300 m downgradient from the geologic contact as human-impacted wells.

--- SENTENCE 99 ---
Wells located less than 300 m downgradient from the contact were identified as less-impacted wells (marked in Table

--- SENTENCE 100 ---
While this distance of 300 m is somewhat arbitrary, it provided the best approximation of the delineation between impacted and less impacted wells that clearly have different geochemical signatures based upon their proximity to nonpoint pollution sources

--- SENTENCE 101 ---
Two wells in particular (MOA-168 and MOA-169) are located in an undeveloped area of the Tafuna–Leone Plain with no upgradient human development (Figure [2\)](#page-3-0).

--- SENTENCE 102 ---
Additionally, one of the upland spring samples, PAV-2R, was collected within a stream very near the spring source, located on a hill.

--- SENTENCE 103 ---
Therefore, despite being in the middle of the plain in map view (Figure [2\)](#page-3-0), the sample location is elevated from surrounding human development.

--- SENTENCE 104 ---
Three samples (ASI-1S, FALU-179, FASA-143) were collected north of the Tafuna–Leone Plain, and are included in the statistical analysis, representing pristine samples that are not heavily impacted by anthropogenic sources.

--- SENTENCE 105 ---
All *p*-values were determined with a Kruskal–Wallis H-test.

--- SENTENCE 106 ---
Radon*

Groundwater <sup>222</sup>Rn concentrations range from 42 to 26,000 Bq/m<sup>3</sup> across Tutuila (Table S1).

--- SENTENCE 107 ---
The spatial distribution of <sup>222</sup>Rn generally follows the geology of the island, where groundwater from the older volcanic rocks and alluvium contained higher <sup>222</sup>Rn values (*n*=21, average 10,636±7020 Bq/m<sup>3</sup> ,

Water 2020, 12, 2118 6 of 19

median 11,137 Bq/m³) and groundwater from the younger Leone Volcanics contained lower  $^{222}$ Rn values (n = 24, average  $2932 \pm 4075$  Bq/m³, median 1411 Bq/m³)

--- SENTENCE 108 ---
Currently, a drinking water standard for  $^{222}$ Rn is not federally enforced.

--- SENTENCE 109 ---
The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), however, recommends that  $^{222}$ Rn levels do not exceed 148,000 Bq/m³ (4000 pCi/L) in drinking water

--- SENTENCE 110 ---
All measured values in this study were at least an order of magnitude below the EPA recommendation.

**Figure 3.**

Spatial distribution of <sup>222</sup>Rn concentrations measured from groundwater in Tutuila, American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 114 ---
The Olomoana Volcanics, Pago Volcanics, and Taputapu Volcanics formed during the Pliocene to earliest Pleistocene Epoch while the Leone Volcanics and sedimentary rocks formed during the recent Quaternary Period

--- SENTENCE 115 ---
This relationship between <sup>222</sup>Rn concentrations and geology was expected since <sup>222</sup>Rn concentrations increase as groundwater residence times increase until steady-state concentrations are reached within about 20 days [13].

--- SENTENCE 116 ---
As the host rock surface area increases, more <sup>238</sup>U and <sup>226</sup>Ra become exposed at the mineral–water interface, thus enabling <sup>222</sup>Rn to dissolve into the groundwater system [20].

--- SENTENCE 117 ---
Measured <sup>222</sup>Rn activities therefore reflect the geology of the groundwater flow over the last 20 days before sampling.

--- SENTENCE 118 ---
A distinct difference was detected between <sup>222</sup>Rn concentrations in groundwater of the Tafuna–Leone Plain and groundwater sampled throughout the rest of Tutuila (Figure 3).

--- SENTENCE 119 ---
This is consistent with the geochemical differences as previously described by [21] between the established geologic units [22].

--- SENTENCE 120 ---
The <sup>222</sup>Rn chemistry indicates that samples were collected from two geochemically distinctive aquifer systems.

#### 4.

--- SENTENCE 219 ---
Remobilization of U and V

In the Tafuna–Leone Plain, U concentrations display a positive correlation with  $NO_3^-$  levels and follow a spatial gradient of increasing concentrations toward the coastline and with urbanization (Figure 5a,c).

--- SENTENCE 220 ---
Uranium is a redox-sensitive trace metal, commonly weathered from igneous parent rocks [6,30].

--- SENTENCE 221 ---
The mobility of U is primarily dependent on its oxidation state, where oxidized U(VI) is generally more soluble than reduced U(IV)

--- SENTENCE 222 ---
With an increasing amount of oxidants present in the groundwater system, uraninite (UO<sub>2</sub>) will dissolve from minerals to aqueous forms of U [32].

--- SENTENCE 223 ---
For example,  $NO_3^-$  can oxidize  $UO_2$  following the reaction:

$$UO_2 + NO_3^- + 2H^+ \rightarrow UO_2^{2+} + NO_2^- + H_2O_7$$
 (1)

therefore being reduced to  $NO_2^-$  while  $UO_2$  becomes a uranyl cation ( $UO_2^{2+}$ )

--- SENTENCE 224 ---
N(III) contained within  $NO_2^-$  has a relatively unstable oxidation state

--- SENTENCE 225 ---
Thus,  $NO_2^-$  is typically unstable in oxic groundwater systems and is readily oxidized to  $NO_3^-$

--- SENTENCE 226 ---
Further, during the denitrification process,  $NO_2^-$  acts as an electron acceptor while oxidizing organic matter within the groundwater and is thus reduced to  $N_2O$  or  $N_2$  [36].

--- SENTENCE 227 ---
As such, despite the proposed production described by Equation (1), changes in groundwater  $NO_2^-$  concentrations are undetectable and <0.05% of TN is comprised of  $NO_2^-$  in the current study.

--- SENTENCE 228 ---
Water 2020, 12, 2118

In its oxidized state, U(VI) can be transported as  $UO_2^{2+}$  when exposed to oxic–alkaline groundwater conditions [37].

--- SENTENCE 229 ---
Anoxic groundwater conditions, on the other hand, inhibit U mobility [38,39].

--- SENTENCE 230 ---
The groundwater samples at the 22 analyzed sites had a dissolved oxygen (DO) content >64% (average 80 ± 9%), therefore exhibiting reasonably oxic conditions (Table S1).

--- SENTENCE 231 ---
Although DO is clearly present in this groundwater system, these values are relatively low compared to a fully oxygen-saturated state of groundwater observed in the upstream pristine locations and at other tropical, volcanic aquifers, where mean DO content typically averages >90% [40].

--- SENTENCE 232 ---
Despite the relatively high DO content measured in the Tafuna–Leone Plain, it has been shown in other studies that DO is not as effective at oxidizing U(IV) as  $NO_3^-$  [6,31].

--- SENTENCE 233 ---
Indeed we find no statistically significant correlation between DO and U ( $r^2 = 0.0006$ ).

--- SENTENCE 234 ---
There are numerous complexes formed around UO<sub>2</sub><sup>2+</sup>, which are typically more soluble and mobile than covalently bonded U(IV)

--- SENTENCE 235 ---
With a positive correlation between U and  $NO_3^-$  ( $r^2 = 0.25$ ,  $p \le 0.01$ ) (Figure 4a), our data suggest that the previously reported mechanism of U mobilization by NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> introduced into the groundwater system may be taking place.

--- SENTENCE 236 ---
The solubility is further enhanced by the presence of DIC, as suggested by Cumberland et al.

--- SENTENCE 237 ---
[10] and described in Section 4.2.

--- SENTENCE 238 ---
Within the Tafuna–Leone Plain, groundwater generally flows from the mountains to the coast

--- SENTENCE 239 ---
The Tafuna–Leone Plain contains 32% of Tutuila's total population

--- SENTENCE 240 ---
[41] and 2800 OSDS units [3].

--- SENTENCE 241 ---
As groundwater contaminant plumes travel downgradient from the pollution sources, NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>, acting as an oxidant, may be interacting with U by accepting electrons from the immobile U(IV), and oxidizing U to its mobile U(VI) form [6,7].

--- SENTENCE 242 ---
There is even the potential for NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> to remobilize U that has been previously bioreduced [33].

--- SENTENCE 243 ---
Despite the fact that NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> is being consumed during this U oxidation process, a decrease in NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> concentration would not be significant due to the thousand-fold difference between the observed U (nM) and NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> (µM) concentrations.

--- SENTENCE 244 ---
A conversion of 3 nmol/L of U (the highest observed in this study) would result in a <0.01% decrease in NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> concentration and build-up of nmol/L levels of NO<sub>2</sub><sup>-</sup> that are below our detection limits.

--- SENTENCE 245 ---
As such, low to undetectable levels of NO<sub>2</sub><sup>-</sup> and persistently elevated NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> levels despite its consumption during the proposed U oxidation process are consistent with our hypothesis of the proposed oxidative pathway.

--- SENTENCE 246 ---
Similar results were found by Nolan et al.

--- SENTENCE 247 ---
[6] in the High Plains and Central Valley aquifers in the United States, where a strong positive correlation ( $r \ge 0.75$ ) between NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> and U was found.

--- SENTENCE 248 ---
Laboratory experiments conducted by Moon et al.

--- SENTENCE 249 ---
[31] documented a similar relationship where U was reoxidized when in contact with NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>.

--- SENTENCE 250 ---
In these laboratory experiments, NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> even served as a more efficient oxidant than O2, regardless of O2 being a more thermodynamically favorable oxidizer [31].

--- SENTENCE 251 ---
[31] determined that the free energy ( $\Delta G^{"}$ ) of U(IV) oxidation with O<sub>2</sub> and NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> as oxidants was -50.4 and -44.2 kJ/mol of electrons, respectively.

--- SENTENCE 252 ---
Despite this, 1.7 times more U reoxidized when in contact with  $NO_3^-$  (264 µmol of U) relative to  $O_2$  (155 µmol of U)

--- SENTENCE 253 ---
They found that this inconsistency was a result of the breakthrough characteristics of O<sub>2</sub> and NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>, where NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> had a faster breakthrough time (the time when at least 50% of the introduced NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> was detected in the sample) of 12 h compared to the O<sub>2</sub> breakthrough time of 33 days [31].

--- SENTENCE 254 ---
This suggests that O<sub>2</sub> quickly reacts with other species, such as iron (Fe<sup>3+</sup>) and sulfides, before its advancing front comes in contact with U [31].

--- SENTENCE 255 ---
In comparison, NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> does not rapidly react with other species, therefore it is able to more readily oxidize U [31].

--- SENTENCE 256 ---
Additionally, other laboratory experiments suggest that dissimilatory NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> reduction intermediates (nitrite, nitrous oxide, nitric oxide) can abiotically oxidize U(IV)

--- SENTENCE 257 ---
In this study, we found minimal levels of NO<sub>2</sub><sup>-</sup>, so its role in U oxidation and correlation with U could not be evaluated.

--- SENTENCE 258 ---
Finally, numerical model simulations conducted by van Berk et al.

--- SENTENCE 259 ---
[32] confirmed their hypothesis that the addition of NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> (derived from fertilizers) shifted groundwater redox conditions to an oxidizing environment, thus mobilizing U.

Vanadium is also a redox-sensitive trace metal that is commonly found with U in deposits [10].

--- SENTENCE 260 ---
The mobility of V is governed by its oxidation state (V(III), V(IV), V(V)) under various redox and pH conditions [42].

--- SENTENCE 261 ---
It is generally accepted that V is most mobile when it is most oxidized as V(V) and becomes less mobile when in its V(IV) or lower state

--- SENTENCE 262 ---
In natural waters with conditions applicable to U, V is most commonly found as  $H_2VO_4^{-}$ ,  $HVO_4^{2-}$ , and  $VO_2^{2+}$

--- SENTENCE 263 ---
The vanadate

Water 2020, 12, 2118 12 of 19

oxyanions  $H_2VO_4^-$  and  $HVO_4^{2-}$  are not as easily adsorbed onto solid particles while the oxycation  $VO^{2+}$  is much more easily adsorbed [42,45].

--- SENTENCE 264 ---
Vanadium is thus more soluble when in oxidizing waters [45].

--- SENTENCE 265 ---
Mobile V(V) is most stable in oxic groundwater conditions, where there is an abundant amount of  $O_2$  present to act as an electron acceptor [9,42].

--- SENTENCE 266 ---
Following the simplified equation:

$$4V + 5O_2 \rightarrow 2V_2O_5,$$
 (2)

V(IV) can be oxidized to V(V) using  $O_2$  as the redox reagent [46].

--- SENTENCE 267 ---
Based on these thermodynamic characteristics, V may also be mobilized by the presence of excess  $NO_3^-$  in groundwater, similarly to U.

--- SENTENCE 268 ---
When in the oxidized state, V(V) is also able to combine with U(VI)

--- SENTENCE 269 ---
[47] found a positive correlation between V and  $NO_3^-$  in groundwater from the Gulf Coast aquifer in Texas.

--- SENTENCE 270 ---
The V samples had a positive correlation with arsenic, which may suggest a geologic source, but both V and  $NO_3^-$  displayed a negative correlation with well depth [47].

--- SENTENCE 271 ---
Since the highest V and  $NO_3^-$  concentrations were measured at the shallowest depths, they suggest that these samples are affected by land surface sources [47].

--- SENTENCE 272 ---
Thus, the introduction of anthropogenically elevated  $NO_3^-$  has the potential to increase groundwater concentrations of naturally sourced metals that, in high concentrations, may pose a health risk to humans.

Besides acting alone as an electron acceptor for U and V, NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> can also correlate with elevated levels of DIC because of their common source of organic matter remineralization (Figure 6).

--- SENTENCE 325 ---
Secondly, Pb could have been introduced to the aquifer as lead-based antiknock gasoline additives (tetraethyl lead or tetramethyl lead) in the past, which were widely used across the United States from the 1920s to 1970s

--- SENTENCE 326 ---
As an example of this, Polidoro et al.

--- SENTENCE 327 ---
[61] detected a high concentration of Pb in sediments from Utulei, which is located just north of Fagaalu.

--- SENTENCE 328 ---
It was determined that this elevated Pb levels, which was double that of other samples, was likely the result of leaking oil storage tanks

--- SENTENCE 329 ---
To help determine if Pb is naturally or anthropogenically sourced, other trace metal concentrations (Cu, Cr, and Ni) were evaluated based on human impact levels.

--- SENTENCE 330 ---
Eight samples collected from areas of low human impact, where little to no urbanization occurs upstream, have average Pb, Cu, Cr, and Ni concentrations of  $2.4 \pm 1.4$  nmol/L,  $59.6 \pm 42.0$  nmol/L,  $12.4 \pm 6.5$  nmol/L, and  $2.4 \pm 1.3$  nmol/L,

*Water* **2020**, *12*, 2118 15 of 19

respectively.

--- SENTENCE 331 ---
Most of these values are lower than the seven samples collected from obviously human impacted areas: 3.9 ± 2.6 nmol/L (Pb), 36.2 ± 20.4 nmol/L (Cu), 27.5 ± 15.6 nmol/L (Cr), 10.5 ± 11.8 nmol/L (Ni).

--- SENTENCE 332 ---
The only metal with a higher averaged concentration in the less-impacted areas compared to populated areas is Cu.

--- SENTENCE 333 ---
There is a statistically significant difference between less-impacted and human-impacted wells of Cr and Ni concentrations (*p* = 0.04 and *p* = 0.005, respectively), but there is no statistically significant difference between the Pb and Cu concentrations measured in less-impacted wells compared to human-impacted wells.

--- SENTENCE 334 ---
This spatial distribution suggests that urbanization slightly affects the heavy metal concentrations, but not drastically enough where they surpass health standards.

--- SENTENCE 335 ---
Additionally, all of the detected Cu, Cr, and Ni concentrations are lower than various health standards (Figure [9\)](#page-13-0).

--- SENTENCE 336 ---
The positive correlation between low Cr and Ni concentrations (Figure [7\)](#page-9-0) also suggests a natural source of trace metals.

--- SENTENCE 337 ---
Thus, we assume that trace metals detected in the Tafuna–Leone Plain are mostly naturally sourced with a few exceptions of anthropogenically sourced Pb, Cr, and Ni.

## *4.4.

--- SENTENCE 338 ---
Comparison of Trace Metal Chemistry in Similar Environments*

Overall, trace metal concentrations found across the Tafuna–Leone Plain are lower than U.S. EPA standards and are lower than trace metal concentrations found in groundwater of other Pacific islands.

--- SENTENCE 339 ---
[\[62\]](#page-18-14) found U concentrations ranging from <1 to 4 nmol/L and V concentrations ranging from 698 to 2176 nmol/L in Kona, Hawai'i Island.

--- SENTENCE 340 ---
[\[63\]](#page-18-15) measured groundwater discharge from Kahekili Beach Park, Maui, a wastewater affected site, and found average U and V concentrations of 0.8 nmol/L and 291 nmol/L, respectively, which are slightly higher than this study's U and V averages of 0.7 nmol/L and 286 nmol/L. Additionally, Swarzenski et al.

--- SENTENCE 341 ---
[\[5\]](#page-16-2) also found an average Cr concentration of 82.8 nmol/L, which is higher than this study's average Cr concentration of 18.6 nmol/L.

--- SENTENCE 342 ---
As part of the National Water-Quality Assessment Program (NAWQA), the U.S. Geological Survey analyzed groundwater samples for trace metals from different aquifer groups across the United States

--- SENTENCE 343 ---
The eight aquifer groups were categorized as unconsolidated sand and gravel (USG), glacial unconsolidated sand and gravel (GLA), semiconsolidated sand (SCS), sandstone (SAN), sandstone and carbonate rock (SCR), carbonate rock (CAR), basaltic and other volcanic rock (BAV), and crystalline rock (CRL).

--- SENTENCE 344 ---
Less than 1% of samples exceeded Cu and Cr human-health benchmarks (HHBs) from all aquifer groups, and within the BAV group, none of the trace metals discussed in this paper exceeded HHBs in at least 1% of samples

--- SENTENCE 345 ---
The only four trace metals that exceeded HHBs in the BAV group were Ar, Fe, Mg, and Rn

--- SENTENCE 346 ---
In comparison, the two aquifer groups who had the largest percent of samples exceed HHBs were the USG and GLA groups

--- SENTENCE 347 ---
Climate also greatly influenced trace metal concentrations, where Cu and Ni, as well as trace metals that form oxyanions (including U, V, Cr), had higher concentrations in dry regions compared to humid regions

--- SENTENCE 348 ---
Additionally, older groundwater (pre-1953) generally had more oxyanion-forming trace metals than young groundwater

--- SENTENCE 349 ---
The only exception here is that in humid regions, U concentrations were higher in young groundwater, which is attributed to the fact that old groundwater in humid regions typically have lower dissolved oxygen concentrations, which can prevent U mobility

--- SENTENCE 350 ---
The Tafuna–Leone Plain would be best classified as a BAV aquifer in a humid climate.

--- SENTENCE 351 ---
Similar to the results reported from well samples in BAV aquifers across the United States, trace metal levels measured in Tafuna–Leone samples did not exceed HHBs.

--- SENTENCE 352 ---
Additionally, Tafuna–Leone groundwater with lower <sup>222</sup>Rn concentrations, which we attributed to mineral U content and weathering, also generally had higher U concentrations.

--- SENTENCE 353 ---
The negative correlation between U and <sup>222</sup>Rn (*r <sup>2</sup>* = −0.41) is comparable to the trends found in other humid BAV aquifer regions.

--- SENTENCE 354 ---
Conclusions**

Land use and anthropogenic activities can impact groundwater pH and redox conditions, which in turn can affect the solubility and mobility of naturally-produced trace metals in groundwater aquifer systems

--- SENTENCE 355 ---
Positive correlations were found between trace metals, nutrients, and DIC from Water 2020, 12, 2118 16 of 19

groundwater samples collected within the Tafuna–Leone Plain, Tutuila, American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 356 ---
The  $\delta^{13}C_{DIC}$  signatures suggest that DIC is derived from remineralization of organic matter, albeit is most likely partially derived from natural sources and partially from pollution.

--- SENTENCE 357 ---
These geochemical relationships suggest that anthropogenically-sourced organic matter and nutrients alter the groundwater redox conditions, therefore mobilizing naturally occurring trace metals.

--- SENTENCE 358 ---
Major findings show:

- 1.

--- SENTENCE 359 ---
Elevated levels of anthropogenically-sourced NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> are positively correlated with U and V concentrations.

--- SENTENCE 360 ---
The NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> likely acts as an electron acceptor, oxidizing U and V, making them soluble and mobile in the groundwater.
- 2.

--- SENTENCE 361 ---
Elevated DIC concentrations in the Tafuna–Leone Plain are most likely a result of anthropogenic organic matter and are positively correlated with NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>, U, and V. DIC forms soluble complexes with U and V, thus mobilizing both in the groundwater.

--- SENTENCE 362 ---
Observed groundwater concentrations of Pb, Cu, Cr, and Ni were all relatively low and positively correlated with each other.

--- SENTENCE 363 ---
This indicates that trace metals, including U and V, in sampled groundwaters are assumed to be sourced from natural dissolution of aquifer materials rather than anthropogenically sourced.

--- SENTENCE 364 ---
This further justifies the hypothesis that the increased U and V concentrations are due to remobilization initiated by NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> and DIC.

--- SENTENCE 365 ---
These results support our hypothesis that organic matter and nutrients introduced through anthropogenic activities can induce secondary contamination, therefore affecting the groundwater quality by remobilizing trace metals such as U and V. Further understanding of Tutuila's groundwater system is crucial as the island's water quality continues to be affected by population increases.

--- SENTENCE 366 ---
Trace metal consumption can negatively impact the health of residents.

--- SENTENCE 367 ---
It is therefore important to understand how anthropogenic pollution can influence trace metal solubilization in groundwater, even though U and V concentrations in American Samoa are currently lower than EPA limits and generally lower than concentrations detected in the groundwater of other Pacific Islands.
