In October, 1979 ten fish aggregation devices (FAD's) were deployed in the waters surrounding American Samoa under a program funded by the National Marine Fisheries Service (NMFS) and the Pacific Tuna Development Foundation (PTDF).

--- SENTENCE 6 ---
Because of the success of this program as well as other FAD projects in Hawaii, Western Samoa, etc., the project described herein was implemented.

--- SENTENCE 7 ---
Its objectives were 1) to re-establish six aggregation devices in the waters around Tutuila Island; 2) to "beef up" the materials from which the FAD's were constructed, thereby giving them life expectancies of five to ten years; and 3) to reap the benefits accruing from the system.

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
Tage 2...

Most of the FAD's deployed by this program were designed and prefabricated by Ala-Pac, Inc. of Seattle, Washington which was contracted to provide eight float.

--- SENTENCE 9 ---
(three toroid buoys and five spar buoys) and six complete mooring systems.

--- SENTENCE 10 ---
The foam-filled fiberglass floats were 3 ft diameter toroids (doughnut-shaped) and 3 ft by 3 ft cylinders with aluminium cores.

--- SENTENCE 11 ---
The latter were donated by NMFS.

--- SENTENCE 12 ---
A tower with a light and radar reflector was mounted on each float.

--- SENTENCE 13 ---
Moorings consisted of a 60 ft chain float bridle, a stainless steel ball-bearing swivel, several lengths of 1 inch hard-lay polypropylene line connected with thimbles and shackles, a 60 ft anchor chain and an anchor constructed from railroad car wheels or concrete.

--- SENTENCE 14 ---
An amendment to project F-4-D allowed the purchase of an additional \$25,000 worth of materials which were used primarily to purchase more line and hardware for mooring systems.

--- SENTENCE 15 ---
Additionally FAD's were fabricated from materials remaining from the NMFS/PTDF project, buoys donated by the Marine Railway, salvaged floats and longline from a derelict fishing vessel, and line and hardware purchased with local (American Samoa Government) funds.

--- SENTENCE 16 ---
The report which follows is not limited to the FAD's purchased with U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) funds.

--- SENTENCE 17 ---
Rather, it covers all FAD's deployed in American Samoa during the report period.

## FAD Loss

The factors responsible for the loss of the FAD's described above are summarized in Table I.

--- SENTENCE 99 ---
The major cause appears to be related to the mooring line floating on or near the surface where it was cut by a boat keel or propellor.

--- SENTENCE 100 ---
The problem could be solved by the elimination of slack or scope from the mooring, the use of negatively buoyant line, or the use of a counterweight to sink the line.

--- SENTENCE 101 ---
Electrolysis was the second most important cause of failure.

--- SENTENCE 102 ---
Care should be taken to ensure that dissimilar metals

are not allowed in contact with one another.

--- SENTENCE 103 ---
If they must be used, the more active metal should be protected with a sacrificial anode.

--- SENTENCE 104 ---
The mooring system recommended by the South Pacific Commission study will eliminate most of the causes of failure and should be used if possible for all future FAD deployments.

--- SENTENCE 105 ---
The upper section consists of a negatively buoyant length of nylon line of insufficient length to reach the bottom where it would be subject to abrasion.

--- SENTENCE 106 ---
The lower section consists of a length of buoyant polypropylene line which has sufficient buoyancy to lift at least ten feet of anchor chain off the bottom but which is not long enough to reach the surface where it could be fouled or cut by a boat.

--- SENTENCE 107 ---
Lines will be skillfully and properly spliced to eliminate the need for metal connectors subject to electrolysis and corrosion.

## FAD Benefits

An assessment of the benefits of the FAD program in terms of increased catches, decreased fuel consumption, the likelihood of a pleasing recreational experience, etc, was not an objective of the present project.

--- SENTENCE 108 ---
Some data are available, however, which indicate increased fishing success in the vicinity of FAD's around Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 109 ---
One source of data is Job III-I of Project F-2-R and also funded by USFWS.

--- SENTENCE 110 ---
The objective of this job is to measure long-term trends in the pelagic sport fish resource through standardized monthly trolling surveys.

--- SENTENCE 111 ---
Portions of the surveys, which have been continuous since 1975, are periodically conducted in the vicinities of FAD's.

--- SENTENCE 112 ---
A total of 13.25 hours of survey effort has been expended around FAD's B and C since 1981 and 125.75 hours have been expended elsewhere.

--- SENTENCE 113 ---
A total of 374 lbs of fish were caught near the FAD's for an average catch of 28.2 lbs/hr.

--- SENTENCE 114 ---
A total of 1977 lbs were caught elsewhere for an average of 15.7 lbs/hr.

--- SENTENCE 115 ---
Catch rate in the vicinity of FAD's was, thus, almost twice as great as in other areas.

--- SENTENCE 116 ---
The FAD

catches were dominated by blue marlin, yellowfin and dolphin.

--- SENTENCE 117 ---
Another source of trolling data is Job III-2 of project F-2-R.

--- SENTENCE 118 ---
The major objective of this job is to test three FAD drape configurations to determine which is the most attractive to pelagic gamefish.

--- SENTENCE 119 ---
A total of 13.2 hrs of trolling effort was expended around FAD's B and C resulting in a catch of 216 lbs.

--- SENTENCE 120 ---
The catch rate was, therefore, 16.4 lbs/hr with yellowfin and dolphin the dominant species.

--- SENTENCE 121 ---
Catch rate was considerably less than that experienced around the FAD's during the monthly trolling surveys described under Job III-1 above.

--- SENTENCE 122 ---
This may be due to the fact that fewer lines and smaller lures were used during the survey directed at the FAD's.

--- SENTENCE 123 ---
Commercial and recreational trollers are a third source of data.

--- SENTENCE 124 ---
Several of the most active fishermen were asked to provide the following for their effort within a one-half mile radius of each FAD: date, time began and time stopped fishing, and number and weight of catch by species.

--- SENTENCE 125 ---
The data from 36 visits to FAD's A, B and C made between April 2 and August 17, 1983 are summarized in Table II.

--- SENTENCE 126 ---
They indicate an average catch of 3.2 fish weighing 25.6 lbs per hour and 1.9 fish and 15.1 lbs per visit.

--- SENTENCE 127 ---
Dolphin and skipjack were the most commonly caught species.

--- SENTENCE 128 ---
The 545 lbs caught during these 36 visits is an unknown, but probably small, percentage of the total FAD catch landed during the survey period.

--- SENTENCE 129 ---
In addition to trolling surveys, Job III-2 also involved SCUBA diving to make visual observations of the fishes attracted to FAD's.

--- SENTENCE 130 ---
Observations were generally made by two divers for about 15 minute periods.

--- SENTENCE 131 ---
They were conducted to depths of about 60 ft at the bottom of the drape and/or on the mooring line at the swivel between the chain float bridle and the polypropylene line.

--- SENTENCE 132 ---
Between September 9, 1981 and June 10, 1983, 12 dives were made on FAD B and 12 dives on FAD C.

--- SENTENCE 133 ---
Amongst the smaller fishes, the dominant species observed were young bigeyed trevally (Caranx sexfasciatus = lupotā), bigeye scad (Selar crumenophthalmus = atule), and rainbow runner (Elegatis bipin-
