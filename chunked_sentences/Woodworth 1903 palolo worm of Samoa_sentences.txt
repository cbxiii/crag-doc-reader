I arrived at Apia on October 20 and was fortunate in meeting Dr. Kramer who placed at my disposal the notes he had collected during three years in the islands.

--- SENTENCE 42 ---
I nmade my headquarters in the village of Falelatai on the South side of Upolu a little to the eastward of Samatau where Friedlaender obtained his material.

--- SENTENCE 43 ---
After several days of fruitless search on the reef between Samatatu and Falelatai my native friends took me to a shallow bay called Fagaioftu about two miles east of our village.

--- SENTENCE 44 ---
The bay lies between two small promontories and is about one quarter of a mile wide, the distance from the shore to the edge of the fringing reef, which fills the bay, is not more than i5o meters.

--- SENTENCE 45 ---
The place is so shallow that at low tide one can wade from the shore to the edge of the reef.

--- SENTENCE 46 ---
The reef plat form, which is composed chiefly of dead coral and honeycombed reef rock, is interrupted by two narrow deep channels or passages.

--- SENTENCE 47 ---
The reef at Fagaiofu proved to be literally alive with " Palolo."

--- SENTENCE 48 ---
They were discovered by prising off, with a crowbar, masses of the rock at the edges of the channels.

--- SENTENCE 49 ---
They could be seen dangling from the freshly exposed surfaces and wriggling free into the deeper water of the channel to be carried seaxvard by the retreating tide, to the astonishment of my natives who had never seen the " Palolo " before the time appointed for its appear ance this was three days before.

--- SENTENCE 50 ---
Owing to the great length of the entire worm, its fragile structure and intricate association with the cavities of the honeycomb rock, the operation of freeing unbroken specimens is a delicate one.

--- SENTENCE 51 ---
With the aid of chisels .and forceps I succeeded with great difficulty in obtaining, in addition to other material, three worms complete from head to tail.

--- SENTENCE 52 ---
My experiences confirm the discoveries of Kramer and Fried laender as to the origin of the " Palolo."

The " Palolo " makes its appearance in Samoa in the months of October and November during the last quarter of the moon.

--- SENTENCE 80 ---
This is the time of the lowest or spring tides when the reef flats in shallow places are uncovered or only awash, and at this sea- son the sun is nearest to the zenith.

--- SENTENCE 81 ---
I must reserve for my final paper a discussion of the causes of the swarming of the "Palolo " and will only say here that I am inclined to believe in some thermotropic or heliotropic reaction of the eyes borne on the ventral segments of the epitokal part of the worm.

--- SENTENCE 82 ---
These eyes have recently been studied histologically by Hesse I on material collected by Kramer.

--- SENTENCE 83 ---
Hesse states that from their structure the eyes probably do not form images, but function rather in reacting to light of different intensities, the direction of light and possibly to different colors.

--- SENTENCE 84 ---
It is significant that these eyes are found only on a few of the posterior segments of the atokal sedentary part and are not well developed; while on the other hand they are highly developed on all but the anal segments of the epitokal active part which leads such an ephemeral free existence.
