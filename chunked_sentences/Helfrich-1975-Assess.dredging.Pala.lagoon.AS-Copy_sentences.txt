#### INTRODUCTION

American Samoa, located in the tropical South Pacific at about 170° west longitude and 14° south latitude, is a cluster of islands of volcanic origin.

--- SENTENCE 62 ---
The islands all lie eastward of 171° west longitude.

--- SENTENCE 63 ---
Geographically the islands are relatively isolated, lying about 2,300 miles southwest of Hawaii and 1,500 miles north of Auckland, New Zealand (Figure 1).

--- SENTENCE 64 ---
The climate is tropical, with a yearly mean temperature of 70 to 90°F and the relative humidity is 80 to 85 percent.

--- SENTENCE 65 ---
Southeast tradewinds averaging eight knots blow consistently from May through November, but are variable during the remaining months of the year.

--- SENTENCE 66 ---
Rainfall is abundant, usually peaking in the months from December to March.

--- SENTENCE 67 ---
The average annual rainfall in Pago Pago over the past 40 years has been about 508 cm (200 in).

--- SENTENCE 68 ---
The islands are officially classified as an unincorporated territory of the United States and, since 1951, when control was transferred from the Department of the Navy, have been under the administration of the Department of the Interior.

--- SENTENCE 69 ---
Tutuila, the largest and most heavily populated island, houses the Territorial Government of American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 70 ---
The rugged, mountainous island, about 32 km (20 mi) long and up to 9.6 km (6 mi) wide, is surrounded by lush tropical coral reefs (Figure 2).

--- SENTENCE 71 ---
Pago Pago Harbor nearly bisects the central mountain range and has been the center of most of the urban development in the territory.

--- SENTENCE 72 ---
In 1970, the population of American Samoa was 27,769, of which 25,560 resided on the 135 km $^2$  (52 mi $^2$ ) of Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 73 ---
Samoa reports one of the fastest population growth rates in the world.

--- SENTENCE 74 ---
This, coupled with the acute lack of economic opportunity, has precipitated a very high emigration rate to the United States.

--- SENTENCE 75 ---
The present government is attempting to reverse this trend.

--- SENTENCE 76 ---
Sources of export revenue are few, consisting mostly of commercial fishing (two canneries), copra, and handicrafts.

--- SENTENCE 77 ---
In 1967 commercial fishing accounted for 99.7 percent of the export revenue and in 1968 there were 160 fishing vessels based in American Samoa, most of which were Japanese, Korean, and Taiwanese.

--- SENTENCE 78 ---
In 1971 it was estimated that only 12 independent Samoan commercial fishermen were active on Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 79 ---
Tourism is expected to be the major growth industry (Tudor, 1972).

--- SENTENCE 80 ---
Although Pago Pago Harbor is one of the best deep-draft harbors in the world, locally accessible light-draft harbors are lacking elsewhere in American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 81 ---
Three such harbors have been proposed for the southern coast of Tutuila: Fagaalu Bay, Pala Lagoon, and Leone Bay.

--- SENTENCE 82 ---
All are accessible by paved road.

--- SENTENCE 83 ---
To improve the economic and recreational value of Pala Lagoon, the dredging of a channel across the reef into deep water and the dredging of a boat harbor are being proposed.

--- SENTENCE 84 ---
It was concern over the environmental and socio-economic impact of this proposed project that initiated this study.

#### Description of Pala Lagoon

Pala Lagoon is a shallow estuarine body of water located adjacent to the airport at Tafuna--about nine miles from Pago Pago--and is the only large enclosed lagoon on Tutuila (Figure 2).

--- SENTENCE 87 ---
Estuarine conditions are created by the influx of water from two principal streams and from numerous springs near the western and northern shores.

--- SENTENCE 88 ---
The lagoon is roughly circular, approximately 1.6 km in diameter, and has a surface area of about 3 km² (300 hectares).

--- SENTENCE 89 ---
Approximately two-thirds of the inner lagoon area is very flat and shallow, with a depth ranging from 0.3 to 1.5 m depending on the tidal state.

--- SENTENCE 90 ---
The bottom in this area is a muddy, coral sand to silty mud and the overlying water is usually turbid.

--- SENTENCE 91 ---
Pala Lagoon is bordered by mangrove swamps and rocky wooded promontories on the northern side, a residential area and undeveloped wooded land to the west, the runway and taxi strips of the Pago Pago International Airport along the south, and a wooded peninsula (Coconut Point Peninsula) containing a village and private residence on the east.

--- SENTENCE 92 ---
The opening to the sea is aligned to the eastward across a fringing coral reef in the southeast corner of the lagoon where a shallow, narrow pass exists (Figure 3).

--- SENTENCE 93 ---
Generally the biota of the inner lagoon lacks diversity, but it is thought to be an important nursery and spawning ground for fish and invertebrates.

--- SENTENCE 94 ---
The lagoon is presently utilized by the Samoan people for subsistence-type fishing for mullet and carangids.

--- SENTENCE 95 ---
Samoan or mangrove crabs (Scylla serrata) exist in the mangrove swamp along the northern shore and bivalve mollusks are abundant on the mud flats of the inner lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 96 ---
Presently, a rather extensive dredging program is being proposed for Pala Lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 97 ---
Some dredging has already been done during the construction of the airport and will continue in order to supply fill material for public works projects.

--- SENTENCE 98 ---
Further dredging awaits the results and recommendations of this assessment of the ecological implications of the proposed plan.

#### Proposed Plan for Pala Lagoon

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers has conducted a preliminary study and has found that further study of the feasibility of constructing facilities for a small boat harbor in Pala Lagoon is warranted.

--- SENTENCE 99 ---
Such a facility would require dredging of an entrance channel and harbor basin.

--- SENTENCE 100 ---
The proposed facilities in Pala Lagoon would be primarily for utilization by recreational crafts--small power boats and sailboats--although many of these boats would probably engage to some degree in subsistence fishing.

--- SENTENCE 101 ---
The maximum-sized boat which could be accommodated by the proposed facilities would have a length of 50 ft, a beam of 15 ft, and a 6-ft draft.

--- SENTENCE 102 ---
No commercial fishing vessels are expected to use these facilities.

#### TIDES AND CIRCULATION

--- SENTENCE 110 ---
The physical portion of the Pala Lagoon study was undertaken to learn about its present tides and circulation and to predict how these water motions would be changed by dredging a proposed channel through the lagoon entrance.

--- SENTENCE 111 ---
To try to meet these objectives with limited time and manpower, attention was restricted to gross, overall features of water structure and transport.

--- SENTENCE 112 ---
The primary observations consisted of tidal records and some vertical temperature sections.

--- SENTENCE 113 ---
From these it was concluded that the dredging project would have a moderate effect on the tidal rise and fall in the lagoon, but would make an appreciable change in the internal circulation.

--- SENTENCE 114 ---
Whether the dredging would increase the flushing rate of the lagoon is perhaps the most important question, especially since the uses of the lagoon anticipated after dredging will certainly increase the rate of addition of pollutants.

--- SENTENCE 115 ---
Offhand, it might be expected that deepening the entrance channel would greatly increase the flushing rate, but this will not be the case here.

--- SENTENCE 116 ---
The flushing rate might be slightly increased or possibly even slightly decreased by the proposed channel.

--- SENTENCE 117 ---
Although the problem is too complex to allow an exact prediction of which small change would occur, the measurements and analyses do lead firmly to the important overall conclusion: the proposed dredging will not produce greatly increased flushing of the lagoon.

#### Physical Structure and Circulation

Pala Lagoon may be classed as a stratified estuary, but one having unusual features that set it apart from typical continental estuaries.

--- SENTENCE 155 ---
Fresh water enters from about six streams, draining relatively small watersheds that are subject to tropical rain conditions; large, rapid fluctuations in the freshwater input should be expected.

--- SENTENCE 156 ---
Over half the lagoon is less than 1 m deep; solar heating effects are large; and the tidal inflow on each cycle is about 40 percent of the lagoon volume.

--- SENTENCE 157 ---
All these factors tend to make the lagoon a highly variable environment.

--- SENTENCE 158 ---
An additional feature makes it unusual as an estuary--its communication with the ocean is restricted by a very shoal (.5 m) sill.

--- SENTENCE 159 ---
This, together with the bottom topography inside the sill, forces a significant vertical circulation to occur in the outer third of the lagoon during each tidal cycle.

--- SENTENCE 160 ---
In view of the highly variable character of the lagoon, the present survey is inadequate for drawing a comprehensive picture of its physical processes or structure.

--- SENTENCE 161 ---
Instrumental failures cut short the observations which were already restricted in scope by time and manpower.

--- SENTENCE 162 ---
Furthermore, the survey period did not include any time of significant rainfall.

--- SENTENCE 163 ---
The measurements are limited to vertical temperature sections taken at low tide (Figure 8; see Figure 5 for section locations) and to surface temperatures and salinities gathered at somewhat scattered times (Figure 9).

--- SENTENCE 164 ---
These observations will be discussed in relation to the implied gross features of the circulation.

Bottom topography is important in the circulation of Pala Lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 166 ---
There are two distinct regions.

--- SENTENCE 167 ---
A large area adjacent to the airport has already been dredged to obtain fill material.

--- SENTENCE 168 ---
This dredged portion covers about .25 x  $10^6$  m<sup>2</sup>--about one-sixth of the lagoon--and forms a fairly extensive basin inside the sill which presently restricts the entrance channel.

--- SENTENCE 169 ---
This basin has very irregular topography, but may be taken to have a mean depth of roughly 3 m. The remainder of the lagoon, although by no means flat, may be treated as a large shoal area with a mean depth of 1 m or less.

It is convenient to crudely define three classes of water in the lagoon for purposes of discussion:

- 1. <u>Lagoon water</u> can be thought of as having temperature or salinity strongly affected by runoff or solar heating in the shallows.

--- SENTENCE 171 ---
In Figure 8 it might be identified by temperatures exceeding 32.5°C.
- 2.

--- SENTENCE 172 ---
Ocean water is water only slightly different from that in the ocean outside.

--- SENTENCE 173 ---
In Figure 8 it might be identified as having temperatures less than 29.5°C.
- 3.

--- SENTENCE 174 ---
Intermediate water may be thought of as resulting from a mixture of varying amounts of lagoon and ocean water.

--- SENTENCE 175 ---
In the observations comprising Figure 8, it would appear that temperatures ranged between 29.5 and 32.5°C.

--- SENTENCE 176 ---
The tidal circulation can be described in a step-wise manner with the above definitions.

--- SENTENCE 177 ---
Although this approach aids understanding, it should be remembered that the actual processes do not occur in steps and that the three classes of water blend and merge without distinct boundaries.

--- SENTENCE 178 ---
During a medium range, an incoming tide of about 0.8 x 10<sup>6</sup> m<sup>3</sup> of water enters the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 179 ---
Although there may be some slight recycling, most of this inflow is pelagic water and it is denser than the water in the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 180 ---
(The observations show no reason to expect the formation of denser water by processes in the lagoon.)

--- SENTENCE 181 ---
After crossing the sill, most of the ocean water sinks into the dredged basin and mixes with the warmer, fresher, intermediate water already there.

--- SENTENCE 182 ---
This mixing produces a cooler, saltier, intermediate water which remains temporarily in the deeper parts of the basin.

--- SENTENCE 183 ---
During flood tide, relatively undiluted ocean water penetrates somewhat beyond Coconut Point and part of it appears to flow around and into the lagoon on the north side of the point.

--- SENTENCE 184 ---
As ocean water flows into the basin, about  $0.8 \times 10^6 \text{ m}^3$  of intermediate water previously there is displaced.

--- SENTENCE 185 ---
It moves into the lagoon, spreading under and mixing with lagoon water.

--- SENTENCE 186 ---
Since it flows away from the dredged basin and over the interior region where the mean depth is about 1 m, it covers about onethird of the lagoon's area at high tide.

--- SENTENCE 187 ---
During the ebb, the main outflow from the lagoon consists of intermediate water which lies above sill depth (.5 m).

--- SENTENCE 188 ---
As the tide falls, lagoon water also flows outward in a thin surface layer which mixes with underlying intermediate water.

--- SENTENCE 189 ---
The mixing appears to be quite vigorous; outflowing lagoon water loses its identifying characteristics before exiting, except along the shoal north edge of the channel where there is no underlying water with which to mix.

--- SENTENCE 190 ---
This can be seen in sections AA' and CC' of Figure 8.

--- SENTENCE 191 ---
Also during the ebb, the velocity shear which must develop over the basin will produce vertical mixing between outflowing water and the denser portions of the intermediate water in the basin, warming and decreasing the salinity of the latter.

--- SENTENCE 192 ---
The most important point about the tidal circulation is that pelagic water entering the lagoon on each cycle cannot leave again without mixing fairly extensively with lagoon water.

--- SENTENCE 193 ---
This is a result of the existence of the shallow entrance sill and the basin inside, which is large enough to contain the volume

of tidal inflow.

--- SENTENCE 194 ---
(In contrast, seawater in most estuaries flows freely in and out underneath the estuarine water and much of the seawater leaving during an ebb is merely the same water that entered during the flood.)

--- SENTENCE 195 ---
The prevailing easterly winds drive surface water toward the western side of the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 196 ---
Surface drogues, placed for two hours during a rising tide, traced the tracks shown in Figure 5.

--- SENTENCE 197 ---
The importance of this movement is that surface water, normally containing whatever pollutants have been introduced by streams and outfalls, will collect in the northwest sector of the lagoon and its removal by tidal circulation will be slowed.

--- SENTENCE 198 ---
The problem is compounded by the fact that this same sector of the lagoon is apparently already receiving pollutants from surface and underground streams.

--- SENTENCE 199 ---
Although no measurements were designed to detect them, there are undoubtedly many small-scale circulation patterns in the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 200 ---
However, it is felt that the dominant circulation is the tidal pulsation described above, with a superimposed wind-driven surface drift.

#### Flushing and Residence Times

The present mean residence time for water in Pala Laboon is about 30 hours.

--- SENTENCE 202 ---
(The mean total lagoon volume is about  $2 \times 10^6 \text{ m}^3$ ; a volume equal to about 40 percent of this exchanged during a semidiurnal tidal cycle.)

--- SENTENCE 203 ---
This establishes some overall figures for the rate of flushing and is of limited use for specific applications because the lagoon is not completely mixed.

--- SENTENCE 204 ---
In reality, residence time will vary from about 12 hours for water near the entrance to a period perhaps on the order of two weeks for water in the western extremities of the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 205 ---
Furthermore, it must be recalled that these figures apply for a time of almost no runoff.

--- SENTENCE 206 ---
During a rainy period, residence time, at least in the surface waters, would decline.

--- SENTENCE 207 ---
The above rather crude ideas about flushing rates can be somewhat refined.

--- SENTENCE 208 ---
The new channel would increase the volume flow through the lagoon entrance, but would create a substantial change in another way.

--- SENTENCE 209 ---
It would cut through the entrance sill to a depth about equal to the predominant depth of the existing basin inside; an appreciable fraction of the ocean water that enters the basin could leave through the channel without being forced to mix first with water inside the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 214 ---

--- SENTENCE 215 ---

--- SENTENCE 216 ---
The above estimates are based solely on tidal exchange volumes.

--- SENTENCE 217 ---
Since these are major determinants of the flushing process, the estimates provide a good, first-order idea of the rates and of the effect of the proposed channel.

--- SENTENCE 218 ---
However, they cannot be taken as exact numerical predictions because at least two things have been neglected.

--- SENTENCE 219 ---
The proposed channel would slightly increase the flow of momentum into the lagoon and, by itself, this would tend to increase mixing and flushing of interior water relative to the estimated values.

--- SENTENCE 220 ---
However, the new channel would also increase vertical stratification within the lagoon by creating a bottom topography that allows a salt wedge to flow in and out.

--- SENTENCE 221 ---
By itself, this would tend to depress mixing within the lagoon and increase residence times.

--- SENTENCE 222 ---
Thus the neglected processes would modify the predictions in opposite directions and it is not possible to say here whether the actual, overall mixing rates would be somewhat larger or smaller than those computed from the tidal exchange volumes alone.

--- SENTENCE 224 ---
In the "before dredging" example used above, .85 x  $10^6$  m<sup>3</sup> of water leaves the lagoon every 12 hours and water from the interior of the lagoon comprises a fraction of this.

--- SENTENCE 225 ---
Probably the fraction is at least one-tenth and at most one-half.

--- SENTENCE 226 ---
The volume of the inner lagoon (landward of the dredged basin) is about 1.18 x  $10^6$  m<sup>3</sup>, so the mean residence time of the water there is between 33 and 165 hours.

#### BACTERIAL CONTAMINATION

Pala Lagoon supports large populations of fish and invertebrates, some of which are harvested for food.

--- SENTENCE 227 ---
At the time of this survey, sewage treatment in the surrounding watershed was nonexistent and raw sewage was presumably being transported directly into the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 228 ---
Although this sampling was conducted during an unusually dry period, Pala Lagoon is subject to large and rapid fluctuations in freshwater input.

--- SENTENCE 229 ---
The major effluent, Papa Stream (Figure 10), drains approximately 2 km² and has an average volume transport of 111 liters/sec to the lagoon (U.S. Department of Interior, Geological Survey, 1971).

--- SENTENCE 230 ---
The other notable input, Vaitele Stream, was dry at the time of this survey; normally it has a volume transport of 60 to 85 liters/sec.

--- SENTENCE 231 ---
A series of 20 stations were established (Figure 10) based upon information from an earlier reconnaissance.

--- SENTENCE 232 ---
Sampling occurred, in all cases, between 0900 and 1000 hours while high tide conditions prevailed.

--- SENTENCE 233 ---
The period of time between sample collection and analysis was minimized by sampling alternate halves of the lagoon on subsequent days.

--- SENTENCE 234 ---
Samples were collected using sterile 500-ml prescription bottles and were kept in darkness prior to analysis.

People deporting themselves in water of high coliform content take a definite risk of contracting a disease.

--- SENTENCE 271 ---

--- SENTENCE 273 ---

#### PHYTOPLANKTON PRODUCTIVITY

This portion of the Pala Lagoon survey was designed to answer several fundamental questions about the dynamics of the planktonic algal (phytoplankton) community in the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 312 ---
Phytoplankton are an important food source for many herbivorous filter-feeders such as clams and oysters.

--- SENTENCE 313 ---
Algal abundance and productivity are therefore intimately linked to faunal abundance.

--- SENTENCE 314 ---
Furthermore, because of their position at the base of the food chain (utilizing inorganic nutrients) and their rapid reproductive rates, phytoplankton are more directly and immediately affected by environmental perturbations than are faunal components of the system.

--- SENTENCE 315 ---
The intent of this survey was to determine the present density and growth rate of phytoplankton in representative areas of the lagoon and to relate the observed variations to ambient concentrations of selected essential plant nutrients.

--- SENTENCE 316 ---
To accomplish this, a model was employed to analytically relate the growth rate per unit of population to the concentration of the limiting nutrient.

--- SENTENCE 317 ---
The model allows prediction of the characteristics of any subsequent steady-state situation resulting from a reasonably well-defined modification of the present environment with respect to the concentration of the limiting nutrient, found to be nitrogen, for a large portion of the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 318 ---
This portion of the report is somewhat more detailed and technical because, should the dredging take place, phytoplankton productivity will be used as a key indicator in the quantitative assessment of environmental impact.

--- SENTENCE 319 ---
To establish station locations for the detailed study of phytoplankton productivity, an initial reconnaissance of the plant pigment concentrations in Pala Lagoon was undertaken using *in vivo* fluorometry (Lorenzen, 1966).

Rustrak recorder).

--- SENTENCE 321 ---
The lagoon was circumnavigated while pumping water continuously from a depth of about 0.5 m through an opaque length of garden hose.

--- SENTENCE 322 ---
Based on these results a series of stations were established within the lagoon (Figure 1.).

--- SENTENCE 323 ---
On the following two days, working from a smaller rubber boat, discrete surface water samples from each of these stations were collected.

--- SENTENCE 324 ---

--- SENTENCE 325 ---

--- SENTENCE 326 ---

In order to draw any conclusions from the limited amount of data available it must be assumed that the phytoplankton population in the lagoon is in steady state with respect to its supply of essential nutrients.

--- SENTENCE 375 ---
The fact that the period immediately preceding the survey was one of low rainfall makes this a tenable assumption.

--- SENTENCE 376 ---
In a non-nutrient-limiting environment the growth rate of a population is maximal and limited only by its own intrinsic rate of increase under the prevailing conditions of temperature and light.

--- SENTENCE 377 ---
Sub-maximal growth rates would then reflect nutritional deficiencies in the water mass.

--- SENTENCE 378 ---
Nutrient-limited growth of phytoplankton has been shown to require a two-step model: the first step utilizes a hyperbola to relate uptake rate to nutrient concentration in the medium; the second step, directly coupled to the first, utilizes a similar hyperbola to relate growth rate to the intracellular nutrient concentration (Droop, 1968; Caperon, 1968; Caperon and Meyer, 1972a).

--- SENTENCE 379 ---
This work has shown that under steady-state conditions a similar hyperbola can be used for a good approximation to describe growth rate directly as a function of environmental nutrient concentration, but the half-saturation constant here is much less than the half-saturation constant for uptake rate.

--- SENTENCE 380 ---
The work of MacIsaac and Dugdale (1969) and Caperon and Meyer (1972b) suggests that this kinetic description is applicable to mixed phytoplankton populations adapted to the same nutrient-limited regime.

--- SENTENCE 381 ---
The hyperbola  $\mu = \mu_{\rm m} S/(K_{\rm s}+S)$ , where  $\mu$  is growth rate, S is substrate concentration and  $\mu$  and  $K_{\rm s}$  are constants, is used to investigate the hypothesis that the stations in sector 1 (Figure 11) represent a nutrient-limited regime.

--- SENTENCE 382 ---
In sector 1, fixed nitrogen is in the nutrient-limited range for phytoplankton adapted to oligotrophic environments (Eppley et al., 1969).

--- SENTENCE 383 ---
Figure 12 is a plot of PI as a function of total fixed-nitrogen at these stations.

--- SENTENCE 384 ---
The curve is a least squares fit of the data to this hyperbola, with a half saturation constant,  $K_{\text{S}}$ , of 0.13 µg-atom/liter and maximum PI,  $\mu_{\text{m}}$ , of 15.6 mg C/mg Chlorophyll a/hr.

--- SENTENCE 385 ---
It is assumed that growth rate does not reach zero until the nutrient concentration falls to zero.

--- SENTENCE 386 ---
It has been shown in laboratory experiments (Caperon and Meyer, 1972b) that this is not usually the case for nitrate-limited situations.

--- SENTENCE 387 ---
Growth, or nutrient uptake, stops while there is still some small amount of limiting nutrient in the water.

--- SENTENCE 388 ---
Determining this intercept requires extremely precise measurements and is best accomplished under controlled laboratory conditions.

--- SENTENCE 389 ---
The assumption of a zero-zero intercept is adequate for the present purpose.

--- SENTENCE 390 ---
Since ammonium is preferred over nitrate it may be more appropriate to consider only this form of fixed-nitrogen.

--- SENTENCE 391 ---
Figure 13 is a plot of PI as a function of ammonium concentration and the fitted hyperbola has a  $K_{S}$  of 0.049  $\mu g$ -atom/liter and a  $\mu_{m}$  of 15.9.

--- SENTENCE 392 ---
The half-saturation constants for these two curves compare with 0.13 µg-atom/liter for nitrate-limited laboratory populations and 0.02 µg-atom/liter for ammonium-limited laboratory populations (Caperon and Meyer, 1972b).

--- SENTENCE 393 ---
Unfortunately, due to the limitations already mentioned regarding the method of ammonia determination, little emphasis should be placed on this excellent fit, since all but one of the values are below the lower limits of good reliability of the method.

Sediment dominated the inshore regions of reef flat.

According to a local resident of Coconut Point, the shoreline has receded about 40 feet since the airport was constructed.

#### Reef Coral Tolerance to High Temperatures

Considering that the reef flat uncovers at nearly every low tide, it is surprising to find the abundance of living corals so high.

--- SENTENCE 543 ---
During midday lows in late February (or late summer in the southern hemisphere), temperatures in isolated pools of water containing living reef corals approached 40°C.

--- SENTENCE 544 ---
Air temperatures may also have been quite high during these periods.

--- SENTENCE 545 ---
Repeated visits to these tracts during the study revealed that few of the colonies succumbed to the heat.

--- SENTENCE 546 ---
A review of the literature indicated that reef coral tolerance of high temperatures in Pala Lagoon was among the

highest reported for any reef area.

--- SENTENCE 547 ---
It is remarkable that the corals, especially *Porites*, *Psammocora*, and *Pavona*, survived and continued to grow during the temperature conditions observed.

--- SENTENCE 548 ---
There are marked local variations in the coral fauna in and outside of Pala Lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 549 ---
Except for the few deeper areas of the lagoon, temperature levels and diurnal variations in temperature are very high.

--- SENTENCE 550 ---
There is little doubt that temperature extremes affect the composition of the coral fauna of each region.

--- SENTENCE 551 ---
The lack of corals in zone 4 further suggests an environmental stress most likely associated with poor circulation.

--- SENTENCE 552 ---
The currents were stronger in the few local areas of zone 3 where corals were common.

--- SENTENCE 553 ---
However, the lack of hard substrate in the inner areas of the lagoon tended to inhibit recruitment of corals since coral larvae do not settle on fine sediment.

#### Echinoderms of Pala Lagoon

From the surveys conducted in the lagoon, it became apparent that the echinoderms were distributed in a similar manner as the corals, hence the same regional classification used for the corals will be used for the echinoderms.

--- SENTENCE 554 ---
The inner lagoon zone 4 contains few echinoderms.

--- SENTENCE 555 ---
*Polyplectana* sp. was noted on occasion and a few specimens of *Holothuria atra* were seen; both of these sea cucumbers were confined to shallow sandy flats near the lagoon entrance area.

--- SENTENCE 556 ---
In zone 3 the echinoderms were more common.

--- SENTENCE 557 ---
On the shallow flats adjacent Coconut Point, the holothurians *Stichopus chloronotus*, *Polyplectana*, and *Actinopyga* sp. were common.

--- SENTENCE 558 ---
The sea urchins *Echinothrix* sp. and *Echinometra mathaei* were rarely noted in the zone.

--- SENTENCE 559 ---
In some areas zone 2 contained the largest concentrations of echinoderms.

--- SENTENCE 560 ---
On the reef flats adjacent to Coconut Point, the sea cucumber Stichopus occurred in dense concentrations.

--- SENTENCE 561 ---
This is similar to what Mayor (1924) reported for inshore areas of Aua Reef.

--- SENTENCE 562 ---
During midday hours, water temperatures over these shallow areas exceeded 40°C.

--- SENTENCE 563 ---
Several specimens of Stichopus which had been unable to remain under water during the falling of the tide had succumbed to heat and desiccation.

--- SENTENCE 564 ---
Pattern statistics (the index of dispersion) show a tendency for Stichopus (site E) to be evenly spaced, suggesting an active response against crowding.

--- SENTENCE 565 ---
Under rocks at the same survey site, Holothuria (Mertensiothuria) pervicax also occurred at high densities but was more randomly distributed--probably because suitable rock cover was also randomly distributed.

--- SENTENCE 566 ---
Stichopus are active during daytime hours while Holothuria are night feeders.

--- SENTENCE 567 ---
Echinoids (sea urchins) were also very common in zone 2 but this was apparent only after night diving.

--- SENTENCE 568 ---
During daylight hours most sea urchins remained under rocks, burrows, and cracks.

--- SENTENCE 569 ---
Because of the difficulty of adequately locating these animals during daylight hours when the surveys

were conducted, accurate estimates of their densities could not be obtained.

--- SENTENCE 570 ---
This was especially true for sea urchins such as <code>Diadema</code>, <code>Echinothrix</code>, and <code>Echinometra</code>.

--- SENTENCE 571 ---
Comparison of the surveys carried out at sides D and E showed that, in general, sea cucumbers were more common at inner areas of the region while sea urchins were more common in areas closer to open ocean circulation.

--- SENTENCE 572 ---
There is probably little competition between these organisms since the holothurians are predominantly sediment feeders while sea urchins scrape algae off hard substrate surfaces.

--- SENTENCE 573 ---
<code>Echinometra mathaei</code> was by far the most abundant of the sea urchins and approached densities of 5 per square meter or greater (Table 4).

--- SENTENCE 574 ---
There were many species of echinoderms in zone 1 although none seemed to be abundant.

--- SENTENCE 575 ---
Several species were not found in the other regions.

--- SENTENCE 576 ---
Results of bottom type surveys carried out at transect A (Figure 15) indicated that echinoderms make up only a minor fraction of bottom cover in the area.

--- SENTENCE 577 ---
However, the reef flat was cavernous so that accurate estimates of density could not be made.

--- SENTENCE 578 ---
The density of echinoderms may be much higher than is apparent.

--- SENTENCE 579 ---
The sea cucumbers *Stichopus* and *Polyplectana* were also common, especially near the shore.

--- SENTENCE 580 ---
The sea urchin *Echinometra* was conspicuous in more offshore regions.

--- SENTENCE 581 ---
Circulation patterns within the lagoon probably strongly influence the distribution of the echinoderms.

--- SENTENCE 582 ---
There is also some evidence of temperature extremes and substrate type controlling the distribution of some of the species.

--- SENTENCE 583 ---
Sea urchins were most common on rubble and other hard substrate areas and holothurians on sandy shallow areas, while starfish were mostly confined to the outer fringing reef and flats.

--- SENTENCE 584 ---

--- SENTENCE 585 ---

Tows were made in four locations (Figure 16) during two periods, one in daylight and the other in darkness.

--- SENTENCE 588 ---
Because of the average depth of water (0.9 m) and the shoals and coral heads, the net was towed in circular paths and only during high tide.

The analysis of data was conducted by Mr. Walter Matsumoto, fishery biologist at the Honolulu Laboratory, NMFS.

Other zooplankton organisms which are abundant in these collections include crustaceans and chaetognaths.

--- SENTENCE 608 ---
These data, although incomplete, indicate that Pala Lagoon serves as a nursery ground for some species of fish, both those resident in the inner lagoon such as the gobies and those which probably range in and out of the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 609 ---
An effort is being made to complete the analysis of these collections in order to substantiate trends suggested by the March and April data.

#### SOCIO-ECONOMIC CONSIDERATIONS

Presently, Pala Lagoon supports some minor fisheries and serves as a source of dredged fill material.

--- SENTENCE 674 ---
Perhaps the most important fishery is that for clams on the intertidal mud flats on the north end of the lagoon (Glude, 1972).

--- SENTENCE 675 ---
A few Samoans, using gill nets, reportedly catch crabs in the deeper pockets along the western side of the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 676 ---
A fish weir is operated on the south side of Coconut Point and dredging activity (crane-drag line) continues periodically along the south shore as sediments are needed for earth fill by the Public Works Commission of the Government of American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 677 ---
During the survey period, boats other than those supporting the survey were rarely observed operating in Pala Lagoon and several parties of picnic-kers were observed along the western shore.

--- SENTENCE 678 ---
The entire southern shore is in the airport complex from which the public is excluded.

--- SENTENCE 679 ---
Because of time limitations and because of the lack of personnel who could make an effective assessment of the socio-economic impact of the proposed modification of Pala Lagoon, this aspect of the study is admittedly deficient.

--- SENTENCE 680 ---
A series of discussions were conducted with personnel of the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers in Honolulu regarding the costs and benefits of the dredged harbor proposed for Pala Lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 681 ---
The basis for these discussions was the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers' report, Fagaalu Bay, Pala Lagoon, and Leone Bay, American Samoa: Reconnaissance Report on Navigational Improvements, 14 May 1971, which proposes projects in response to the needs of the area.

--- SENTENCE 682 ---
Whether the projection of

needs in this report, which are based upon standards of living in Hawaii, are realistic in the context of Samoan culture is questioned.

--- SENTENCE 683 ---
The initial configuration of the Pala Lagoon small boat harbor proposed by the Army Corps of Engineers was based on the accommodation of 350 trailer-type craft, 10 pleasure boats of the inboard-outboard class, and 40 sailboats without auxiliary power.

--- SENTENCE 684 ---
The estimated cost of construction of a harbor to accommodate these crafts is \$1,490,000.

--- SENTENCE 685 ---
Despite some discussions with Samoans in an attempt to gain insight into local attitudes and desires vis a vis a boat harbor in Pala Lagoon, language and cultural barriers did not permit an accurate prediction of the future needs for Pala Lagoon marine facilities or whether what was being proposed is realistic.

--- SENTENCE 686 ---
Based upon the existing economy with an expected modest increase in tourism and light industrial development over the next ten years, it appears unlikely that the level of boat ownership in American Samoa will approach the level in Hawaii.

--- SENTENCE 687 ---
(Boat ownership data from Hawaii served as the basis for the Army Corps of Engineers' figures.)

--- SENTENCE 688 ---
Certainly this potential for boat ownership must be examined in more detail.

--- SENTENCE 689 ---
In view of the uncertainties involved and in order to meet immediate needs, an alternate proposal for a somewhat flexible scheme follows.

#### DISCUSSION

Because the proposed channel dredging will have little positive effects and possibly a negative effect on the mixing and flushing in the lagoon and because it will probably be attended by increased pollution by petroleum, sewage, and trash, it is almost certain that the project would have the overall effect of further degrading Pala Lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 690 ---
It should also be pointed out in passing that the map of the proposed channel (Figure 4) could be misleading.

--- SENTENCE 691 ---
The map gives the impression that the new channel would be quite long and perhaps because of this it might look as though it would bring ocean water much farther into the lagoon than it now penetrates.

--- SENTENCE 692 ---
This is not so.

--- SENTENCE 693 ---
Most of the area through which the proposed new "cut" passes is in fact the basin which is already dredged to an appropriate depth.

--- SENTENCE 694 ---
What the "channel" would actually do would be to remove a few rather isolated shoals that remain in the basin and cut through the outer reef flat--destroying its sill effect.

--- SENTENCE 695 ---
The yield of seafood from Pala Lagoon (clams, crabs, and finfish) was not assessed except to note the occasional occurrence of a crab trap fisherman or a clam collector.

--- SENTENCE 696 ---
Assessing the magnitude of this casual subsistence-type fishery would require more time and effort than the time allowed for this survey.

--- SENTENCE 697 ---
The seafood is undoubtedly important to those who harvest it regularly.

--- SENTENCE 698 ---
As previously noted, the proposed dredging would probably increase the tidal fluctuation, as well as to increase the residence time of water in the lagoon creating detrimental conditions relating to bacterial contamination, thermal and salinity stress, and

possible desiccation to organisms in the inner lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 699 ---
Thus the proposed dredging would probably be detrimental to the limited subsistence fishery presently being pursued in Pala Lagoon,

The zones of primary concern relative to enteric bacterial concentrations are in the northern and western areas of the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 700 ---
Though not a strictly quantifiable measure of the health hazard, the coliform data suggest that these areas support high bacterial populations and possibly the existence of pathogenic organisms.

--- SENTENCE 701 ---
Because the microbial quality of the inputs in question will vary directly with the health of the human population in the Pala Lagoon watershed, the implications of the lagoon influence in possible epidemic situations are obvious, but must be interpreted with caution.

--- SENTENCE 702 ---
Because of the geography and topography of its drainage basin, Pala Lagoon is subject to large and rapid fluctuations of freshwater input, resulting in similar fluctuations of bacterial contamination.

--- SENTENCE 703 ---
The weeks immediately preceding this survey were unusually dry and coliform concentrations were substantial.

--- SENTENCE 704 ---
This indicates that subsurface waters, which may receive bacteria from the many cesspools present on the western edge of the lagoon, are probably very important sources of microbial contamination.

--- SENTENCE 705 ---
The average surface and subsurface inputs to the lagoon, estimated at 20 to 25 ft<sup>3</sup>/sec (C. Bently, 1972: personal communication), drain almost exclusively into the northern lagoon area.

--- SENTENCE 706 ---
During this analysis, Papa Stream appeared as a defined surface flow in the region of the paved road to the north of the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 707 ---
Lagoonward, it was reduced into vadose water and gravity seepage occurred toward the lagoon until a minor surface flow reformed approximately 100 ft from the lagoon, Vadose water, rich in nutrients probably derived from cesspool seepage, is capable of supporting very large bacterial population.

--- SENTENCE 708 ---
Vadose inputs explain the high nutrient levels in the northwestern area (Krasnick and Caperon, 1973) and account for the maintenance of high coliforn concentrations under conditions of minimal surface input.

--- SENTENCE 709 ---
The combined occurrence of high coliform concentrations and low surface salinity values in the western region when the major stream, Vaitele, was dry strongly implies subsurface input.

--- SENTENCE 710 ---
Data indicate a major input near station 6 and suggest numerous inputs in the area of stations 1 to 4.

--- SENTENCE 711 ---
The absence of coliforms to the north of station 6 substantiates the description of circulation patterns in this area of the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 712 ---
The bacteriological character of the lagoon is very dependent on its physical dynamics.

--- SENTENCE 713 ---
Restricted circulation inhibits extensive tidal exchange and limits the dilution of contaminants entering into the northern and western regions.

--- SENTENCE 714 ---
Prevailing easterly winds reinforce this distributional pattern by driving surface water and the contained pollutants westward and account for the limited influence of Papa Stream on the area of station 12.

--- SENTENCE 715 ---
Both wind mixing, effective throughout the shallow water column, and periodic dredging operations cause high turbidity and reduce

visibility to a few centimeters.

--- SENTENCE 716 ---
The abundance of suspended material enhances the growth and survival of bacteria.

--- SENTENCE 717 ---
Bacteria adsorbed to particles in suspension (Zobell, 1946) eventually settle to the lagoon floor where prolific growth may occur (Rubentschik et al., 1936; Weiss, 1951).

--- SENTENCE 718 ---
Bacterial concentrations in sediments were not determined but those reported from the water column are probably a fraction of those occurring in the sediments.

--- SENTENCE 719 ---
On the basis of water quality standards for microbes (Appendices A and B), the northern and western regions of Pala Lagoon are unacceptable areas for the propagation of shellfish and marine life, and it appears that the utilization of marine foods from these areas is unwise.

--- SENTENCE 720 ---
It is further apparent that substantial precipitation, occurring after dry conditions would cause bacterial concentrations to rise by orders of magnitude in such a way that the consumption of marine organisms from this area might be dangerous.

--- SENTENCE 721 ---
Alterations in the physical dynamics, resulting from the proposed dredging operation, will probably have a moderate effect on the tidal range and flushing characteristics of the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 722 ---
On one hand, periodic purging of prolific sediment bacteria may result as the intertidal zone experiences longer and more frequent exposure to direct radiation.

--- SENTENCE 723 ---
However, the uncertain effects on the benthic biota and bacterial predators cause one to view this possible advantage with great caution.

--- SENTENCE 724 ---
The major improvement of microbial quality would be derived from the proposed dredging only if it resulted in increased tidal mixing and concurrent flushing of the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 725 ---
However, as previously stated, large benefits in terms of increased flushing cannot be expected from the dredging being proposed for Pala Lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 726 ---
Hence, improvements in the lagoon water quality, from a microbial aspect, are not expected to result from the proposed dredging and any increase in human activity in the lagoon or its watershed can only amplify the imbalance between microbial inputs and the bactericidal and dilutive effects of lagoon waters.

--- SENTENCE 727 ---
In view of the probable reduction of circulation in the main body of the lagoon, if the proposed boat harbor is actually dredged, it is suggested that alternatives utilizing much of the existing dredged area be considered.

--- SENTENCE 728 ---
An alternative that would not require very extensive dredging and would maintain the present circulation and mixing pattern is therefore proposed.

--- SENTENCE 729 ---
The main elements of this scheme, which largely utilizes existing dredged areas, are: (1) to widen and deepen the outer entrance where boats now encounter breaking waves during very rough weather; (2) to establish and mark a channel and turning basin; (3) to construct a dock or docks and a boat ramp; and (4) to construct a parking lot and ancillary shore facilities.

--- SENTENCE 730 ---

--- SENTENCE 731 ---
Figure 8, section A-A<sup>1</sup>, station 8 shows the schematic position of the sill with the proposed turning basin.

--- SENTENCE 734 ---
While the limit in the depth of the water constrains boating use to shallow-draft vessels (canoes and outboard motor boats), the permitted use should satisfy a considerable portion of the fishing and recreational demand that can be expected from the local people in the immediate neighborhood.

--- SENTENCE 735 ---
It is quite probable that the demand for trailer-type facilities will increase over time.

--- SENTENCE 736 ---
At present these services are almost non-existent in the Pala Lagoon area.

--- SENTENCE 737 ---
Considering the available land adjacent to the already dredged areas in Pala Lagoon, the southern portion of the lagoon is probably the most acceptable and inexpensive location for a small boat trailer-type development in all of Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 738 ---
The minimum required improvements for such a trailer-type facility in Pala Lagoon include a dock (or docks) to accommodate vessels being launched or retrieved on trailers, one or more launching ramps, and an adequate area for vehicle operation and parking.

--- SENTENCE 739 ---
If this plan is adopted, the principal costs will be to develop the boat ramp system and a suitable dock area adjacent to the ramp.

--- SENTENCE 740 ---
Since the recreational use of boat ramps is one in which peaks of activity can be expected, it would be worth building a wide ramp or double ramp capable of easily accommodating two to four trailers at once.

--- SENTENCE 741 ---
The ramp should be located between modest docks of 100 to 150 feet along the shore.

--- SENTENCE 742 ---
It is not within the scope of this study to plan such a facility in detail.

--- SENTENCE 743 ---
However, a survey of the expected use by boat type and the potential for trailer-borne shallow-draft boats should be studied before such an alternate plan is adopted.

--- SENTENCE 744 ---
This alternate plan has obvious cost advantages, but more important it will provide facilities for one class of boat operator while having a minimum impact on the water quality of Pala Lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 745 ---
It probably should be considered in conjunction with expanded facilities for deeper draft vessels in Pago Pago Harbor.

#### SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS

At the request of the Governor of American Samoa, this study was conducted to assess the biological and socio-economic effects of a dredging project proposed for Pala Lagoon, American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 746 ---
The survey was conducted by a team of scientists from the Hawaii Institute of Marine Biology, the National Marine Fisheries Service, and the staff of the Office of Marine Resources of the Government of American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 747 ---
Emphasis was placed upon the expected impact the project would have upon the tides and circulation, bacterial contamination, phytoplankton productivity, coral and echinoderms, and fishes of Pala Lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 748 ---
The lagoon is roughly circular, approximately one mile in diameter, and has a surface area of about 300 hectares.

--- SENTENCE 749 ---
The inner two-third has a flat mud bottom with a depth of from 0.3 to 1.5 m depending on the tidal state.

--- SENTENCE 750 ---
The water is usually turbid in much of the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 751 ---
The tide is about 85 percent as large as the ocean tide and follows it slightly in time.

--- SENTENCE 752 ---
A mathematical model was developed to predict the tides and currents under various conditions before and after the proposed dredging.

--- SENTENCE 753 ---
The effect of dredging upon the tide would be to make low tide typically 10 cm lower than at present which could result in an adverse effect upon organisms in intertidal and shallow regions of the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 754 ---
Because over half of Pala Lagoon is less than 1 m in depth and it is fed by six small streams subject to large fluctuations in flow, the lagoon is a highly variable environment.

--- SENTENCE 755 ---
The interaction and movement of the lagoon and intermediate and ocean water masses during the tidal cycle were examined.

--- SENTENCE 756 ---
The gross mean residence time of water is about 30 hours and about 40 percent of the estimated volume of 2 x  $10^2$  m³ is exchanged on each semidiurnal tidal cycle.

--- SENTENCE 757 ---
The water residence time near the entrance to the lagoon is about 12 hours, while that in the western extremities is on the order of two weeks during periods of low rainfall.

--- SENTENCE 758 ---
The data indicate that proposed dredging would remove an existing sill and, although the volume flow through the entrance channel would be increased, the turbulent mixing process between ocean and lagoon water masses caused by water crossing the sill would be decreased and, as a result, the average residence time of water in the lagoon would be increased by 75 percent.

--- SENTENCE 759 ---
The northern and western regions of Pala Lagoon contain the highest concentrations of coliform bacteria and do not conform to limits applicable to waters appropriate for the propagation of seafood.

--- SENTENCE 760 ---
Conditions of low rainfall prior to this investigation suggest that the bacterial levels are minimal.

--- SENTENCE 761 ---
The character of these areas results from both surface and subsurface inputs and is maintained by nearly continuous easterly winds, limited tidal exchange, and restricted internal circulation.

--- SENTENCE 762 ---
The maintenance of high bacterial concentrations under low rainfall conditions apparently results from subsurface gravity seepage and subsequent leaching from cesspools proximate to the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 763 ---
As the residence time of water in this area is increased, the natural bactericidal effects of seawater is reduced accordingly.

--- SENTENCE 764 ---
Additional quantities of silt in lagoon waters provide an increased amount of substrate matter for bacterial growth.

--- SENTENCE 765 ---
Since the dredging proposed for Pala Lagoon is not believed to enhance the effective tidal dilution, this project is not expected to improve the microbial quality of the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 766 ---
The dynamics of the phytoplankton (planktonic algae) population in Pala Lagoon were studied because of their importance to the animal

communities and their immediate and direct responses to environmental perturbations.

--- SENTENCE 767 ---
The density and growth rate of phytoplankton in various portions of the lagoon were related to plant nutrient concentrations and a model was analytically employed to relate growth rate per unit population to the concentration of the limiting nutrient.

--- SENTENCE 768 ---
Growth of planktonic algae is presently controlled by two regimes.

--- SENTENCE 769 ---
Growth rates in that portion of the lagoon nearest the entrance channel are controlled by the supply of fixed-nitrogen present as waste effluents entering the lagoon with tidal incursions.

--- SENTENCE 770 ---
The more isolated northern and western portions of the lagoon receive abundant nutrients from sewage wastes entering that portion of the lagoon directly.

--- SENTENCE 771 ---
The effects of the anticipated circulatory changes would be to reduce the growth rate and productivity of the phytoplankton community in the pelagic half of the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 772 ---
Population size could be expected to increase due to suppressed dilution.

--- SENTENCE 773 ---
Growth rates in the inner half of the lagoon would be relatively unaffected, although here again increasing algal concentrations would be expected as a result of decreased dilution.

--- SENTENCE 774 ---
This rather eutrophic area would expand to include a larger portion of the lagoon area.

--- SENTENCE 775 ---
Although the study was made during a period of low rainfall, stream and nearshore sampling indicated that phytoplankton productivity may respond in a positive manner to increase terrestrial runoff, specifically to nutrients in sewage carried by stream water.

--- SENTENCE 776 ---
The abundance and distribution of two of the major invertebrate faunal components of the Pala Lagoon benthic community--corals and echinoderms (sea urchins, sea cucumbers, etc.)--were assessed.

--- SENTENCE 777 ---
Corals formed the physical base for the ecosystem in the outer portion of the lagoon and almost all of the biotic components of the systems were intimately associated with them.

--- SENTENCE 778 ---
Therefore any perturbations affecting the coral (such as dredging) can be expected to have a profound effect on the rest of the community for an extended period.

--- SENTENCE 779 ---
Corals flourish in most of the areas where the dredging is proposed with the exception of those places dredged between 1959 and 1961 for airport construction.

--- SENTENCE 780 ---
On the reef flat adjacent to Coconut Point, a distinct zonation of corals was noted reflecting adaptation to the environmental gradient found across the reef.

--- SENTENCE 781 ---
Corals in portions of Pala Lagoon are exposed to extremely high temperatures and this parameter may be a factor limiting the present distribution of corals.

--- SENTENCE 782 ---
Temperature is influenced by circulation and the magnitude of tidal changes in the lagoon and a decrease in circulation coupled with a drop in the low tide level would probably cause increased mortality to the corals followed by a decrease in the biomass of fish and other organisms associated with corals.

--- SENTENCE 783 ---
The distribution of echinoderms related directly to the substrate type, with temperature and tidal extremes undoubtedly being influencing factors.

--- SENTENCE 784 ---
The collection of fish eggs and larvae in Pala Lagoon confirms its role as a nursery ground for both resident fishes and those outside the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 785 ---
The gobiid fish larvae were dominant among identifiable fish and the adults were among the conspicuous residents of all parts of

Pala Lagoon, particularly in the inner portions.

--- SENTENCE 786 ---
Increased residence time of water in the lagoon would result in environmental stress that would probably be detrimental to delicate fish larvae.

--- SENTENCE 787 ---
The distribution, diversity, and relative abundance of fish were roughly assessed by sampling from six stations distributed from the northwestern shore of the inner lagoon to a point near the outer ocean reef front.

--- SENTENCE 788 ---
A general gradient of species diversity, numbers, and probably total biomass of fish was observed between the two extreme stations.

--- SENTENCE 789 ---
A dredged area approximately the same distance from the open ocean was compared with a non-dredged area and the latter had more than twice the number of species than the former.

--- SENTENCE 790 ---
The planned dredging would probably initially reduce the number of species in the area, but the expected long-range effect would be one in which the coral repopulated-provided an adequate substrate was available--and the biomass and diversity of fish would be equal to or greater than the original populations, at least in the vicinity of the dredged area.

--- SENTENCE 791 ---
Pala Lagoon presently supports minor subsistence fishing and it is used as a source of dredged fill material.

--- SENTENCE 792 ---
The harbor proposed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers was reviewed from the standpoint of its effect upon the environment and its usefulness as a facility for people living on western Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 793 ---
The proposed harbor would accommodate 350 trailer-type craft, 10 inboard-outboard pleasure boats, and 40 sailboats and would cost an estimated \$1,490,000.

--- SENTENCE 794 ---
The basis of the estimate for this number of boats is questioned.

--- SENTENCE 795 ---
In view of the predicted detrimental biotic affects and other uncertainties related to the proposed project, an alternate proposal for a more modest shallow-draft harbor is suggested.

--- SENTENCE 796 ---
This alternate plan would require some deepening and widening of the channel entrance, retention of a sill in the channel, marking of the channel, and establishing a marked turning basin.

--- SENTENCE 797 ---
In addition a boat ramp, loading docks, and vehicle parking facilities are recommended.

--- SENTENCE 798 ---
This alternate plan would minimize the expected environmental impact while providing a safe and conveninet facility for shallow-draft boat owners.

#### RECOMMENDATIONS

####

--- SENTENCE 799 ---
It is recommended:

- 1. that the harbor, proposed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, not be dredged because of the probable overall detrimental effects it will have on the biota of Pala Lagoon.
- 2. that the present sill (described in this report) near the lagoon entrance not be altered as it would have a detrimental effect on the biota of Pala Lagoon.

- that an assessment be made of the present and future boat use pattern by the residents of the west end of Tutuila to determine if a significant number of shallow-draft vessels might benefit from a harbor designed specifically to serve them in Pala Lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 800 ---
If this assessment indicates that such a shallow-draft facility is warranted, then the alternate proposal presented in this report should be considered.

--- SENTENCE 801 ---
- 4. that an attempt be made to restrict the flow of raw sewage into Pala Lagoon and/or full cognizance be taken of the dangers to the health of those persons swimming in and collecting seafood from the highly polluted portions of Pala Lagoon.

## Appendix A. Microbiological Requirements Applicable to Particular Water Types \*

#### Applicable to: Class AA waters

The median coliform bacteria shall not exceed 70 per 100 ml, nor shall samples exceed 230 per 100 ml at any time.

#### Applicable to: Classes A, 1 & 2 waters

The median coliform bacteria shall not exceed 1,000 per 100 ml, nor shall more than 10 percent of the samples exceed 2,400 per 100 ml.

--- SENTENCE 925 ---
Fecal coliform content shall not exceed an arithmetic average of 200/100 ml during any 30-day period nor shall more than 10 percent of the samples exceed 400/100 ml in the same time period.

--- SENTENCE 926 ---
For such portion of Class 1 waters from which water is withdrawn for distribution for drinking water supply or food processing following simple chlorination, the fecal coliform content shall not exceed an arithmetic average of 20/100 ml during any calendar month.

#### Applicable to: Class B waters

Fecal coliform content shall not exceed an arithmetic average of 400/100 ml during any 30-day period nor shall more than 10 percent of the samples exceed 1,000/100 ml in the same time period.

--- SENTENCE 927 ---

#### Appendix B. Classification of Water Uses

Public Health Regulations: Department of Health, State of Hawaii, Chapter 37-A. Water Quality Standards

#### A. Classification of Coastal Water Uses

Coastal waters are classified in accordance with the uses to be protected in each class as follows:

#### 1.

--- SENTENCE 929 ---
Class AA waters

The uses to be protected in this class of waters are oceanographic research, propagation of shellfish and marine life, conservation of coral reefs, and wilderness areas and aesthetic enjoyment

--- SENTENCE 930 ---
It is the objective of this class of waters that they remain in as nearly their natural, pristine state as possible with an absolute minimum of pollution from any source.

--- SENTENCE 931 ---
To the extent possible, the wilderness character of such areas shall be protected.

--- SENTENCE 932 ---
No zones of mixing will be permitted in these waters.

--- SENTENCE 933 ---
The classification of any water area as Class AA shall not preclude other uses of such waters compatible with these objectives and in conformance with the standards applicable to them.

#### 2.

--- SENTENCE 934 ---
The uses to be protected in this class of waters are recreational, including fishing, swimming, bathing and other water-contact sports and aesthetic enjoyment.

--- SENTENCE 935 ---
It is the objective for this class of waters that their use for recreational purposes and aesthetic enjoyment not be limited in any way.

--- SENTENCE 936 ---
Such waters shall be kept clean of any trash, solid materials or oils and shall not act as receiving waters for any effluent which has not received the best practicable treatment or control compatible with the standards established for this class.

--- SENTENCE 937 ---
Class B waters

The uses to be protected in this class of waters are small boat harbors, commercial, shipping and industrial, bait fishing and aesthetic enjoyment.

--- SENTENCE 938 ---
It is the objective for this class of waters that discharges of any pollutant be controlled to the maximum degree possible and that sewage and industrial effluents receive the best practicable treatment or control compatible for the standards established for this class.

--- SENTENCE 939 ---
The Class B designation shall apply only to a limited area next to boat docking facilities in bays and harbors.

--- SENTENCE 940 ---
The rest of the water area in such bay or harbor shall be Class A unless given some other specific designation in Section 5.

#### B. Classification of Fresh Water Uses

Fresh waters are classified in accordance with the uses to be protected as follows:

#### 1.

--- SENTENCE 941 ---
Class 1 waters

The uses to be protected in this class of waters are drinking water supply and food processing.

--- SENTENCE 942 ---
It is the objective of this class of waters that they remain as nearly the natural state as possible with an absolute minimum of pollution from any source.

--- SENTENCE 943 ---
Class 2 waters

The uses to be protected in this class of waters are bathing, swimming, recreation, growth and propagation of fish and other aquatic life and agricultural and industrial water supply.

--- SENTENCE 944 ---
It is the objective of this class of waters that their use for recreational purposes, propagation of fish and other aquatic life and agricultural and industrial water supply not be limited in any way.

--- SENTENCE 945 ---
Such waters shall be kept clean of trash, solid materials or oils and shall not act as receiving waters for any effluent which has not received the best practicable treatment compatible with the standards established for this class.
