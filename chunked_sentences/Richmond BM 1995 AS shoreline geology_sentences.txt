#### **ABSTRACT**

Tutuila, American Samoa, is a high volcanic island with a highly embayed and irregular coastline.

--- SENTENCE 30 ---
A narrow fringing reef borders most of the coast.

--- SENTENCE 31 ---
As part of this study, 47 sites along the coast of Tutuila were examined using a combination of brief field visits (where accessible), aerial reconnaissance overflight, and reference to selected maps, vertical aerial photographs, and publications.

--- SENTENCE 32 ---
This report contains a brief review of some of the relevant geologic parameters, descriptions of the 7 research areas and individual 47 sites, and a discussion of the "natural" coast.

--- SENTENCE 33 ---
Coastal plains, composed of a mixture of terrigenous and marine sediment, occupy the heads of most embayments.

--- SENTENCE 34 ---
Headlands of volcanic rocks, often forming steep cliffs, separate the embayments.

--- SENTENCE 35 ---
Beaches, wetlands, alluvial fans, stream valleys, and mangrove forests are the main physiographic components of the coastal plains.

--- SENTENCE 36 ---
Depositional beach morphological features include pocket beaches, barrier spits, tombolos, and small flood-deltas at stream mouths.

--- SENTENCE 37 ---
Somewhat atypical environments on Tutuila include: a) the deep harbor of Pago Pago, b) the shallow lagoon, barrier reef, and extended barrier spit in the Pala Lagoon / Coconut Point area, c) recent basalt flows forming a low cliffed coastline with marginal fringing reef from Fogagogo to Sogi, and, d)

--- SENTENCE 38 ---
the extensive coral rubble coastal plain of 'Aunu'u Island.

--- SENTENCE 39 ---
The coast of Tutuila has been shaped by volcanic activity, changes in relative sea level, reef growth, erosion of the hinterland, and more recently by storms and human activities.

--- SENTENCE 40 ---
Tutuila lies within the easterly tradewind zone where winds, and hence waves, are consistently high along east- and southeast-facing coasts.

--- SENTENCE 41 ---
Long distance swell waves and tropical storms are less common but can affect any part of the Tutuila coastline.

--- SENTENCE 42 ---
Waves and wave-generated currents are responsible for transporting reef detritus landward resulting in beach deposits dominated by carbonate sand.

--- SENTENCE 43 ---
Approximately 20% of the Tutuila coast is fronted by beaches.

--- SENTENCE 44 ---
Where villages, buildings, or roads have been threatened by erosion, these beaches are bordered by some type of engineering structure such as revetments (sloping structure), seawalls (vertical structure), or loosely placed rip-rap.

--- SENTENCE 45 ---
This shoreline armoring has caused a severe reduction in the amount of beached coastline available for traditional uses.

--- SENTENCE 46 ---
While engineering structures may stabilize the position of the shoreline they often result in the loss of "normal" recreational beach shoreline.

--- SENTENCE 47 ---
Although the proliferation of shoreline engineering structures may be necessary to protect investments, it has led to a decline in the natural state of Tutuila beaches.

#### INTRODUCTION

--- SENTENCE 48 ---
Tutuila is the largest island of American Samoa and the center of government and commerce.

--- SENTENCE 49 ---
The 1980 population was about 30,000 (American Samoa Government, 1981).

--- SENTENCE 50 ---
It is a high volcanic island with a maximum elevation of 653 m (2,142') and is roughly 32 km long (20 mi.) and 10 km wide (6 mi.) covering an area of about 137 km² (53 sq. mi.; Fig. 1).

--- SENTENCE 51 ---
Of the 125 km of coast (78 mi.) approximately 60% is bordered by a fringing reef and about 20% of the natural coast was fronted by beach (much of this coast is now fronted by engineering structures).

--- SENTENCE 52 ---
The rugged interior is covered by dense tropical rain forest.

--- SENTENCE 53 ---
A broad gently-rolling plain has developed on older basalt flows in the Leone-Tafuna area (detailed locations and topography are shown in map segments of Appendix II).

--- SENTENCE 54 ---
Low-lying coastal plains of relatively low relief occupy a narrow fringe around the island, separating the rugged highlands from the reefs.

--- SENTENCE 55 ---
The islands of the Samoan Archipelago are formed primarily from large gently-sloping shield volcanoes and their associated volcanics (Stearns, 1944).

--- SENTENCE 56 ---
Exposed rocks include basalt and andesite lavas, tuff and tuff cones, plugs and dykes, all of which form the base upon which the coral reefs have developed.

--- SENTENCE 57 ---
Erosion of the volcanics has resulted in seacliffs, stream valleys, and alluvial plains.

--- SENTENCE 58 ---
Eruptives range in age from Pliocene to Late Quaternary (Stearns, 1944; American Samoa Government, 1981).

--- SENTENCE 59 ---
Recent basalt flows in the Leone-Tafuna area flowed over a fringing reef creating the most extensive gently-sloping land area of Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 60 ---
The shoreline of American Samoa has been described in detail in the American Samoa Shoreline Inventory (U.S. Army Corps of Engineers; 1980, 1989).

--- SENTENCE 61 ---
These two comprehensive reports cover most of American Samoa's developed shoreline, with the emphasis on engineering structures and erosion but also containing much other useful information.

#### Methodology and Analysis Conditions

Forty-seven sites were chosen for detailed study by the Coastal Resources Inventory Team in consultation with government representatives of American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 62 ---
These sites were chosen to represent the variety of coastal environments on Tutuila with emphasis on areas of cultural, economic, or environmental significance.

--- SENTENCE 63 ---
This report describes the coastal geology from these sites and is based on field work conducted from April 8-16, 1992.

--- SENTENCE 64 ---
All Tutuila sites accessible by road, and the sites on 'Aunu'u were visited during this study.

--- SENTENCE 65 ---
Sites that are accessible only by boat were not examined, these include: Pola Island (20), Agapie Cove (21), Fagafue Bay (23), and A'asu (Massacre Bay; 24).

--- SENTENCE 66 ---
Work undertaken at each site included: a) reconnaissance-scale observations of shoreline type, coastal plain characteristics, adjacent reef features, and anthropogenic development; b) reference photographs of the coast; and c) narrated videotape of the coast.

--- SENTENCE 67 ---
The site locations are shown in Figure 1 and Table I lists some of their physical characteristics.

--- SENTENCE 68 ---
Appendix I includes individual site descriptions for the 47 sites and Appendix II shows the site locations on segments of the USGS 1:24,000 scale topographic map of Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 69 ---
The objectives of this work are to provide an accurate physical characterization of the natural coastal system to compliment the biological information collected on the adjacent reefs and coastal vegetation by other members of the team.

--- SENTENCE 70 ---
This information

should assist other workers on the natural environment of American Samoa with their management and planning activities.

--- SENTENCE 71 ---
Climatology (after American Samoa Government, 1981)

Tutuila lies within the easterly tradewind zone where winds from the east and southeast predominate.

--- SENTENCE 72 ---
The tradewinds are more consistent in the winter (June - September) months with more variable winds and greater rainfall occurring during the summer months (December - March).

--- SENTENCE 73 ---
Tropical storms and cyclones are also more likely to occur during summer months and have an average recurrence interval of about one every 2.5 years.

--- SENTENCE 74 ---
Although little is known about ocean currents in the area, the tradewinds drive a general westward-flowing current which may undergo local reversals as it passes the islands.

--- SENTENCE 75 ---
The average diurnal tide range is 0.73 m (2.4').

--- SENTENCE 76 ---
Breaking waves are the primary agent of mechanical reef breakdown and transportation of reef debris to the shore.

--- SENTENCE 77 ---
The most common waves striking the shore are tradewind-generated and arrive from the eastern sectors.

--- SENTENCE 78 ---
These waves are typically low and short-period.

--- SENTENCE 79 ---
Long-distance swell waves, originating from either Antarctic or North Pacific storms, are less common but can have very large heights when they reach the shores of Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 80 ---
Swell waves can affect all coasts of the island.

--- SENTENCE 81 ---
Waves associated with tropical cyclones are probably the most destructive to strike Tutuila because they are of high energy and coincident with elevated water levels due to storm surge and wind set-up.

--- SENTENCE 82 ---
Tsunamis are relatively rare and probably only have a significant effect within the embayments, such as Pago Pago Harbor.

#### CHARACTERISTICS OF THE NATURAL COASTAL SYSTEM

Most of the shoreline features of Tutuila are geologically young.

--- SENTENCE 84 ---
They are Holocene in age, having formed during approximately the last 6,000 years, the period where sea level stabilized near its present position.

--- SENTENCE 85 ---
About 18,000 years ago, during the last glacial maximum, sea level was approximately 125 m below present level and subsequently rose rapidly to its present position as the glacial ice melted.

--- SENTENCE 86 ---
The Quaternary has been marked by significant changes in sea level position as the ice caps advance and recede.

--- SENTENCE 87 ---
Presently, there is a warm period where the ice caps are relatively small and sea level is high.

--- SENTENCE 88 ---
This is significant because, in geologic terms, coastal landforms have had only a relatively brief period (~ 6,000 yrs) to develop at the present base level (mean sea level; msl).

--- SENTENCE 89 ---
Coral reefs thrive in warm, clear tropical water.

--- SENTENCE 90 ---
Framework-building corals are limited to between the approximate mean low-water level and shallow water depths (generally < 20m).

--- SENTENCE 91 ---
Corals and the organisms living in the reef provide much of the sediment that forms the coastal deposits in tropical areas.

--- SENTENCE 92 ---
The reefs also provide protection to the coast by dissipating wave energy.

--- SENTENCE 93 ---
Therefore, the stability of the shoreline is dependent to a large extent upon the health of the reefs.

--- SENTENCE 94 ---
Any environmental changes, such as a decrease in water quality due to excess turbidity or nutrient loading, can ultimately have a negative impact on shoreline stability.

--- SENTENCE 95 ---
Reef health and shoreline stability are complex issues whose interrelationships are poorly understood at present.

--- SENTENCE 96 ---
Fringing reefs are the common reef form on Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 97 ---
They consist of a reef flat extending out from the shoreline and terminating in a reef crest.

--- SENTENCE 98 ---
A steep reef front marks the seaward margin.

--- SENTENCE 99 ---
A shallow submarine terrace commonly borders the reef front and is often visible in the vertical aerial photographs.

--- SENTENCE 100 ---
There are two primary types of coastline on Tutuila: a) depositional coasts consisting of coastal plains, beaches, wetlands and mangrove forests, and b) erosional coasts characterized by seacliffs.

--- SENTENCE 101 ---
Although there is overlap between the two, such as a cliffed coast fronted by a narrow cobble or sand beach, in general they are distinguishable.

--- SENTENCE 102 ---
Moreover, a beach undergoing erosion is still primarily a depositional feature.

#### **Beach and Coastal Plain Environments**

Coastal plains are low-lying, low-relief depositional features formed by a combination of alluvial fan, stream valley, and beach-building processes.

--- SENTENCE 103 ---
Erosion of hillsides results in alluvial fan deposition, stream flooding causes overwash deposition on floodplains, and storm waves and strong winds result in deposition above normal tidal limits.

--- SENTENCE 104 ---
Typically, terrestrial deposits mark the landward margins of the plains and marine (beach) sediments flank the seaward margins.

--- SENTENCE 105 ---
The highly embayed coastline of Tutuila is characteristic of areas where drowned stream valleys are filled with sediment.

--- SENTENCE 106 ---
Several types of depositional coastal morphological features (Fig. 2) are widespread on Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 107 ---
*Pocket beaches* are beaches bounded by rocky headlands and are generally arcuate in outline.

--- SENTENCE 108 ---
Adjacent pocket beaches can have either similar characteristics or can vary markedly as a result of isolated or unique processes affecting them.

--- SENTENCE 109 ---
*Barrier spits* typically form at stream mouths.

--- SENTENCE 110 ---
They have an "attached" end where the spit is a continuation of the shoreline and a "free" end that recurves and typically "points" in the downdrift direction.

--- SENTENCE 111 ---
Where an offshore obstruction such as a sea stack or

small islet occurs, the adjacent depositional shoreline commonly forms a seaward bulge or tombolo.

--- SENTENCE 112 ---
The obstruction causes a reduction in wave energy reaching the shore behind it resulting in reduced sediment transport rates and, hence, deposition of sediment.

--- SENTENCE 113 ---
Streams that debouche directly onto the reef flat often form small flood deltas.

--- SENTENCE 114 ---
During high rainfall events, ephemeral streams deposit their sediment loads as lobate fans on the reef flat because the flows are no longer constrained by the stream valley walls.

--- SENTENCE 115 ---
These deposits are rapidly reworked with the finer sediment being transported alongshore leaving behind a lag deposit of gravel-size material.

--- SENTENCE 116 ---
Coastal plain sediment type is dependent upon material availability and energy levels at the depositional site.

--- SENTENCE 117 ---
As a general rule coarser sediment, such as gravel, is associated with deposition under higher energy conditions than fine-grained sediment, however, this is subject to size of material available.

--- SENTENCE 118 ---
Reefs produce sediment from limemud through coral-rubble size ranges.

--- SENTENCE 119 ---
Likewise streams and alluvial fans incorporate a wide-range of sediment sizes.

--- SENTENCE 120 ---
Therefore, because there is a wide size range of available sediment on Tutuila, sediment texture can be used as a rough guide for relative energy level at the depositional site with coarser material indicating higher energy and finer material indicative of a more protected environment.

--- SENTENCE 121 ---
Coastal plains are extremely important as sites for villages and roads.

--- SENTENCE 122 ---
Most stream valleys that have well-developed coastal plains on Tutuila also have a village.

--- SENTENCE 123 ---
Embayments developed at major stream valleys and headed by coastal plains with established villages include: Pago Pago Harbor (numerous), Fagaitua Bay, Aoa Bay, Masefau, Afono Bay, Vatia Bay, Fagasa Bay, Massacre Bay, Poloa Bay, Amanave Bay, Nua-Se'etaga Bay, Leone Bay, and Pala Lagoon/Nu'uuli.

--- SENTENCE 124 ---
In addition there are numerous smaller embayments with well-developed but narrow coastal plains.

--- SENTENCE 125 ---
Areas with predominantly carbonate sediment marine-derived coastal plains include perched beaches such as Vaitogi and Fogagogo, and the broad beach-ridge plains between Matuli Pt. and Fagasa Pt. (the eastern villages of Alao and Tula).

--- SENTENCE 126 ---
The south and west coasts of 'Aunu'u Island are also a broad beach-ridge plain built in the lee of a recent tuff cone.

--- SENTENCE 127 ---
Coastal Wetlands (adapted from American Samoa Government, 1981).

--- SENTENCE 128 ---
The two types of coastal wetlands occurring in American Samoa are coastal marsh and mangrove forest.

--- SENTENCE 129 ---
Coastal marshes, composed of herbaceous vegetation, are isolated depressions with fresh to slightly brackish water.

--- SENTENCE 130 ---
The sediments typically have a high organic content with either a mud, sand, or gravel matrix.

--- SENTENCE 131 ---
Because this habitat is used extensively for taro cultivation nearly all natural areas have been modified with the exception of the marsh within 'Aunu'u crater on 'Aunu'u.

--- SENTENCE 132 ---
In contrast, mangrove forest occurs in open brackish or saline environments.

--- SENTENCE 133 ---
The woody mangrove species present in American Samoa include *Bruguiera gymnorhiza* and *Rhizophora samoense*.

--- SENTENCE 134 ---
Areas where mangrove forest remains include Pala Lagoon, Masefau, Aoa, and Pala Lake on 'Aunu'u.

--- SENTENCE 135 ---
The sediments are typically organic-rich peaty mud and sand several meters or more in thickness.

--- SENTENCE 136 ---
Mangrove forests are important for maintaining shoreline stability and are habitats for many juvenile marine fishes.

--- SENTENCE 137 ---
They should be preserved wherever possible.

#### Cliffed Coasts

As soon as volcanic islands form, terrestrial and marine erosion processes begin attacking the newly formed land.

--- SENTENCE 141 ---
Seacliffs can be created by wave attack along the shore or by mass wasting (landslides) associated with caldera buildup and collapse.

--- SENTENCE 142 ---
Coastal cliffs are often remnants of giant landslides that create humongous debris fields scattered on the ocean floor (Moore and others, 1989).

--- SENTENCE 143 ---
Where the steep slopes continue offshore, there is no substrate capable of supporting broad reef development and the cliffs are maintained.

--- SENTENCE 144 ---
In some cases seacliffs may also be constructional in origin.

--- SENTENCE 145 ---
For example, recent basalt flows in southwest Tutuila between Nu'uuli and Leone (Area 6) flowed over older barrier reef deposits forming cliffs along their seaward margin where they poured over the reef front (Stearns, 1944).

--- SENTENCE 146 ---
Most of the north coast of Tutuila is cliffed with little or no fringing reef development with the exception of the deep embayments at Aoa, Masefau, Afono, Vatia, Fagasa, Fagafue, Aasu, Aoloau, and Maloata.

--- SENTENCE 147 ---
Cliffs of 60 m (200 ft) are common with some steep hillsides extending for 300 m (1,000 ft).

--- SENTENCE 148 ---
The south coast is also cliffed however, in general, the cliffs are lower, less rugged, and interrupted by more prominent valley development than along the north coast.

--- SENTENCE 149 ---
Nearly continuous cliffs occur along most of the coast between Nu'uuli and Leone, consisting of low (5 - 25 m; 20 - 80 ft) basalt cliffs and high cliffs of the old craters at Fagatele and Fagalua (Larsen) Bays.

#### AREA DESCRIPTIONS

#### Area 1: Pago Pago Harbor

Area 1 incorporates Pago Pago Harbor extending from Niuloa Point (Point Distress) on the western entrance to Breakers Point at the eastern entrance.

--- SENTENCE 151 ---
This protected harbor contains coastal inventory sites 1 through 4 and 45 to 47.

--- SENTENCE 152 ---
Rugged volcanic mountains surround the harbor.

--- SENTENCE 153 ---
Coastal plains of predominantly alluvial material are best developed in stream valleys (Plate 1), notably the Vaipito and Lalolamauta Valleys, and along the northern harbor boundary.

--- SENTENCE 154 ---
Most of the shoreline within Pago Pago Harbor is armored; seawalls (vertical structures) and revetments (sloping structures) are common features.

--- SENTENCE 155 ---
Reclaimed coastal land is also common and includes a park in Fagaalu, hotel development at Nu'tutai Point (Rainmaker Hotel), the main dock and customs house, the playing fields at the head of the harbor, the cannery docks, and sanitary landfill at Anasosopo.

--- SENTENCE 156 ---
Based on examination of aerial photographs, nearshore reef-flat dredging operations appear to have taken place opposite Utulei village between Tulutulu and Goat Island Points.

#### Area 2: Breakers point to Cape Matatula

The coast in Area 2 can be subdivided into three separate segments based on variations in coastal morphology and exposure: 1) Breakers Point to Matuli Point, 2) Matuli Point to Cape Matatula, and 3) 'Aunu'u Island.

--- SENTENCE 157 ---
1) Breakers Point to Matuli Point.

--- SENTENCE 158 ---
The southeast coast of Tutuila is characterized by a moderate to steep volcanic coast broken by alluvial valleys and pocket beaches fronted by a narrow to moderately wide (up to 500 m wide) fringing reef (Plates 2 & 3).

--- SENTENCE 159 ---
Numerous streams enter the sea along the coast with the larger streams commonly associated with alluvial valley and coastal plain deposits.

--- SENTENCE 160 ---
Most of the coast faces to the south or southeast except for the eastern margin of Fagaitua Bay which faces west.

--- SENTENCE 161 ---
Most of the coast therefore, is exposed to southeasterly tradewinds.

--- SENTENCE 162 ---
With the exception of Cape Fogausa, the main highway follows the coast very closely for most of the length of Area 2.

--- SENTENCE 163 ---
To protect the road, seawalls and revetments of various designs and degrees of structural integrity have been constructed along the majority of the coast fronting the coastal road.

--- SENTENCE 164 ---
Where these structures occur, it presumably signifies an earlier erosion problem necessitating remedial action.

--- SENTENCE 165 ---
Other erosion indicators include recent backbeach scarps, World War II pillboxes that are undermined and tilted, and beachrock exposures on the lower beachface.

--- SENTENCE 166 ---
Overall, shoreline erosion is a moderate to severe problem with the continued loss of recreational beaches likely.

--- SENTENCE 167 ---
The beaches are composed mostly of carbonate sand and gravel with minor amounts of terrigenous material.

--- SENTENCE 168 ---
They are exclusively of the pocket beach type, typically concave seaward in plan view, but occasionally convex seaward or near-linear such as at Lauliifou, Aumi, Avaio, and Utumea.

--- SENTENCE 169 ---
Beach heights, or in most cases elevation of the coastal road because the natural beach ridge has been modified, varies from a low at Alofau of about 2 m above mlwl (mean low-water level) to a high of nearly 5 m above

mlwl at Lauliifou and Aganoa.

--- SENTENCE 170 ---
Beach ridge height appears to be directly related to exposure with higher elevations occurring at more exposed localities.

--- SENTENCE 171 ---
In addition to road protection structures, other man-made features include a small boat harbor at Auasi and former dredging sites on the inner reef flat at Alofau and Fagaitua.

--- SENTENCE 172 ---
The harbor was completed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers in 1981 and was possibly responsible for accelerated erosion at adjacent beaches.

--- SENTENCE 173 ---
Revetments were constructed to stabilize the shoreline.

--- SENTENCE 174 ---
Former dredge pits at Alofau and Fagaitua are visible as rectangular-shaped depressions and causeway remnants on the reef flat.

--- SENTENCE 175 ---
Both areas are presently extensively bordered by coastal protection works.

--- SENTENCE 176 ---
2) Matuli Point to Cape Matatula.

--- SENTENCE 177 ---
This area does not contain any of the offshore inventory sites but it is extremely important as a coastal site because it represents the longest, relatively undisturbed, opencoast sandy shoreline on Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 178 ---
This windward coast faces east directly towards the predominant tradewind direction.

--- SENTENCE 179 ---
A gently-curved carbonate sand beach extends about 2 km from Lealaeli Hill to Fagasa Point (Plate 4).

--- SENTENCE 180 ---
The villages of Alao and Tula occur on coastal plain deposits, extending nearly 500 m inland in places, and are separated by a ridge (Tapepe Ridge) of Olomoana Volcanics.

--- SENTENCE 181 ---
The Ma'alatetele Rocks appear to be the offshore extension of Tapepe Ridge and result in a cuspate headland (convex seaward bulge in the shoreline).

--- SENTENCE 182 ---
The beach is composed of medium to coarse carbonate sand and ranges in elevation from 2.5 m above mlwl at Tula to about 4.5 m above mlwl at Alao.

--- SENTENCE 183 ---
Beachrock occurs at several locations and several backbeach scarps were observed but, in general, the erosion problem does not appear to be severe.

--- SENTENCE 184 ---
The fringing reef averages about 100 m wide except in the vicinity of the Ma'alatetele Rocks where it widens to about 250 m.

The coastal road follows the shoreline closely up to Tula where it turns inland.

--- SENTENCE 185 ---
Numerous buildings are on the seaward side of the road in Tula, elsewhere they occur on the landward side.

--- SENTENCE 186 ---
Shore protection structures occur in front of the school in Tula and to the north of Maliuga Point.

--- SENTENCE 187 ---
The eastern half of Aunu'u Island is the semi-circular remnant of a cinder cone with steep seaward slopes and very little reef development.

--- SENTENCE 188 ---
The western (leeward) half of Aunu'u is depositional consisting of a prominent coastal plain of carbonate beach ridge deposits and backbeach wetlands.

--- SENTENCE 189 ---
The semi-concentric beach ridge deposits form a hemispherical, convex seaward, border around the island.

--- SENTENCE 190 ---
The beaches range from a poorly-sorted coral rubble along north and south stretches to coarse carbonate sand on the leeward (western) shore.

--- SENTENCE 191 ---
Beach ridge heights up to 5 m above mlwl occur, and beachrock outcrops are common on the north and south shores.

--- SENTENCE 192 ---
A small-boat harbor was constructed on the eastern shore and at present it is bordered by erosional backbeach scarps on the adjacent beaches.

--- SENTENCE 193 ---
There is a low seawall constructed on the backbeach near the school on the south shore of the island.

#### Area 3: Cape Matatula to Pola Island

The northeast coast of Tutuila is characterized by high cliffs separated by broad, flat, alluvial valleys which form embayments within the rugged coast (Plates 4 & 5).

--- SENTENCE 194 ---
The shoreline is either cliffed, with a narrow or occasionally absent shore platform, or, comprised of beach ridges and valley fill within the embayments.

--- SENTENCE 195 ---
The low beach ridges are composed of a mixture of carbonate and terrigenous sediment.

--- SENTENCE 196 ---
Beach-ridge height varies with exposure from a low of about 1.5 m at Aoa (14), Masefau (17), and Vatia (18) to a high of nearly 4 m at exposed Sailele.

--- SENTENCE 197 ---
No engineering structures were observed along the cliffed coasts, however bare rock surfaces attest to a high landslide susceptibility, which is exasperated by wave abrasion at the base of the seacliffs.

--- SENTENCE 198 ---
There are some protective structures constructed within the embayments to protect roads and buildings.

--- SENTENCE 199 ---
Revetments occur in NW Vatia Bay, a small one in Afono Bay, and along the road in Masefau and Aoa Bays.

--- SENTENCE 200 ---
In general, shoreline erosion does not appear critical except for Masefau Bay where there is nearly 800 m of seawall.

--- SENTENCE 201 ---
Much of the cliffed coast plunges directly into the sea with little or no reef development.

--- SENTENCE 202 ---
Fringing reefs line the embayments with a maximum width of about 400 m in Aoa Bay; average widths are less than 150 m.

#### Area 4: Pola Island to Cape Taputapu.

--- SENTENCE 203 ---
NW Tutuila (Area 4) is morphologically very similar to NE Tutuila (Area 3).

--- SENTENCE 204 ---
Rugged cliffs, which plunge directly into the sea, are separated by embayments formed at the mouths of stream valleys (Plate 6).

--- SENTENCE 205 ---
In general, the embayments are smaller and less indented than those in Area 3 resulting in greater wave exposure as indicated by numerous gravel beaches.

--- SENTENCE 206 ---
A terrigenous gravel beach occurs at Fagasa Bay (22) and coral gravel beaches occur at Fagamalo (25) and Poloa Bay (27).

--- SENTENCE 207 ---
Beach heights are about 2.4 m in Fagasa and over 6 m at Poloa.

--- SENTENCE 208 ---
Although Poloa is located on the leeward side of the island the high beach height indicates exposure to occasional large waves.

--- SENTENCE 209 ---
Most of the shoreline is remote and accessible only by sea.

--- SENTENCE 210 ---
Villages are few and limited to stream-mouth embayments.

--- SENTENCE 211 ---
Consequently, coastal engineering structures are few, notably a road protection structure in Fagasa Bay and a massive revetment protecting the school at Poloa.

--- SENTENCE 212 ---
As in Area 3, the fringing reefs are best developed within embayments where a suitable sloping platform occurs.

--- SENTENCE 213 ---
The open coast is typically comprised of seacliffs with little or no adjacent reef development.

#### Area 5: Cape Taputapu to Logologo Pt.

--- SENTENCE 214 ---
Southwest Tutuila is an area with numerous protected embayments backed by low-lying land, which provides the setting for many coastal villages (Plates 6 & 7).

--- SENTENCE 215 ---
As elsewhere on Tutuila the embayments form at the mouths of coastal streams.

--- SENTENCE 216 ---
The largest of these is Leone Bay, developed at the mouth of Leafu Stream and an area where a small coastal mangrove forest is developed.

--- SENTENCE 217 ---
The embayments and associated arcuate beaches are separated by rocky headlands and seacliffs.

--- SENTENCE 218 ---
Terrigenous sand is the dominant beach

sediment within Leone Bay whereas carbonate sand predominates along the other beaches.

--- SENTENCE 219 ---
A narrow finger of beachrock in line with the current shoreline extends across the mouth of Leafu Stream at Leone, which probably denotes a former shoreline position of the barrier spit and beach.

--- SENTENCE 220 ---
Shoreline engineering structures are intermittent and typically consist of loosely dumped basalt boulders.

--- SENTENCE 221 ---
Natural basalt boulders and basalt benches also back the shoreline in this area.

--- SENTENCE 222 ---
The fringing reef is well-developed and nearly continuous.

--- SENTENCE 223 ---
Maximum reef widths are about 300 m in Leone Bay and opposite Mu Pt.

--- SENTENCE 224 ---
Storm deposits of reef-derived rubble (Cyclone Ofa?) occur on the reef flat opposite Leone.

#### Area 6: Logologo Pt. to Fogagogo

The southernmost shoreline of Tutuila can be distinguished from the rest of the coast by the presence of an extensive lava seacliff of recent origin.

--- SENTENCE 225 ---
These basalt flows form a bench that varies from a few meters to nearly 30 m above the adjacent reef.

--- SENTENCE 226 ---
The relatively low-lying basalt flows are broken by high cliffs (up to 150 m) formed by the old craters at Fagatele and Larsen Bays.

--- SENTENCE 227 ---
Because access to the ocean is limited in this area there only a few villages with Vailoatai and Vaitogi (Plate 8) being the largest.

--- SENTENCE 228 ---
The beaches in Area 6 are perched on the basalt terraces above the high tide level, presumably the result of storm wind and wave action.

--- SENTENCE 229 ---
Beaches occur on less than 10% of this cliff-dominated coast which prevents the accumulation of extensive coastal deposits.

--- SENTENCE 230 ---
Coastal engineering structures are not present because there are few developments to protect and the basalt shorelines are very stable.

--- SENTENCE 231 ---
Reef flats and fringing reef are present in a limited manner only near Vailoatai.

#### Area 7: Fogagogo to Breakers Pt. (excluding Pago Pago Harbor).

--- SENTENCE 232 ---
Three types of coast occur in this area.

--- SENTENCE 233 ---
A basalt-cliffed shoreline stretching from Fogagogo to Matautuotafuna Pt. (Pt. Deceit).

--- SENTENCE 234 ---
This coast is essentially a continuation of Area 6 where recent basalt flows form steep cliffs fronted by little or no reef development.

--- SENTENCE 235 ---
Small perched beaches of storm-derived material are scattered on the basalt bench.

--- SENTENCE 236 ---
2) Shallow barrier reef and lagoon and barrier spit occur in the Pala Lagoon and Coconut Pt. area.

--- SENTENCE 237 ---
This is the largest shallow reef/barrier spit complex on the island.

--- SENTENCE 238 ---
The lagoon entrance and shallow reef have been extensively modified by the construction of the main International Airport (Plate 9), which has included reef flat dredging combined with land reclamation (thereby seriously affecting water exchange over the reef).

--- SENTENCE 239 ---
The Coconut Pt. barrier spit is composed mostly of carbonate sand and the morphology indicates a growth direction (net longshore drift direction) from northeast to southwest.

--- SENTENCE 240 ---
Most of the seaward edge of the spit shoreline is undergoing erosion with rates of 1 to 1.5 m/yr in the south and about 0.5 m/yr in the north (U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, 1980, 1989).

--- SENTENCE 241 ---
Crude basalt boulder seawalls have been constructed in an attempt to halt the erosion.

--- SENTENCE 242 ---
3) From Nu'uuli to Breakers Pt. is an embayed coastline with pocket beaches separated by headlands typical of most of Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 243 ---
The main coastal road parallels the shoreline and is protected by various seawall and revetment structures.

--- SENTENCE 244 ---
Villages nestled within small embayments occur at Faganeanea, Vasa'aiga, and Matu'u.

#### **DISCUSSION**

Tutuila has many characteristics typical of a slowly subsiding, relatively young, volcanic island such as drowned river valleys, fringing and incipient shallow barrier reefs, and narrow coastal plains.

--- SENTENCE 245 ---
The coastal plains are generally small in extent but are very important as sites for both traditional and modern anthropogenic development.

--- SENTENCE 246 ---
Coastal plains are composed primarily of unconsolidated sediment, and therefore are prone to rapid erosional and depositional events.

--- SENTENCE 247 ---
Extreme care should be exercised in the management of these features to prevent further loss of valuable beach and land areas.

--- SENTENCE 248 ---
There is about 26 km of beach shoreline on Tutuila (excluding inner Pago Pago Harbor) constituting nearly 20% of the total shoreline with pocket beaches being the predominant type of beach landform.

--- SENTENCE 249 ---
The majority of villages are sited on the coast and most of these villages, along with large segments of the connecting coastal road, are located on coastal plains composed mostly of back-beach deposits.

--- SENTENCE 250 ---
Because of their importance to human occupation, the coastal plains are one of the most significant landforms on Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 251 ---
Extensive segments of the Tutuila shoreline are fronted by engineering structures designed to protect adjacent land.

--- SENTENCE 252 ---
Where rigid structures are constructed on beaches, they essentially prevent the shoreline from migrating in response to extreme events.

--- SENTENCE 253 ---
Beaches typically erode during large storms and accrete during fair-weather conditions.

--- SENTENCE 254 ---
Fixed structures often interfere with this natural cycle of beach response and can prevent the long-term accretion of beach sediment.

--- SENTENCE 255 ---
Structures may stabilize the shoreline position and protect adjacent property but at the cost of an overall reduction in the amount of recreational beach.

--- SENTENCE 256 ---
This is clearly evident on Tutuila where the recent (over the last several decades) proliferation of seawalls and revetments has greatly reduced the amount of sandy shoreline.

--- SENTENCE 257 ---
With the exception of the windward (east) coast and isolated pocket beaches, most Tutuila beaches have at least a portion fronted by a structure, thereby reducing the natural, traditional (in a cultural sense), and recreational appeal of the beach.

--- SENTENCE 258 ---
The remaining natural beaches on Tutuila should be viewed as a valuable natural resource and protected accordingly.

--- SENTENCE 259 ---
Healthy, natural beaches are an essential ingredient to successful tourism in the Pacific and the cost of beach preservation is usually small when compared to beach value.

--- SENTENCE 260 ---
A few general conclusions and recommendations can be summarized as follows:

- 1)

--- SENTENCE 261 ---
Although Tutuila is a high volcanic island, most of the sediment composing the beaches is derived from the adjacent reef.

--- SENTENCE 262 ---
Therefore any activity, whether natural or human-induced, that affects the health of the reef and its ability to produce carbonate sediment may adversely affect the islands beaches.

--- SENTENCE 263 ---
In particular, changes in water quality due to excess nutrients (such as sewage disposal) and turbidity (from either on-land erosion or reef-flat mining) should be closely monitored.
- 2) Catastrophic storms are a natural and geologically frequent phenomena that are responsible for the much of the long-term geologic development of the coast.

--- SENTENCE 264 ---
Storms are a primary mechanism responsible for the deposition of sediment above normal tide limits.

--- SENTENCE 265 ---
The recognition and mapping of storm deposits can be used in some instances in the prevention and avoidance of costly damage associated with catastrophic events.

- 3)

--- SENTENCE 266 ---
Many of the embayed shorelines of Tutuila have been significantly modified by human activities.

--- SENTENCE 267 ---
Further coastal development should be carefully examined and designed to protect the diminishing "natural" embayed shorelines.

--- SENTENCE 268 ---
The headlands are in better condition except in areas where the coastal road follows extremely close to the shore.
- 4)

--- SENTENCE 269 ---
Recent evidence of shoreline erosion is widespread on Tutuila and the causes are poorly understood.

--- SENTENCE 270 ---
In some cases the erosion exhibited a possible link to human activities, in other areas there is no obvious causative factor.

--- SENTENCE 271 ---
The proliferation of shoreline engineering structures attests to the widespread nature of the problem.

--- SENTENCE 272 ---
Possible reasons for the apparent increase in erosion include: accelerated sea-level rise, a reduction in carbonate productivity of the reefs, beach sand mining, recent storm activity, and interference of natural processes by engineering structures.

--- SENTENCE 273 ---
Further research is required to document the extent and the magnitude of the problem in Samoa and the significant causes.
- 5)

--- SENTENCE 274 ---
Beaches are a natural buffer that protect the coast during extreme events, they are a recreational resource, and they have high cultural significance, hence, beach preservation should be a high priority among coastal planners and managers in Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 275 ---
All beach-sand mining should be discouraged and the potential adverse impacts of engineering structures should be carefully considered before any new structures are built.

--- SENTENCE 923 ---

--- SENTENCE 925 ---

--- SENTENCE 926 ---

--- SENTENCE 927 ---

--- SENTENCE 928 ---

--- SENTENCE 933 ---
Coastal Geologic and Morphologic Setting: Punaoa valley, along with Utulei, is part of a double coastal plain embayment with Utulei Ridge separating the two coastal plains.

--- SENTENCE 934 ---

--- SENTENCE 935 ---
Vailoa Stream is the main stream entering the bay.

--- SENTENCE 936 ---

--- SENTENCE 937 ---

--- SENTENCE 938 ---
Engineering Structures and Coastal Stability: An erosional scarp was present along most of the reclaimed area.

--- SENTENCE 939 ---
The fringing reef is about 150 m wide and is characterized by numerous dredged borrow pits on the inner reef flat.

--- SENTENCE 940 ---
Remarks: Little remains of the natural shoreline.
