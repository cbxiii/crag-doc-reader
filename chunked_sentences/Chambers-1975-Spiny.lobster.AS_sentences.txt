The Spiny Lobster, Panulirus penicillatus (Oliver) in American Samoa

#### Introduction '

The Samoan Islands are located at latitude 13° 26' south to 14° 22' south and from longitudes 168° 10' west to 17° 48' west (Figure 1).

--- SENTENCE 76 ---
The six islands east of longitude 171° west constitute the American Samoan Islands (Figure 2).

--- SENTENCE 77 ---
The Islands of American Samoa have been held as a possession by the United States since 1929 and are administered by the U. S. Department of Interior, with a governor appointed by the President of the U. S. (Encyclopedia Britannica 1964).

--- SENTENCE 78 ---
From May 1973 to March 1974, the Department of Marine Resources,

Government of American Samoa, undertook a project to determine the extent

of the island's lobster resources.

--- SENTENCE 79 ---
Under the direction of Dr. Stanley

Swerdloff, department director, four Vista volunteers, James Chambers,

William Nunes, Gordon Yamasaki and Sam Lathem were assigned for a year

term with the department to study the biology of the local lobsters.

--- SENTENCE 80 ---
George (1972) reported Panulirus penicillatus and Parribacus caledonicus

to be local to the island.

--- SENTENCE 81 ---
In addition to these two lobster specimens

of Panulirus femeristrica, previously not reported east of New Calidonia,

were taken.

--- SENTENCE 82 ---
In this study information concerning the feasibility of es
tablishing a commercial lobster fishery for the islands was of prime

importance.

--- SENTENCE 83 ---
The spiny lobsters (Palinuridae) yield an annual harvest around the world of 80,000 tons (1.N., F.A.O. 1972).

--- SENTENCE 84 ---
Panulirus is one of eight genera which make up the family Planuridae with 14 recognized species

--- SENTENCE 89 ---
Parribacus, belongs to the Scyllarid family and is commonly known as the "slipper" or "Lutterfly" lobster (George 1969).

--- SENTENCE 90 ---
Panulizus penicillates was originally described by Oliver in 1911 and is distinguished from other members of the genus by having four spanes on the dorsal surface between the antennae (Tinker 1965).

--- SENTENCE 91 ---
It has an extremely wide distribution and occurs from East Africa up to the Red Sea, and through the Indian and Pacific Oceans including Hawaii (Johnson 1971).

--- SENTENCE 92 ---
Through studies on the phyllosoma larvae, Johnson felt the wide distribution to be the result of its unique ability to endure long fransport in oceanic currents during the ten larval stages.

--- SENTENCE 93 ---

Adult P. Penicillatus have a predilection for island habits, regarded by George (1959) as an ecological requirement for clear water and strong current and surf conditions.

--- SENTENCE 94 ---
George (1972) further reported P. penicillatus to live in a truly oceanic position on the outer reef edges or channels which are subject to high energy water movement.

--- SENTENCE 95 ---
Further, he correlates movements onto the reef at night with absence of moon and a tide height that supplies a flow of water across the reef flat.

--- SENTENCE 96 ---
It is also probable, he states that larger males guard a "harem" of mixed sexes where shelter allows aggregation.

--- SENTENCE 97 ---
DeBruin (1962) also found this species to be nocturnal in its habitat in Ceylon.

--- SENTENCE 98 ---
Reproduction in P. penicillatus is typical of the rest of the genus.

--- SENTENCE 99 ---
Taken from Natthews (1951) and Lindberg (1955)

--- SENTENCE 100 ---
the reproductive cycle is as follows.

--- SENTENCE 101 ---
Fertilization occurs from sperm contained in tubules of a putty-like spermatophore.

--- SENTENCE 102 ---
The spermatophore is produced internally in the male testis, and consists of a highly convoluted tube which contains the sperm and a putty-like matrix.

--- SENTENCE 103 ---
This spermatophore is deposited by the male ventrally on the carapace of the female.

--- SENTENCE 104 ---
At first the spermatophore is a light grey, later it turns to a black color.

--- SENTENCE 105 ---
The presence of a spermatophore indicates what is called the "tarred" condition for the

female.

--- SENTENCE 106 ---
The carred female may carry this spermatophore for several months before extruding her eggs and mechanically opening the spermatophore with pinchers on her fifth pair of walking legs.

--- SENTENCE 107 ---
The sperm then fertilize the eggs as they are passed across the broken spermatophore.

--- SENTENCE 108 ---
After fortilization the bright orange eggs are funneled through a tube made by the swimmerets which are filled up.

--- SENTENCE 109 ---
The eggs attach together in a bundled mass and turn to a deep margon with later development.

--- SENTENCE 110 ---
A female with eggs is said to be in the "berried" condition.

--- SENTENCE 111 ---
The incubation time period has been reported by Lindberg (1955) to be about two months.

--- SENTENCE 112 ---
The everage brood size in P. interruptus females with a carapace length of 8 cm. was found to be 50,000 eggs (Wilson 1948, Lindberg 1955).

--- SENTENCE 113 ---
Panuluris eggs hatch into leaflike larvae called phyllosoma and float in the plankton for a period of at least eight months (Johnson 1971).

--- SENTENCE 114 ---
During this period the phyllosoma grow by a succession of molts or eced-yses.

--- SENTENCE 115 ---
At least 10 phyllosoma stages of P. penicillatus have been identified by Johnson.

--- SENTENCE 116 ---
Figure number 3 illustrates the final or tenth stage of larval development.

--- SENTENCE 117 ---
This last stage in the plankton metamorphoses into a puerulus stage or post larval stage which begins to resemble the edult.

--- SENTENCE 118 ---
The puerulus state is at first translucent like the phyllosoma larvae, and seeks the bottom where it will become pigmented and live its adult life (Johnson 1971).

--- SENTENCE 119 ---
As is typical of all crustaceans lobsters grow only after a molting period.

--- SENTENCE 120 ---
The molting period for P. interruptus was reported by Lindberg (1955) to be followed by a 5-8 day period of shell hardening and an additional 2-3 day period until feeding resumes.

--- SENTENCE 121 ---
Upon reaching a mature size an immature female reaches sexual maturity after one molt (Lindberg 1955).

#### Methods and Materials

Several methods of lobster capture were employed, including day and night SCUEA diving, night snorkel diving, tangle nets and traps.

--- SENTENCE 128 ---
Through our diving we studied behavior in the natural environment, obtained samples, and through a catch per unit effort had some figure of the availability or presence of lobsters.

--- SENTENCE 129 ---
Recording the number of personnel, the hours diving and the take, we were able to come up with a per man-hour catch, which we have used in moon phase correlation, population distribution on the island and recruitment into an area after heavy take of lobsters.

--- SENTENCE 130 ---
Night snorkel diving over the reefs, during the high tide provided the most specimens of P. penicillatus.

--- SENTENCE 131 ---
Divers were equipped with full snorkel gear; including masks, fins, snorkel, underwater lights, nylon collection bags and Hawaiian slings (three pronged spear).

--- SENTENCE 132 ---
The underwater light provided a direct beam which mesmorized or temporarily blinded the lobster.

--- SENTENCE 133 ---
Lobster eyes gleam when exposed to the light beam up to distances of 12 meters.

--- SENTENCE 134 ---
Standard SCURA was also employed in numerous exploratory daylight dives to familiarize ourselves to an area planned for a night dive.

--- SENTENCE 135 ---
Hight SCURA equipment was employed to obtain more prolonged deeper night

 $\angle \cdot \wedge$ 

dives and was used to determine lobster vertical stratification.

--- SENTENCE 136 ---
Experimental netting and trapping was initiated in an effort to test their efficiency in catching lobsters.

--- SENTENCE 137 ---
The net was a 4-6 inch (10-15 cm.)

--- SENTENCE 138 ---
bottom tangle net with a typical bottom lead line and top float line.

--- SENTENCE 139 ---
Two types of traps were also used, the first was constructed of a 3 by 4 feet (90 by 122 cm.)

#### Discussion

Tutuila island was found to provide an ideal habitat for P. penicillatus.

--- SENTENCE 260 ---
The water is exceptionally clear, there is a fringing reef upon which lobster forage for algal cover, the reefs are subject to considerable wave and current action.

--- SENTENCE 261 ---
Collection sites yielded more lobsters in inaccessable areas which were subject to stronger wave action and current, such as Nu'uuli, Matu'u and Lau'ullii.

Paulirus Penicillatus inhabits the upper reef zones in areas of high oxygen content brought about by high water movement.

--- SENTENCE 269 ---
This species is basically nocturnal in its habit, as DeBruin (1962) found for the species in Ceylon.

--- SENTENCE 270 ---
Although P. penicillatus has been termed to be emnivorous (Tinker 1965), we found the species on Tutuila Island to be herbivorous, this proved to be a marked deterent to conventional trapping methods.

--- SENTENCE 271 ---
Panulirus femoristriga found in deeper water is trapable, but due to the steep island shelf slope, we believe the numbers of this lobster to be limited.

--- SENTENCE 272 ---
Our net and trap work with P. femoristriga showed this species to prefer deeper and quieter waters than P. penicillatus, and this seems to be in agreement with observations reported by George (1972).

--- SENTENCE 273 ---
Interesting to note with P. penicillatus, is the ability to endure wide salinity changes.

--- SENTENCE 274 ---
Cur cave diving found the lobsters to congregate near a freshwater outflow.

--- SENTENCE 275 ---
Further, the island is subject to large amounts of rainfall which has a definite effect on the salinity concentrations in the shallow reef waters.

--- SENTENCE 276 ---
Dives during rainy nights, however, showed no decrease in lobster abundance.

--- SENTENCE 277 ---
contrary to George (1972), no male dominance over harems was observed.

--- SENTENCE 278 ---
Panulirus penicillatus was found to aggregate in two's, three's and occasionally in numbers up to eight.

--- SENTENCE 279 ---
Most large males were taken alone.

--- SENTENCE 280 ---
Further, no correlation of lobster activity with moon phase as reported by George (1972) was observed.

--- SENTENCE 281 ---
From Table 5, the catch per man-hour at different periods of the moon, superficially indicated a larger percentage of catch during the first and last quarters, over the new and full moon, but the validity of this data was rejected based on a simple "F" test.

--- SENTENCE 282 ---
Female P. pencillatus (Table 3) were observed to be reproductively active throughout the year.

--- SENTENCE 283 ---
This information suggests that spawning occurs year round, a condition also observed by Chittleborough (1969), who found P. pencillatus phyllosoma larvae at all periods of the year, off the west coast of Australia.

--- SENTENCE 284 ---
Cut of 440 specimens of <u>P. penicillatus</u> collected, we found five animals with semi-hard shells indicating recent molting.

--- SENTENCE 285 ---
These lobsters were found sporatically over the eleven month period.

--- SENTENCE 286 ---
It appears there is no general molting period and <u>P. penicillatus like P. interruptus</u> has a molt followed by at least a one week period of inactivity.
