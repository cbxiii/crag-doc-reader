## TRIP REPORT

Rose Atoll National Wildlife Refuge

--- SENTENCE 21 ---
January 14<sup>th</sup>-January 22<sup>nd</sup>, 2005

Katerine Schletz Saili, Wildlife Biologist Dept. of Marine & Wildlife Resources P.O. Box 3730 Pago Pago, AS 96799

<u>Purpose of Trip</u>: Survey Rose Island for the presence of nesting sea turtles in addition to assessing the status of wildlife and prospects for future wildlife work in remote islands for SWG-CWCS planning.

--- SENTENCE 22 ---
# Background:

Sea turtle activity in Rose Atoll National Wildlife Refuge (American Samoa) has been reported as early as 1873 (Tuato'o-Bartley, 1993).

--- SENTENCE 23 ---
Sporadic flipper tagging occurring on Rose Island since the 1970's has resulted in the tagging of 46 nesting green turtles as of 1993 (Balazs, 1996).

--- SENTENCE 24 ---
It is estimated that 24-36 green turtles were nesting there in the early 1990's (Tuato'o-Bartley, 1993).

--- SENTENCE 25 ---
Turtle surveys on Rose Island since 1993 have included daytime surveys (i.e., locking for crawls and/or presence of turtles) that were conducted by DMWR biologists in 1997 & 2002 in conjunction with other wildlife work.

--- SENTENCE 26 ---
Although Rose Atoll NWR is protected by strict laws and its isolated location, nesting green turtles are still at risk because they migrate to areas outside of US territorial waters (e.g., Fiji) (Craig et al., 2004).

--- SENTENCE 27 ---
Continued monitoring at the NWR is necessary to assess any trends in population numbers and mitigate threats at both local and regional levels.

--- SENTENCE 28 ---
# Methods:

#### **NIGHTLY SURVEYS**

- 1.

--- SENTENCE 29 ---
Beginning shortly before dusk (~1830) and ending shortly after sunrise (~0530), hourly walk around the perimeter of the island looking for fresh crawls.

--- SENTENCE 30 ---
Rely only upon ambient light so as not to frighten away potential nesting turtles.

--- SENTENCE 31 ---
Upon discovery of a fresh crawl, follow it to locate the nesting turtle.

--- SENTENCE 32 ---
Approach the turtle from behind in order to remain undetected.

--- SENTENCE 33 ---
Observe the turtle using ambient light and record the following:
  - a. Time of emergence/discovery, tide at emergence, duration of nest digging/egg laying, time of return to sea
  - b. Number of body pits/egg chambers dug prior to the actual nest
  - c. Number of eggs laid, if any
  - d. GPS location of nest
  - e.

--- SENTENCE 34 ---
After eggs have been laid and covered, use a headlamp (soft, red light) to do the following:
    - i. Confirm species
    - ii.

--- SENTENCE 35 ---
Measure CCL and CCW in cm
    - iii.

--- SENTENCE 36 ---
Determine presence of flipper tags and record any tag numbers
    - Note distinguishing marks or features and overall condition of the turtle

3.

--- SENTENCE 37 ---
Upon discovery of a hatchling, look for other hatchlings and locate the nest, if possible.

--- SENTENCE 38 ---
Count number of hatchlings and GPS nest location (GPS site of entry into the sea if nest is not found).

--- SENTENCE 39 ---
Note incidence of predation or other factors affecting hatchling survival.

## Results:

The MV Tokelau departed Pago Pago Harbor around 1500 on January 14

--- SENTENCE 40 ---
<sup>th</sup> and arrived in Rose Atoll NWR at approximately 0500 on January 15<sup>th</sup> for a total travel time of about 14hrs.

--- SENTENCE 41 ---
For the purpose of this report, January 14<sup>th</sup> is Day 1 of the trip.

--- SENTENCE 42 ---
I conducted daytime surveys (i.e., counts and GPS of existing sea turtle nests) on Days 2, 7, and 8 totaling approximately twelve hours.

--- SENTENCE 43 ---
Nighttime surveys were conducted on Days 2, 3, 4, 5, and 7.

--- SENTENCE 44 ---
Rather than camping on the island, I slept on the MV Tokelau during the day, returned to the island around 1800, and departed the next morning around 0700 on most days.

--- SENTENCE 45 ---
The survey on Day 6 was cancelled due to inclement weather and rough seas making it unsafe to enter the channel into the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 46 ---
Between Days 2 and 8, six new nests were counted.

--- SENTENCE 47 ---
Of these, two were confirmed by visual observation during egg laying and four were discovered within a few hours of the nesting event.

--- SENTENCE 48 ---
Nests were identified primarily by the existence of a noticeable escarpment formed at the end of a nesting event when a turtle throws sand using its front flippers to mask the nest.

--- SENTENCE 49 ---
Four additional "possible" nests were identified as such due to ambiguous features and/or an inability to determine the day on which they were made (possibly prior to Day 1).

--- SENTENCE 50 ---
A total of three false crawls were identified between Days 2 and 8.

--- SENTENCE 51 ---
One false crawl was discovered on Day 2; the other two occurred on Days 6 and 8 and were made by the same turtle (visual confirmation).

--- SENTENCE 52 ---
Nests occurring during the trip were distributed evenly around the island.

--- SENTENCE 53 ---
None of the three turtles seen had flipper tags.

--- SENTENCE 54 ---
Six separate hatches were observed during the trip.

--- SENTENCE 55 ---
Two of these hatches were significant with 30+ hatchlings counted.

--- SENTENCE 56 ---
They both occurred around dusk on opposite sides of the island (SW and E).

--- SENTENCE 57 ---
The other "hatches" each involved one to four hatchlings around dusk, with one hatchling discovered at 2430.

--- SENTENCE 58 ---
These small numbers likely represent the last few stragglers from a larger hatch on a previous night.

--- SENTENCE 59 ---
Additional hatches probably occurred during the week, but were not detected.

--- SENTENCE 60 ---
During the week, five dead hatchlings were found; one was found at the entrance of a crab hole, two had severed heads (possibly inflicted by birds), and one was found inland, body in tact.

--- SENTENCE 61 ---
Several black-tipped reef sharks were observed near shore in the lagoon throughout the night on all nights.

--- SENTENCE 62 ---
These sharks were observed eating hatchlings and appeared to be enjoying a "feeding frenzy" during one of the larger hatches.

--- SENTENCE 63 ---
The greatest concentration of black-tipped reef sharks was regularly observed on the western (lagoon) side of the island.

--- SENTENCE 64 ---
Although I did not personally visit Sand Islet, a member from the Moana Divers crew (contracted to clean up a shipwreck on the reef) reported on Day 2 that he had counted eight "nests" under the existing shrubs on the islet.

--- SENTENCE 65 ---
Although these "nests" may have been false crawls, it is evident that turtles nevertheless attempt to nest on the islet.

--- SENTENCE 66 ---
Nests on Sand Islet are at risk of inundation during high tides and storms.

--- SENTENCE 67 ---
In addition to turtle activity, I was able to observe seabirds (at all hours and locations) and some invertebrates.

--- SENTENCE 68 ---
The birds seem to be thriving.

--- SENTENCE 69 ---
Brown noddies seemed to be most abundant on the island preferring the sand flat on the northern side.

--- SENTENCE 70 ---
Common fairy terns, red-footed boobies, brown boobies, and sooty terns were also common.

--- SENTENCE 71 ---
Other species identified were great frigatebirds, lesser frigatebirds, sandpipers, 1 whimbrel (possibly a bristle-thighed curlew?), and red-tailed tropicbirds.

--- SENTENCE 72 ---
(This list is not complete; formal surveys were not

conducted.)

--- SENTENCE 73 ---
Spiders, ants, and several types of land crabs were also abundant throughout the island.

--- SENTENCE 74 ---
I looked briefly at a few of the remaining *Pisonia* trees and noted that the scale insect is still present.

--- SENTENCE 75 ---
Although some leaves and entire branches showed no evidence of the insect, other leaves on the same tree exhibited concentrations of the insect at the leaf stem.

--- SENTENCE 76 ---
A small pool was discovered on the north side of the island just inland of the sand flat.

--- SENTENCE 77 ---
The pool is home to four fish, representing three species.

--- SENTENCE 78 ---
Some turtle egg shells were found near the pool.

--- SENTENCE 79 ---
A turtle nest may have been inundated at this site.

--- SENTENCE 80 ---
## Summary:

Green turtles have been nesting throughout Rose Island this breeding season since at least November (based on expected egg incubation duration).

--- SENTENCE 81 ---
The data gained from this trip is insufficient to determine the number of females nesting on the island, although it seems reasonable that the number is close to that suggested by Tuato'o-Bartley (1993), 22-36.

--- SENTENCE 82 ---
Nests are distributed throughout the island, with no side obviously preferred.

--- SENTENCE 83 ---
Threats to hatchlings are natural predators (i.e., birds, crabs, and reef sharks) with reef sharks seeming to take the largest percentage of hatchlings upon entry into the sea.

--- SENTENCE 84 ---
Future research needs include continued monitoring, flipper tagging, and tissue sampling.

--- SENTENCE 85 ---
Tissue sample data should be stored in a regional database to be compared to samples obtained in other areas of the Pacific.

--- SENTENCE 86 ---
This data would help in determining the home range of the nesting green turtles on Rose Island.

--- SENTENCE 87 ---
The fact that these turtles have been shown to migrate west to countries such as Fiji makes it imperative to create and support regional cooperatives/regulations to protect these turtles in both their nesting and feeding grounds.
