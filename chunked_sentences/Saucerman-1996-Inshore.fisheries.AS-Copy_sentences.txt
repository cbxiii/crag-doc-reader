# 1.

--- SENTENCE 2 ---
Study objectives

The Inshore Survey monitoring program was initiated by the Department of Marine and Wildlife Resources (DMWR) in 1990 to compare results with a similar study conducted in 1978-1979.

--- SENTENCE 3 ---
The survey has continued and evolved over the past five years.

--- SENTENCE 4 ---
The objectives of the survey are to monitor public usage of the local inshore marine resources to obtain information on the catch and effort and species composition of this multi-species, multigear fishery in order to track the trends and/or changes of the fishery over time.

--- SENTENCE 5 ---
The results of this survey provide DMWR with information needed to make decisions on what areas of the fishery merit further studies, which species/families are most important in the fishery and therefore merit further biological studies.

--- SENTENCE 6 ---
Further, the survey has the potential over time to provide information on indicators of overfishing such as shifts in species composition, and decreases in average lengths of specific species.

--- SENTENCE 7 ---
Since data collection began in July 1990, the survey has continued to the present with only moderate changes in order to maintain the ability to compare results year by year.

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
Results presented in this report include only those data which represent entire years, which include information collected in 1991 (Ponwith), 1992 and 1993 (McConnaughey), 1994 and 1995 (Saucerman).

#### **Analysis**

Data were entered into a database (DBASE IV) and a program designed by Western Pacific Fisheries Information Netwok personnel is used to expand the sample data to annual catch and effort estimates, and species composition for the study area.

--- SENTENCE 50 ---
For effort (gear hour) estimates, the average number of fishers for the survey runs is multiplied by the number of hours in the fishing day to obtain the estimate of fishing hours in that day.

--- SENTENCE 51 ---
To estimate the number of gear hours, the estimate of fishing hours is divided by the mean trip length, which is taken from the catch interviews:

$$\overline{E} = \frac{\sum_{d=1}^{n} E_{d}}{n}$$

where  $\bar{E}$  is average gear hours, E is gear count per run, n is number of runs by type day in time period, and d is day, the variance is calculated by:

$$\operatorname{Var}(E) = \frac{\sum_{d=1}^{n} E_d^2 - \frac{\sum_{d=1}^{n} E_d^2 - \frac{n}{n}}{n-1}}{n-1}$$

and:

$$Var \ \overline{E} = \frac{Var(E)}{n} * \left(1 - \frac{n}{N}\right)$$

where N is the total number of hours in the same type of day in the same time period.

--- SENTENCE 52 ---
Then the expanded number of gear hours becomes:

$$\hat{E} = \bar{E} * N$$

Estimated CPUE per gear type increased in 1995 from 1994 values for all but thrownets (Table 4).

#### **Species composition**

The proportion of resident coral reef fishes increased in the catch in 1995 from previous years (Fig. 4).

--- SENTENCE 76 ---
The total landings of invertebrates increased also, particularly in proportion.

--- SENTENCE 77 ---
Migratory fishes decreased in total pounds and proportion of the catch in 1995.

--- SENTENCE 78 ---
Migratory fishes made up 66% of the total catch in 1991 (Fig. 4), largely due to the migratory atule catch for that year, which contributed 56% (131,676 lbs.)

--- SENTENCE 79 ---
of the total catch for 1991.

--- SENTENCE 80 ---
In contrast, migratory species made up only 16 - 17% of the catch in 1992 and 1993.

--- SENTENCE 81 ---
In 1992, resident reef fishes made up the largest part of the catch (48%), followed by invertebrates (36%).

--- SENTENCE 82 ---
In 1993 invertebrates made up 60% of the catch and resident reef fishes comprised 23%.

--- SENTENCE 83 ---
A break down of resident coral reef fish species encountered during survey interviews is listed in Table 5.

--- SENTENCE 84 ---
In 1995, more parrotfishes were seen during survey than acanthurids for the first time in five years.

--- SENTENCE 85 ---
In 1995 scarids increased an order of magnitude from 1994.

--- SENTENCE 86 ---
Acanthurids still made up 22% of the catch (Table 6), a large percentage, but scarids comprised 33% of the total catch in 1995.

--- SENTENCE 87 ---
Serranids, Holocentrids, Lutjanids, etc.) appear to fluctuate in occurance in the survey over the years.

--- SENTENCE 88 ---
For non-resident fishes encountered during interviews for the inshore fishery, small jacks made up the major proportion of the catch (Table 7) in 1995.

--- SENTENCE 89 ---
Skipjack tuna and sharks also showed up in the catch, but atule were not encountered at all during 1995.

--- SENTENCE 90 ---
Octopus and white sea urchins made up the majority of the catch of invertebrates for all five years (Table 8).

--- SENTENCE 91 ---
Most years octopus is around 50% of the invertebrates encountered in the survey.

--- SENTENCE 92 ---
Sea urchins in general are also popular in the fishery, and are mostly taken by gleaners, though they were far higher in proportion (29%) in the catch in 1991 than in 1995 (12%).

The fishery is fluctuating.

one of two things might occur: growth of algae due to decrease in sea urchin grazers might attract more herbivorous fishes, or, the algae may over grow the reef areas and prevent the coral reefs from recovery.
