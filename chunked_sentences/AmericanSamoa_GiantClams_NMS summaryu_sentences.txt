# **American Samoa Giant Clams**

#### *Climate Change Vulnerability Assessment Summary*

*An Important Note About this Document:

--- SENTENCE 2 ---
This document represents an initial evaluation of vulnerability for giant clams based on workshop input and existing information.

--- SENTENCE 3 ---
The aim of this document is to expand understanding of species vulnerability to changing climate conditions, and to provide a foundation for developing appropriate adaptation responses.

--- SENTENCE 4 ---
[](_page_0_Picture_5.jpeg)

#### **Species Description**

Giant clams, *Tridacna maxima*, are generally found in the Rose atoll, the North and west sides of Ta'u Island, and on the tops or slopes of the reef in shallow and clear waters.

--- SENTENCE 5 ---
They are a traditional and culturally important American Samoa cultural heritage, the fa'a-Samoa, and have become increasing in popularity for the ornamental trade, thus are at risk to be overfished.

--- SENTENCE 6 ---
<sup>1</sup> Fa'alavelave, traditional gatherings among communities and extended

families, include offerings of giant clams when available.

--- SENTENCE 7 ---
There have been some aquaculture efforts for *Tridacna sp.* in Tutuila and initiating grow-out facilities in Aunu'u and the Manu'a islands.

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
Giant clams, *Tridacna sp.* were listed vulnerable on the IUCN red List in 2006.

## **Species Vulnerability**

**Moderate** 

!

--- SENTENCE 10 ---
[](_page_0_Picture_12.jpeg)

Workshop participants and expert evaluated giant clams in American Samoa to have a moderate relative vulnerability to climate change due to moderate-high sensitivity to climate and non-climate stressors, moderate exposure to future climate changes, and moderate-high adaptive capacity.

--- SENTENCE 11 ---
Giant clams are sensitive to several climate stressors, including ocean acidification, sea surface temperature, and currents, mixing, and stratification.

--- SENTENCE 12 ---
These stressors can directly affect recruitment and growth of giant clams.

--- SENTENCE 13 ---
## **Sensitivity**

**Moderate** 

!

--- SENTENCE 14 ---
Giant clams have moderate sensitivity to several climate and non-climate stressors including ocean acidification, increased sea surface temperature, changes in currents, and over harvest.

--- SENTENCE 15 ---
Ocean acidification will directly affect the survival and growth of giant clams reducing calcification and impairing growth and recruitment.4

#### **Exposure† Moderate Exposure** Low High **Mod Confidence**

Under future climate conditions over the next 20 years, Giant clams will experience warmer sea surface temperatures, altered precipitation, and ocean acidification which will likely affect recruitment, and survival.
