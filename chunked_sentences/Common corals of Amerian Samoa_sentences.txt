# Common Corals of American Samoa

!

--- SENTENCE 2 ---
The Coral Reef Advisory Group (CRAG) coordinates American Samoa's coral reef management efforts and activities.

--- SENTENCE 3 ---
The group is a collaboration of five local agencies: the Department of Marine and Wildlife Resources (DMWR), the Department of Commerce (DOC; including AS Coastal Management Program & Fagatele Bay National Marine Sanctuary), the American Samoa Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), the American Samoa Community College (ASCC), and the National Park of American Samoa (NPS).

--- SENTENCE 4 ---
Member agencies work together by mutual consensus to manage coral reefs with the vision "to protect and conserve reefs for the benefit of the people of American Samoa, the United States, and the world".

--- SENTENCE 5 ---
The group works toward this vision by implementing strategies to address various areas of coral reef conservation; from education and outreach, policy and enforcement, to scientific research and monitoring.

## Introduction

 American Samoa is part of the Samoan Archipelago near the center of the Pacific Ocean, 2500 miles south of Hawaii.

--- SENTENCE 26 ---
It consists of seven islands, five of which are high, volcanic islands, and two of which are small atolls.

--- SENTENCE 27 ---
American Samoa is lined with fringing coral reefs that harbor a multitude of the marine life.

--- SENTENCE 28 ---
Coral reefs are geological structures composed of calcium carbonate rock, made by living organisms.

--- SENTENCE 29 ---
Corals are a major contributor of the calcium carbonate that builds the reef, and form the hard framework that holds it together and provides habitat for many other species in the ecosystem.

--- SENTENCE 30 ---
Coral reefs are the most diverse marine ecosystem known in the world, harboring an estimated million species.

--- SENTENCE 31 ---
Major groups of organisms living on reefs include corals, fish, algae, and many different kinds of invertebrate animals.

--- SENTENCE 32 ---
Coral reefs break the power of waves, and protect tropical shorelines from being eroded away.

--- SENTENCE 33 ---
Reef fish provide high-protein food for millions of people around the world.

--- SENTENCE 34 ---
Coral reefs also are ideal for snorkeling and diving, with warm, clear water, and a kaleidoscope of colorful animals and plants.

--- SENTENCE 35 ---
Reef tourism is an important part of the economy in many parts of the world.

#### Reef structure

 In American Samoa the fringing reefs lining the shoreline are divided into four different zones (Fig. 1).

--- SENTENCE 36 ---
In a few areas, pools can be found near the shore that are up to 25 feet deep and called "backreef pools."

--- SENTENCE 37 ---
Along most of the shoreline though, there is hard, nearly flat reef that begins right at the low tide line, called "reef flat."

--- SENTENCE 38 ---
It is the area that people walk out on at low tide and it can be snorkeled at mid to high tide.

--- SENTENCE 39 ---
The outer edge of the reef flat, where the waves break, is called the "reef crest".

--- SENTENCE 40 ---
Beyond the reef crest, the reef slopes down into deep water, hence called "reef slope" or fore reef.

--- SENTENCE 41 ---
It extends from near the very shallow at the reef crest, down to as much as 100 feet deep.

--- SENTENCE 42 ---
At the bottom of the reef slope, it reaches a nearly flat shelf, most of which is covered with sand or rubble and has little or no coral on it.

--- SENTENCE 45 ---
The coral animal

 Corals are closely related to sea anemones, and more distantly related to jellyfish.

--- SENTENCE 46 ---
Thus, they are animals.

--- SENTENCE 47 ---
The basic unit of the body of a coral is called a polyp, which is a hollow bag filled with sea water, with finger-like structures called tentacles in a circle on the top (Fig. 2).

--- SENTENCE 48 ---
In the center of the ring of tentacles is a mouth.

--- SENTENCE 49 ---
Corals, like anemones and jellyfish are predators.

--- SENTENCE 50 ---
They have stingers on the tentacles that can stun small organisms, which are then moved to the mouth and digested.

--- SENTENCE 51 ---
Each polyp sits in its own shallow "cup" called corallite which can vary in size and shape depending on species.

--- SENTENCE 52 ---
Corals are unusual animals because they are modular, meaning they are made up of thousands of repeating units, the polyps, each connected to one another.

--- SENTENCE 53 ---
Hence, corals are also referred to as "coral colonies".

!

--- SENTENCE 54 ---
2: Anatomy of coral polyps (Polyp image: http://web.stanford.edu).

#### Zooxanthellae

 Corals are very unusual among animals, in that their tissues contain tiny, single-celled plants or algae, called "zooxanthellae" (Fig. 3).

--- SENTENCE 55 ---
When sunlight strikes these tiny algae cells, they convert it into food, just like the leaves of plants on land.

--- SENTENCE 56 ---
The algae cells leak most of that food out, which is taken up by the coral polyp, helping the coral to grow.

--- SENTENCE 57 ---
As a result, reef building corals live only in sun-lit, relatively shallow waters, less than 300 feet deep.

!

--- SENTENCE 58 ---
3: Magnification of coral polyps (Image: ocean.si.edu) showing algae cells within the tissue (greenish flecks).

--- SENTENCE 59 ---
The inset shows a magnification of those single-celled algae (Image: www.captivereefs.com).

--- SENTENCE 60 ---
The coral skeleton

 Corals build a hard calcium carbonate skeleton which is all one piece, unlike our skeleton.

--- SENTENCE 61 ---
They add to it continually throughout their life, so coral reefs are constantly growing.

--- SENTENCE 62 ---
However, that growth is slow in many species.

--- SENTENCE 63 ---
Depending on the coral species, growth rates can range from 0.1 to 4 inches per year.

--- SENTENCE 64 ---
The shape of the skeleton is also very different in different coral species.

--- SENTENCE 65 ---
Some species produce a branching skeleton, others produce a single, mound-like (massive) skeleton, and still others produce a very thin skeleton that covers the bottom like a layer of paint (encrusting), or even a plate-shaped skeleton (Fig. 4).

--- SENTENCE 66 ---
The shape of the coral is the primary way that species are distinguished.

--- SENTENCE 67 ---
But not just the shape of the whole coral colony, but also the details of the corallite (cup) that the polyp sits in, are important to distinguish a species.

--- SENTENCE 68 ---
The color of the coral is usually not very helpful as it can be quite variable within a species.

!

--- SENTENCE 69 ---
4: Different shapes (growth forms) of coral colonies (Image: coral.biota.biodiv.tw).

### Summary

A coral is very unique in that it is an animal that contains tiny algae cells, and produces a rock skeleton.

--- SENTENCE 70 ---
Corals are found throughout the different reef zones and are conspicuous, yet they can be quite difficult to identify to species.

--- SENTENCE 71 ---
There are over 250 coral species in American Samoa alone, and coral colonies even within a species can be variable in their appearance.

--- SENTENCE 72 ---
The species composition in the different reef zones as well as between locations is often variable.

--- SENTENCE 73 ---
However, some common species are relatively easy to distinguish.

--- SENTENCE 74 ---
This guide presents the 22 most common species found in American Samoa, organized by reef zone.

--- SENTENCE 75 ---
## Common corals: back reef pools

#### Acropora intermedia (Staghorn coral) - Previously referred to as A. nobilis

 Acropora intermedia forms staghorn colonies that look similar to A. muricata and A. pulchra (see below).

--- SENTENCE 76 ---
However, it has thicker branches than the other two species.

--- SENTENCE 77 ---
The tip of the branch is about ½ inch thick and the base of the branch can be up to an inch thick or more.

--- SENTENCE 78 ---
Another distinguishing feature are the corallites lining the sides of the branches.

--- SENTENCE 79 ---
They are short, uniform and have thick walls with angled openings pointing towards the tip of the branch.

--- SENTENCE 80 ---
Big and small corallites are interspersed between each other.

--- SENTENCE 81 ---
It is usually brown but can be tan, and always has a white branch tip.

--- SENTENCE 82 ---
A. intermedia is the least common of the staghorn corals found in backreef pools, and it is also found on reef slopes.

!

--- SENTENCE 83 ---
[](_page_6_Picture_4.jpeg)

#### Acropora muricata (Staghorn coral) - Previously referred to as A. formosa

 Acropora muricata forms staghorn colonies with branches about ½ inch thick that are covered in corallites in the shape of small thin tubes that are sticking out from the branches (in contrast to A. intermedia).

--- SENTENCE 84 ---
It is tan to brown, and it has a purple tip on the end of every branch.

--- SENTENCE 85 ---
The purple tip is a very good distinguishing feature of this species in American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 86 ---
A. muricata is by far the most common "staghorn coral" in the backreef pools, but it is not found on the reef slopes.

!

--- SENTENCE 87 ---
[](_page_7_Picture_2.jpeg)

#### Acropora pulchra (Staghorn coral)

--- SENTENCE 88 ---
Acropora pulchra forms staghorn colonies with branches about ½ inch thick (the same size as A. muricata).

--- SENTENCE 89 ---
A distinguishing feature is that it usually has its tentacles extended during the day, giving it a smooth look from a distance, and a fuzzy look closer up.

--- SENTENCE 90 ---
A close look will also reveal a few ear-shaped corallites sticking out between the tentacles.

--- SENTENCE 91 ---
It is usually a little more reddish in color than A. muricata, and has white branch tips.

--- SENTENCE 92 ---
A. pulchra is the second most common "staghorn coral" found in the backreef pools but it is not found on reef slopes.

!

--- SENTENCE 93 ---
[](_page_8_Picture_3.jpeg)

#### Pocillopora damicornis (Cauliflower coral)

 Pocillopora damicornis can reach about 10 inches in diameter and forms small bushy colonies with lots of small branches that are packed close together.

--- SENTENCE 94 ---
The branches appear very irregular and knobby, but can on occasion be farther apart and straighter.

--- SENTENCE 95 ---
Branch tips are always round and are usually a shade of light brown.

--- SENTENCE 96 ---
Colonies and are relatively common in some pools and on the reef crest.

--- SENTENCE 97 ---
It is also found on the reef slope.

!

--- SENTENCE 98 ---
[](_page_9_Picture_3.jpeg)

#### Porites cylindrica (Finger coral)

--- SENTENCE 99 ---
Porites cylindrica can form fields in which it is difficult to distinguish between individual colonies.

--- SENTENCE 100 ---
The branches are finger-like and have rounded tips.

--- SENTENCE 101 ---
It is most commonly a grey-brown, but some colonies are yellow-green.

--- SENTENCE 102 ---
It dominates backreef pools along with Acropora muricata but it is also found on the reef slope.

!

--- SENTENCE 103 ---
[](_page_10_Picture_3.jpeg)

## Common corals: reef flats

#### Acropora aspera

 Acropora aspera colonies are somewhere in between being a small staghorn and being bushy.

--- SENTENCE 104 ---
Branches are usually about ¼ inch in diameter and tend to grow straight upward.

--- SENTENCE 105 ---
Corallites lining the branches are short, thick walled, and have openings angled towards the tip of the branch.

--- SENTENCE 106 ---
It is usually brown and always has white branch tips.

--- SENTENCE 107 ---
It is only found on the reef flat and at low tide clumps of branches commonly stick out of the water.

!

--- SENTENCE 108 ---
[](_page_11_Picture_4.jpeg)

#### Pavona frondifera

 Pavona frondifera forms very crinkly colonies of small vertical plates that have many ridges running down the sides.

--- SENTENCE 109 ---
The ridges grow until they themselves become small plates.

--- SENTENCE 110 ---
Plates turn and twist in many directions and are densely packed together.

--- SENTENCE 111 ---
It is yellow-brown and prefers reef flats and edges of backreef pools.

!

--- SENTENCE 112 ---
[](_page_12_Picture_3.jpeg)

## Common corals: reef crests

#### Acropora nana

 Acropora nana colonies are usually around 3-6 inches diameter and form small bushes of radiating thin branches.

--- SENTENCE 113 ---
Branches are about the thickness of a pencil, and usually are vertical or radiate from the center.

--- SENTENCE 114 ---
Corallites lining the branches point toward the tip, though some are tubular and project.

--- SENTENCE 115 ---
It is commonly a reddish-brown.

--- SENTENCE 116 ---
This species is usually the most common species on reef crests.

!

--- SENTENCE 117 ---
[](_page_13_Picture_4.jpeg)

#### Millepora platyphylla (Fire coral)

 Millepora platyphylla forms encrusting colonies with thick paddle-shaped columns and plates growing upwards.

--- SENTENCE 118 ---
The paddles can be a variety of shapes and sizes, and are up to about ½ inch thick.

--- SENTENCE 119 ---
The upper edges of the paddles and plates are flat or rounded and the encrusting base is usually bumpy.

--- SENTENCE 120 ---
It is brown or yellow-brown in color.

--- SENTENCE 121 ---
It can be common on outer reef flats, reef crests and moderately common on reef slopes.

--- SENTENCE 122 ---
Fire corals have their name because they can produce a fiery sting on tender skin.

--- SENTENCE 123 ---
Their stings are not dangerous and only cause light pain for a short period of time.

!

--- SENTENCE 124 ---
[](_page_14_Picture_3.jpeg)

#### Pocillopora verrucosa

 Pocillopora verrucosa forms dense colonies of thick, nearly round branches that are covered with little bumps.

--- SENTENCE 125 ---
Colonies reach about 6 inches to a foot in diameter, with branches being about ½ inch thick.

--- SENTENCE 126 ---
Colonies are usually light brown, and sometimes pink or green.

--- SENTENCE 127 ---
P. verrucosa is particularly common on the reef crest but also found on the reef flat and the reef slope.

!

--- SENTENCE 128 ---
[](_page_15_Picture_3.jpeg)

## Common corals: reef slopes

Please see also page 1; 4; 5; 9 and 10.

#### Acropora clathrata (Table coral)

--- SENTENCE 129 ---
Acropora clathrata forms large table corals composed of little branchlets that grow nearly horizontal, pointing outward and slightly upward.

--- SENTENCE 130 ---
The branchlets are often fused to each other on their sides and retain a uniform thickness throughout the table.

--- SENTENCE 131 ---
Colonies are typically grey.

--- SENTENCE 132 ---
Especially common on offshore banks, but also present on the reef slope and occasionally in backreef pools.

!

--- SENTENCE 133 ---
[](_page_16_Picture_4.jpeg)

#### Acropora hyacinthus (Table coral)

--- SENTENCE 134 ---
Acropora hyacinthus forms tables that in some cases can have multiple tiers.

--- SENTENCE 135 ---
In contrast to A. clathrata, the upper surface is composed of small vertical branchlets that taper strongly.

--- SENTENCE 136 ---
Branchlets are lined with corallites that have a projecting lower lip.

--- SENTENCE 137 ---
It is usually a brown or reddish brown, but some colonies have a slight greenish tint.

--- SENTENCE 138 ---
This species is most common on upper reef slopes.

!

--- SENTENCE 139 ---
[](_page_17_Picture_3.jpeg)

#### Diploastrea heliopora

 Diploastrea heliopora colonies are among the largest coral colonies found in American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 140 ---
It forms large encrusting sheets on steep slopes with thick overhanging lower edges.

--- SENTENCE 141 ---
The corallites are very large, about ½ inch diameter, and resemble little volcanoes with costae (ridges) running down their side.

--- SENTENCE 142 ---
They are a dull green or sometimes grey color with light centers.

!

--- SENTENCE 143 ---
#### Fungia fungites (Mushroom coral)

--- SENTENCE 144 ---
Fungia fungites forms discs that are composed of a single polyp (in contrast to most other corals found on the reef).

--- SENTENCE 145 ---
The slit in the center is where the mouth is.

--- SENTENCE 146 ---
It is "free-living", meaning it is not attached to the substrate unlike most other corals.

--- SENTENCE 147 ---
It can reach up to about one foot in diameter and has radiating ridges (septa) that are close together and have small visible teeth.

--- SENTENCE 148 ---
Small green tentacles are usually extended.

--- SENTENCE 149 ---
It is commonly referred to as "mushroom coral" for their resemblance to an overturned mushroom cap.

--- SENTENCE 150 ---
It is usually either a yellow-green with tentacles extended, or purple without tentacles extended.

--- SENTENCE 151 ---
It is common in backreef pools and on the reef slopes.

!

--- SENTENCE 152 ---
[](_page_19_Picture_3.jpeg)

#### Isopora crateriformis

 Isopora crateriformis forms encrusting sheets, which on a slope can have a plate lower edge.

--- SENTENCE 153 ---
The surface often has an irregular network of thin ridges and is covered with small round tubular corallites that sit very close together.

--- SENTENCE 154 ---
Colonies are up to about a foot and a half in diameter, and are a reddish-brown, sometimes with green centers on the tubular corallites.

--- SENTENCE 155 ---
It is most commonly observed on upper reef slopes, and is most abundant in some areas on the SW part of the island.

--- SENTENCE 156 ---
This species has recently been listed under the Endangered Species Act (ESA).

!

--- SENTENCE 157 ---
[](_page_20_Picture_3.jpeg)

#### Leptoria phrygia (Brain coral)

 Leptoria phrygia is often referred to as "brain coral" due to its appearance.

--- SENTENCE 158 ---
It forms massive, encrusting or lumpy colonies with small meandering ridges that are very short, rounded, smooth and uniform.

--- SENTENCE 159 ---
It is most often brown or cream but sometimes it has fluorescent green between the ridges.

--- SENTENCE 160 ---
This coral is common on reef slopes and uncommon on reef flats.

!

--- SENTENCE 161 ---
#### Lobophyllia hemprichii

 Lobophyllia hemprichii colonies are often large, from 1 to10 feet or more in diameter.

--- SENTENCE 162 ---
The surface is composed of large corallites that commonly are about 2 inches in diameter, and are often oval or even lobed.

--- SENTENCE 163 ---
The outer edge of the polyp is raised and rounded, the center is depressed.

--- SENTENCE 164 ---
There is a very thin crack between adjacent polyps which can go several feet down into the colony because each polyp actually sits on the end of a branch.

--- SENTENCE 165 ---
However, the branches can only be seen if the colony is broken.

--- SENTENCE 166 ---
L. hemprichii is brown and common on medium depth reef slopes.

!

--- SENTENCE 167 ---
[](_page_22_Picture_2.jpeg)

#### Montastrea curta

 Montastrea curta forms small "massive" or encrusting colonies.

--- SENTENCE 168 ---
The corallites are about ¼ inch in diameter, circular and relatively uniform.

--- SENTENCE 169 ---
A narrow groove separates the corallites from each other.

--- SENTENCE 170 ---
Smaller, daughter corallites that appear as though they have been squeezed into the available space can be observed.

--- SENTENCE 171 ---
This process is called "extra-tentacular budding" and it gives the colony a crowded look.

--- SENTENCE 172 ---
M. curta is cream to white in color and common on reef slopes.

!

--- SENTENCE 173 ---
[](_page_23_Picture_3.jpeg)

#### Montipora grisea

 Montipora grisea, forms thin encrusting colonies.

--- SENTENCE 174 ---
The surface is covered in tiny polyps with tiny bumps or spines in between giving this species the appearance of coarse sandpaper.

--- SENTENCE 175 ---
Most commonly it is brown, but sometimes you can find green or purple colonies.

--- SENTENCE 176 ---
It is the most common coral on reef slopes.

!

--- SENTENCE 177 ---
[](_page_24_Picture_3.jpeg)

#### Pavona chiriquensis

 Pavona chiriquensis forms encrusting colonies with small but widely spaced bumps.

--- SENTENCE 178 ---
Corallites are small and irregularly spaced between the bumps.

--- SENTENCE 179 ---
The bumps have small ridges running up the side.

--- SENTENCE 180 ---
It is generally a shade of brown or grey and it is often found a bit recessed, in cracks or between other reef substrate.

--- SENTENCE 181 ---
It is common on the reef slopes and sometimes also found on reef flats.

!

--- SENTENCE 182 ---
[](_page_25_Picture_2.jpeg)

#### Pavona varians

 Pavona varians forms small encrusting colonies usually less than 10 inches in diameter.

--- SENTENCE 183 ---
The surface is covered with small winding ridges.

--- SENTENCE 184 ---
Corallites are small and barely visible between the ridges.

--- SENTENCE 185 ---
It is often found a bit recessed, in cracks or between other reef substrate.

--- SENTENCE 186 ---
It is generally a shade of brown and very common on the reef slopes.

!

--- SENTENCE 187 ---
[](_page_26_Picture_3.jpeg)

#### Porites rus

 Porites rus colonies form a combination of thin nearly horizontal plates and vertical columns that are irregular and slightly bumpy.

--- SENTENCE 188 ---
In some cases the columns can be fused together, and a colony with fused columns may not have any plates.

--- SENTENCE 189 ---
Colonies can get very large, over 5 feet in diameter.

--- SENTENCE 190 ---
Colonies are often surrounded by lots of small colonies as broken off pieces start to form new colonies.

--- SENTENCE 191 ---
A distinctive pattern of little white ridges can be seen on the tops of columns.

--- SENTENCE 192 ---
It is almost always brown, but rarely blue-grey.

--- SENTENCE 193 ---
P. rus is very common on the reef slope and it is occasionally also found in backreef pools.

!

--- SENTENCE 194 ---
[](_page_27_Picture_3.jpeg)

#### Porites (mound)

 There are several species of Porites that form mounding (also referred to as massive) colonies which are difficult to distinguish.

--- SENTENCE 195 ---
We therefore only refer to them as mounding Porites in this guide.

--- SENTENCE 196 ---
Mounding Porites form rounded, solid colonies of any size from small to very large.

--- SENTENCE 197 ---
The surface of the colony is often slightly bumpy.

--- SENTENCE 198 ---
They are commonly a yellow-brown or green, sometimes brown, rarely purple.

--- SENTENCE 199 ---
They are very common on reef slopes, but also on inner reef flats and in backreef pools.

--- SENTENCE 200 ---
Fun fact: The southwest coast of Ta'u contains multiple giant Porites colonies which are among the biggest in the world.

--- SENTENCE 201 ---
The largest of these colonies was named "Big Momma", with a circumference of 135 feet and a height of 20 feet!

--- SENTENCE 202 ---
It was estimated to be over 500 years old.

!
