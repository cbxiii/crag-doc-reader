## THE FISHES OF ROSE ATOLL - Supplement

--- SENTENCE 2 ---

by Richard C. Wass

Dept. of Marine and Wildlife Resources biological report series no. 5

A previous paper titled THE FISHES OF ROSE ATOLL discusses the results of a fish survey conducted November 12-13, 1980 within the four lagoon habitats of Rose Atoll, American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 3 ---
The present paper supplements the original effort by listing and discussing the fishes observed within the Reef Front Habitat which is the major habitat outside the lagoon.

--- SENTENCE 4 ---
Additional species occurring within the lagoon and species records unique to Rose Atoll are also listed.

--- SENTENCE 5 ---
The survey described herein was conducted March 23-25, 1982.

--- SENTENCE 6 ---
Observations were made during three SCUBA dives and while being towed on the surface behind an outboard-powered skiff at the locations noted in Figure 1.

--- SENTENCE 7 ---
As in the previous study, the observer simply listed every species seen.

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
Again, the list is strongly biased in favor of larger and easily visible fishes.

--- SENTENCE 9 ---
Each species was subjectively assigned to one of three categories reflecting numerical abundance and relative biomass.

--- SENTENCE 10 ---
## Results

The Reef Front Habitat is located on the seaward side of the reef.

--- SENTENCE 11 ---
As defined in the present study, it begins at a depth of about 4 m. and consists of an irregular and often steep slope to a depth of about 50 m. The upper

portion may be bisected by ridges and surge channels.

--- SENTENCE 12 ---
In some areas a narrow terrace with little slope occurs at 5-20 m. before the bottom plunges steeply to greater depths.

--- SENTENCE 13 ---
The irregular substrate is calcareous and compacted with coralline algae predominating.

--- SENTENCE 14 ---
Corals are profuse and species are diverse.

--- SENTENCE 15 ---
The predominance of coralline algae and the complete lack of table and staghorn Acropora distinguish this substrate from that found in similar habitats around Tutuila and the Manu'a Islands.

--- SENTENCE 16 ---
Š

The most visible fishes are those which occur well above the bottom in the midwater reaches.

--- SENTENCE 17 ---
Dominant species include planktivorous surgeonfishes (Naso vlamingii, N. brevirostris, N. hexacanthus), butterflyfishes (Hemitaurichthys thompsoni), damselfishes (Chromis acares), anthiids (Anthias pascalus) and fusiliers (Pterocaesio tile) as well as carnivorous snappers (Aphareus furcatus, Lutjanus bohar), groupers (Gracila albomarginata), jacks (Caranx lugubris) and triggerfish (Melichthys niger).

--- SENTENCE 18 ---
Dominant fishes associated closely with the bottom are groupers (Cephalopholis guttatus, C. urodelus and an undescribed Cephalopholis) and an angelfish (Centropyge loriculus).

--- SENTENCE 19 ---
A total of 105 fishes were observed within the habitat.

--- SENTENCE 20 ---
They are listed in Table I along with a designation of relative abundance and comments on their distribution.

When compared to reef fronts around Tutuila, the fish community at Rose Atoll is lacking in diversity.

--- SENTENCE 22 ---
The number of species observed during 31 surveys in reef front habitats around Tutuila averaged 106 even though considerably less time was spent listing species than during the present study.

--- SENTENCE 23 ---
Also, the 31 surveys were each conducted at a single location.

--- SENTENCE 24 ---
Two families are noticibly less diverse at Rose.

--- SENTENCE 25 ---
The lack of damselfish species that was noted during the earlier study of the lagoon habitats also appears to be true for the reef front.

--- SENTENCE 26 ---
Only eight species were observed at Rose while 12-15 damsels were commonly observed on reef fronts around Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 27 ---
Likewise, 8-12 species of parrotfishes were usually observed on reef fronts around Tutuila while only three were seen at Rose.

--- SENTENCE 28 ---
Large, carnivorous species of groupers, jacks and snappers, however, are more abundant at Rose than at Tutuila.

--- SENTENCE 29 ---
The lack of fishing pressure probably accounts for their increased biomass.

--- SENTENCE 30 ---
The factors which limit the number of damselfish and parrotfish species are unknown but may be related to substrate composition.

## Additional Notes

Six species can be added to the list of lagoon fishes at Rose Atoll.

--- SENTENCE 32 ---
They were observed by the author during November, 1981 and/or March 1982.

--- SENTENCE 33 ---
They are: Adioryx tiere,

Flammeo opercularis, Carangoides ferdau, Chaetodon flavirostris, C. lineolatus and Scarus atropectoralis (tentatively identified as Scarus caudofasciatus in the previous report).

--- SENTENCE 34 ---
All are uncommon with the exception of Carangoides ferdau which is common in shallow sandy areas on the lagoon side of the reef.
