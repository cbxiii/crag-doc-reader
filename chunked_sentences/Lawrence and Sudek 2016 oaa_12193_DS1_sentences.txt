# **American Samoa Territorial Monitoring Program Manu'a Reef Flat Baseline Surveys, February 2015.

--- SENTENCE 2 ---
**

Report prepared by Alice Lawrence and Mareike Sudek, May 2016

As part of the CRAG-DMWR American Samoa Territorial Monitoring Program, baseline coral reef monitoring surveys of reef flat sites in Ofu and Olosega were conducted between 14th – 18th February 2015 by Dr. Mareike Sudek (benthic ecologist) and Alice Lawrence (reef fish ecologist).

--- SENTENCE 3 ---
Surveys were conducted at 12 sites around the islands of Ofu and Olosega as shown by the red lines in figures 1 and 2 (GPS coordinates for each of the sites are listed in Appendix 1).

--- SENTENCE 4 ---
The aim of these surveys was to conduct baseline surveys from which to detect temporal change if it should occur.

--- SENTENCE 5 ---
Benthic surveys were conducted using line-point intercept survey methods to document benthic cover and coral growth forms (results shown in Figure 5, Figure 6, Figure 7, and Table 2 and Table 3).

--- SENTENCE 6 ---
Line-point intercept was conducted on four 25 meter transects at a 50 centimeter interval (50 data points per transect).

--- SENTENCE 7 ---
Opportunistic swims were carried out at the end of each survey (about 15 mins) to photograph bleaching and diseases.

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
At each site fish surveys were conducted using the underwater visual census (UVC) method along four 20 metertransects.

--- SENTENCE 9 ---
All non-cryptic fish species observed within a 5 meter wide belt along the transect were recorded to species level and fish sizes (Total Length) were estimated to the nearest cm.

--- SENTENCE 10 ---
Fish survey results are shown in Figure 3 and Figure 4 and data results are listed in Table 1.

#### **Fish Survey Data Summary**

--- SENTENCE 59 ---
Overall, the highest mean fish biomass were recorded within the National Park boundary between Pool 300 and Pool 600.

--- SENTENCE 60 ---
The highest mean fish biomass was recorded at Pool 400A with a mean biomass of 46.8 g/m-2 , of which 69% of the total biomass along this transect was attributed to herbivorous fish.

--- SENTENCE 61 ---
Noticeably, a much lower mean biomass of 12.9 g/m-2 was observed at the nearby transect Pool 400B, suggesting a high variability of mean fish biomass within the same pool.

--- SENTENCE 62 ---
The lowest mean fish biomass of 4.3 g/m-2 was recorded at Olosega C, which was a site adjacent to the Olosega landfill site.

--- SENTENCE 63 ---
The relatively moderate mean fish biomass observed along the three transects at Pool 200, adjacent to the Ofu airport, suggests that this area has overall lower biomass than the areas inside the National Park.

--- SENTENCE 64 ---
Mean fish biomass at Pool 200 ranged from 11.0 g/m-2 at Pool 200A to 20.9 g/m-2 at Pool 200C.

--- SENTENCE 65 ---
The highest proportion of the biomass along these transects was attributed to herbivorous fish, ranging from 65% at Pool 200A to 86% at Pool 200C.

The three transects surveyed along the reef flat in front of Olosega village showed high variability in fish biomass, ranging from 4.3 g/m-2 at Olosega C to 29.3 g/m-2 at Olosega B.

--- SENTENCE 66 ---
The trophic composition of the fish observed along these transects was noticeably different than at all the other survey sites, with an equal or higher proportion of the total mean biomass being attributed to lower carnivorous fish compared to herbivorous fish.

--- SENTENCE 67 ---
A similar fish population composition was observed at Pool 100B, in front of Ofu village, where 59% of the total mean biomass was attributed to herbivorous fish and 41% attributed to lower carnivorous fish.

#### **Benthic Survey Data Summary**

Overall, Pool 100B, 200C, 300, 400A and 600 had the highest coral cover (19-24%).

--- SENTENCE 68 ---
Highest coral cover was found in Pool 300 (24%) (Table 2 and Figure 6).

--- SENTENCE 69 ---
Out of the coral cover documented for the specific sites, mounding, encrusting and branching coral was most often encountered (Table 3 and Figure 7).

--- SENTENCE 70 ---
All sites also showed high percentages of sand cover (8-40%) and hard bottom covered in turf (11-45%) which is characteristic for these pool environments (hard substrate bommies with sand channels).

--- SENTENCE 71 ---
Highest macroalgae cover was found in pool 200A (10%) which was mostly Halimeda spp.

--- SENTENCE 72 ---
Crustose-coralline algae (CCA cover) cover was low in most pools (majority 0-13%) with high CCA cover found at one site (Pool 600 with 25%).

--- SENTENCE 73 ---
Soft coral was only found in Pool 100A (23%) which had very high flushing rates.

--- SENTENCE 74 ---
High amounts of coral rubble were found at Olo A, Olo B, Olo C, Pool 200A and Pool 200B (34-43%) indicating disturbance (see Table 2 and Figure 6).

--- SENTENCE 75 ---
Observations of benthic indicators suggesting nutrient input were recorded on the nearshore reef flat areas in front of both villages as indicated by the green stars in Figure 5.

--- SENTENCE 76 ---
On the reef flat in Ofu Village a grey encrusting sponge was observed covering the benthic substrate and on the reef flat in Olosega village mats of cyanobacteria were observed.

--- SENTENCE 77 ---
Photographs from these areas are shown in Appendix 2.

--- SENTENCE 78 ---
Additionally, low to intermediate levels of bleaching were seen (genera mostly affected were Millepora and Acropora, some massive Porites) and low levels of disease observed.
