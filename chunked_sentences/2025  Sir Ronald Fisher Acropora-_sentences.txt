--- SENTENCE 37 ---
There is a pattern of shifting the predominance of fast-growing to slow-growing scleractinians when environmental conditions becom e more stressful that has been repeated on three scales: geological time, ecological time, an d in laboratory simulation experiments.

--- SENTENCE 38 ---
There have been three periods in geologic history of strong reef growth by scleractinians, and in these periods, fast-growing genera were prevalent.

--- SENTENCE 39 ---
Kleypas et al. (2001) mad e a good point that the biological an d geological performances of coral reefs are largely independent at the ecological scale.

--- SENTENCE 40 ---
But for now, on the geological scale, w e assum e that during periods in which reef-growth wa s strong, the environment wa s good for corals.

--- SENTENCE 41 ---
The first period wa s in the later Triassic when the fast-growing

phaceloid (the "branching form" of the Triassic) genus *Retiophyllia* prevailed, with at least 34 species and was found all around the tropical world of the time.

--- SENTENCE 42 ---
The second period was the mid-Jurassic when the fast-growing *Thamnasteria* also prevailed around the tropical world with at least 57 species.

--- SENTENCE 43 ---
The third period is the present Neogene, in which it appears that *Acropora* may be a representative of fast-growing, speciose genera.

--- SENTENCE 44 ---

--- SENTENCE 56 ---
Competition for space has a major influence on coral community structure and we automatically include competition as a default trait acted upon by natural selection.

--- SENTENCE 57 ---
*Acropora* is often categorized as a fast-growing, r-selected, "competitive" coral because of its predominance.

--- SENTENCE 58 ---
It is true that some relatively slow-growing corals (K-selected) are adapted to competition (Lang 1973), but Fisher's runaway natural selection may favor rapid growth in three dimensions for increased fecundity to the extent that *Acropora* may become a superior competitor as a byproduct.

--- SENTENCE 59 ---
With Occam's razor, we might consider that r-selected *Acropora* is a superior competitor because it grows fast, rather than it grows fast as an adaptation to be a superior competitor.

--- SENTENCE 60 ---
Winning in competition has indirect benefits for fecundity, but we should compare the total fecundity provided by fast growth of living cover with the fecundity made possible by winning living cover in competition.

--- SENTENCE 61 ---
There are a number of genera of slow-growing corals that have been called "Lazarus corals" because they reoccur after mass mortalities (Rosen 2000).

--- SENTENCE 62 ---
Fast-growing corals are sometimes called "Faustian corals" because runaway selection gives them lives of glory and dominance by increased investing in growth by divesting many defenses and tolerances; but when conditions change for the worse, they become victims of their glorious dominance in the past.
