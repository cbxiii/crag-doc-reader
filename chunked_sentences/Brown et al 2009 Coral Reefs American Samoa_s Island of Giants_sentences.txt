## American Samoa's island of giants: massive Porites colonies at Ta'u island

Received: 28 August 2008 / Accepted: 25 March 2009

Springer-Verlag 2009

!

--- SENTENCE 2 ---
Porites colony in Ta'u, American Samoa

One of the largest and oldest single hermatypic coral colonies known is a massive Porites coral located on the southwest corner of Ta'u island (14-15¢S, 169-30¢W) in the Manu'a group of American Samoa.

--- SENTENCE 3 ---
The coral is believed to be Porites lutea based on colony and corallite morphology; however, Porites taxonomy is notoriously difficult and recent genetic work has revealed surprising levels of cryptic diversity (Forsman et al. 2009).

--- SENTENCE 4 ---
Small (1 cm) cores were collected for genetic and microscopic work, and to determine skeletal density.

--- SENTENCE 5 ---
DNA sequence for mitochondrial (COI, COIII) and nuclear (ITS region) markers were deposited in GenBank (accession numbers ITS-FJ416523-6, mt COI-FJ423967, and mt control FJ427369), and skeletal voucher specimens have been deposited at the Bishop Museum (SC4164).

--- SENTENCE 6 ---
The colony is hemi-ellipsoidal in shape, and sits atop a pedestal of dead skeleton (Fig. 1).

--- SENTENCE 7 ---
It is located in 17 m of water and measures 7 m tall, 41 m in circumference, has a

maximum straight-line length of 17 m and a roughly estimated minimum straight-line length of 12 m. It is 34 m · 20 m across the arc of live tissue, not including the base of skeleton upon which it rests.

--- SENTENCE 8 ---
Unlike some other large Porites that have had anthropogenic damage (Soong et al. 1999), this colony appears quite healthy, with approximately 98% live tissue.

--- SENTENCE 9 ---
There is however one, 1 m · 2 m persistent tumor on the colony.

--- SENTENCE 10 ---
The colony is composed of around 200 million polyps, assuming a smooth hemispherical surface and a polyp size of 1 mm<sup>2</sup> , and has a skeletal density of 1.4 g/cm<sup>3</sup> (~52% average porosity).

--- SENTENCE 11 ---
Using the volume and density estimates, the skeleton was estimated to have a dryweight of approximately 129 metric tons.

--- SENTENCE 12 ---
Based on published growth rates for massive Porites (Lough and Barnes 2000; Potts et al. 1985), the core skeletal density, and annual mean water temperature, this coral is estimated to be between 360 and 800 years old, though it may be considerably older.

--- SENTENCE 13 ---
It is surrounded by several smaller colonies, which measure between 4 and 28 m in circumference.

--- SENTENCE 14 ---
Approximately 1 km to the south of this site are another dozen massive Porites measuring 16–24 m in circumference as well as several this size on the northeast corner of the island, and a few others in the waters of nearby islands.

--- SENTENCE 15 ---
While these corals in Ta'u may not be as old as some deep water corals, they are among the oldest known on tropical hermatypic reefs.

--- SENTENCE 16 ---
Their large size, good health, and close proximity to each other implies that this location has had conditions conducive to coral growth for a long time.
